# 66 Bits of Wisdom From Warren Buffett: The Berkshire Letters (1965-2012)

Warren Buffett’s letters to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders are full of wisdom. In my [previous article](https://www.linkedin.com/today/post/article/20140507130556-21597783-buffett-s-core-concepts-the-berkshire-letters-1965-2012?trk=prof-post), I shared some of his more in-depth business anecdotes. Here I’ve collected some of the short quotes and comments peppered throughout his writing. They represent a broad cross section of Buffett’s interests and observations, ranging from economics and business to human behavior, education, and even music.

You’ll notice that although the letters start in 1965, the excerpts start in 1981. That is intentional — not because the older letters don’t have important lessons to offer, but because 1981 marks a distinct change in the style and nature of Buffett’s writing.

Prior to 1981, Buffett’s compositions were much shorter and very business-focused, much of what you’d expect when writing to shareholders. The 1981 letter represents a major stylistic change that would continue to the present day, filled with much broader topics and packed with little bits of wisdom.

While I would highly recommend [reading (and re-reading) each letter](http://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html), it is an intimidating commitment. Hopefully the following excerpts will convince you it would be worth your time.

1.   The Noah principle: “Predicting rain doesn’t count; building arks does.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1981

2.   “It has struck me that all men’s misfortunes spring from the single cause that they are unable to stay quietly in one room.” - Pascal via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1982

3.   “It’s difficult for an empty sack to stand upright.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1982

4.   “You can observe a lot by just watching.” - Yogi Berra via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1982

5.   “Don’t ask the barber whether you need a haircut.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1982

6.   “When ideas fail, words come in very handy.” - Goethe via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1983

7.   “The difficulty lies not in the new ideas but in escaping from the old ones.” - John Maynard Keynes via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1983

8.   “Phil Graham, when publisher of The Washington Post, described the daily newspaper as ‘a first rough draft of history.’” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1984

9.   “Academics seem to say, ‘Well, it may be all right in practice, but it will never work in theory.’” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1984

10.   “All I want to know is where I’m going to die so I’ll never go there.” - Warren Buffett discussing Charles Munger, Berkshire Letters 1985

11.   “More than any other time in history, mankind faces a crossroads. One path leads to despair and utter hopelessness, the other to total extinction. Let us pray we have the wisdom to choose correctly.” - Woody Allen via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

12.   “The intellect should be the servant of the heart, but not its slave.” - Comte via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

13.   “A horse that can count to 10 is a remarkable horse — not a remarkable mathematician.” - Samuel Johnson via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

14.   “Negotiating with one’s self seldom produces a barroom brawl.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

15.   “‘If it ain’t broke, don’t fix it’ is preferable to ‘purity at any price.’” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

16.   “I’ll be happy to accept a lottery ticket as a gift — but I’ll never buy one.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

17.   “I have but one lamp by which my feet are guided, and that is the lamp of experience.” - Patrick Henry via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

18.   “The future ain’t what it used to be.” - Pogo via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

19.   “No matter how great the talent or effort, some things just take time: You can’t produce a baby in one month by getting nine women pregnant.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1985

20.   “Marrying for money — probably a bad idea under any circumstances, but absolute madness if you are already rich.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1986

21.   “While the lamb may lie down with the lion, the lamb shouldn’t count on getting a whole lot of sleep.” - Woody Allen via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1986

22.   “I would rather be vaguely right than precisely wrong.” - John Maynard Keynes via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1986

23.   “What witch doctor has ever achieved fame and fortune by simply advising ‘Take two aspirins’?” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1987

24.   “It’s not wise to pick fights with people who buy ink by the barrel.” - Warren Buffett referencing Mark Twain, Berkshire Letters 1987

25.   “It has been far safer to steal large sums with a pen than small sums with a gun.”

- Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1988

26.   “Too much of a good thing can be wonderful.” - Mae West via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1988

27.   “If something can’t go on forever, it will end.” - Herb Stein via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1988

28.   “Never is there just one cockroach in the kitchen.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1989

29.   “Most men would rather die than think. Many do.” - Bertrand Russell via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1990

30.   “If history books were the key to riches, the Forbes 400 would consist of librarians.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1990

31.   “Fanaticism consists of redoubling your effort when you’ve forgotten your aim.” 

- George Santayana via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1992

32.   “Practice doesn’t make perfect; practice makes permanent.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1992

33.   “Fear is the foe of the faddist, but the friend of the fundamentalist.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1994

34.   “It’s probably true that hard work never killed anyone, but I figure why take the chance?” - Ronald Reagan via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1994

35.   “Why must bands play as if they will be paid by the decibel?” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1996

36.   “As investors...our reaction to a fermenting industry is much like our attitude toward space exploration: We applaud the endeavor but prefer to skip the ride.” 

- Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 1996

37.   “A small leak can sink a great ship.” - Benjamin Franklin via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2000

38.   “History teaches us that a crisis often causes problems to correlate in a manner undreamed of in more tranquil times.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2002

39.   “Who ever washes a rental car?” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2003

40.   “Many people who are smart, articulate, and admired have no real understanding of business. That’s no sin; they may shine elsewhere.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2003

41.   “As the country song laments, ‘How can I miss you if you won’t go away?’” 

- Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2003

42.   “Ignorance more frequently begets confidence than does knowledge.” - Charles Darwin via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2003

43.   “You only learn who has been swimming naked when the tide goes out.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2004

44.   “Worldly wisdom teaches that it is better for reputation to fail conventionally than to succeed unconventionally.” - John Maynard Keynes via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2004

45.   “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” - Matthew 6:21 via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2004

46.   “A man who tries to carry a cat home by its tail will learn a lesson that can be learned in no other way.” - Mark Twain via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2005

47.   “When a problem exists, whether in personnel or in business operations, the time to act is now.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2005

48.   “An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure.” - Benjamin Franklin via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2005

49.   “Whose bread I eat, his song I sing.” - German proverb via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2005

50.   “We shape our buildings, and afterwards our buildings shape us.” - Winston Churchill via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2006

51.   “We must hang together, or assuredly we shall hang separately.” - Benjamin Franklin via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2006

52.   “Once you’ve flown NetJets [privately], returning to commercial flights is like going back to holding hands.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2006

53.   “If his IQ was any lower, you would have to water him twice a day.” - Molly Ivins via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2007

54.   “Those who travel the high road in Washington need not fear heavy traffic.” - Alan Simpson via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2007

55.   “Tony and I feel like two hungry mosquitoes in a nudist camp. Juicy targets are everywhere.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2008

56.   “Beware of geeks bearing formulas.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2008

57.   “When forced to choose, I will not trade even a night’s sleep for the chance of extra profits.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2008

58.   “It’s not just whom you sleep with, but also whom _they_ are sleeping with.” 

- Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2008

59.   “When it’s raining gold, reach for a bucket, not a thimble.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2009

60.   “No matter how serene today may be, tomorrow is _always_ uncertain.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2010

61.   “Hire well, manage little.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2010

62.   “A house can be a nightmare if the buyer’s eyes are bigger than his wallet and if a lender — often protected by a government guarantee — facilitates his fantasy.” 

- Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2010

63.   “There’s plenty of money to be made in the center of the court.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2010

64.   “It’s not what you look at that matters, it’s what you see.” - Henry David Thoreau via Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2011

65.   “News, to put it simply, is what people don’t know that they want to know.” 

- Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2012

66.   “Wishing makes dreams come true only in Disney movies; it’s poison in business.” - Warren Buffett, Berkshire Letters 2012

There’s plenty more wisdom in the Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letters, and I’ll be putting together a few more articles highlighting Warren Buffett’s investment theories and management practices. Of course, the best way to glean these lessons is to [read the letters](http://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html) for yourself.
