The **University of Illinois Chicago** (**UIC**) is a [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university "Public university")[research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university "Research university") in the [Near West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_West_Side,_Chicago "Near West Side, Chicago") community area of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), Illinois, United States.

The university was the second institution established within the [University of Illinois System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_System "University of Illinois System") and it is now the largest university in the [Chicago metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area "Chicago metropolitan area"), having more than 35,000 students[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-6) enrolled in its 16 colleges. It is [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Classification_of_Institutions_of_Higher_Education "Carnegie Classification of Institutions of Higher Education") among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Carnegie-7)

### Beginnings

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Beginnings")]

The University of Illinois Chicago traces its origins to several health colleges founded during the late 19th century, including the Chicago College of Pharmacy, which opened in 1859, the College of Physicians and Surgeons (1882), and the Columbian College of Dentistry (1893).[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Columbian_College_of_Dentistry-8)

The [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois "University of Illinois") was chartered in 1867 in [Champaign-Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign-Urbana "Champaign-Urbana"), as the state's [land-grant university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university "Land-grant university"). In exchange for agreeing to the Champaign-Urbana location, Chicago-area legislators were promised that a "[polytechnical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_technology "Institute of technology")" branch would open in Chicago.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-IIU_established-9) The Chicago-based health colleges affiliated with the university in 1896–97, becoming fully incorporated into the University of Illinois in 1913, as the **Colleges of Medicine, Dentistry, and Pharmacy** on Chicago's west side. Medical education and research expanded in the succeeding decades, leading to the development of several other [health science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_science "Health science") colleges, which were brought together as the University of Illinois **Chicago Professional Colleges**. In 1936, the first act of newly elected state representative [Richard J. Daley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Daley "Richard J. Daley") was to introduce a resolution calling for the establishment of an undergraduate Chicago campus of the University of Illinois.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Daley_for_UIC-10)

### Navy Pier campus

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Navy Pier campus")]

As [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") was drawing to a close, Congress passed the [G.I. Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Bill "G.I. Bill") in 1944, which sought to reward veterans for their military service. Among other benefits, it provided educational funding, making college degrees far more attainable to a much-wider selection of the American public. In 1945, Daley, who was then a state senator, introduced four bills calling for a university in Chicago.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Daley_for_UIC-10) In 1946, realizing that they would be "besieged with applications," University of Illinois officials opened what was to be a temporary branch campus called the **Chicago Undergraduate Division** (CUD) on [Navy Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Pier "Navy Pier").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-pier-11) The campus was not a junior college, but rather had a curriculum based on Urbana's courses, and students who successfully completed the first two years' requirements could go on to Urbana and finish their degree.

Classes at the CUD campus began in October 1946, and approximately 4,000 students enrolled each semester.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-NavyPierHist-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-GIBillHist-13) Nicknamed "[Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard") on the rocks," three-quarters of its students were veterans on the [G.I. Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Bill "G.I. Bill"), many of whom were immigrants and most of whom worked other part-time jobs to support themselves. It also accommodated first-generation college students from working families who commuted from home. (Navy Pier's Undergraduate Division makes an appearance in [Robert Pirsig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pirsig "Robert Pirsig")'s _[Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen\_and\_the\_Art\_of\_Motorcycle\_Maintenance "Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance")_; its protagonist taught freshman English there.)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-pier-11)

Due to high demand for a public university education in Chicago, the university made plans to create a permanent degree-granting campus in Chicago. Students at CUD generally needed to transfer after two years to a more expensive private college in Chicago,[[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-14) or go to the [main campus in Champaign–Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign "University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign"), where there were fewer opportunities for work, while at the same time as going to school.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-pier-11)

### Congress Circle campus

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Congress Circle campus")]

[![Image 9](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago_-2.jpg/250px-University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago_-2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago_-2.jpg)

UIC's campus

Daley succeeded in getting the state senate in 1951 to pass a bill calling for a Chicago campus. Daley then became mayor of Chicago in 1955 and pressed the University of Illinois to upgrade the Chicago Undergraduate Center to a full-fledged four-year institution.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Daley_for_UIC-10) After a long and controversial site decision process,[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-RelocationHist-15) in 1961, Mayor Daley offered the Harrison and [Halsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halsted_Street "Halsted Street") Streets site for the new campus.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-CircleCampHist-16) In December 1961, the final decision to establish a four-year university in Chicago was made. In that same year, the Chicago Professional Colleges became the **University of Illinois at the Medical Center** (UIMC).[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-auto-17)

Florence Scala led the fight against Mayor Richard J. Daley's plan to demolish her Italian-American neighborhood to construct the campus. In 1963, the trustees of [Hull House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_House "Hull House") accepted an offer of $875,000 for the settlement building. [Jessie Binford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Binford "Jessie Binford") and Scala took the case to the Supreme Court. Scala led marches to protest the construction. The U.S. Supreme Court declined to consider an appeal and the settlement was closed on March 28, 1963. During the construction of the 100-acre (0.40 km 2) UIC campus, 200 businesses and 800 homes were bulldozed in Little Italy, with 5,000 residents displaced.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-18)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-auto-17)

In 1963, construction began on the university's new Chicago campus at Harrison and Halsted Streets south of the [Greektown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greektown,_Chicago "Greektown, Chicago") neighborhood. In February 1965, the new Chicago campus opened and was to be named the **University of Illinois at Congress Circle** (UICC) referencing the nearby [Circle Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Interchange "Circle Interchange") of [I-290](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_290_(Illinois) "Interstate 290 (Illinois)") and [I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Illinois "Interstate 90 in Illinois")/[I-94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_94_in_Illinois "Interstate 94 in Illinois")).[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-CircleCamp2Hist-19) Shortly before opening, the Congress Expressway was renamed the Eisenhower Expressway and the interchange Chicago Circle, so the campus was renamed to **University of Illinois at Chicago Circle** (UICC). UICC was designed in the [brutalist style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture "Brutalist architecture") by [Walter Netsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Netsch "Walter Netsch") of [Skidmore, Owings and Merrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skidmore,_Owings_and_Merrill "Skidmore, Owings and Merrill"), a Chicago-based architectural firm responsible for many of today's tallest skyscrapers.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-UICHist-20)[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-UICArchDept-21)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-encyclopedia_of_chicago-22) Unlike the CUD campus, Circle was a degree-granting institution. Within five years of the campus' opening, in addition to [undergraduate degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_degrees "Undergraduate degrees"), virtually every department offered [graduate degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_degrees "Graduate degrees").

### Consolidation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Consolidation")]

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/University_of_Illinois_Chicago_School_of_Law_%2851575326034%29.jpg/250px-University_of_Illinois_Chicago_School_of_Law_%2851575326034%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:University_of_Illinois_Chicago_School_of_Law_(51575326034).jpg)

The university absorbed the [School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago_School_of_Law "University of Illinois Chicago School of Law") in 2019.

In September 1982, the University of Illinois system consolidated UICC and UIMC to form the _University of Illinois at Chicago_ (**UIC**), later dropping the "at" in 2020[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Writing_Style_Guide_-_University_of_Illinois_System-23) to become **University of Illinois Chicago**.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-CircleCamp3Hist-24)

In 2000, UIC began developing the south campus. The expansion of UIC south of [Roosevelt Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Road "Roosevelt Road") increased on-campus living space and research facilities.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-When_East_Met_West-25)

In 2015, UIC conducted its first university-wide winter commencements, adding that as a permanent option going forward.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-26)

In 2019, the John Marshall Law School, founded in 1899 and named for the American jurist [John Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Marshall "John Marshall"), became affiliated with the university.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-27) In 2021, the UI Trustees voted to officially rename UIC John Marshall Law School as [UIC School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago_School_of_Law "University of Illinois Chicago School of Law"), citing Marshall's "role as a slave trader, slave owner of hundreds of slaves, pro-slavery jurisprudence, and racist views."[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-28)

## Campus

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Campus")]

[![Image 11](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago_-1.jpg/250px-University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago_-1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago_-1.jpg)

[University Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Hall_(UIC) "University Hall (UIC)"), located on UIC's East Campus

UIC is Chicago's largest university with more than 33,000 students, 12,000 employees, 16 colleges and the state's major public medical center. The East Campus was designed in the [brutalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist "Brutalist") style by [Walter Netsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Netsch "Walter Netsch").[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-29) The plan included second-story walkways that connected all of the buildings.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-30) Some of the later buildings in Netsch's design were not rectilinear (the Behavioral Sciences Building) and even irregularly shaped (Science and Engineering South, and the never-completed Art & Architecture building). These demonstrated his idea of "field theory": designs which used squares and rotations of squares superimposed on one another. While the buildings are largely intact, the walkways were taken down in phases during the early 1990s to make the campus more welcoming.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-31) Considerable effort has been expended to modify the original Netsch campus plan to create the feel of a traditional college campus. For example, the area in front of the main administration building, University Hall, has been the site of several renovations in the last decade; and, [Credit Union 1 Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Union_1_Arena "Credit Union 1 Arena") (formerly the UIC Pavilion, a sports arena) was added in 1982.

[![Image 12](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/UIC_East_Campus.jpg/250px-UIC_East_Campus.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UIC_East_Campus.jpg)

A view of the quad from the top floor of University Hall

The East Campus is located on the [Near West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_West_Side,_Chicago "Near West Side, Chicago"), just south of [Greektown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greektown,_Chicago "Greektown, Chicago") and a 15-minute walk from [downtown Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Loop "Chicago Loop"). The juxtaposition of campus and commercial density was a direct result of large-scale urban renewal led by Mayor [Richard J. Daley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Daley "Richard J. Daley").[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-RelocationHist-15)

The Circle Court Shopping Center was a [shopping center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_center "Shopping center") and [office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office "Office") complex at 500 South Racine Avenue in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"). Completed in 1973, the five-acre property contained 209,600 square feet of space and 350 parking spaces.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-32) In 1990, the University of Illinois authorized negotiations to acquire the property for university use.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-33) By 1993, university records identified it as the Student Services Building and described it as the former Circle Court Shopping Center.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-34) By May 1993, university records referred to the property as the Student Services Building and identified it as the former Circle Court Shopping Center.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-35)

The West Campus, also on the [Near West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_West_Side,_Chicago "Near West Side, Chicago"), is much older and includes some buildings built in the [collegiate gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_gothic "Collegiate gothic") style. The colleges of Medicine, Pharmacy, Nursing, Dentistry, Applied Health Sciences and Public Health, as well as the Library of the Health Sciences are all located on the West Campus. The West Campus is in the heart of the [Illinois Medical District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Medical_District "Illinois Medical District") where the [University of Illinois Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Medical_Center "University of Illinois Medical Center") is located.

[![Image 13](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Platform_at_UIC-Halsted%2C_looking_east.jpg/250px-Platform_at_UIC-Halsted%2C_looking_east.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Platform_at_UIC-Halsted,_looking_east.jpg)

[UIC–Halsted station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIC%E2%80%93Halsted_station "UIC–Halsted station") on the [CTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Transit_Authority "Chicago Transit Authority")'s [Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(CTA) "Blue Line (CTA)")

The [Chicago Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Transit_Authority "Chicago Transit Authority")'s [Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(CTA) "Blue Line (CTA)"), part of the [Chicago 'L'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_'L' "Chicago 'L'"), runs through the median of the [Eisenhower Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenhower_Expressway "Eisenhower Expressway") along the north side of the campus. Three Blue Line stations are close to the university: [UIC-Halsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIC-Halsted_(CTA) "UIC-Halsted (CTA)"), [Racine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racine_(CTA) "Racine (CTA)"), and [Illinois Medical District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Medical_District_(CTA) "Illinois Medical District (CTA)"). The [Pink Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Line_(CTA) "Pink Line (CTA)") serves UIC's west campus on [Polk Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polk_(CTA) "Polk (CTA)") and runs directly to [the Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Loop_(CTA) "The Loop (CTA)").

### Campus housing

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Campus housing")]

[![Image 14](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/James_Stukel_Towers.jpg/250px-James_Stukel_Towers.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:James_Stukel_Towers.jpg)

UIC's James Stukel Towers residence hall with downtown Chicago and [Willis Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Tower "Willis Tower") in the background

UIC offers ten residence halls for its students. The East Campus contains five residence halls, the South Campus contains three, and the West Campus contains two. Until the South Campus expansion, UIC students were still predominantly commuters. However, the administration has worked to change the campus to one where most students are residential.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-36) Nearly 6,000 students live within one-and-a-half miles of campus.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-37) 3,800 students, including over half of all freshmen, live in UIC's nine residence halls.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-38) There are also thousands of apartments within walking distance to classes.

On the East Campus, Commons West and Commons South are traditional halls with double rooms opening into a common hallway; each floor shares a common bathroom. Courtyard and Commons North are cluster-style buildings with rooms grouped to share a small private bathroom. These four buildings are connected to the Student Center East which houses a cafeteria, the campus bookstore, a convenience store, bowling/billiards, a barber shop, and the Inner Circle (an assortment of fast food restaurants).

[![Image 15](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/UIC_Courtyard_%26_Commons_Dormitory.jpg/250px-UIC_Courtyard_%26_Commons_Dormitory.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UIC_Courtyard_&_Commons_Dormitory.jpg)

Courtyard & Commons dormitory buildings on the east campus (next to the building of Architecture, Design, & The Arts)

West Campus housing is composed of the Single Student Residence (SSR, apartments for graduate students) and Polk Street Residence (cluster style rooms). South Campus is home to Marie Robinson Hall and Thomas Beckham Hall, both apartment style buildings. In the fall of 2007, James Stukel Towers opened containing suite style rooms with a bathroom and living room. In keeping with UIC culture, students often refer to residence halls by abbreviations instead of their full names (e.g. "TBH" instead of "Thomas Beckham Hall").

The main purpose of the SSR is to house graduate students, undergraduate students of the ages 24 and older, and professional students. The third and fourth floors house undergraduate students of the ages of 21 through 23. The nursing house is located on the 16th floor. The fifth floor of the SSR has a pilot program for students with families. The program can house up to 15 families.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-SSRDetail-39) Residents of the family program are zoned to [Chicago Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Public_Schools "Chicago Public Schools").[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-SSRDetail-39) Families living in the SSR are assigned to Washington Irving Elementary School and [Crane High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crane_High_School_(Illinois) "Crane High School (Illinois)").[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-40)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-41)

The Academic and Residential Complex, ARC, was opened in response to the ongoing enrollment increase at the university. The building consists of large lecture halls, smaller discussion section classrooms, meeting rooms, and student housing. The building also has a small gym for student occupants and a [Starbucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starbucks "Starbucks").[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-42)

### Student recreation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Student recreation")]

The UIC Student Recreation Facility (SRF) is a recreational complex for UIC students. Opened in spring 2006, the SRF features a three-story [climbing wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climbing_wall "Climbing wall"), multipurpose courts for games, and a pool with adjoining lazy river. There is also a recreational facility on the west side of the campus. The Sport and Fitness Center, SFC, comprises a fitness floor, Olympic pool, steam room, multipurpose courts, and racquetball courts and much more. UIC Campus Recreation also oversees the Outdoor Field Complex, OFC, on the south side of campus. This outdoor complex comprises two large multipurpose fields.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-43) Campus Recreation hosts a variety of programs that promotes the wellness and well-being of students throughout the academic year. Such events includes RecFest, Destress fest, and many more.

### Medical center and College of Medicine

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Medical center and College of Medicine")]

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/UIC_College_of_Medicine_West_Tower_-_3182467036.jpg/250px-UIC_College_of_Medicine_West_Tower_-_3182467036.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UIC_College_of_Medicine_West_Tower_-_3182467036.jpg)

UIC College of Medicine West Tower

The University of Illinois College of Medicine offers a four-year program leading to the MD degree at three different sites in Illinois: Chicago, Peoria, and Rockford. (Urbana-Champaign faculty no longer participate in the University of Illinois College of Medicine, with the establishment of the [Carle Illinois College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carle_Illinois_College_of_Medicine "Carle Illinois College of Medicine"), hosted entirely at the flagship campus.) UIC is a major part of the [Illinois Medical District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Medical_District "Illinois Medical District") (IMD). While IMD's billing itself "the nation's largest urban medical district" may be up for debate, the district is a major economic force contributing $3.3 billion to the local economy and supporting 50,000 jobs. In popular culture, UIC College of Medicine was the Medical School affiliated with Cook County Hospital in the television series E.R.

### Campus renovations

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Campus renovations")]

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/UIC_Campus.jpg/250px-UIC_Campus.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UIC_Campus.jpg)

Douglass, Grant, and Lincoln Halls with the [Chicago Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Loop "Chicago Loop") skyline in the background

After the major $550 million South Campus expansion, the university began renovating existing facilities on campus. This focus is in part due to a constrained state budget: state funds for new buildings are scarce. Since renovation is less costly, this approach is being used to update facilities.

The first such renovations were to three original "pillbox" buildings: Grant Hall, Lincoln Hall, and Douglas Hall.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-44) The renovated buildings use solar and geothermal power which required digging fifty wells 500 feet into the ground east of University Hall. The energy savings of 20–25% led to Lincoln Hall and Douglas Hall being certified US [LEED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_in_Energy_and_Environmental_Design "Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design") Gold. Grant Hall did not apply for LEED certification even though it was the prototype for many of the features found on Lincoln and Douglas Halls.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Lincoln_Hall_sustainability-45)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Grant_Hall_sustainability-46)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Douglas_Hall_latest-47)[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-Douglas_Hall_sustainability-48) The new fascia are all glass and the buildings contain facilities for faculty and students. There are plans to expand the renovation project to every lecture hall cluster if the pilot program is successful.

Other campus renovations include the roof of the Behavioral Sciences Building and the terrace of the Education, Performing Arts, and Social Work building.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-49) A brand new building housing the College of Engineering was opened in 2019 to accommodate the increased enrollment in the college. It features the only [high bay structural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_bay_warehouse "High bay warehouse") laboratory in the city of Chicago.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-50)

To accommodate the rising enrollment in the computer science department, a new building is being constructed called the Computer Design Research and Learning Center (CDRLC); it is slated to be completed in the fall of 2024, with a budget of $117.8 million.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-51)

East campus in the spring

The university's Office of Sustainability was founded in January 2008.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-About_the_Office_of_Sustainability-52) Current sustainability initiatives include lighting upgrades, building envelope improvements, metering upgrades, and landscape waste composting.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-2010_Annual_Report_on_Sustainability-53) Recent work on Grant, Lincoln, and Douglas Halls included upgrading them to use geothermal heat pumps, which efficiently heat and cool the building. The Illinois–Chicago (UIC) athletic teams are called the Flames, a reference to the [1871 Great Chicago Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Great_Chicago_Fire "1871 Great Chicago Fire"). The university is a member of the [Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I "NCAA Division I") level of the [National Collegiate Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association "National Collegiate Athletic Association") (NCAA), primarily competing in the [Missouri Valley Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Valley_Conference "Missouri Valley Conference") (MVC) since the 2022–23 academic year.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-89) The Flames previously competed in the D-I [Horizon League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_League "Horizon League") from 1994–95 to 2021–22; in the D-I [Mid-Continent Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_League "Summit League") (Mid-Con, now currently known as the Summit League since the 2007–08 school year) from 1982–83 to 1993–94; as an [NCAA D-I Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_independent_schools "NCAA Division I independent schools") during the 1981–82 school year; and in the [Chicagoland Collegiate Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicagoland_Collegiate_Athletic_Conference "Chicagoland Collegiate Athletic Conference") (CCAC) of the [National Association of Intercollegiate Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Intercollegiate_Athletics "National Association of Intercollegiate Athletics") (NAIA) from 1949–50 to about 1980–81.

UIC competes in 18 intercollegiate varsity sports:[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago#cite_note-90) Men's sports include baseball, basketball, cross country, soccer, swimming & diving, tennis and track & field (indoor and outdoor); while women's sports include basketball, cross country, golf, soccer, softball, swimming & diving, tennis, track & field (indoor and outdoor) and volleyball.

### Men's soccer

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Men's soccer")]

In September 2006, the men's soccer team earned its highest ranking in school history when the SoccerTimes.com College Coaches Poll pegged the Flames at No. 6 in the country. In November 2006, UIC defeated Western Illinois 3–0 in the opening round of the NCAA tournament before falling in the second round to Notre Dame 1–0. UIC finished the 2006 season as the nation's best defensive squad after allowing a mere eight goals in over 1993 minutes of play during 21 matches for a goals-against average (GAA) of 0.36. The GAA was tops in the nation in 2006 and it also ranked fifth all-time in NCAA history. UIC posted 13 shutouts and never allowed more than a single goal in a match. UIC also allowed just two goals after intermission the entire season. Along with the GAA mark, UIC posted the nation's best save percentage with a 0.908 rate.

In 2007, UIC soccer's successful season culminated in an Elite-Eight appearance in the NCAA tournament by way of wins over No. 12 St. Louis, Northwestern, and No. 8 Creighton. In a bid for a Final-Four appearance, UIC fell to Massachusetts 2–1. At season's end, UIC had a record of 13–6–6 and was named a top 10 team by the [National Soccer Coaches Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_Coaches "United Soccer Coaches") (NSCAA).

### Baseball

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Baseball")]

UIC's baseball team recorded 30 or more victories in a nine-year stretch from 2001 to 2010, won seven straight Horizon League Championships, and advanced to an NCAA regional four times (2003, 2005, 2007, 2008). UIC baseball has recorded regional wins against No. 1 Long Beach State in 2007 and No. 2 Dallas Baptist University in 2008.

### Gymnastics

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Chicago&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Gymnastics")]

In 1978 and 1979 the UIC men's gymnastics team won the school's only NCAA team titles at the Division II Championships. Following the 1979 season the men's gymnastics program entered Division I competition and finished the season ranked 10th, Nationally. After the successful 1980 season the remainder of the UIC athletic teams ascended to Division I status. The 1996 men's gymnastics team finished the season in 9th place, the school's highest final ranking among all sports. UIC Men's Gymnastics individual Division I All America honorees include: Paul Fina (Rings), Mike Costa (Pommel Horse, twice), Barry McDonald (Parallel Bars), Shannon Welker (Floor Exercise), Neil Faustino (Vault), and Andrew Stover (Horizontal Bar).
