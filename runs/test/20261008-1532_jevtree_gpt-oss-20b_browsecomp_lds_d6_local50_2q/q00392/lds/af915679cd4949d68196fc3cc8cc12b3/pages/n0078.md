**Johns Hopkins University** (**JHU**), often abbreviated as **Hopkins**, is a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university "Private university")[research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university "Research university") in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"), Maryland, United States. Incorporated in 1867 and opened in 1876 based on the European research institution model, Johns Hopkins is considered to be the first research university in the U.S.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-9)

The university was named for its first benefactor, the American businessman and [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers "Quakers") philanthropist [Johns Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins "Johns Hopkins").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-There_is_only_one_Johns_Hopkins-10) Hopkins's $7 million bequest (equivalent to $166 million in 2024)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-inflation-USGDP-11) to establish the university and the affiliated [Johns Hopkins Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Hospital "Johns Hopkins Hospital") in Baltimore was the largest [philanthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropy "Philanthropy") gift in U.S. history up to that time.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Facts_at_a_Glance-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-racial_record-13)[Daniel Coit Gilman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Coit_Gilman "Daniel Coit Gilman"), who was inaugurated as [Johns Hopkins's first president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Presidents_of_Johns_Hopkins_University "Category:Presidents of Johns Hopkins University") on February 22, 1876,[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Inaugural_Address_of_Daniel_Coit_Gilman-14) led the university to revolutionize higher education in the U.S. by integrating teaching and research.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Benson_Page_x-15) In 1900, Johns Hopkins became a founding member of the [Association of American Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Universities "Association of American Universities").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-16) The [School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine"), established in 1893, has achieved international recognition for its pioneering biomedical research.

The university consists of ten academic divisions mostly divided among four campuses in Baltimore, with some graduate campuses in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy"), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), and [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.")[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-17) The university's two undergraduate divisions, the [Zanvyl Krieger School of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanvyl_Krieger_School_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Zanvyl Krieger School of Arts and Sciences") and the [Whiting School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiting_School_of_Engineering "Whiting School of Engineering"), are located on the [Homewood campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homewood_Campus_of_Johns_Hopkins_University "Homewood Campus of Johns Hopkins University") adjacent to Baltimore's [Charles Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Village,_Baltimore "Charles Village, Baltimore") neighborhood.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-homewoodcampus-18) The [School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine"), [School of Nursing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Nursing "Johns Hopkins School of Nursing"), and [Bloomberg School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_School_of_Public_Health "Bloomberg School of Public Health") are located on the medical campus in East Baltimore, alongside Johns Hopkins Hospital.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-eastbaltimorecampus-19) The university also consists of the [Peabody Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Institute "Peabody Institute") in Baltimore's [Mount Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon,_Baltimore "Mount Vernon, Baltimore") neighborhood, [Applied Physics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Physics_Laboratory "Applied Physics Laboratory") in [Howard County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_County,_Maryland "Howard County, Maryland"), [School of Advanced International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Advanced_International_Studies "School of Advanced International Studies"), [School of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Education "Johns Hopkins School of Education"), and [Carey Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carey_Business_School "Carey Business School").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-hopkinscampuses-20)

Founded in 1883, the [Johns Hopkins Blue Jays men's lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Blue_Jays_men's_lacrosse "Johns Hopkins Blue Jays men's lacrosse") team, which is an affiliate member in the [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference "Big Ten Conference"), has won 44 national titles.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-22) The university's other sports teams compete in [Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III "NCAA Division III") of the [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association "National Collegiate Athletic Association"), where they are members of the [Centennial Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_Conference "Centennial Conference").

[![Image 11](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Heidelberg_Universit%C3%A4tsbibliothek_2003_b.jpg/250px-Heidelberg_Universit%C3%A4tsbibliothek_2003_b.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Heidelberg_Universit%C3%A4tsbibliothek_2003_b.jpg)

The university model of [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University") in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), Germany, was replicated in the founding of Johns Hopkins University

On his death in 1873, [Johns Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins "Johns Hopkins"), a [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers "Quakers") businessman and childless bachelor, bequeathed $7 million (equivalent to $166 million in 2024)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-inflation-USGDP-11) to fund a hospital and university in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore").[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-HC-23)

At the time, this donation, generated primarily from the [Baltimore and Ohio Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_and_Ohio_Railroad "Baltimore and Ohio Railroad"),[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Who_Was_Johns_Hopkins?-24) was the largest philanthropic gift in the history of the United States,[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Facts_at_a_Glance-12) and endowment was then the largest in America.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-racial_record-13)

Hopkins’s relationship to slavery has been the subject of historical debate. In 2020, research commissioned by the university, and led by historian [Martha S. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_S._Jones "Martha S. Jones"), cited 1840 and 1850 census records as evidence that individuals recorded as enslaved were his property and associated with his household.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-26) Subsequent scholarship has questioned this interpretation, noting the limitations of census data, particularly the ambiguity of the 1850 slave schedule, and the absence of corroborating documentation such as tax or probate records linking Hopkins directly to slaveholding.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-27)

The first name of philanthropist Johns Hopkins comes from the surname of his great-grandmother, Margaret Johns, who married Gerard Hopkins.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Who_Was_Johns_Hopkins?-24) They named last their son Johns Hopkins, who named his son Samuel Hopkins. Samuel named one of his sons for his father, and that son became the university's benefactor. [Milton Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Eisenhower "Milton Eisenhower"), a former university president, once spoke at a convention in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh") where the [master of ceremonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_ceremonies "Master of ceremonies") introduced him as "President of _John_ Hopkins". Eisenhower retorted that he was "glad to be here in _Pitt_ burgh".[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Cheesecake_on_the_Tart_Side-28)

The original board opted for an entirely novel university model dedicated to the discovery of knowledge at an advanced level, extending that of contemporary Germany.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-muller-29) Building on the [Humboldtian model of higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldtian_model_of_higher_education "Humboldtian model of higher education"), the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") education model of [Wilhelm von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Humboldt "Wilhelm von Humboldt"), it became dedicated to research. It was especially [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University") and its long academic research history on which the new institution tried to model itself.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-muller-29)[_[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_] Johns Hopkins thereby became the model of the modern research university in the United States. Its success eventually shifted higher education in the United States from a focus on teaching revealed and/or applied knowledge to the scientific discovery of new knowledge.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-30)

### 19th century

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 19th century")]

Further information: [Daniel Coit Gilman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Coit_Gilman "Daniel Coit Gilman"), [Johns Hopkins Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Hospital "Johns Hopkins Hospital"), [Johns Hopkins School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine"), and [Johns Hopkins University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University_Press "Johns Hopkins University Press")

[![Image 13](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Daniel_Coit_Gilman1.jpg/250px-Daniel_Coit_Gilman1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Daniel_Coit_Gilman1.jpg)

[Daniel Coit Gilman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Coit_Gilman "Daniel Coit Gilman"), the first president of Johns Hopkins University

The trustees worked alongside four notable university presidents, [Charles William Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_William_Eliot "Charles William Eliot") of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), [Andrew D. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Dickson_White "Andrew Dickson White") of [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), [Noah Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Porter "Noah Porter") of [Yale College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_College "Yale College"), and [James B. Angell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Burrill_Angell "James Burrill Angell") of [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"). They each supported [Daniel Coit Gilman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Coit_Gilman "Daniel Coit Gilman") to lead the new university and he became the university's first president.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-PH-31) Gilman, a [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University")-educated scholar, had been serving as president of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") prior to this appointment.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-PH-31) In preparation for the university's founding, [Daniel Coit Gilman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Coit_Gilman "Daniel Coit Gilman") visited [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg "University of Freiburg") and other German universities.

Gilman launched what many at the time considered an audacious and unprecedented academic experiment to merge teaching and research. He dismissed the idea that the two were mutually exclusive: "The best teachers are usually those who are free, competent and willing to make original researches in the library and the laboratory," he stated.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-32)

[![Image 14](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Hopkins_Hall%2C_1885.jpg/250px-Hopkins_Hall%2C_1885.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hopkins_Hall,_1885.jpg)

Hopkins Hall on the original [Downtown Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Baltimore "Downtown Baltimore") campus, c.1885

To implement his plan, Gilman recruited internationally known researchers including the mathematician [James Joseph Sylvester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joseph_Sylvester "James Joseph Sylvester"); the biologist [H. Newell Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Newell_Martin "H. Newell Martin"); the physicist [Henry Augustus Rowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Augustus_Rowland "Henry Augustus Rowland"), the first president of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society"), the [classical scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_scholars "Classical scholars")[Basil Gildersleeve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_Gildersleeve "Basil Gildersleeve"), and Charles D. Morris;[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-university-33) the economist [Richard T. Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_T._Ely "Richard T. Ely"); and the chemist [Ira Remsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Remsen "Ira Remsen"), who became the second president of the university in 1901.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-34)

Gilman focused on the expansion of graduate education and support of faculty research. The new university fused advanced scholarship with such professional schools as medicine and engineering. Hopkins became the national trendsetter in [doctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") programs and the host for numerous scholarly journals and associations.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-35) The [Johns Hopkins University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University_Press "Johns Hopkins University Press"), founded in 1878, is the oldest American [university press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_press "University press") in continuous operation.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-36)

[![Image 15](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Johns_Hopkins_Hospital%2C_early_photo.jpg/250px-Johns_Hopkins_Hospital%2C_early_photo.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Johns_Hopkins_Hospital,_early_photo.jpg)

[Johns Hopkins Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Hospital "Johns Hopkins Hospital"), c.1880s–1890s

With the completion of [Johns Hopkins Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Hospital "Johns Hopkins Hospital") in 1889 and the [medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine") in 1893, the university's research-focused mode of instruction soon began attracting world-renowned faculty members who would become major figures in the emerging field of academic medicine, including [William Osler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Osler "William Osler"), [William Halsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Halsted "William Halsted"), [Howard Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Atwood_Kelly "Howard Atwood Kelly"), and [William Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Welch "William H. Welch").[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-37) During this period the university further made history by becoming the first medical school to admit women on an equal basis with men and to require a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree"), based on the efforts of [Mary E. Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Garrett "Mary Garrett"), who had endowed the school at Gilman's request.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-38) The [school of medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine") was America's first coeducational, graduate-level medical school, and became a prototype for academic medicine that emphasized bedside learning, research projects, and laboratory training.

In his will and in his instructions to the trustees of the university and the hospital, Hopkins requested that both institutions be built upon the vast grounds of his Baltimore estate, Clifton. When Gilman assumed the presidency, he decided that it would be best to use the university's endowment for recruiting faculty and students, deciding to, as it has been paraphrased, "build men, not buildings."[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-39) In his will Hopkins stipulated that none of his endowment should be used for construction; only interest on the principal could be used for this purpose. Unfortunately, stocks in The [Baltimore and Ohio Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_and_Ohio_Railroad "Baltimore and Ohio Railroad"), which would have generated most of the interest, became virtually worthless soon after Hopkins's death. The university's first home was thus in Downtown Baltimore, delaying plans to site the university in Clifton.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-HC-23)

### 20th century

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 20th century")]

Further information: [Applied Physics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Physics_Laboratory "Applied Physics Laboratory"), [School of Advanced International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Advanced_International_Studies "School of Advanced International Studies"), [Peabody Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Institute "Peabody Institute"), and [Whiting School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiting_School_of_Engineering "Whiting School of Engineering")

In the early 20th century, the university outgrew its buildings and the trustees began to search for a new home. Developing Clifton for the university was too costly, and 30 acres (12 ha) of the estate had to be sold to the city as public park. A solution was achieved by a team of prominent locals who acquired the estate in north Baltimore known as the [Homewood Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homewood_Campus_of_Johns_Hopkins_University "Homewood Campus of Johns Hopkins University"). On February 22, 1902, this land was formally transferred to the university. The flagship building, [Gilman Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilman_Hall "Gilman Hall"), was completed in 1915. The [School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiting_School_of_Engineering "Whiting School of Engineering") relocated in fall of 1914 and the [Zanvyl Krieger School of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanvyl_Krieger_School_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Zanvyl Krieger School of Arts and Sciences") followed in 1916. These decades saw the ceding of lands by the university for the public Wyman Park and Wyman Park Dell and the [Baltimore Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Museum_of_Art "Baltimore Museum of Art"), coalescing in the contemporary area of 140 acres (57 ha).[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-HC-23)

Prior to becoming the main Johns Hopkins campus, the Homewood estate had initially been the gift of Charles Carroll of [Carrollton, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrollton,_Maryland "Carrollton, Maryland"), a planter and signer of the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence"), to his son Charles Carroll Jr. The original structure, the 1801 [Homewood House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homewood_Museum "Homewood Museum"), still stands and serves as an on-campus museum.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-HH-40) The brick and marble [Federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_architecture "Federal architecture") style of Homewood House became the architectural inspiration for much of the university campus versus the [Collegiate Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_Gothic "Collegiate Gothic") style of other historic American universities.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-HH-40)

In 1909, the university was among the first to start adult [continuing education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuing_education "Continuing education") programs and in 1916 it founded the nation's first [school of public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Bloomberg_School_of_Public_Health "Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health").[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-41)

Since the 1910s, Johns Hopkins University has famously been a "fertile cradle" to [Arthur Lovejoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Lovejoy "Arthur Lovejoy")'s [history of ideas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ideas "History of ideas").[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Paulson1970-42)

Since 1942, the [Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory (APL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Physics_Laboratory "Applied Physics Laboratory") has served as a major governmental defense contractor. In tandem with on-campus research, Johns Hopkins has every year since 1979 had the highest federal research funding of any American university.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-43)

Professional schools of international affairs and music were established in 1950 and 1977, respectively, when the [School of Advanced International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Advanced_International_Studies "School of Advanced International Studies")[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-44) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), and the [Peabody Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Institute "Peabody Institute")[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-45) in Baltimore were incorporated into the university.

### 21st century

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 21st century")]

Further information: [Carey Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carey_Business_School "Carey Business School") and [Johns Hopkins School of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Education "Johns Hopkins School of Education")

The early decades of the 21st century saw expansion across the university's institutions in both physical and population sizes. Notably, a planned 88-acre expansion to the medical campus began in 2013.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Medical_Campus_Expansion-46) Completed construction on the [Homewood campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homewood_campus "Homewood campus") has included a new [biomedical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_engineering "Biomedical engineering") building in the [Johns Hopkins University Department of Biomedical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University_Department_of_Biomedical_Engineering "Johns Hopkins University Department of Biomedical Engineering"), a new library, a new biology wing, an extensive renovation of the flagship Gilman Hall, and the reconstruction of the main university entrance.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Charles_Street_Reconstruction-47)

These years also brought about the rapid development of the university's professional schools of education and business. From 1999 until 2007, these disciplines had been joined within the School of Professional Studies in Business and Education (SPSBE), itself a reshuffling of several earlier ventures. The 2007 split, combined with new funding and leadership initiatives, has led to the simultaneous emergence of the [Johns Hopkins School of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Education "Johns Hopkins School of Education") and the [Carey Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carey_Business_School "Carey Business School").[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-new_professional_schools-48)

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Legg_mason_tower.jpg/250px-Legg_mason_tower.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Legg_mason_tower.jpg)

[Legg Mason Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legg_Mason_Tower "Legg Mason Tower"), home of the new [Carey Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carey_Business_School "Carey Business School")

On November 18, 2018, it was announced that [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg "Michael Bloomberg") would make a donation to his alma mater of $1.8 billion, marking the largest private donation in modern history to an institution of [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education "Higher education") and bringing Bloomberg's total contribution to the school in excess of $3.3 billion.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-49)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-50)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-51)[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-52)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-53)[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-54) Bloomberg's $1.8 billion gift allows the school to practice [need-blind admission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need-blind_admission "Need-blind admission") and meet the full financial need of admitted students.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-55)[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-56)

In January 2019, the university announced[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-57) an agreement to purchase the [Newseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newseum "Newseum"), located at 555 Pennsylvania Ave. NW, in the heart of [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), with plans to locate all of its Washington, D.C.–based graduate programs there. In an interview with _[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Atlantic "The Atlantic")_, the president of Johns Hopkins stated that, "the purchase is an opportunity to position the university, literally, to better contribute its expertise to national- and international-policy discussions."[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-58)

In late 2019, the university's Coronavirus Research Center began tracking worldwide cases of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") by compiling data from hundreds of sources around the world.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-EW-59) This led to the university becoming one of the most cited sources for data about the pandemic.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-EW-59)

In November 2025, following the $1.8 billion gift from alumnus Michael Bloomberg in 2018, the university announced tuition-free plans, for current students in the spring 2026 semester and for new enrollees in the 2026-27 year who were from families earning less than $200,000, in support of making education available to all students based on merit and not means, and citing U.S. Census data showing that around 85% of American households would be eligible. For students from families earning under $100,000, additional aid would cover tuition, fees and living expenses, thus requiring no support from their families.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-60)

#### Johns Hopkins Police Department

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Johns Hopkins Police Department")]

In 2019, Johns Hopkins sought and received permission from the [Maryland General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_General_Assembly "Maryland General Assembly") to create a private police force to patrol in and around the three Baltimore campuses.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-61) The proposal generated opposition from students, faculty, and surrounding community associations.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-62)[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-neighborhoods-map-63)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-64)[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-inside-HE-concerns-65) A resolution expressing concerns and raising questions about accountability and oversight of the proposed police department was unanimously passed by the Homewood Faculty Assembly.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-pugh-corruption-garland-66) Protests culminated in a month-long sit-in and building occupation at Garland Hall, the university's administrative building, which ended after [Baltimore Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Police_Department "Baltimore Police Department") surrounded the building and arrested the participants in May 2019.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-garland-arrest-67)[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-68)

Following the murder of [George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd "George Floyd") in 2020, thousands of faculty, staff, students, and community members petitioned the university to reconsider the department.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-69) In June 2020, university president [Ronald J. Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Daniels_(academic) "Ron Daniels (academic)") announced a pause of at least two years in the development and implementation of the department.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-70)[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-71)

As part of the agreement with the university on authorizing the police force, the Maryland General Assembly required the creation of a civilian accountability board and a [memorandum of understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorandum_of_understanding "Memorandum of understanding") (MOU) with the Baltimore Police Department. A draft MOU was released for public comment in September 2022 amidst continued protest and criticism regarding the university's engagement with the community.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-72)[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-73)[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-74) The finalized MOU, issued in December 2022, grants the Johns Hopkins Police Department primary jurisdiction over university-controlled property and adjacent public areas.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-75) Officer recruitment and training began in spring 2024, with active patrols commencing in summer 2024.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-76)[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-77)

#### Second Trump Administration

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Second Trump Administration")]

Beginning in January 2025, federal funding cuts by the Trump administration's [Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency "Department of Government Efficiency") targeting the [U.S. Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development "United States Agency for International Development") (USAID) caused Johns Hopkins to lose $800 million in grants and forced the university to lay off thousands of employees and cancel research projects.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-78) In September, the university reported a total decline in research grants awarded by the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") (NIH) by approximately 40% compared to the previous year and the termination of more than 80 existing grants, reducing the value of the university's research portfolio by $500 million.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-79) In response to these challenges, the university imposed a hiring freeze on new staff members, limited discretionary spending, and indefinitely paused wage increases for employees. After the Trump administration announced it would impose a cap of 15% on indirect or "overhead" research costs, which would reduce the university's research funding by $300 million annually, Johns Hopkins joined 40 other universities in a successful lawsuit seeking to reverse the change.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-80)

In February 2025, [Leo Terrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Terrell "Leo Terrell"), the head of the Trump administration's [Task Force to Combat Antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_the_United_States#Efforts_to_combat_antisemitism "Antisemitism in the United States"), announced that he would investigate Johns Hopkins University as part of the Department of Justice's broader investigation into [antisemitism on college campuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_US_higher_education "Antisemitism in US higher education").[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-81) In July 2025, [America First Legal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_First_Legal "America First Legal") founder, the Trump adviser and [white nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism "White nationalism")[Stephen Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Miller "Stephen Miller"), requested that the assistant attorney general [Harmeet K Dhillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmeet_Dhillon "Harmeet Dhillon") and the justice department investigate "illegal" diversity, equity, and inclusion practices and "a discriminatory DEI regime as a core institutional mandate" in the university.

### Civil rights

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Civil rights")]

#### African-Americans

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: African-Americans")]

Hopkins was a prominent [abolitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States "Abolitionism in the United States") who supported [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln "Abraham Lincoln") during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"). After his death, reports said his conviction and [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker "Quaker") beliefs were decisive factors in enrolling Hopkins's first [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American "African-American") student, [Kelly Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Miller_(scientist) "Kelly Miller (scientist)"), a graduate student in physics, astronomy and mathematics.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-82) As time passed, the university adopted a "separate but equal" stance more like other Baltimore institutions.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-racial_record-13)

The first black undergraduate entered the school in 1945 and graduate students followed in 1967.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-timeline_JHSPH-83) James Nabwangu, a British-trained Kenyan, was the first black graduate of the medical school.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-In_a_Sea_of_White_Faces-84) African-American instructor and laboratory supervisor [Vivien Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivien_Thomas "Vivien Thomas") was instrumental in developing and conducting the first successful [blue baby operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_baby_syndrome "Blue baby syndrome") in 1944.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-85) Despite such cases, racial diversity did not become commonplace at Johns Hopkins institutions until the 1960s and 1970s.

#### Women

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Women")]

Hopkins's most well-known battle for women's rights was the one led by daughters of trustees of the university; [Mary E. Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Garrett "Mary Garrett"), [M. Carey Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Carey_Thomas "M. Carey Thomas"), [Mamie Gwinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamie_Gwinn_Hodder "Mamie Gwinn Hodder"), [Elizabeth King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_King_Ellicott "Elizabeth King Ellicott"), and Julia Rogers.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-women-86) They donated and raised the funds needed to open the medical school, and required Hopkins's officials to agree to their stipulation that women would be admitted. The [nursing school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing_school "Nursing school") opened in 1889 and accepted women and men as students.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-87) Other graduate schools were later opened to women by president [Ira Remsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Remsen "Ira Remsen") in 1907. [Christine Ladd-Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Ladd-Franklin "Christine Ladd-Franklin") was the first woman to earn a PhD at Hopkins, in mathematics in 1882.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-88) The trustees denied her the degree for decades and refused to change the policy about admitting women. In 1893, [Florence Bascomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Bascom "Florence Bascom") became the university's first female PhD.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-women-86) The decision to admit women at undergraduate level was not considered until the late 1960s and was eventually adopted in October 1969. As of 2009–2010, the undergraduate population was 47% female and 53% male.[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Johns_Hopkins_University-89) In 2020, the undergraduate population of Hopkins was 53% female.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-90)[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-91)

#### Freedom of speech

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Freedom of speech")]

On September 5, 2013, cryptographer and Johns Hopkins university professor [Matthew Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_D._Green "Matthew D. Green") posted a blog entitled, "On the NSA", in which he contributed to the ongoing debate regarding the role of [NIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST "NIST") and [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA "NSA") in formulating U.S. [cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptography "Cryptography") standards. On September 9, 2013, Green received a take-down request for the "On the NSA" blog from interim Dean Andrew Douglas from the Johns Hopkins University [Whiting School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiting_School_of_Engineering "Whiting School of Engineering").[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-GuardianOnTakeDown-92) The request cited concerns that the blog had links to sensitive material. The blog linked to already published news articles from _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Guardian "The Guardian")_, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_, and [ProPublica.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProPublica.org "ProPublica.org"). Douglas subsequently issued a personal on-line apology to Green.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-DaraKerrOnApology-93) The event raised concern over the future of academic freedom of speech within the cryptologic research community.

## Organization

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Organization")]

The Johns Hopkins entity is structured as two corporations, the university and The Johns Hopkins Health System, formed in 1986. The president is JHU's chief executive officer and the university is organized into nine academic divisions.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-overview-102)

JHU's bylaws specify a board of trustees of between 18 and 65 voting members. Trustees serve six-year terms subject to a two-term limit. The alumni select 12 trustees. Four recent alumni serve 4-year terms, one per year, typically from the graduating class. The bylaws prohibit students, faculty or administrative staff from serving on the board, except the president as an ex-officio trustee.[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Bylaws-103) The Johns Hopkins Health System has a separate board of trustees, many of whom are doctors or health care executives.[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Johns_Hopkins_Medicine_Adds_Four_to_Board-104)

## Academics

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Academics")]

The full-time, four-year undergraduate program is "most selective" with low transfer-in and a high graduate co-existence.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Carnegie-105) The [Princeton Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princeton_Review "The Princeton Review") rates the selectivity of Johns Hopkins as 99/99. As of 2025, the cost of attendance per academic year without financial aid is approximately $89,000.[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-106) However, 51% of full-time undergraduates receive financial aid covering 100% of their need.[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-107) The admit rate of Hopkins undergraduates to medical school is 80% and to law school is 97%, some of the highest rates in the US.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-GF-108) The university is one of fourteen founding members of the [Association of American Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Universities "Association of American Universities") (AAU); it is also a member of the [Consortium on Financing Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium_on_Financing_Higher_Education "Consortium on Financing Higher Education") (COFHE) and the [Universities Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_Research_Association "Universities Research Association") (URA).

The university's undergraduate programs are among the most highly selective in the nation. In 2025 the Office of Admissions accepted 5.1% of its 49,112 applicants.[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-:1-135) The median range [SAT score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT "SAT") for the enrolled Class of 2029 was 1530-1570 with a median ACT of 35.[[137]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-:2-137) 99% of enrolled students graduated in the top 10% of their high school class.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-GF-108) Over time, applications to Johns Hopkins University have risen steadily; as a result, the selectivity of Johns Hopkins University has also increased. [Early Decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Decision "Early Decision") I is an option at Johns Hopkins University for students who wish to demonstrate that the university is their first choice. These students, if admitted, are required to enroll. This application is due November 1. There is also another binding Early Decision II application due January 3. Many students, however, apply Regular Decision, which is a traditional non-binding round. These applications are due January 3 and students are notified in mid-March. The cost to apply to Hopkins is $70, though fee waivers are available. In 2014, Johns Hopkins ended legacy preference in admissions.[[139]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-139) Johns Hopkins practices [need-blind admission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need-blind_admission "Need-blind admission") and meets the full financial need of all admitted students.[[140]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-140)

### Libraries

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Libraries")]

Further information: [George Peabody Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peabody_Library "George Peabody Library")

[![Image 21](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/George-peabody-library.jpg/250px-George-peabody-library.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:George-peabody-library.jpg)

The [George Peabody Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peabody_Library "George Peabody Library") at Johns Hopkins University

The Johns Hopkins University Library system houses more than 3.6 million volumes[[152]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-American_Library_Association_Fact_Sheet-152) and includes ten main divisions across the university's campuses. The largest segment of this system is the Sheridan Libraries, encompassing the [Milton S. Eisenhower Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_S._Eisenhower_Library "Milton S. Eisenhower Library") (the main library of the [Homewood campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homewood_campus "Homewood campus")), the Brody Learning Commons, the Hutzler Reading Room ("The Hut") in Gilman Hall, the John Work Garrett Library at [Evergreen House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen_House "Evergreen House"), and the [George Peabody Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peabody_Library "George Peabody Library") at the [Peabody Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Institute "Peabody Institute") campus.[[153]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-153)

The main library, constructed in the 1960s, was named for [Milton S. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_S._Eisenhower "Milton S. Eisenhower"), former president of the university and brother of former U.S. president [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower "Dwight D. Eisenhower"). The university's stacks had previously been housed in Gilman Hall and departmental libraries.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-gil-154) Only two of the Eisenhower library's six stories are above ground, though the building was designed so that every level receives natural light. The design accords with campus lore that no structure can be taller than Gilman Hall, the flagship academic building. A four-story expansion to the library, known as the Brody Learning Commons, opened in August 2012. The expansion features an energy-efficient, state-of-the-art technology infrastructure and includes study spaces, seminar rooms, and a rare books collection.[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-JHU_News_Releases-155)

### Johns Hopkins University Press

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Johns Hopkins University Press")]

Main article: [Johns Hopkins University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University_Press "Johns Hopkins University Press")

The Johns Hopkins University Press is the publishing division of the Johns Hopkins University. It was founded in 1878 and holds the distinction of being the oldest continuously running [university press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_press "University press") in the United States.[[156]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-About_the_Press-156) To date the Press has published more than 6,000 titles and currently publishes 65 scholarly periodicals and over 200 new books each year. Since 1993, the Johns Hopkins University Press has run [Project MUSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_MUSE "Project MUSE"), an online collection of over 250 full-text, peer-reviewed journals in the humanities and social sciences. The Press also houses the Hopkins Fulfilment Services (HFS), which handles distribution for a number of university presses and publishers. Taken together, the three divisions of the Press—Books, Journals (including MUSE) and HFS—make it one of the largest of America's university presses.

### Center for Talented Youth

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Center for Talented Youth")]

Main article: [Center for Talented Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Talented_Youth "Center for Talented Youth")

The Johns Hopkins University also offers the [Center for Talented Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Talented_Youth "Center for Talented Youth") program, a nonprofit organization dedicated to identifying and developing the talents of the most promising K-12 grade students worldwide. As part of the Johns Hopkins University, the "Center for Talented Youth" or CTY helps fulfill the university's mission of preparing students to make significant future contributions to the world.[[157]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-157) The Johns Hopkins Digital Media Center (DMC) is a multimedia lab space as well as an equipment, technology and knowledge resource for students interested in exploring creative uses of emerging media and use of technology.[[158]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-158)

## Research

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Johns_Hopkins_University&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Research")]

[![Image 22](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/New_Horizons_LORRI.jpg/250px-New_Horizons_LORRI.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:New_Horizons_LORRI.jpg)

[![Image 23](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/15-150-NasaTeam-NewHorizonsCallsHomeAfterPlutoFlyby-20150714.jpg/250px-15-150-NasaTeam-NewHorizonsCallsHomeAfterPlutoFlyby-20150714.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:15-150-NasaTeam-NewHorizonsCallsHomeAfterPlutoFlyby-20150714.jpg)

View of Mission Operations at the Applied Physics Laboratory in Laurel, Maryland

The opportunity to participate in important research is one of the distinguishing characteristics of Hopkins's undergraduate education. About 80 percent of undergraduates perform independent research, often alongside top researchers.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Carnegie-105)[[161]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-Johns_Hopkins_News_Release_%E2%80%93_JHU-161) In fiscal year 2020, Johns Hopkins spent nearly $3.1 billion on research, more than any other U.S. university for over 40 consecutive years.[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-June-162) Johns Hopkins has had seventy-seven members of the [Institute of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medicine "Institute of Medicine"), forty-three [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") Investigators, seventeen members of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering "National Academy of Engineering"), and sixty-two members of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"). As of March 2025, 34 Nobel Prize winners have been affiliated with the university as alumni, faculty members or researchers, with the most recent winners being [Gregg Semenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_L._Semenza "Gregg L. Semenza") and [William G. Kaelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kaelin_Jr. "William Kaelin Jr.").[[163]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-The_Johns_Hopkins_University:_Nobel_Prize_Winners-163)

Between 1999 and 2009, Johns Hopkins was among the most cited institutions in the world. It attracted nearly 1,222,166 citations and produced 54,022 papers under its name, ranking third globally after [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") and the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society") in the number of _total_ citations published in Thomson Reuters-indexed journals over 22 fields in America.[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-ScienceWatch.com-164) In 2020, Johns Hopkins University ranked 5 in number of utility patents granted out of all institutions in the world.[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-165)

In 2000, Johns Hopkins received $95.4 million in research grants from the [National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aeronautics_and_Space_Administration "National Aeronautics and Space Administration") (NASA), making it the leading recipient of [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA")[research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development "Research and development") funding.[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-JHU_NASA_R&D_2000-166) In FY 2002, Hopkins became the first university to cross the $1 billion threshold on either list, recording $1.14 billion in total research and $1.023 billion in federally sponsored research.

In 2025, per a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation") (NSF) ranking, Johns Hopkins University performed $4.13 billion in total [R&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R&D "R&D"), over $2.5 billion of which went to its Applied Physics Laboratory, making it the largest spender on R&D among U.S. educational institutions.[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-167)

In 2013, the [Bloomberg Distinguished Professorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Distinguished_Professorships "Bloomberg Distinguished Professorships") program was established by a $250 million gift from [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg "Michael Bloomberg"). This program enables the university to recruit fifty researchers from around the world to joint appointments throughout the nine divisions and research centers. Each professor must be a leader in [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary "Interdisciplinary") research and be active in [undergraduate education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_education "Undergraduate education").[[168]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-anderson-168)[[169]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-169) Directed by Vice Provost for Research [Denis Wirtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Wirtz "Denis Wirtz"), there are currently thirty two Bloomberg Distinguished Professors at the university, including three [Nobel Laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize"), eight fellows of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science"), ten members of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), and thirteen members of the National Academies.[[170]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University#cite_note-170)
