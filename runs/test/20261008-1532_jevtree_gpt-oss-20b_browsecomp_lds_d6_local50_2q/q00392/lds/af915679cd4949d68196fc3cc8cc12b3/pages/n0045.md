The **University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign** (**UIUC**, **U of I**, **Illinois**, or **University of Illinois**) is a [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university "Public university")[land-grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university "Land-grant university")[research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university "Research university") in the [Champaign–Urbana metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign%E2%80%93Urbana_metropolitan_area "Champaign–Urbana metropolitan area"), Illinois, United States. Established in 1867, it is the founding campus and [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship#Colleges_and_universities_in_the_United_States "Flagship") institution of the [University of Illinois System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_System "University of Illinois System"). With over 59,000 students, the University of Illinois is one of the [largest public universities by enrollment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_public_university_campuses_by_enrollment "List of United States public university campuses by enrollment") in the United States.

The university contains 16 schools and colleges[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-9) and offers more than 150 undergraduate and over 100 graduate programs of study. The university holds 651 buildings on 6,370 acres (2,578 ha)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Illinois_Facts-6) and its annual operating budget in 2016 was over $2 billion.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-10) The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign also operates [a research park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Park_at_the_University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign "Research Park at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign") home to innovation centers for over 90 start-up companies and [multinational corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation "Multinational corporation").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-research.illinois.edu-11)

The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign is a member of the [Association of American Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Universities "Association of American Universities") and is [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Classification_of_Institutions_of_Higher_Education "Carnegie Classification of Institutions of Higher Education") among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity".[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Carnegie-12) In fiscal year 2019, research expenditures at Illinois totaled $652 million.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-2018_R&D-13)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-research.illinois.edu-11) The campus library system possesses the [fourth-largest university library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_libraries_in_the_United_States "University libraries in the United States") in the United States by holdings.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-14) The university also hosts the [National Center for Supercomputing Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Supercomputing_Applications "National Center for Supercomputing Applications").[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-15)

The alumni, faculty members, or researchers of the university include 24 [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureates, 27 [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize") winners, 2 [Fields medalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal"), and 2 [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award "Turing Award") winners. Illinois athletic teams compete in [Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I "NCAA Division I") of the [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association "National Collegiate Athletic Association") and are collectively known as the [Fighting Illini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Fighting_Illini "Illinois Fighting Illini"). They are members of the [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference "Big Ten Conference") and have won the [second-most conference titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference#Conference_titles "Big Ten Conference"). [Illinois Fighting Illini football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Fighting_Illini_football "Illinois Fighting Illini football") won the [Rose Bowl Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Game "Rose Bowl Game") in 1947, 1952, 1964 and a total of five national championships. Illinois athletes have won 29 medals in [Olympic events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games").

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Main article: [History of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign "History of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign")

### Illinois Industrial University (1867–1885)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Illinois Industrial University (1867–1885)")]

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/IllinoisIndustrialUniversity.jpg/250px-IllinoisIndustrialUniversity.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:IllinoisIndustrialUniversity.jpg)

University Hall stood from 1871 until 1938 and was replaced by Gregory Hall and the [Illini Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illini_Union "Illini Union"). Pieces were used in the erection of Hallene Gateway.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-16)

The University of Illinois, originally named "Illinois Industrial University", was one of the 37 universities created under the first [Morrill Land-Grant Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrill_Land-Grant_Acts "Morrill Land-Grant Acts"), which provided public land for the creation of agricultural and industrial colleges and universities across the United States. Among several cities, [Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana,_Illinois "Urbana, Illinois") was selected in 1867 as the site for the new school.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Illini_years_pg6-17)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-University_of_Illinois_Archives-18) From the beginning, President [John Milton Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Milton_Gregory "John Milton Gregory")'s desire to establish an institution firmly grounded in the [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts "Liberal arts") tradition was at odds with many state residents and lawmakers who wanted the university to offer classes based solely around "industrial education".[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-manifesto-19) The university opened for classes on March 2, 1868, and had two faculty members and 77 students.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-story-20)

The library, which opened with the school in 1868, started with 1,039 volumes. Subsequently, President [Edmund J. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_J._James "Edmund J. James"), in a speech to the board of trustees in 1912, proposed to create a research library. It is now one of the world's largest public academic collections.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-University_of_Illinois_Archives-18)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-illinois2-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-About_the_University_Library-22) In 1870, the Mumford House was constructed as a model farmhouse for the school's experimental farm. The Mumford House remains the oldest structure on campus.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-23) The original University Hall (1871) was the fourth building built; it stood where the Illini Union stands today.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-UIHistories-24)

The University of Illinois' Undergraduate Library (UGL) was constructed underground to preserve open space on campus and to prevent casting shadows on the adjacent Morrow Plots, the oldest continually used experimental agricultural fields in the United States.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-25) This unique design inspired The Other Guys, a student a cappella group, to create the "Morrow Plots Song," humorously explaining that the library was built underground "'Cause you can't throw shade on the corn". [[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-26) The song has become a beloved piece among students and alumni, celebrating the university's history and traditions.

### University of Illinois (1885–1977)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: University of Illinois (1885–1977)")]

[![Image 9](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/University_of_Illinois_1900.jpg/250px-University_of_Illinois_1900.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:University_of_Illinois_1900.jpg)

Full Membership certificate of the University of Illinois, issued 22 March 1900

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Alma-front.jpg/250px-Alma-front.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alma-front.jpg)

_[Alma Mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma\_Mater\_(Illinois\_sculpture) "Alma Mater (Illinois sculpture)")_ by [Lorado Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorado_Taft "Lorado Taft"), located in front of [Altgeld Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altgeld_Hall "Altgeld Hall")

In 1885, the Illinois Industrial University officially changed its name to the "University of Illinois", reflecting its agricultural, mechanical, and liberal arts curriculum.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-manifesto-19) According to educational historian [Roger L. Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_L._Geiger "Roger L. Geiger"), Illinois and a few other public and private universities set the standard for what the [research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university "Research university") in the United States would become.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Crow-27)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Geiger_Page_3-28) During his presidency, [Edmund J. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_J._James "Edmund J. James") (1904–1920) set the policy of building a massive research library.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Solberg-I-67-29) He also laid the foundation for the large Chinese international student population on campus.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-IAM-30) James established ties with China through the Chinese Minister to the United States [Wu Ting-Fang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Tingfang "Wu Tingfang"). Also during James's time in 1916, [St. Elmo Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Elmo_Brady "St. Elmo Brady") became the first African American nationally to earn a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry").[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-31) Class rivalries and [Bob Zuppke's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Zuppke "Robert Zuppke") winning football teams contributed to campus morale.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-University_of_Illinois_Archives-18)

[_Alma Mater_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_Mater_(Illinois_sculpture) "Alma Mater (Illinois sculpture)"), a prominent statue on campus created by alumnus [Lorado Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorado_Taft "Lorado Taft"), was unveiled on June 11, 1929. It was funded from donations by the Alumni Fund and the classes of 1923–1929.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Alma_Mater-32)

The [Great Depression in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States "Great Depression in the United States") slowed construction and expansion on the campus. The university replaced the original university hall with Gregory Hall and the [Illini Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illini_Union "Illini Union"). After World War II, the university experienced rapid growth. The enrollment doubled and the academic standing improved.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-33) This period was also marked by large growth in the Graduate College and increased federal support of scientific and technological research. During the 1950s and 1960s the university experienced the turmoil common on many American campuses. Among these were the water fights of the 1950s and 1960s.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-34)

### University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (1977–present)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (1977–present)")]

[![Image 11](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/UIUCEngineeringHall.jpg/250px-UIUCEngineeringHall.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UIUCEngineeringHall.jpg)

Engineering Hall is located along Boneyard Creek on the Engineering Campus.

By 1967, the [University of Illinois system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_system "University of Illinois system") consisted of a main campus in Champaign-Urbana and two Chicago campuses, Chicago Circle (UICC) and Medical Center (UIMC), and people began using "Urbana-Champaign" or the reverse to refer to the main campus specifically. The university name officially changed to the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign by 1977 (although the word "at" was later dropped for marketing purposes by all U of I System campuses by 2021). While this was a reversal of the commonly used designation for the metropolitan area ([Champaign-Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign-Urbana "Champaign-Urbana")), a majority of the campus is located in Urbana. The name change established a separate identity for the main campus within the [University of Illinois System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_System "University of Illinois System"), which today includes separate institutions at the [University of Illinois Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago "University of Illinois Chicago") (formed by the merger of UICC and UIMC) and [University of Illinois Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Springfield "University of Illinois Springfield").

In 1998, the Hallene Gateway Plaza was dedicated. The Plaza features the original sandstone portal of University Hall, which was originally the fourth building on campus.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-UIHistories-24) In recent years, state support has declined from 4.5% of the state's tax appropriations in 1980 to 2.28% in 2011, a nearly 50% decline.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-35) As a result, the university's budget has shifted away from relying on state support with nearly 84% of the budget coming from other sources in 2012.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Budget_by_Source_of_Funds-36)

On March 12, 2015, the Board of Trustees approved the creation of a medical school, the first college created at Urbana-Champaign in 60 years.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-37)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-chicagotribune.com-38)[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-news.illinois.edu-39) The [Carle Illinois College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carle_Illinois_College_of_Medicine "Carle Illinois College of Medicine") began classes in 2018.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-40) It is the world's first engineering-based medical school.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-41)

## Campus

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Campus")]

Main article: [Campus of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_of_the_University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign "Campus of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign")

See also: [Campustown (Champaign, Illinois)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campustown_(Champaign,_Illinois) "Campustown (Champaign, Illinois)")

[![Image 12](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/GreenStreetCampustownChampaignIllinois.JPG/250px-GreenStreetCampustownChampaignIllinois.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:GreenStreetCampustownChampaignIllinois.JPG)

Green Street in [Campustown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campustown_(Champaign,_Illinois) "Campustown (Champaign, Illinois)")

The main research and academic facilities are divided almost evenly between the twin cities of [Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana,_Illinois "Urbana, Illinois") and [Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign,_Illinois "Champaign, Illinois"), which form part of the [Champaign–Urbana metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign%E2%80%93Urbana_metropolitan_area "Champaign–Urbana metropolitan area"). Some parts are in [Urbana Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana_Township,_Champaign_County,_Illinois "Urbana Township, Champaign County, Illinois").[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-42)

Four main quads compose the center of the university and are arranged from north to south. The Beckman Quadrangle and the [John Bardeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bardeen "John Bardeen") Quadrangle occupy the center of the [Engineering Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIUC_Engineering_Campus "UIUC Engineering Campus"). [Boneyard Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boneyard_Creek "Boneyard Creek") flows through the John Bardeen Quadrangle, parallel to Green Street. The Beckman Quadrangle, named after [Arnold Orville Beckman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Orville_Beckman "Arnold Orville Beckman"), is primarily composed of research units and laboratories, and features a large solar calendar consisting of an obelisk and several copper fountains. The Main Quadrangle and South Quadrangle follow immediately after the John Bardeen Quad. The former makes up a large part of the [Liberal Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIUC_College_of_Liberal_Arts_and_Sciences "UIUC College of Liberal Arts and Sciences") portion of the campus, while the latter comprises many of the buildings of the [College of Agriculture, Consumer, and Environmental Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIUC_College_of_Agriculture,_Consumer,_and_Environmental_Sciences "UIUC College of Agriculture, Consumer, and Environmental Sciences") (ACES) spread across the campus map.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-43)

Additionally, the research fields of the College of ACES stretch south from Urbana and Champaign into [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy,_Illinois "Savoy, Illinois") and [Champaign County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign_County,_Illinois "Champaign County, Illinois"). The university also maintains formal gardens and a conference center in nearby [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello,_Illinois "Monticello, Illinois") at [Allerton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Allerton_Park "Robert Allerton Park").

The campus is known for its landscape and architecture, as well as distinctive landmarks.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-44) It was identified as one of 50 college or university "works of art" by T.A. Gaines in his book _The Campus as a Work of Art_.[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-45) The campus also has a number of buildings and sites on the [U.S. National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places "National Register of Historic Places") including [Harker Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harker_Hall "Harker Hall"), the [Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_Observatory_(University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) "Astronomical Observatory (University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign)"), [Louise Freer Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Freer_Hall "Louise Freer Hall"), the [Main Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Library_(University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) "Main Library (University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign)"), the [Experimental Dairy Farm Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Experimental_Dairy_Farm_Historic_District "University of Illinois Experimental Dairy Farm Historic District"), and the [Morrow Plots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrow_Plots "Morrow Plots"). [University of Illinois Willard Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Willard_Airport "University of Illinois Willard Airport") is one of the few airports owned by an educational institution.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-46)

[Chesterbrook Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterbrook_Academy?action=edit&redlink=1 "Chesterbrook Academy (page does not exist)") private preschool, which opened in 2007, is located on the campus and serves infants through Pre-K.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-47)[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-48)

### Sustainability

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Sustainability")]

[![Image 14](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/UIUC_morrow_plot.JPG/250px-UIUC_morrow_plot.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UIUC_morrow_plot.JPG)

[Morrow Plots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrow_Plots "Morrow Plots") in front of the [Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_R._Woese_Institute_for_Genomic_Biology "Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology")

In 2008, the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign became a signatory of the American College and University Presidents' Climate Commitment, binding the campus to the goal of [carbon neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutrality "Carbon neutrality") as soon as possible. In 2010, the first Illinois Climate Action Plan (iCAP) was written to chart a path to this goal. The iCAP is a strategic framework for meeting the university's Climate Leadership Commitments to be carbon-neutral by 2050 or sooner and build resilience with its local community. Since then, the iCAP has been rewritten every five years to track the university's progress.

In December 2013, the University of Illinois launched the Institute for Sustainability, Energy, and Environment (iSEE) on the Urbana-Champaign campus. The institute, under the Vice Chancellor for Research and Innovation, leads an interdisciplinary approach to researching solutions for the world's most pressing sustainability, energy, and environmental needs. In addition, iSEE has engaged students, faculty, staff, and campus leadership in the iCAP process — especially in the areas of zero waste and conservation of energy, food, water, land, and natural resources — as well as sustainability outreach and immersive educational programs.

In 2022, new solar and geothermal energy projects, a reduction in water use, and wide-ranging sustainability research helped the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign earn its fifth consecutive gold certification in the [Sustainability Tracking, Assessment & Rating System (STARS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability_Tracking,_Assessment_&_Rating_System "Sustainability Tracking, Assessment & Rating System").[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-49) Illinois has consistently achieved gold certification since it began reporting data through STARS in 2013, and the 2022 score was one of its highest to date.

Currently, the campus features 27 LEED-certified buildings.

### Online learning

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Online learning")]

In addition to the university's Illinois Online platform, in 2015 the university entered into a partnership with the Silicon Valley educational technology company [Coursera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coursera "Coursera") to offer a series of master's degrees, certifications, and specialization courses, currently including more than 70 joint learning classes. In August 2015, the Master of Business Administration program was launched through the platform.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-68) On March 31, 2016, Coursera announced the launch of the Master of Computer Science in Data Science from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-69) At the time, the university's computer-science graduate program was ranked fifth in the United States by _[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.\_News\_&\_World\_Report "U.S. News & World Report")_.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-70) On March 29, 2017, the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign launched their Master's in Accounting (iMSA) program, now called the Master of Science in Accountancy (iMSA) program. The iMSA program is led through live sessions, headed by Illinois faculty.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-71)

Similar to the university's on-campus admission policies, the online master's degrees offered by the U of I through Coursera also has admission requirements. All applicants must hold a bachelor's degree, and have earned a 3.0 GPA or higher in the last two years of study. Additionally, all applicants must prove their proficiency in English.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-coursera.org-72)[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-73)

The U of I also offers online courses in partnership with Coursera, such as Marketing in a Digital World, which focuses on how digital tools like internet, smartphone and 3D printers are changing the marketing landscape.

## Research

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Research")]

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Dairy_farm_4390.jpg/250px-Dairy_farm_4390.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dairy_farm_4390.jpg)

[Round barns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_barns_in_Illinois "Round barns in Illinois") on the [University of Illinois Experimental Dairy Farm Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Experimental_Dairy_Farm_Historic_District "University of Illinois Experimental Dairy Farm Historic District"), part of [ACES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIUC_College_of_Agriculture,_Consumer,_and_Environmental_Sciences "UIUC College of Agriculture, Consumer, and Environmental Sciences")

The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign is often regarded as a world-leading magnet for engineering and sciences (both applied and basic).[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-95) According to the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), the university spent $625 million on research and development in 2018, ranking it 37th in the nation.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-2018_R&D-13)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-research.illinois.edu-11) It is also listed as one of the top 25 American research universities by [The Center for Measuring University Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Center_for_Measuring_University_Performance "The Center for Measuring University Performance").[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-96)

Besides the annual influx of grants and sponsored projects, the university manages an extensive modern research infrastructure.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-97) The university has been a leader in computer-based education and hosted the [PLATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLATO_(computer_system) "PLATO (computer system)") project, which was a precursor to the internet and resulted in the development of the [plasma display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_display "Plasma display"). Illinois was a 2nd-generation [ARPAnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARPAnet "ARPAnet") site in 1971 and was the first institution to license the [UNIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIX "UNIX") operating system from Bell Labs. In [Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates "Bill Gates")' 2004 talk as part of his five-university campus tour titled "Software Breakthroughs: Solving the Toughest Problems in Computer Science,"[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-98) he mentioned that [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") hired more graduates from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign than from any other university in the world.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-99)

### Centers and institutes

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Centers and institutes")]

Main articles: [National Center for Supercomputing Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Supercomputing_Applications "National Center for Supercomputing Applications"), [Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckman_Institute_for_Advanced_Science_and_Technology "Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology"), [Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_R._Woese_Institute_for_Genomic_Biology "Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology"), and [Prairie Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Research_Institute "Prairie Research Institute")

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/BeckmanInstitute.jpg/250px-BeckmanInstitute.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BeckmanInstitute.jpg)

The [Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckman_Institute_for_Advanced_Science_and_Technology "Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology") is the largest interdisciplinary facility on campus, at 313,000 square feet (29,100 m 2).

The university hosts the [National Center for Supercomputing Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Supercomputing_Applications "National Center for Supercomputing Applications") (NCSA), which created [Mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_(web_browser) "Mosaic (web browser)"), the first graphical [web browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browser "Web browser"), the [Apache HTTP server](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_HTTP_server "Apache HTTP server"), and [NCSA Telnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCSA_Telnet "NCSA Telnet"). The Parallel@Illinois program hosts several programs in [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing "Parallel computing"), including the [Universal Parallel Computing Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPCRC_Illinois "UPCRC Illinois"). The university contracted with [Cray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cray "Cray") to build the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation")-funded supercomputer [Blue Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Waters "Blue Waters").[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-100)[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-101)[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-102) The system also has the largest public online storage system in the world with more than 25 petabytes of usable space.[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-103) The university celebrated January 12, 1997, as the "birthday" of [HAL 9000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_9000 "HAL 9000"), the fictional supercomputer from the novel and film _2001: A Space Odyssey_; in both works, HAL credits "Urbana, Illinois" as his place of operational origin.

The [Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckman_Institute_for_Advanced_Science_and_Technology "Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology") supports interdisciplinary collaborative research in the broad areas of intelligent systems, neuroscience, molecular science and engineering, and biomedical imaging.

The [Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_R._Woese_Institute_for_Genomic_Biology "Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology") supports research in genomics and related areas of biology.

[![Image 18](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/UofIPrarieResearch.JPG/250px-UofIPrarieResearch.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UofIPrarieResearch.JPG)

One of the university's research [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie "Prairie") fields, located off Florida Avenue

The [Prairie Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Research_Institute "Prairie Research Institute") on campus houses several divisions, including the [Illinois Natural History Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Natural_History_Survey "Illinois Natural History Survey"), Illinois State Geological Survey, Illinois State Water Survey, Illinois Sustainable Technology Center, and Illinois State Archaeological Survey. Researchers focus on areas such as agriculture, biodiversity, climate, public health, emerging pests, energy, mineral resources, pollution mitigation, and water resources. The Illinois Natural History Survey holds extensive collections, including one of North America's largest insect collections. The Illinois State Geological Survey manages the Illinois Geological Samples Library and paleontological collections. The Illinois State Archaeological Survey preserves a large collection of Illinois archaeological artifacts, including those from the [Cahokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia "Cahokia") Mounds.[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-104)

The Technology Entrepreneur Center at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign offers resources for students to develop their entrepreneurial ideas, including classes, competitions, and workshops.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-105) It hosts events including the Cozad New Venture Challenge, Silicon Valley Entrepreneurship Workshop, Illinois I-Corps, and SocialFuse. The Cozad Challenge, held annually since 2000, provides mentorship and workshops on venture creation, with teams competing for funding.[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-106) The Silicon Valley Workshop, a week-long event in January, exposes students to startups, technology companies, and entrepreneurial alumni in [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley "Silicon Valley").[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-107) Illinois I-Corps helps National Science Foundation grantees identify valuable product opportunities from academic research through customer discovery and entrepreneurship training.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-108)[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-109) SocialFuse is a pitching and networking event where students can present ideas and connect with potential teammates.[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-110)

The Center for Plasma-Material Interactions was established in 2004 by Professor David N. Ruzic to research the complex behavior between [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion "Ion"), [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron"), and [energetic atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energetic_neutral_atom "Energetic neutral atom") generated in [plasmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics) "Plasma (physics)") and the surfaces of materials. CPMI encompasses [fusion plasmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion "Nuclear fusion") in its research.[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-111)[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-112)[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-113)

In 2007, the university-hosted research [Institute for Condensed Matter Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Condensed_Matter_Theory "Institute for Condensed Matter Theory") (ICMT) was launched, with the director [Paul Goldbart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Goldbart "Paul Goldbart") and the chief scientist [Anthony Leggett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Leggett "Anthony Leggett"). ICMT is located at the Engineering Science Building on campus.

[![Image 19](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/University_of_Illinois_Research_Park_Aerial_View_2017.jpg/330px-University_of_Illinois_Research_Park_Aerial_View_2017.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:University_of_Illinois_Research_Park_Aerial_View_2017.jpg)

Located in the southwest part of campus, [Research Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Park_at_the_University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign "Research Park at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign") opened its first building in 2001 and has grown to encompass 13 buildings. Tenants of the Research Park facilities include prominent Fortune 500 companies Capital One, John Deere, State Farm, Caterpillar, and Yahoo, Inc. Companies also employ about 400 total student interns at any given time throughout the year. The complex is also a center for entrepreneurs, and has over 50 startup companies stationed at its EnterpriseWorks Incubator facility.[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-114)

In 2011, Urbana, Illinois, was named number 11 on _Popular Mechanics_' "14 Best Startup Cities in America" list, in a large part due to the contributions of Research Park's programs.[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-115) The park has gained recognition from other notable publications, such as inc.com and Forbes magazine. For the 2011 fiscal year, Research Park produced an economic output of $169.5M for the state of Illinois.[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-116)

### Notable discoveries and innovations

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Notable discoveries and innovations")]

[![Image 20](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/GraingerEngineeringLibrary.jpg/250px-GraingerEngineeringLibrary.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:GraingerEngineeringLibrary.jpg)

[Grainger Engineering Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grainger_Engineering_Library "Grainger Engineering Library")

In the field of natural sciences, the [BCS theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCS_theory "BCS theory"), a groundbreaking theory of [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity"), was proposed by [John Bardeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bardeen "John Bardeen") in collaboration with [Leon Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Cooper "Leon Cooper") and his doctoral student [John Robert Schrieffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Robert_Schrieffer "John Robert Schrieffer"). Their work earned them the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1972.[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-washpost-117) In the realm of agricultural science, John Laughnan, while a professor, developed [sweet corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_corn#Genetics "Sweet corn") with higher-than-normal sugar content, a significant advancement in crop science.[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-DLLarson-118)

In computer and applied sciences, several notable achievements originated from the University of Illinois. The [ILLIAC I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILLIAC_I "ILLIAC I"), built in 1952, was the first computer entirely constructed and owned by a U.S. educational institution. It was also used by [Lejaren Hiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lejaren_Hiller "Lejaren Hiller") and [Leonard Issacson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Issacson "Leonard Issacson") to create the _[Illiac Suite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiac\_Suite "Illiac Suite")_, the first known composition written by an electronic computer.[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-119)[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Baggi-120) The development of [LLVM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LLVM "LLVM"), initially started by [Vikram Adve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram_Adve "Vikram Adve") and [Chris Lattner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Lattner "Chris Lattner"), is now recognized as a major project in compiler infrastructure.[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-121) Another milestone was the development of the [Mosaic web browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_(web_browser) "Mosaic (web browser)") at the [National Center for Supercomputing Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Supercomputing_Applications "National Center for Supercomputing Applications") in 1993.[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-mosaic_and_th_w3-122)

[NAMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAMD "NAMD"), a molecular dynamics simulation code, was pioneered by [Klaus Schulten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Schulten "Klaus Schulten") and his team at the Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology, further advancing computational biophysics. The [PLATO system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLATO_(computer_system) "PLATO (computer system)"), also developed at the University of Illinois, was the first generalized [computer assisted instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_assisted_instruction "Computer assisted instruction") system, which by the late 1970s supported thousands of terminals globally, introducing many concepts foundational to modern multi-user computing such as forums, instant messaging, and online testing.[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-123)[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-124) In terms of interface technologies, [Donald Bitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Bitzer "Donald Bitzer") was instrumental in the 1960s development of both [touchscreens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchscreen "Touchscreen") and [plasma displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_display "Plasma display").[[125]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-125) Furthermore, Doug Brown and David R. Woolley created [Talkomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talkomatic "Talkomatic") in 1973 on the PLATO system, an early online chat system enabling real-time text communication among small groups.[[126]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-126)[[127]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-FalkDrayton-127)[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Bidgoli2004-128)

In the realm of audio-visual technology, [Joseph Tykociński-Tykociner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Tykoci%C5%84ski-Tykociner "Joseph Tykociński-Tykociner") publicly demonstrated for the first time a motion picture with a soundtrack optically recorded directly onto the film in 1922.[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-129)

#### Student government

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Student government")]

[![Image 22](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/%222010%22_University_of_Illinois.jpg/250px-%222010%22_University_of_Illinois.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:%222010%22_University_of_Illinois.jpg)

[Foellinger Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foellinger_Auditorium "Foellinger Auditorium")

U of I has an extensive history of past student governments. Two years after the university opened in 1868, John Milton Gregory and a group of students created a constitution for a student government. Their governance expanded to the entire university in 1873, having a legislative, executive, and judicial branch. For a period of time, this government had the ability to discipline students. In 1883, however, due to a combination of events from Gregory's resignation to student-faculty infighting, the government formally dissolved itself via [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite "Plebiscite").[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-149)

It was not until 1934, when the Student Senate, the next university-wide student government, was created. A year before, future Illinois Dean of Students, Fred H. Turner and the university's Senate Committee on Student Affairs gave increased power to the Student Council, an organization primarily known for organizing dances. A year after, the Student Council created a constitution and became the Student Senate, under the oversight of the Committee on Student Affairs. This Student Senate would last for 35 years.[[147]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-150) The Student Senate changed its purpose and name in 1969, when it became the Undergraduate Student Association (UGSA). It ceased being a representational government, becoming a [collective bargaining agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_bargaining_agency "Collective bargaining agency") instead. It often worked with the Graduate Student Association to work on various projects[[148]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-151)

In 1967, [Bruce A. Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_A._Morrison "Bruce A. Morrison") and other U of I graduate founded the Graduate Student Association (GSA). GSA would last until 1978, when it merged with the UGSA to form the Champaign-Urbana Student Association (CUSA).[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-152)[[150]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-illinois1948-153) CUSA lasted for only two years when it was replaced by the Student Government Association (SGA) in 1980. SGA lasted for 15 years until it became the Illinois Student Government (ISG) in 1995. ISG lasted until 2004.[[150]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-illinois1948-153)

The current university student government, created in 2004, is the Illinois Student Senate, a combined undergraduate and graduate student senate with 54 voting members. The student senators are elected by college and represent the students in the Urbana-Champaign Senate (which comprises both faculty and students), as well as on a variety of faculty and administrative committees, and are led by an internally elected executive board of a president, external vice president, internal vice president, and treasurer. As of 2012[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit), the executive board is supported by an executive staff consisting of a chief of staff, clerk of the Senate, parliamentarian, director of communications, Intern Coordinator, and the historian of the Senate.[[151]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-154)

### Residence halls

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Residence halls")]

[![Image 23](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/BuseyHall_Urbana_Illinois_4543.jpg/250px-BuseyHall_Urbana_Illinois_4543.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BuseyHall_Urbana_Illinois_4543.jpg)

[Busey-Evans Residence Halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busey-Evans_Residence_Halls "Busey-Evans Residence Halls") is one of many buildings on the [NRHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRHP "NRHP").

The university provides housing for undergraduates through [24 residence halls in Urbana and Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_of_the_University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#Residence_halls "Campus of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign"). Incoming freshmen are required to live in student housing (campus or certified) their first year on campus. The university also maintains two graduate residence halls, which are restricted to students who are sophomores or above, and three university-owned apartment complexes. Some undergraduates choose to move into apartments or the Greek houses after their first year. There are a number of private dormitories around campus, as well as 15 private, certified residences that partner with the university to offer a variety of different housing options, including ones that are cooperatives, single-gender or religiously affiliated.[[152]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-PCH16-155) U of I is known for being one of the first universities to provide accommodations for students with disabilities.[[153]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-Education_secretary_UI-156) In 2015, the University of Illinois announced that they would be naming its newest residence hall after [Carlos Montezuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Montezuma "Carlos Montezuma"), also known as Wassaja. He is the first [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States "Native Americans in the United States") graduate, and is believed to be one of the first Native Americans to receive a medical degree.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-157)

### Transportation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Transportation")]

[![Image 27](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/CUMTD_Bus_2105.jpg/250px-CUMTD_Bus_2105.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:CUMTD_Bus_2105.jpg)

A [Champaign–Urbana Mass Transit District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign%E2%80%93Urbana_Mass_Transit_District "Champaign–Urbana Mass Transit District") (MTD) bus

The bus system that operates throughout the campus and community is operated by the [Champaign-Urbana Mass Transit District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign-Urbana_Mass_Transit_District "Champaign-Urbana Mass Transit District"). The MTD receives a student-approved transportation fee from the university, which provides [unlimited access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_transit_pass "Universal transit pass") for university students, faculty, and staff.

Daily [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak "Amtrak") trains through [Illinois Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Terminal "Illinois Terminal") connect Champaign-Urbana with Chicago and [Carbondale, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbondale,_Illinois "Carbondale, Illinois"). This includes the corridor service _[Illini and Saluki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illini\_and\_Saluki "Illini and Saluki")_ and the long-distance _[City of New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City\_of\_New\_Orleans\_(train) "City of New Orleans (train)")_, which provides a direct route to [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee "Memphis, Tennessee"); [Jackson, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson,_Mississippi "Jackson, Mississippi"); and [New Orleans, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans,_Louisiana "New Orleans, Louisiana") southbound, in addition to Chicago northbound.

[Willard Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Willard_Airport "University of Illinois Willard Airport"), opened in 1954 and is named for former University of Illinois president Arthur Cutts Willard. The airport is located in Savoy. Willard Airport is home to university research projects, along with flights from [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines "American Airlines"). In 2013, the university's [Institute of Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Institute_of_Aviation "University of Illinois Institute of Aviation") was closed at the University of Illinois and the program was transferred to [Parkland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkland_College_(United_States) "Parkland College (United States)").

### Security

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Security")]

The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign has a dedicated police department, UIPD, which operates independently from CPD, the department that serves the surrounding Champaign area.

On June 9, 2017, Yingying Zhang, a Chinese international student, was [abducted and murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Yingying_Zhang "Murder of Yingying Zhang") in a case that made national headlines at the time. The university subsequently announced plans to install additional, high-definition, security cameras across the campus.[[170]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-WQAD8Security-173)

In July 2022, the university announced that it was partnering with local businesses to invest $300,000 to combat violent crime in Champaign County.[[171]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-174)

In September 2022, the City of Champaign transferred responsibility for a large swath of Campustown from CPD (Champaign Police Department) to UIPD, claiming that doing so would reduce response times and improve the quality of service. As part of the jurisdictional reforms, the city agreed to pay a substantial portion of the cost to hire seven new officers to patrol the new coverage area.[[172]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-175)

Violent crime fell sharply in 2022 compared to the year prior, with shootings and homicides declining by 50 and 47 percent, respectively. The city attributed the decrease in crime to improved staffing levels and the installation of [automatic license plate readers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_number-plate_recognition "Automatic number-plate recognition").[[173]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign#cite_note-176)
