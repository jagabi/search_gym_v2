**Shashi Tharoor** (Malayalam pronunciation:[[ʃɐʃi t̪ɐɾuːr]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Malayalam "Help:IPA/Malayalam"); born 9 March 1956) is an Indian politician, civil servant, author, public intellectual, diplomat, and bureaucrat. A member of the [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress"), he has represented [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram_(Lok_Sabha_constituency) "Thiruvananthapuram (Lok Sabha constituency)"), [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala"), in the [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha") since 2009. He was formerly an [Under-Secretary-General of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under-Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Under-Secretary-General of the United Nations") and ran for the office of [Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Secretary-General of the United Nations") in 2006, coming second.

## Early life and education

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")]

Shashi Tharoor was born on 9 March 1956[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-1) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"), England as Shashi Krishnan Chandrashekaran Tharoor to Chandra Shekharan Nair Tharoor and Sulekha Menon, a [Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nair "Nair") couple from [Palakkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palakkad "Palakkad"), Kerala, India.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-2) Tharoor has two younger sisters, Shobha and Smitha.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-3) Despite, his mother-tongue being Malayalam, he is also fluent in French.

Tharoor's father worked in various positions in London, [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay "Bombay"), [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta "Calcutta") and [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi "Delhi"), including a 25-year career (culminating as group advertising manager) for _[The Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Statesman\_(India) "The Statesman (India)")_. Tharoor's parents returned to India when he was 2-years old, where he joined the [Montfort School, Yercaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montfort_School,_Yercaud "Montfort School, Yercaud"), in 1962, subsequently moving to Bombay (now Mumbai) and studying at the [Campion School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion_School,_Mumbai "Campion School, Mumbai") (1963–68).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-4) He spent his high school years at [St. Xavier's Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Xavier's_Collegiate_School "St. Xavier's Collegiate School"), Calcutta (1969–71).[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-5)

In 1975, Tharoor graduated with a Bachelor of Arts degree in history from [St. Stephen's College, Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_College,_Delhi "St. Stephen's College, Delhi"), where he had been president of the student union and also founded the St. Stephen's Quiz Club.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-6) Within the same year, Tharoor went to the United States to obtain an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in international relations from [The Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fletcher_School_of_Law_and_Diplomacy "The Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy") at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University "Tufts University") in [Medford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medford,_Massachusetts "Medford, Massachusetts"). After obtaining his M.A. in 1976, Tharoor further obtained his [Master of Arts in Law and Diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_in_Law_and_Diplomacy "Master of Arts in Law and Diplomacy") in 1977 and his Ph.D. in International Relations and Affairs in 1978.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-7) While he was pursuing his doctorate, Tharoor was awarded the Robert B. Stewart Prize for best student and was also the first [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief "Editor-in-chief") of the [Fletcher Forum of International Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fletcher_Forum_of_World_Affairs "The Fletcher Forum of World Affairs").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-8) At the age of 22, he was the youngest person to receive a doctorate in the history of the Fletcher School.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-9)

## Diplomatic career (1978–2007)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Diplomatic career (1978–2007)")]

Tharoor's career in the United Nations began in 1978 as a staff member of the [UN High Commissioner for Refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_High_Commissioner_for_Refugees "UN High Commissioner for Refugees") (UNHCR) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva"). From 1981 until 1984 he was head of the UNHCR office in Singapore, during the [boat people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat_people "Boat people") crisis, leading the organisation's rescue efforts at sea and succeeding in resettling a backlog of Vietnamese refugees. He also processed Polish and Acehnese refugee cases.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-10) After a further stint at the UNHCR headquarters in Geneva, during which he became the first chairman of the staff elected by UNHCR personnel worldwide, Tharoor left UNHCR. In 1989 he was appointed special assistant to the Under-secretary-general for special political affairs, the unit that later became the [Peacekeeping Operations Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping "United Nations peacekeeping") in New York. Until 1996, he led the team responsible for [peacekeeping operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Protection_Force "United Nations Protection Force") in the former [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia "Yugoslavia"), spending considerable time on the ground during the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars "Yugoslav Wars") there.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-12)

### UN Assistant Secretary and Under-Secretary-General

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: UN Assistant Secretary and Under-Secretary-General")]

In 1996, Tharoor was appointed director of communications and special projects and executive assistant to Secretary-general [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan "Kofi Annan"). In January 2001, Tharoor was appointed as Interim Head[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-ST_UN-13) of the Department of Public Information (DPI) at the assistant-secretary-general level.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-ST_UN-13) He was subsequently confirmed as the under-secretary-general for communications and public information (UNDPI) with effect from 1 June 2002.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-ST_UN-13) In this capacity, he was responsible for the United Nations' communications strategy, enhancing the image and effectiveness of the organisation. In 2003 the secretary-general gave him the additional responsibility of United Nations coordinator for multilingualism. During his tenure at the UNDPI, Tharoor reformed the department and undertook a number of initiatives, ranging from organizing and conducting the first-ever UN seminar on Anti Semitism, the first-ever UN seminar on [Islamophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamophobia "Islamophobia") after the [11 September attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_September_attacks "11 September attacks"), and launching an annual list of "Ten Under-Reported Stories the World Ought to Know about", which was last produced in 2008 by his successor.

On 9 February 2007, Tharoor resigned from the post of under-secretary-general and left the UN on 1 April 2007.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-15)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-16)

### 2006 campaign for Secretary-General

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 2006 campaign for Secretary-General")]

See also: [2006 United Nations Secretary-General selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection "2006 United Nations Secretary-General selection")

In 2006, the government of India nominated Tharoor for the post of [UN Secretary General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Secretary_General "UN Secretary General").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-17) Had he won, the 50-year-old Shashi Tharoor would have become the second-youngest secretary-general, after the 46-year-old [Dag Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dag_Hammarskj%C3%B6ld "Dag Hammarskjöld").[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-18) Although all previous Secretaries-General had come from small countries, Prime Minister [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh "Manmohan Singh") and National Security Advisor [M. K. Narayanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._K._Narayanan "M. K. Narayanan") felt that Tharoor's candidacy would demonstrate India's willingness to play a larger role at the United Nations.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-open-19)

Tharoor finished second, behind [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon "Ban Ki-moon") of South Korea, in each of the four straw polls conducted by the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council "UN Security Council").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-20) In the final round, Ban emerged as the only candidate not to be vetoed by one of the [permanent members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council "Permanent members of the United Nations Security Council"), while Tharoor received one veto from the United States. U.S. ambassador [John Bolton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Bolton "John R. Bolton") later revealed his instructions from [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice "Condoleezza Rice"): "We don't want a strong Secretary-General." Tharoor was a protégé of the independently minded Kofi Annan,[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-21) and a senior American official told Tharoor that the US was determined to have "No more Kofis."[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-open-19) After the vote, Tharoor withdrew his candidacy and declined Ban Ki-moon's invitation to remain in service beyond the expiry of his term as under-secretary-general.

In February 2007, amidst speculation about his post-UN future, the Indian press reported that Tharoor might be inducted into the Council of Ministers of Prime Minister Manmohan Singh as Minister of State for External Affairs. In the same month, an American gossip blog reported that Tharoor was a finalist for the position of dean of the [USC Annenberg School for Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Annenberg_School_for_Communication_and_Journalism "USC Annenberg School for Communication and Journalism") in Los Angeles, but he withdrew his name from consideration at the final stage.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-22) Instead, Tharoor became chairman of Dubai-based Afras Ventures,[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-Rediff-23) which established the Afras Academy for Business Communication (AABC) in [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram "Thiruvananthapuram"), Kerala, the city in which he would go on to win a record four parliamentary elections. He also spoke around the world about India and Kerala, where he spent increasing amounts of time before moving to India in October 2008.

Prior to embarking on his political career, Tharoor also served on the board of overseers of the [Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fletcher_School_of_Law_and_Diplomacy "Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy"), the board of trustees of the [Aspen Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen_Institute "Aspen Institute"), and the advisory boards of the [Indo-American Arts Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-American_Arts_Council "Indo-American Arts Council"), the [American India Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_India_Foundation "American India Foundation"), the [World Policy Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Policy_Journal "World Policy Journal"), the Virtue Foundation, and the human rights organisation [Breakthrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_(human_rights) "Breakthrough (human rights)").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-UNESCO-24) At the Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy in 1976, he founded and was the first chair of the editorial board of _The Fletcher Forum of World Affairs_, a journal examining issues in international relations.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-25) Tharoor was an international adviser to the [International Committee of the Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_of_the_Red_Cross "International Committee of the Red Cross") in Geneva from 2008 to 2011. He served on the advisory council of the [Hague Institute for International Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Institute_for_International_Justice?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hague Institute for International Justice (page does not exist)")[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-The_Hague_Institute_for_Global_Justice-26) and was elected Fellow of the [New York Institute for the Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Institute_for_the_Humanities "New York Institute for the Humanities") during 1995–96.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-27) He also supported various educational causes, including as Patron of [GEMS Modern Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEMS_Modern_Academy "GEMS Modern Academy") in [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai").[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-28)

## Political career (2009–present)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Political career (2009–present)")]

### In government (2009–2014)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: In government (2009–2014)")]

[![Image 6](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/The_President%2C_Shri_Pranab_Mukherjee_administering_the_oath_as_Minister_of_State_to_Dr._Shashi_Tharoor%2C_at_a_Swearing-in_Ceremony%2C_at_Rashtrapati_Bhavan%2C_in_New_Delhi_on_October_28%2C_2012.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_President,_Shri_Pranab_Mukherjee_administering_the_oath_as_Minister_of_State_to_Dr._Shashi_Tharoor,_at_a_Swearing-in_Ceremony,_at_Rashtrapati_Bhavan,_in_New_Delhi_on_October_28,_2012.jpg)

[13th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_India "List of Presidents of India")[President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India")[Pranab Mukherjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pranab_Mukherjee "Pranab Mukherjee") administering the oath as Minister of State to Shashi Tharoor at a Swearing-in Ceremony, at [Rashtrapati Bhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrapati_Bhavan,_New_Delhi "Rashtrapati Bhavan, New Delhi"), in New Delhi in 2012.

When Tharoor began his political career, he was approached by the Congress, the Communists, and the BJP. He chose the INC because he felt ideologically aligned with the party.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-telegraphindia.com-29) In March 2009, Tharoor contested the Indian General elections as a candidate for the Congress Party in [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram_Lok_Sabha_constituency "Thiruvananthapuram Lok Sabha constituency"), Kerala. His opponents included P. Ramachandran Nair of the [Communist Party of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_India "Communist Party of India") (CPI), Neelalohitadasan Nadar of the [Bahujan Samaj Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahujan_Samaj_Party "Bahujan Samaj Party") (BSP), MP Gangadharan of the [Nationalist Congress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Congress_Party "Nationalist Congress Party") (NCP), and PK Krishna Das of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party "Bharatiya Janata Party") (BJP). Despite criticism that he was an "elite outsider",[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-30) Tharoor won the elections by a margin of 99,989. He was then selected as a minister of state in the Council of Ministers of Prime Minister Manmohan Singh. On 28 May 2009, he was sworn in as minister of state for external affairs, in charge of Africa, Latin America, and the Gulf, including the Hajj pilgrimage, and the consular, passports, and visas services of the ministry. As minister of state for external affairs, he re-established long-dormant diplomatic relationships with African nations, where his fluency in French made him popular with Francophone countries and their heads of state.

Tharoor was a pioneer in using social media as an instrument of political interaction. He was India's most-followed politician on Twitter until 2013, when he was overtaken by future prime minister [Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi "Narendra Modi"). Some of his Twitter posts have proved controversial in the past and were highlighted negatively by the opposition and press.

Tharoor was also the first Indian minister to visit Haiti after the devastating [2010 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake "2010 Haiti earthquake"). He reformed the arrangements relating to the conduct of the Hajj pilgrimage. He initiated new policy-planning activities on the Indian Ocean and represented India at various global events during his 11-month tenure as minister. In April 2010, Tharoor resigned from the position as Minister of State for External Affairs following allegations that he had misused his office to get shares in an [Indian Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Premier_League "Indian Premier League") (IPL) cricket franchise. He denied the charges and, during his resignation speech in Parliament, called for a full inquiry. In a 2014 rejoinder he defended his position: "I was never involved in a scam of any sort in the IPL- I was brought down because...[I had] antagonised some powerful political cricketing interests" and added that he had "cooperated extensively with the detailed investigation conducted by the Enforcement Directorate into the entire issue", and no wrongdoing had been found.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-31)

[![Image 7](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Manmohan_Singh_addressing_after_unveiling_the_commemoration_plaque_of_the_offsite_Campus_of_Central_University_of_Kerala%2C_at_Thiruvananthapuram%2C_in_Kerala._The_Governor_of_Kerala%2C_Shri_Nikhil_Kumar.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Manmohan_Singh_addressing_after_unveiling_the_commemoration_plaque_of_the_offsite_Campus_of_Central_University_of_Kerala,_at_Thiruvananthapuram,_in_Kerala._The_Governor_of_Kerala,_Shri_Nikhil_Kumar.jpg)

[13th prime minister of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_India "List of Prime Ministers of India") Manmohan Singh with Shashi Tharoor and Chief Minister Oommen Chandy unveiling the commemoration plaque of the offsite Campus of Central University of Kerala at Thiruvananthapuram, in Kerala.

Between 2010 and 2012, Tharoor remained active in Parliament and was member-convenor of the Parliamentary Forum on Disaster Management, a member of the Standing Committee on External Affairs, of the Consultative Committee of Defence, [the Public Accounts Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Accounts_Committee "Public Accounts Committee"), and the Joint Parliamentary Committee on Telecoms. He participated in several important debates of the 15th Lok Sabha, including on the Lokpal Bill, the demand for grants of the Ministry of External Affairs and of the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, the black money debate, and so on. In the special debate on the 60th anniversary of the Indian Parliament, Tharoor was one of four members of the Congress Party, including party President [Sonia Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Gandhi "Sonia Gandhi"), Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, and Leader of the House [Pranab Mukherjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pranab_Mukherjee "Pranab Mukherjee"), to be invited to address the Lok Sabha.

In 2012, Tharoor was re-inducted into the Union Council of Ministers by Prime Minister Manmohan Singh with the portfolio of [minister of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_state "Minister of state") for [Human Resource Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_(India) "Ministry of Education (India)"). In this role, he took special interest in the problems and challenges of [adult education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_education "Adult education"), [distance education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_education "Distance education") and enhancing high-quality research by academic institutions. He was responsible for the ministry's written answers to Parliament's questions and responded to oral questions on education during the Lok Sabha's Question Hour. He addressed forums and conferences on education, explained a vision of India's educational challenges in the context of the country's demographic opportunities, and stressed that education was not only a socioeconomic issue, but also a national security issue.

As Member of Parliament for [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram_district "Thiruvananthapuram district"), Tharoor became the first elected representative in India to issue annual reports on his work as MP, including furnishing accounts of his MPLADS expenditure. In 2012, he published a half-term report followed in 2014 by a full-term report.

### In opposition (2014–present)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: In opposition (2014–present)")]

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Hibi_Eden_and_Sasi_Tharoor.jpg/250px-Hibi_Eden_and_Sasi_Tharoor.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hibi_Eden_and_Sasi_Tharoor.jpg)

Shashi Tharoor at a march parade with [NSUI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSUI "NSUI") President [Hibi Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibi_Eden "Hibi Eden") and other Congress workers in [Ernakulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernakulam "Ernakulam"), Kerala.

In May 2014, Tharoor won his re-election from Thiruvananthapuram, defeating [O. Rajagopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Rajagopal "O. Rajagopal") of the Bharatiya Janata Party by a margin of around 15,700 votes, and became a member of the 16th [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha"), sitting in Opposition. Following a rout of the INC, he was named chairman of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on External Affairs.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-32)

In regards to Tharoor's removal from the post of INC spokesperson after he made positive remarks regarding Narendra Modi, Kolkata's _[The Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Telegraph\_(Kolkata) "The Telegraph (Kolkata)")_ opined, "For an Opposition MP to have and to exercise the freedom to appreciate a good thing done by the government and for a ruling party MP to speak and vote against the party line is not just legitimate parliamentary practice, it is the very essence of parliamentary democracy. Shashi Tharoor, from the ranks of Congress, has tried to do that; there is not one BJP MP who has matched him. Blind conformism is not loyalty, nor independent thinking, dissent."[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-33)

In 2014, Tharoor was asked to help the treasury benches draft a statement condemning Pakistan for freeing Zaki-ur-Rehman Lakhvi, the Lashkar-e-Toiba commander, who masterminded the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks "2008 Mumbai attacks") that killed 166 people. In January 2015, Tharoor asked not to debunk genuine accomplishments of Ancient Indian Science due to exaggerations of the [Hindutva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindutva "Hindutva") brigade,[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-36) amid [2015 Indian Science Congress ancient aircraft controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Indian_Science_Congress_ancient_aircraft_controversy "2015 Indian Science Congress ancient aircraft controversy").[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-37)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-38)

In March 2017, Tharoor called for the [Victoria Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Memorial,_Kolkata "Victoria Memorial, Kolkata") in [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata "Kolkata") to be converted into a museum on the effects of [British colonial rule in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"). Tharoor wrote in an _[Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al\_Jazeera\_English "Al Jazeera English")_ article that the British "conquered one of the richest countries in the world (27 per cent of global gross domestic product in 1700) and reduced it to, after over two centuries of looting and exploitation, one of the poorest, most diseased and most illiterate countries on Earth by the time they left in 1947. ...Nor is there any memorial to the massacres of the Raj, from Delhi in 1857 to [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jallianwala_Bagh_massacre "Jallianwala Bagh massacre") in 1919, the [deaths of 35 million Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_major_famines_in_India_during_British_rule "Timeline of major famines in India during British rule") in unnecessary [famines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine_in_India "Famine in India") caused by British [policies]".[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-39)

He declined and dismissed any suggestions regarding the potential of him being named as the UPA's prime ministerial candidate in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indian_general_election "2019 Indian general election").[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-40)[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-41)

Tharoor has also attempted to introduce several Private Members' Bills in the Parliament, rare for an Indian parliamentarian. Notably, his efforts to amend [Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_377_of_the_Indian_Penal_Code "Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code") were voted out by the majority of parliamentarians on two occasions. The [Apex court of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India "Supreme Court of India")[later ruled in favor of amending the controversial article in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navtej_Singh_Johar_v._Union_of_India "Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India"), thereby vindicating the position advocated by Tharoor.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-42)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-43) Tharoor got elected to the AICC Working committee on 20 August 2023. In the 2024 election, he was re-elected, defeating BJP cabinet minister [Rajeev Chandrashekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajeev_Chandrasekhar "Rajeev Chandrasekhar").[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-44)

While in opposition, he was appointed to multiple positions by the Modi-led government. In 2019, he was appointed as the chairman of Standing Committee on Communications and Information Technology.[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-Tharoor_IT-45) On 26 September 2024, he was appointed as the chairman of [Committee on External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_External_Affairs "Committee on External Affairs").[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-CEA-46)[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-IFS_India_US_China-47)[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-IFS_Foreign_Influence-48)[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-IFS_Global-49)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-IFS_relevance-50)

Tharoor, then chairman of Committee on External Affairs in [16th Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_Lok_Sabha "16th Lok Sabha"), presented the 12th report for expanding and building the numbers, quality and capacity of India's diplomats.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-EA_12th_Report_IFS-51)[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-Shashi_Tharoor_Panel_IFS-52)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-IFS_Gap-53) In March 2023, [Parliamentary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_parliamentary_committees "List of Indian parliamentary committees") on External Affairs criticized the service for being severely short-staffed and under-budgeted.

In May 2025, he led the Indian government's [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") and Panama delegation for a diplomatic outreach program to key partner countries over the April 22 [Pahalgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahalgam "Pahalgam") terror attack and India's subsequent retaliation against terror camps located in Pakistan, named [Operation Sindoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sindoor "Operation Sindoor"). He termed the opportunity as a "great honour" and a "matter of duty" as a citizen of India and said he looks forward to playing his part in leading the delegation even as his party, Congress, has frowned upon his inclusion. He asserted that only national interest is above everything else.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-54)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-55) Tharoor explicitly identified Pakistan as the principal perpetrator. He emphasized in diplomatic briefings and public statements that the attack was carried out by militants linked to Lashkar-e-Taiba, operating with Pakistan's support, and stressed that India's response targeted only terrorist infrastructure.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-56)[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-57)[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-58) During the delegation's visit to the [9/11 Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_September_11_Memorial_&_Museum "National September 11 Memorial & Museum") in New York, Tharoor highlighted the shared experience of India and the US as victims of terrorism and called for global solidarity in holding state sponsors of terror accountable.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-59)[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-60)

## Publications

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Publications")]

Tharoor has been a columnist in each of India's three best-known English-language newspapers,[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-61) most recently for _[The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Hindu "The Hindu")_ (2001–2008) and in a weekly column, "Shashi on Sunday," in the _[Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times\_of\_India "Times of India")_ (January 2007 – December 2008). Following his resignation as Minister of State for External Affairs, he began a fortnightly column on foreign policy issues in the _[Deccan Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan\_Chronicle "Deccan Chronicle")_. Previously he was a columnist for the _[Gentleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentleman\_(magazine) "Gentleman (magazine)")_ magazine and the _[Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian\_Express "Indian Express")_ newspaper, as well as a frequent contributor to _[Newsweek International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek\_International "Newsweek International")_ and the _[International Herald Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International\_Herald\_Tribune "International Herald Tribune")_. His [op-eds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-eds "Op-eds") and [book reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_reviews "Book reviews") have appeared in _[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Washington\_Post "The Washington Post")_,[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-62)_[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-63) and the _[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los\_Angeles\_Times "Los Angeles Times")_,[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-64) amongst other [papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers "List of newspapers").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] His monthly column, "India Reawakening", distributed by [Project Syndicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Syndicate "Project Syndicate"), appears in 80 newspapers around the world.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-65) As of 2021, Tharoor has written twenty-three books in English.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-66)

Tharoor began writing at the age of 6,[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-67) and his first published story appeared in the Sunday edition of _[The Free Press Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Free\_Press\_Journal "The Free Press Journal")_, in Mumbai at age 10.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-68) His [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II")[adventure novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_novel "Adventure novel")_Operation Bellows_ about an [RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force "Royal Air Force") pilot Reginald Bellows, inspired by the [Biggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biggles "Biggles") books, was serialised in the _Junior Statesman_ starting a week before his 11th birthday.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-69)[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-70)_[The Great Indian Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Great\_Indian\_Novel "The Great Indian Novel")_ had had 43 reprints as of October 2014, and a Silver Jubilee special edition was issued on the book's 25th anniversary in October 2014, by Viking Penguin India. _The Elephant, the Tiger and the Cellphone_ has also undergone several hardback re-prints.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-71)[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-72) President [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") cited Shashi Tharoor's book _India From Midnight to the Millennium_ in his speech to the Indian parliament in 2000.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-73)

Tharoor has lectured widely on India,[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-74) and is often quoted for his observations,[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] including, "India is not, as people keep calling it, an underdeveloped country, but rather, in the context of its history and cultural heritage, a highly developed one in an advanced state of decay."[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-75) He also coined a comparison of India's "thali" to the American "melting pot": "If America is a [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot "Melting pot"), then to me India is a [thali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thali "Thali") – a selection of sumptuous dishes in different bowls. Each tastes different, and does not necessarily mix with the next, but they belong together on the same plate, and they complement each other in making the meal a satisfying repast".[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-76)

Shashi Tharoor's non-fiction work _An Era of Darkness_, published later in the United Kingdom as _Inglorious Empire: What the British Did to India_, arose out of a [speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor's_Oxford_Union_speech "Shashi Tharoor's Oxford Union speech")[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-77) he made at the [Oxford Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Union "Oxford Union"), was published in 2016. It has sold over 100,000 copies in hardback reprints and continues to be a bestseller in the country. The British edition rose to Number 1 in the _[London Evening Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London\_Evening\_Standard "London Evening Standard")_ bestseller lists. Since then, he has published two other non-fiction books: _Why I Am A Hindu_ (2018) and _The Paradoxical Prime Minister_ (2018), both of which have been published in the Indian subcontinent by the Aleph Book Company.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-78)[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-79) The two books, both mega-bestsellers in India. _Why I Am a Hindu_ makes the point that it is precisely because Hindus form the majority that India has survived as a plural, secular democracy, a status that come under threat in the present world. _The Paradoxical Prime Minister_ was a critical study of the present Prime Minister Narendra Modi and the effect he has had on India, along with other questions about a leader who is reviled and worshipped in equal measure.

Victor Mallet in the _[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial\_Times "Financial Times")_ said Tharoor "wants us to understand the origins of the difficulties that confronted India" after [Indian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Independence_Act_1947 "Indian Independence Act 1947").[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-80) An article by the _[New Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New\_Statesman "New Statesman")_ said it was especially important for readers in Britain in the light of post-[Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit") discussions.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-81) Tharoor has called for the [British government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom "Government of the United Kingdom") to pay "colonial [reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reparations_(transitional_justice) "Reparations (transitional justice)")" to India.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-82)

In September 2019, he published a new book, _The Hindu Way: An Introduction_, in line with his research into Hindu culture and ways of life of late.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-83) In 2020 he published _The New World Disorder And the Indian Imperative,_ co-authored with Samir Saran, President of the [Observer Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_Research_Foundation "Observer Research Foundation") (ORF). The book is a crucial study on the current state of chaos in international politics and identifies India's imminent role, as a non-hegemonic global power, in scripting an equitable ethic for a new international order.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-84)

## Personal life

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Personal life")]

Tharoor's first wife was Tilottama Mukherji, a half-[Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis "Bengalis") and half-[Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiris "Kashmiris") academic,[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-85) and the granddaughter of politician [Kailash Nath Katju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailash_Nath_Katju "Kailash Nath Katju").[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-86) Tharoor and Mukherji had been college sweethearts and were married in 1981.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-87) After their marriage, Tilottama took her husband's last name and began teaching English at [Ngee Ann Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngee_Ann_Polytechnic "Ngee Ann Polytechnic") and also worked as a freelance writer.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-88) Their twin sons,[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-89) Kanishk and Ishaan, were born prematurely in 1984 at the [KK Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Hospital "KK Hospital") in Singapore.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-90)[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-91)[Ishaan Tharoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishaan_Tharoor "Ishaan Tharoor") grew up in Geneva, Switzerland, and was a foreign affairs journalist at _The Washington Post_.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-92) Kanishk is a former editor at Open Democracy and is the author of the highly praised short story collection _Swimmer Among The Stars_.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-93)[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-94) Tilottama is currently a professor of humanities at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University").[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-95)

Tharoor and Tilottama were divorced at some point. In 2007, Tharoor married Christa Giles, a Canadian diplomat working at the United Nations.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-96) This marriage was short-lived. Tharoor next married Dubai-based businesswoman a Kashmiri Pandit [Sunanda Pushkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunanda_Pushkar "Sunanda Pushkar") at his ancestral home in Elavanchery village in Kerala's [Palakkad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palakkad_district "Palakkad district") on 22 August 2010. He became her third husband, and step-father to her son Shiv Menon, born of a previous marriage. On 17 January 2014, Pushkar (aged 51) died at The Leela Hotel in [Chanakyapuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanakyapuri "Chanakyapuri"), New Delhi, under mysterious circumstances.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-97) In May 2018, Tharoor was charged with encouraging the suicide of his wife and marital cruelty under sections 306 and 498A of the Indian Penal Code.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-98)[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-99) On 18 August 2021, a court in Delhi discharged Tharoor from all the charges.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-100)

Tharoor is a vegetarian and he "abhors the idea of consuming the corpses of animals," although he claimed that he does not have a problem with those who do. He has stated that he is "very proud of being a [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu")" and that he's a "worshipping" and "believing Hindu". Tharoor also claims to have read a "fair amount" of the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads "Upanishads").[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-101)

In April 2019, Tharoor had an accident when praying during a [Tulabharam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulabhara "Tulabhara") ritual at a temple in Thiruvananthapuram. After being discharged, he sought a probe by the government into the incident.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-102)

## IPL Controversy

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Shashi_Tharoor&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: IPL Controversy")]

The IPL controversy related with Kochi Tuskers Kerala in 2010 involved an Indian businessman [Lalit Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalit_Modi "Lalit Modi"), Tharoor, then a Union Minister and his then friend and later wife Sunanda Pushkar.

Modi, who was the founder and first chairman of the Indian Premier League (IPL) was banned by the [Board of Control for Cricket in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Control_for_Cricket_in_India "Board of Control for Cricket in India") (BCCI) for life on September 25, 2013 involving many disputes arising after the Kochi Tuskers matter. The core allegations related to improper use of political coercion and that of "sweat equity". The controversy erupted when Modi publicly revealed the details as regards the ownership of the then newly awarded Kochi Tuskers franchise. He said that a 25% "free equity" of Kochi Tuskars had been allocated to Sunanda Pushkar. Modi said he would not sign this franchise agreement until the financial origins of this stake were brought on record in a clear and distinct manner.

This had resulted in massive political and sports controversy, leading to Tharoor's resignation from Union Cabinet and life time ban to Modi. [[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-Amol_ESPN_2013-107)[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-108)[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-109)[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-110)[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-111)

Modi made a spate of podcast and media interviews in June 2026. In these interviews, including a detailed interview with ANI Editor-in-Chief Smita Prakash, Modi made several sensational claims against many Congress leaders, including Tharoor and ex Congress President [Sonia Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Gandhi "Sonia Gandhi"). Modi claimed that his strict crackdown on illegal betting and match-fixing had angered the mafia and the underworld. He claimed that Gandhi had backed Tharoor. Modi claimed Tharoor initially "sucked up" to him, visiting his house and making sweet talks to accommodate the franchise arrangements to Sunanda Pushkar in Kochi Tuskers. When Modi allegedly declined to do so, Tharoor allegedly directly threatened Modi with government raids if he questioned the equity structure of the Kochi Tuskers. He even named Sonia Gandhi, claiming that "all guns were trained on me", naming [Ahmed Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Patel "Ahmed Patel"), Pranab Mukherjee, and [Rajiv Shukla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Shukla "Rajiv Shukla"), as pressurizing him to clear the Kochi franchise deal on behalf of Gandhi. He alleged that the then BCCI President [Shashank Manohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashank_Manohar "Shashank Manohar") had also asked him to do so, specifically mentioned 10-Janpath pressure. Modi claimed the Congress party is running a long-term vendetta against him, where [Rahul Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahul_Gandhi "Rahul Gandhi") continues to target him during elections even today. He said he possesses huge amount of evidences of this targeted witch-hunt against him.[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-112)[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-113)[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-114)[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-115)[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-116)[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-117)[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-118)[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor#cite_note-119)

So far there has been no response from Tharoor or the Congress Party.
