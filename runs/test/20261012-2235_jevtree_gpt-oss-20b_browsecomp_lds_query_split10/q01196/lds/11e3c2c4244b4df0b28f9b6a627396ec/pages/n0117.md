The politics and government in the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") work in adherence to the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India"), which was adopted on November 26, 1949, by the [Constituent Assembly of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_India "Constituent Assembly of India"), and took effect on January 26, 1950. India is a [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic")[secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular "Secular")[democratic republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_republic "Democratic republic"), described as a “[sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign "Sovereign"), socialist, secular democratic republic” in its constitution.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-India_777-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-India_888-3)

[Part I of the Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_I_of_the_Constitution_of_India "Part I of the Constitution of India") describes India as a "Union of States," and is used interchangeably to refer to the "union government" or [Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India") to denote the federal authority. India is [divided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_India "Administrative divisions of India") among [28 states and 8 union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India") for a total of 36 [subnational entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnational_entities "Subnational entities"). So, Its governance of those entities is described in the constitution as a quasi-federal system that incorporates elements from both [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism "Federalism") and [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") forms of government. The [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_and_union_territory_capitals_in_India "List of state and union territory capitals in India") of India are self-governing administrative divisions each having a [state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_India "State governments of India"), whereas the [union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory "Union territory") are governed directly by the union government, and are administered by a lieutenant governor or administrator appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India"). India follows a [dual federalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_federalism "Dual federalism") system, however the Constitution defines the organizational powers and limitations of both states and union territories of India. Government operations are led by the president of India who, as the [ceremonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_Head_of_state "Ceremonial Head of state") head of state, holds formal executive power. So, In accordance to [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_elections_of_India "Parliamentary elections of India"), the president appoints the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India") as the head of government, along with prospective ministers who are [members of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_parliament "Member of parliament").

The legal power to manage affairs in each state is shared or divided between the particular state government and the national [union government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India"). The union territories are directly governed by the union government with no state level government existing in these jurisdictions. There is a provision for a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism "Bicameralism")[legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature "Legislature") consisting of an [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house"), the [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha "Rajya Sabha") (Council of States), which represents the states of the Indian federation, and a [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house"), the [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha") (House of the People), which represents the people of India as a whole. [Vidhan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_assemblies_of_India "State legislative assemblies of India") (state legislative assemblies) represents the people residing in one of the 28 states.

The Constitution provides for an independent judiciary, which is headed by the [Supreme Court of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India "Supreme Court of India"). The court's mandate is to protect the Constitution, to settle disputes between the central government and the states, to settle inter-state disputes, to nullify any central or state laws that go against the Constitution and to protect the fundamental rights of citizens, issuing [writs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writ "Writ") for their enforcement in cases of violation.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEM._Lakshmikanth2012389%E2%80%93390-4)

There are 543 members in the Lok Sabha, who are elected using [plurality voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting "Plurality voting") (first past the post) system from 543 [Single-member district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_district "Single-member district"). There are 245 members in the Rajya Sabha, out of which 233 are elected through indirect elections by single transferable vote by the members of the state legislative assemblies; 12 other members are elected/nominated by decree from the president of India. Union and state governments are formed through [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_India "Elections in India") held every five years (unless otherwise specified), by parties that secure a majority of members in their respective lower houses.

India had [its first general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951%E2%80%9352_Indian_general_election "1951–52 Indian general election") in 1951, which was won by the [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress"), a political party that went on to dominate subsequent elections until [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Indian_general_election "1977 Indian general election"), when a non-Congress government was formed for the first time in independent India. The 1990s saw the end of [single-party domination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant-party_system "Dominant-party system") and the rise of coalition governments. The latest [18th Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Lok_Sabha "18th Lok Sabha") elections was conducted in seven phases from 19 April 2024 to 1 June 2024 by the Election commission of India. The results bought in the NDA ([National Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance "National Democratic Alliance")) to form in the next government [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-5)

In recent decades, Indian politics has become a dynastic affair.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-6) Possible reasons for this could be the party stability, absence of party organisations, independent civil society associations that mobilise support for the parties and centralised financing of elections.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-ppq.sagepub.com-7) According to the [V-Dem Democracy indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Democracy_indices "V-Dem Democracy indices") India in 2023 was the 19th most electoral [democratic country in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Asia "Democracy in Asia").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-vdem_dataset-8)

## Union government

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Union government")]

Main article: [Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India")

The Government of India is modelled after the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system "Westminster system").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-9) The Union government (also called as the Central government) is mainly composed of the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_(government) "Executive (government)"), the [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature "Legislature"), and the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary "Judiciary"), and powers are vested by the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India") in the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India"), [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India"), and the [supreme court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India "Supreme Court of India"), respectively. The [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India") is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") and the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of the [Indian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Armed_Forces "Indian Armed Forces"), while the [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_India "Elections in India") prime minister acts as the head of the executive and is responsible for running the Union government.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-10) The parliament is [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral "Bicameral") in nature, with the [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha "Rajya Sabha") being the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house"), and the [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha") the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house"). The judiciary systematically contains an apex supreme court, 25 [high courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_India "High Courts of India"), and 688 [district courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Courts_of_India "District Courts of India"), all inferior to the supreme court.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-11)

The basic [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_(common_law) "Civil law (common law)") and criminal laws governing the citizens of India are set down in major parliamentary legislation, such as the [civil procedure code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_Civil_Procedure_(India) "Code of Civil Procedure (India)"), the [penal code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Penal_Code "Indian Penal Code"), and the [criminal procedure code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_Criminal_Procedure_(India) "Code of Criminal Procedure (India)").[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-12) Similar to the Union government, individual state governments each consist of executive, legislative and judiciary branches. The legal system as applicable to the Union and individual state governments is based on the [English common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Law "Common Law") and [Statutory law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute "Statute"). The term _New Delhi_ is commonly used as a [metonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonym "Metonym") for the Union government, as the seat of the central government is in [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi "New Delhi").

### Rajya Sabha

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Rajya Sabha")]

Main article: [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha "Rajya Sabha")

The **Rajya Sabha**, constitutionally the **Council of States**, is the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house") of the [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral "Bicameral")[Parliament of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India"). It has a maximum membership of 250, of which 238 are elected by the [legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_assemblies_of_India "State legislative assemblies of India") of the states and union territories using [single transferable votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_transferable_vote "Single transferable vote") through open ballots, while the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India") can appoint 12 members for their contributions to art, literature, science, and social service. The total allowed capacity is 250 (238 elected, 12 appointed) according to article 80 of the [Indian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India"). The current potential seating capacity of the Rajya Sabha is 245 (233 elected, 12 appointed).

Members sit for [staggered terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staggered_elections "Staggered elections") lasting six years, with about a third of the 238 designates up for election every two years, in even-numbered years. Unlike the [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha"), the Rajya Sabha is a continuing chamber and hence not subject to dissolution. However, the Rajya Sabha, like the Lok Sabha, can be [prorogued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prorogation "Prorogation") by the president.

The Rajya Sabha has equal footing in legislation with the Lok Sabha, except in the area of [supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_of_supply "Loss of supply"), where the latter has overriding powers. In the case of conflicting legislation, a [joint sitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Session_of_Indian_Parliament "Joint Session of Indian Parliament") of the two houses can be held, where the Lok Sabha would hold a greater influence because of its larger membership. The [vice president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president_of_India "Vice president of India") is the _[ex-officio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex-officio "Ex-officio")_ chairperson of the Rajya Sabha, who presides over its sessions. The [deputy chairperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chairperson_of_the_Rajya_Sabha "Deputy Chairperson of the Rajya Sabha"), who is elected from amongst the house's members, takes care of the day-to-day matters of the house in the absence of the chairperson.

### Lok Sabha

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Lok Sabha")]

Main article: [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha")

The **Lok Sabha**, constitutionally the **House of the People**, is the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house") of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India")'s [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism "Bicameralism")[Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India"). [Members of the Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament,_Lok_Sabha "Member of Parliament, Lok Sabha") are elected by an adult [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage "Universal suffrage") and a [first-past-the-post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-past-the-post "First-past-the-post") system to represent their respective [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_constituencies_of_the_Lok_Sabha "List of constituencies of the Lok Sabha"), and they hold their seats for five years or until the body is dissolved by the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India") on the advice of the [council of ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers "Union Council of Ministers"). The house meets in the Lok Sabha Chambers of the [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Parliament_House,_New_Delhi "New Parliament House, New Delhi"), New Delhi.

The maximum membership of the House allotted by the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India") is 552 (initially 500 in 1950) Currently, the house has 543 seats which are filed by the election of up to 543 elected members. The new parliament has a seating capacity of 888 for Lok Sabha. A total of 131 seats (24.03%) are reserved for representatives of [Scheduled Castes (84) and Scheduled Tribes (47)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes "Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes"). The quorum for the House is 10% of the total membership.

The Lok Sabha, unless sooner dissolved, continues to operate for five years from the date appointed for its first meeting. However, while a [proclamation of emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Emergency_in_India "State of Emergency in India") is in operation, this period may be extended by [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India") by law or decree.

Members of Lok Sabha (House of the People) or the lower house of India's Parliament are elected by being voted upon by all adult citizens of India, who crossed 18 years from a set of candidates who stand in their respective constituencies. Every adult citizen of India can vote only in their constituency.

#### Office of the Prime Minister

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Office of the Prime Minister")]

Main article: [Prime Minister of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India")

The Prime Minister of India is the [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government") of the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India "Republic of India"). Executive authority is vested in the prime minister and his chosen Council of Ministers, despite the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India") being the nominal head of the executive. The prime minister has to be a member of one of the houses of bicameral [Parliament of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India"), alongside heading the respective house. The prime minister and their cabinet are at all times responsible to the Lok Sabha.

The prime minister is appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India"); however, the prime minister has to enjoy the confidence of the majority of Lok Sabha members, who are directly elected [every five years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_India#Parliamentary_general_elections_(Lok_Sabha) "Elections in India"), lest the prime minister shall resign. The prime minister can be a member of the Rajya Sabha or of the [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha"), the upper house of the parliament. The prime minister controls the selection and dismissal of members of the [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers "Union Council of Ministers"); and allocation of posts to members within the government.

#### Office of the Council of Ministers

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Office of the Council of Ministers")]

Main article: [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers "Union Council of Ministers")

The Union Council of Ministers is the [principal executive organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_(government) "Cabinet (government)") of the [Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India"), which functions as the senior decision-making body of the executive branch. It is chaired by the prime minister and consists of the heads of each of the executive government ministries.

A smaller executive body called the **Union Cabinet** is the supreme decision-making body in India; it is a subset of the Union Council of Ministers who hold important portfolios and [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_(government_department) "Ministry (government department)") of the government.

Pursuant to Article 75, a minister who works at the pleasure of the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India"), is appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister. There are five categories of the council of ministers as given below, in descending order of [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_order_of_precedence "Indian order of precedence"):

*   [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India"): Leader of the Union Council of Ministers.
*   [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_India "Deputy Prime Minister of India") (if any): Presides as prime minister in his absence or as the senior most cabinet minister.
*   Cabinet Minister: A member of the Union cabinet; leads a [ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_(government_department) "Ministry (government department)").
*   Minister of State (Independent charge): Junior minister not reporting to a Cabinet Minister.
*   [Minister of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State "Minister of State") (MoS): Deputy Minister reporting to a Cabinet Minister, usually tasked with a specific responsibility in that ministry.

### President & Vice-President of India

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: President & Vice-President of India")]

#### Office of the President of India

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Office of the President of India")]

Main article: [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India")

The President of India is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") of the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India "Republic of India"). The president is the nominal head of the executive, the first citizen of the country, as well as the [supreme commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of the [Indian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Armed_Forces "Indian Armed Forces").

The new president is chosen by an [electoral college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(India) "Electoral College (India)") consisting of the elected members of both houses of [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India") ([MPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(India) "Member of Parliament (India)")), the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies ([Vidhan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Sabha "Vidhan Sabha")) of all States and the elected members of the legislative assemblies ([MLAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_(India) "Member of the Legislative Assembly (India)")) of union territories with legislatures, i.e., [National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi "Delhi"), [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_(union_territory) "Jammu and Kashmir (union territory)") and [Puducherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puducherry_(union_territory) "Puducherry (union territory)").

The election process of the president is a more extensive process than of the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India") who is also elected indirectly (elected by the members of the majority party (or union) in the [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha")). Whereas President being the constitutional head with duties to protect, defend and preserve the constitution and [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law "Rule of law") in a [constitutional democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_democracy "Constitutional democracy") with [constitutional supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouster_clause "Ouster clause"), is elected in an extensive manner by the members of Rajya Sabha, [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha") and [state legislative assemblies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Sabha "Vidhan Sabha") in a secret ballot procedure.

#### Office of the Vice-President of India

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Office of the Vice-President of India")]

Main article: [Vice President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_India "Vice President of India")

The Vice President of India is the deputy to the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") of the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India "Republic of India"), i.e. the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India"). The office of vice president is the second-highest constitutional office after the president and ranks second in the [order of precedence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_precedence_in_India "Order of precedence in India") and first in the line of succession to the [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency "Presidency"). The vice president is also the _ex officio_ chairperson of the [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha "Rajya Sabha").

Article 66 of the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India") states the manner of election of the vice president. The vice president is elected indirectly by members of an [electoral college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(India) "Electoral College (India)") consisting of the members of both Houses of Parliament and not the members of state legislative assembly by the system of [proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_Representation "Proportional Representation") using [single transferable votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_transferable_vote "Single transferable vote") and the voting is conducted by [Election Commission of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_India "Election Commission of India") via secret ballot. The vice president also acts as the [chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_(education) "Chancellor (education)") of the Panjab University and Delhi University.

## State governments

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: State governments")]

Main article: [State governments of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_India "State governments of India")

[State governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_government "State government") in India are the governments ruling over [28 states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India") and [8 union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory "Union territory") of India and the head of the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers "Council of Ministers") in a state is the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_minister_(India) "Chief minister (India)"). Each state has a [legislative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_assembly "Legislative assembly"). A state legislature that has one house – the [state legislative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_assemblies_of_India "State legislative assemblies of India") (Vidhan Sabha) – is a [unicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameral "Unicameral") legislature. A state legislature that has two houses – [state legislative council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_councils_of_India "State legislative councils of India") (Vidhan Parishad) and the state legislative assembly – is a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral "Bicameral") legislature. The Vidhan Parishad is the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house") and corresponds to the [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha "Rajya Sabha") of the [Parliament of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India") while the Vidhan Sabha is the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house") and corresponds to the [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha") .

The judicial branch of the state consists of the [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_courts_of_India "High courts of India") at the top, followed by [District and Subordinate Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_courts_of_India "District courts of India"), along with various special courts and tribunals established for specific purposes.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-13)

### Executive branch of the State Government

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Executive branch of the State Government")]

The executive branch of a state government comprises the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_(India) "Governor (India)") and the Council of Ministers, headed by the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_minister_(India) "Chief minister (India)"). The Governor, appointed by the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India"), serves as the constitutional head, while the Chief Minister is the executive head of the government. Like the [Union Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India"), the state government follows a similar structure — the Governor corresponds to the President, the Chief Minister to the [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India"), and the State Council of Ministers to the [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers "Union Council of Ministers").[_[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable\_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_]  
 The Council of Ministers, headed by the Chief Minister, exercises the executive powers of the state and includes Cabinet Ministers and Ministers of State, appointed by the Governor on the Chief Minister's advice. The state government functions through various ministries and departments, each headed politically by a [Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_(government) "Minister (government)"). The [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy "Bureaucracy") in the state is a permanent executive responsible for implementing government policies and decisions. The [Chief Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_secretary_(India) "Chief secretary (India)"), a senior [IAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service "Indian Administrative Service") officer, is the administrative head of the government, and each department is headed administratively by a Secretary to the Government.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-14)

### State legislative council

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: State legislative council")]

Main article: [State legislative councils of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_councils_of_India "State legislative councils of India")

Vidhan Parishad, also called as the _state legislative council_, is the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house") in those states of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") that have a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism "Bicameralism")[state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_(India) "State legislature (India)"); the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house") being the [State Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_assemblies_of_India "State legislative assemblies of India"). Its establishment is defined in Article 169 of the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India").

Only 6 out of [28 states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India") have a legislative council. These are [Andhra Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Council "Andhra Pradesh Legislative Council"), [Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_Legislative_Council "Karnataka Legislative Council"), [Telangana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_Legislative_Council "Telangana Legislative Council"), [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Legislative_Council "Maharashtra Legislative Council"), [Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar_Vidhan_Parishad "Bihar Vidhan Parishad"), and [Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_Legislative_Council "Uttar Pradesh Legislative Council"). No [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory "Union territory") has a legislative council.

The size of the state legislative councils cannot be more than one third of the membership of the state legislative assembly. However, its size cannot be less than 40 members. These members elect the chairperson and deputy chairperson of the state legislative council.

### State legislative assembly

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: State legislative assembly")]

Main article: [State legislative assemblies of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_assemblies_of_India "State legislative assemblies of India")

Vidhan Sabha, also known as state legislative assembly, is a legislative body in each of the states and certain union territories of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"). In [22 states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India") and 3 [union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory "Union territory"), there is a [unicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameralism "Unicameralism") which is the sole legislative body. Vidhan Sabha is present in all the states and 3 union territories.

Each [Member of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_(India) "Member of the Legislative Assembly (India)") (MLA) is directly elected to serve 5-year terms by [single-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_district "Single-member district"). The [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India") states that a state legislative assembly must have no less than 60 and no more than 500 members however an exception may be granted via an [Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Acts_of_the_Parliament_of_India "List of Acts of the Parliament of India") of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India") as is the case in the states of [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa"), [Sikkim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikkim "Sikkim"), [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram "Mizoram") and the union territory of [Puducherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puducherry_(union_territory) "Puducherry (union territory)") which have fewer than 60 members.

A state legislative assembly may be [dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_parliament "Dissolution of parliament") in a [state of emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Emergency_in_India "State of Emergency in India"), by the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_(India) "Governor (India)") on request of the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_(India) "Chief Minister (India)"), or if a [motion of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_no_confidence "Motion of no confidence") is passed against the ruling majority party or coalition.

## Local governments

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Local governments")]

Main article: [Local government in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_India "Local government in India")

[![Image 7](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Tiers_of_Government_in_India.jpg/330px-Tiers_of_Government_in_India.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tiers_of_Government_in_India.jpg)

Tiers of Government in India

Local government in India is governmental jurisdiction below the level of the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India"). Local self-government means that residents in towns, villages and rural settlements are the people who elect local councils and their heads authorising them to solve the important issues. The 73rd and 74th constitutional amendments give recognition and protection to local governments and in addition each state has its own local government legislation.

Since 1992, local government in India takes place in two very distinct forms. Urban localities, covered in the 74th amendment to the Constitution, have Municipality but derive their powers from the individual state governments, while the powers of rural localities have been formalized under the _panchayati raj_ system, under the 73rd amendment to the Constitution.

### Urban local governing bodies

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Urban local governing bodies")]

Main article: [Municipal governance in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_governance_in_India "Municipal governance in India")

The following 3 types of democratically elected urban local bodies are called [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_India "Municipalities of India"). These are classified based on the size of the population, density, revenue, non-agricultural employment, economic importance of the urban settlement.

*   [Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporations_in_India "Municipal corporations in India"), also called the _"Nagar Nigam"_ or _"City Corporation"_, of cities with more than 1 million population.
*   [Municipal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_council_(India) "Municipal council (India)"), also called the _"Nagar Palika"_ or _"Nagar Palika Parishad"_, of cities with more than 25,000 and less than 1 million population.
*   [Town Panchayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_Panchayat "Nagar Panchayat"), also called the _"Town Council"_ or _"Nagar Panchayat"_ or _"Town Panchayat"_ or _"Notified Area Council"_ or _"Notified Area Committee"_ depending on the state within which they lie, these are in the town with more than 10,000 and less than 25,000 population.

### Rural local governing bodies

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Rural local governing bodies")]

Main article: [Panchayati raj in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj_in_India "Panchayati raj in India")

The Constitutional (73rd Amendment) Act, 1992 aims to provide a three-tier system of Panchayati Raj for all States having a population of over 2 million, to hold Panchayat elections regularly every five years, to provide reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Women, to appoint State Finance Commission to make recommendations as regards the financial powers of the Panchayats and to constitute District Planning Committee to prepare a draft development plan for the district.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-:0-15) The following 3 hierarchies of PRI panchayats exist in states or Union Territories with more than two million inhabitants:

*   [Gram Panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_Panchayat "Gram Panchayat") at village level
*   Panchayat Samiti or Mandal Parishad or Block Panchayat or Taluk Panchayat at [Community Development Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Development_Block "Community Development Block") or [Mandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal "Mandal") or Taluk level and
*   [Zila Parishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Panchayat "District Panchayat")/District Panchayat at [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_in_India "List of districts in India") level.

### Election Commission of India

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Election Commission of India")]

Main article: [Election Commission of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_India "Election Commission of India")

The **Election Commission of India** (**ECI**) is a [constitutional body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_body "Constitutional body") established by the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India") empowered to conduct [free and fair elections in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_India "Elections in India"). The Election commission is headed by a [Chief Election Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_of_India "Chief Election Commissioner of India") and consists of two other [Election Commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commissioner_of_India "Election Commissioner of India").

At the [states and union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India"), the Election Commission is assisted by the Chief Electoral Officer of the state or union territory (CEO), who leads the election machinery in the states and union territories. At the district and constituency levels, the [District Magistrates/District Collectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_collector "District collector") (in their capacity as District Election Officers), Electoral Registration Officers and Returning Officers perform election work.

The Election Commission operates under the powers granted by [Article 324](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_XV_of_the_Constitution_of_India "Part XV of the Constitution of India") of the Constitution and subsequently enacted [Representation of the People Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act_(India) "Representation of the People Act (India)"). The election commission decides the dates for the filing of nominations, voting, counting and announcement of results.

A law for the registration process for political parties was enacted in 1989. The registration ensures that the political parties are recognized as national, state and regional parties. The election commission has the right to allot symbols to the political parties depending on the status. The same symbol cannot be allocated to two political parties even if they do not contest in the region.

The commission prepares electoral rolls and updates the voter list. To prevent electoral fraud, Electors Photo Identity Cards (EPIC) were introduced in 1993. However certain legal documents such as [ration cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ration_card_(India) "Ration card (India)") have been allowed for voting in certain situations.

### State Election Commissions (SECs)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: State Election Commissions (SECs)")]

Main article: [State election commission (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_election_commission_(India) "State election commission (India)")

The state election commissions are responsible for conducting local body elections in the respective states.

The **State Election Commission (SEC)** is an independent constitutional authority responsible for conducting free and fair elections to [Local self-government institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_India "Local government in India"), such as panchayats and municipalities, within each state. Established under Article 243K and Article 243ZA of the Constitution, it oversees the preparation of electoral rolls, ensures adherence to the Model Code of Conduct, and supervises the entire election process at the state level. The Commission is headed by a State Election Commissioner, who is appointed by the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_(India) "Governor (India)").

### Political parties in India

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Political parties in India")]

When compared to other democracies, India has had a large number of political parties during its history under democratic governance. It has been estimated that over 200 parties were formed after India became independent in 1947. As per latest publications dated 23 March 2024 from Election Commission of India, and subsequent notifications, there are 6 national parties, 57 state parties, and 2,764 registered unrecognized parties. All registered parties contesting elections need to choose a symbol from a list of available symbols offered by the EC.

#### Types of political parties

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Types of political parties")]

Main article: [List of political parties in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_India "List of political parties in India")

Every political party in India, whether a national or regional/state party, must have a symbol and must be registered with the [Election Commission of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_India "Election Commission of India"). Symbols are used in the Indian political system to identify political parties in part so that illiterate people can vote by recognizing the party symbols.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-16)

In the current amendment to the Symbols Order, the commission has asserted the following five principles:[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-ECI-17)

1.   A party, national or state, must have a legislative presence.
2.   A national party's legislative presence must be in the Lok Sabha. A state party's legislative presence must be in the State Assembly.
3.   A party can set up a candidate only from amongst its own members.
4.   A party that loses its recognition shall not lose its symbol immediately but shall be allowed to use that symbol for some time to try and retrieve its status. However, the grant of such facility to the party will not mean the extension of other facilities to it, as are available to recognized parties, such as free time on [Doordarshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doordarshan "Doordarshan") or [AIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio "All India Radio"), free supply of copies of electoral rolls, etc.
5.   Recognition should be given to a party only on the basis of its own performance in elections and not because it is a splinter group of some other recognized party.

##### National political party

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: National political party")]

A political party shall be eligible to be recognized as a national party if:[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-ECI-17)

1.   it secures at least six percent (6%) of the valid votes polled in any four or more states, at a general election to the Lok Sabha or, to the State Legislative Assembly; and .
2.   in addition, it wins at least four seats in the House of the People from any State or States.
3.   or it wins at least two percent (2%) seats in the House of the People (i.e. 11 seats in the existing House having 543 members), and these members are elected from at least three different states.

Six national political parties are - [Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party "Bharatiya Janata Party"), [Indian National Congress (INC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress"), [Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahujan_Samaj_Party "Bahujan Samaj Party"), [Communist Party of India (Marxist) (CPI-M)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_India_(Marxist) "Communist Party of India (Marxist)"), [Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aam_Aadmi_Party "Aam Aadmi Party") and [National People's Party (NPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Party_(India) "National People's Party (India)").[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-18)

##### State political party

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: State political party")]

A political party shall be entitled to be recognized as a state party, if:

1.   it secures at least six percent (6%) of the valid votes polled in the state at a general election, either to the Lok Sabha or to the Legislative Assembly of the State concerned; and
2.   in addition, it wins at least two seats in the Legislative Assembly of the state concerned.
3.   or it wins at least three percent (3%) of the total number of seats in the Legislative Assembly of the state, or at least three seats in the Assembly, whichever is more.

##### Registered unrecognized political party (RUPP)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Registered unrecognized political party (RUPP)")]

Registered unrecognized political parties are those parties which are either newly registered or which have not secured enough percentage of votes in the assembly or general elections to become a state party or those which have never contested elections since being registered. Registered but unrecognized political parties don't enjoy all the benefits extended to the recognized parties.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-19)

##### Party proliferation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Party proliferation")]

Although a strict anti-defection law had been passed in 1984, there has been a continued tendency amongst politicians to float their own parties rather than join a broad based party such as the Congress or the BJP. Between the 1984 and 1989 elections, the number of parties contesting elections increased from 33 to 113. In the decades since, this fragmentation has continued.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHickenKuhonta2014205-20)

#### National Democratic Alliance (NDA)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: National Democratic Alliance (NDA)")]

Main article: [National Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance "National Democratic Alliance")

[National Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance "National Democratic Alliance") (NDA), a centre-right to right-wing coalition led by the BJP, was formed following the [1998 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Indian_general_election "1998 Indian general election"). The alliance formed the government under the leadership of [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee "Atal Bihari Vajpayee"), with outside support from the [Telugu Desam Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_Desam_Party "Telugu Desam Party") (TDP). However, the government fell after [J. Jayalalithaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Jayalalithaa "J. Jayalalithaa") withdrew support of the [All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Anna_Dravida_Munnetra_Kazhagam "All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam") (AIADMK), leading to the [1999 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Indian_general_election "1999 Indian general election"), in which NDA returned to power with an absolute majority. The coalition government went on to complete the full five-years term, becoming the first non-Congress government to do so.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_India#cite_note-25)

After securing a defeating in 2004 General Election and 2009 General Elections against the United Progressive Alliance (UPA), the NDA once again returned to power in the [2014 General Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Indian_general_election "2014 Indian general election"), for the second time, with a historic mandate of 336 out of 543 Lok Sabha seats. BJP itself won 282 seats, thereby electing [Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi "Narendra Modi") as the head of the government. In a historic win, the NDA stormed to power for the third term in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indian_general_election "2019 Indian general election") with a combined strength of 353 seats, with the BJP itself winning an absolute majority with 303 seats.

In the [2024 Indian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_general_election "2024 Indian general election") the NDA, though lost a couple of seats, succeeded to form the government by winning 293 seats with a major support from allies like TDP (16 seats) and [Janata Dal (United)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janata_Dal_(United) "Janata Dal (United)") (12 seats).

#### United Progressive Alliance (UPA)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: United Progressive Alliance (UPA)")]

Main article: [United Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Progressive_Alliance "United Progressive Alliance")

The United Progressive Alliance was a [political alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_group "Political group") in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") led by the [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress"). It was formed after the [2004 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_general_election "2004 Indian general election") with support from left-leaning political parties when no single party got the majority.

The UPA subsequently governed India from 2004 until 2014 for 2 terms before losing power to their main rivals, the [BJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party "Bharatiya Janata Party")-led [National Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance "National Democratic Alliance"). With strong corruption history within the alliance and becoming increasingly unpopular among the masses, the UPA was dissolved to form the [Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Developmental_Inclusive_Alliance "Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance") on 18 July 2023 ahead of the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Indian_general_election "Next Indian general election"). The UPA used to rule 7 [States and union territories of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India") before it was dissolved.

#### Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance (I.N.D.I.A)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Politics_of_India&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance (I.N.D.I.A)")]

Main article: [Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Developmental_Inclusive_Alliance "Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance")

Unable to defeat the NDA in 2014 General Election and 2019 General Election, and with Congress performing its poorest in same, many political leaders understood the need for a grand alliance of almost all major political parties to stand against the BJP led NDA in the 2024 General Elections which resulted in the formation of the [Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Developmental_Inclusive_Alliance "Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance") (I.N.D.I.A). It was formed by merger of [United Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Progressive_Alliance "United Progressive Alliance") (UPA), Left Front and other smaller alliances, centre-left to left-wing coalition led by [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress") (INC). The alliance, comprising 26 opposition parties, include many political parties that are at loggerhead with the INC in their respective states and territories but are in alliance for the 2024 General Elections.
