**Thiruvananthapuram**, [city](https://www.britannica.com/topic/city), capital of [Keralam](https://www.britannica.com/place/Keralam) state, southwestern [India](https://www.britannica.com/place/India). It is situated along the [Arabian Sea](https://www.britannica.com/place/Arabian-Sea) with isolated hills on a coastal plain.

[![Image 13: Crowd of people in colorful clothing walk and gather in front of the ornate Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, India at sunset.](https://cdn.britannica.com/82/263282-050-D90D37DF/Hinduism-People-visiting-the-Sree-Padmanabhaswamy-Temple-in-Thiruvananthapuram-Trivandrum-Kerala-India-on-April-12-2024.jpg?w=300)](https://cdn.britannica.com/82/263282-050-D90D37DF/Hinduism-People-visiting-the-Sree-Padmanabhaswamy-Temple-in-Thiruvananthapuram-Trivandrum-Kerala-India-on-April-12-2024.jpg)

[Padmanabhaswamy Temple](https://cdn.britannica.com/82/263282-050-D90D37DF/Hinduism-People-visiting-the-Sree-Padmanabhaswamy-Temple-in-Thiruvananthapuram-Trivandrum-Kerala-India-on-April-12-2024.jpg)One of the richest temples in the world, holding valuables estimated to be worth about $22 billion, is the Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram (Trivandrum), Keralam, India.(more)

The [community](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/community) became prominent under Raja Martanda Varma, who made it the capital of his kingdom of [Travancore](https://www.britannica.com/place/Travancore) in 1745. The city’s former name, Trivandrum, was given by the British and is a contraction of Thiruvananthapuram, its name during the Travancore kingdom that was adopted again in 1991.

Thiruvananthapuram’s industries include [mineral processing](https://www.britannica.com/technology/mineral-processing), sugar milling, textiles, and handicrafts. Rice and coconut cultivation and coastal fishing are economically important. Thiruvananthapuram is a rail terminus and road hub and has an airport and a harbor. It is the site of the University of Kerala (1937) and its [affiliated](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/affiliated) colleges and technical schools. It also has a museum, zoological gardens, an observatory, and an art gallery. A large fort contains several palaces and the [Padmanabhaswamy Temple](https://www.britannica.com/place/Padmanabhaswamy-Temple) which is dedicated to the Hindu god [Vishnu](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Vishnu) and is a noted pilgrimage center. The city gets its name from the temple—it is considered the town or [abode](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/abode) (_puram_) of the Lord (_thiru_) Ananta (“endless”), a name of Vishnu and, more commonly, the name of the serpent Shesha on whom Vishnu rests.Pop. (2001) city, 744,983; urban agglom., 889,635; (2011) city, 743,691; urban agglom., 1,679,754.
