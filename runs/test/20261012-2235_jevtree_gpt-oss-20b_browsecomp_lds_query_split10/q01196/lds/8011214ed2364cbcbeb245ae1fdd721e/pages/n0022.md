## CEO update

_At the start of 2026, the Wikimedia Foundation welcomed new leadership with the appointment of Bernadette Meehan as Chief Executive Officer. Bernadette joined the Foundation in January, bringing extensive international experience in diplomacy, public service, and global program leadership. Most recently, she served as U.S. Ambassador to Chile, and previously led global leadership initiatives at the Obama Foundation. Her career has been defined by a commitment to collaboration, public engagement, and strengthening institutions that serve the public good — an approach that aligns closely with Wikimedia’s mission to ensure free knowledge is available to everyone. Below, Bernadette shares her early reflections as CEO._

> **“In a digital world shaped by algorithms, profits, and self-interest, Wikipedia stands apart**“

![Image 6](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/960px-Meehan-12032025CK_0312-square.jpg?w=640)
**Bernadette Meehan**  
CEO of the Wikimedia Foundation

More than 340 edits every minute.More than 66 million articles.By any measure, Wikipedia is extraordinary.But its true power isn’t the information it holds — it’s the people who bring it to life.

People are at the heart of everything we do. Volunteers build and maintain each article with care and rigor, ensuring knowledge remains accurate, current, and trustworthy. Readers turn to Wikipedia every day in moments of curiosity and discovery, carrying that knowledge into classrooms, conversations, and communities. Donors make it all possible, powering the infrastructure, independence, and global reach of free knowledge.

When I joined the Wikimedia Foundation as CEO in January 2026, I began by listening. I’ve spoken with hundreds of volunteers, colleagues, donors, partners, and readers worldwide. I want to understand why they choose Wikipedia in a world with countless sources of information, and what they need from us to carry this shared work forward.

What I’ve heard is both sobering and energizing: Human knowledge has never been more essential, yet it is increasingly under threat. At the same time, there’s a strong sense of possibility and responsibility: a moment for Wikipedia, other Wikimedia projects, and all who care about free knowledge, to collectively shape the next 25 years.

That belief is at the heart of this year’s annual report theme: _“The People’s Knowledge, built by and for humans.”_

You already know Wikipedia. In this report, we invite you to meet some of the people who bring it to life every day. You’ll follow the journey of a single article and see the curiosity, care, and collaboration of the many hands and perspectives that together shape the knowledge that informs and connects the world.

In a digital world shaped by algorithms, profits, and self-interest, Wikipedia stands apart: a truly grassroots endeavor guided by no agenda other than the passions and intellect hundreds of thousands of volunteers bring.This embodies curiosity, wonder, and a burning desire to get it right, using a collaborative model refined over the past 25 years to provide accurate, unbiased, and reliable information.

This is how knowledge should be created and shared: by humans, for humans. Grounded in reasoning and critical thinking, rigorously sourced, and shaped into a neutral point of view through discussion and thoughtful deliberation, not through rote repetition or mechanical reproduction.

Thank you for being part of this story — you _are_ the volunteers, donors, and readers whose curiosity, care, and dedication bring Wikipedia and the world of Wikimedia projects to life.

With gratitude,  
Bernadette  
  
_Bernadette Meehan  
Wikimedia Foundation CEO_

## The ALS article

## The story of a single  
Wikipedia article.

### ****One article. Many hands. Real impact.****

Every Wikipedia article tells a story about Wikipedia’s collaborative reach and impact. That’s 66 million stories. And 66 million narratives about people working together to add important knowledge to the world through Wikipedia. This is the story of a single Wikipedia article. This one article — a medical article on Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) — exemplifies everything unique about Wikipedia, including the behind-the-scenes work that ensures Wikipedia is constantly evolving for the better.

The volunteers, donors, and readers you’ll meet in this report make Wikipedia’s knowledge human-centered. Wikipedia wouldn’t exist without them. A single Wikipedia article tells a universal story: that something powerful grows through the collective efforts of people who choose to give their time, trust, and support to something larger than themselves.

Paul Wicks is a neuropsychologist specializing in digital health who edits medical articles, including the ALS article, with a focus on evidence quality and clarity. He trained in treating patients with ALS and Parkinson’s disease at the Institute of Psychiatry in London, where he received his PhD. As a researcher he studied the psychological consequences of the disease, including the profile of depression, anxiety, and cognitive changes in different subgroups. For over 20 years, he ran online communities connecting thousands of patients and caregivers to advance peer support, advocacy, and clinical research. Multiple worlds collided when a close family member was diagnosed with the disease in 2013 and he became a caregiver and advocate, too. In 2011 he was awarded MIT Technology Review’s TR35 “Humanitarian of the Year” award, and in 2012 he was recognized as a TED Fellow.

> **“Many people will be reading this article on the worst day of their lives. When someone is first diagnosed with ALS, Wikipedia is one of the first articles they and their family members will read. Increasingly, I suspect AI will come to rely on the carefully constructed articles there, too. Therefore it’s critical this information is accurate, well referenced, neutral, and up to date. Wikipedia is also a primary source for journalists, medical students, and policymakers, many of whom lack free access to the peer-reviewed scientific literature. And its readership is vast; I’ve met hundreds of families in my research career, but the ALS page is read [1.5 million times each year](https://dkcgbf04.eu1.hs-sales-engage.com/Ctc/OV+23284/dkcgbF04/Jk84YGXpW5BWr2F6lZ3nHW1ydTNL3v0ZyCW8SCVRQ7dYNdWW8sHgV219T-v2VnQNGy7hMYcjW2sxLw-2FQrY8N3DTQqNFygTsW5WmTvq6hFHCRW6Fdxgp7TZSvBW1-R4PP1w3_hXW3khnP71FVnytW3LJMWF4V1lXkW1mRk6D7vc-T9W1Rcwxh6wHZjxW3jrb5k78yW-4W4NQSVL4dDXGJW3Kj8Bm34tQF3W3nWVSC8CXD5qW1XNl7Q4dj28sW6rQlPd4xNQlZW2t1fnH2lgRvgW73qcmF2hmVJ4W7bRF854hXpW6W8gBN9Q13bCfxW8kY4951rBBlBW163QQg4nFkG_VxcFS_6YC8S-W1pKQg02W9tlxW20J-2X99CWdfW7qmj1V2k6jPqW1MhR6B3v1Y4cN4vmb8kG_xWmW2hRbsx8q01XtW6Qmczj4Fh33YN1RwtgcF0QL7dvwspb04) in English alone which is a scale unmatched by any other resource in the world. #### **Wikipedia is “an extraordinary research tool and I am happy to contribute”**

[Share this story](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/#)

![Image 22](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rudolf-Buitelaar-5.jpg)

Rudolf’s career spans over three decades and centers on economic development issues in Latin America and the Caribbean. He has performed economic research and delivered policy advice, capacity building, and training services — mostly as a United Nations staff member, in particular with its regional commission, the Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean (CEPAL). He has worked across rural development, mining, and industry clusters, with a focus on productivity, competitiveness, innovation, and partnerships between public institutions, academia, and civil society. He has a Master’s degree in Development Economics from the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, Netherlands.

> **“I made an edit to that article, to introduce a wikilink to the [article about Brachial amyotrophic diplegia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachial_amyotrophic_diplegia), which I authored. Brachial amyotrophic diplegia is also called ‘Vulpian-Bernhardt Syndrome’ and ‘flail arm syndrome,’ which is how the link appears in the article. Brachial amyotrophic diplegia is usually considered to be a regional variant of ALS and did not have a stand-alone article. I am happy that my article on Brachial amyotrophic diplegia was well received, was expanded by other editors, and is in good shape. Indeed, I’ve edited Wikipedia since 2020, shortly after I retired from my job as a United Nations staff member. I found Wikipedia to be an extraordinary research tool and I am happy to contribute, both with new articles and with the maintenance of existing articles. A family member of mine has Brachial amyotrophic diplegia and that is why I felt the need to have a Wikipedia article on this rare variant of ALS.”**

### **Select milestones of the ALS article**

January 28, 2003

![Image 25](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/0sources.png?w=187)
A contributor creates the beginnings of the article — not as an actual article but as a “redirect” page that takes users to the then-Wikipedia article about Motor Neuron Diseases. The Motor Neuron Diseases article was itself a work-in-progress, while the ALS page was now established for contributors to begin adding knowledge.

[See what it looked like →](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ALS&oldid=4482583)

January 2, 2006

![Image 26](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2700words.png?w=187)
Three years later, the ALS article has the beginnings of being a real article. It now had six sections — including sections on “Symptoms,” “Diagnosis,” “Cause,” and “Treatment.” References and links to sources were still missing.

[See what it looked like →](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ALS&oldid=33555451)

January 2, 2008

![Image 27](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/26references.png?w=187)
Two years later, hundreds of contributors have expanded the ALS article, with a bolstered “Epidemiology” section, and new sections on “Etymology,” “Etiology,” and “Prognosis.” The article now has 26 references, links to sources, and an “External Links” section for further reading from leading medical organisations in the field.

[See what it looked like →](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ALS&oldid=181618993)

**January 29, 2013**

![Image 28](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/13sections.png?w=187)
The ALS article is much more evolved with 60 references! It also has multiple sub-sections within sections — and its first image: an MRI of a brain. The article also has a “History” timeline that gives readers important context on the disease’s history.

[See what it looked like →](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ALS&oldid=535486335)

January 29, 2018

![Image 29](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/111references.png?w=187)
The article has expanded even more — with a new “Management” section and a new section on “Society and Culture” that features a video of the “ALS Ice Bucket Challenge.” The number of references has grown to 111.

[See what it looked like →](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ALS&oldid=822884212)

January 30, 2023

![Image 30](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/18kwords.png?w=187)
The article has evolved with the latest information about the disease — including knowledge about ALS’s onset and about the disease’s regional variants. The article now has 165 references, many with clickable links that let users look at the original source material.

[See what it looked like →](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ALS&oldid=1136485757)

January 29, 2025

![Image 31](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/good.png?w=187)

April 2026

The article now stands as a powerful example of what collective knowledge can achieve: It exists in 75 languages, connecting readers around the world to vital, life-changing information. Knowledge from a Wikipedia article is more than a moment of insight. Its impact on people’s lives can last a lifetime. We’re also expanding our [Abstract Wikipedia project](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Abstract_Wikipedia), a long-term initiative designed to accelerate language equality across Wikipedia by transforming Wikipedias with small article bases into full-fledged knowledge sources.

The underlying technical infrastructure of Abstract Wikipedia is called “[Wikifunctions](https://www.wikifunctions.org/wiki/Wikifunctions:Main_Page),” and during the 2024-2025 fiscal year, we [announced](https://www.wikifunctions.org/wiki/Wikifunctions:Status_updates/2025-04-16) the first integration of Wikifunctions on Wikipedia, with Wikifunctions live on [Dagbani Wikipedia](https://dag.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol%C9%94%C9%A3u). One of our [five focus languages](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:MyLanguage/Abstract%20Wikipedia/Updates/2021-04-15), [Dagbani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagbani%20language) is a Niger-Congo language that’s spoken in northern Ghana and has about 3 million native speakers. Dagbani Wikipedia has more than 12,000 articles, and it’s written in a Latin-based alphabet.

So, the future of Wikipedia is happening right now. We envision a day when every ALS article on Wikipedia — including those in Dagbani and Swahili — matches the depth, sourcing, and impact of today’s English-language medical articles. Just last year, a Canadian woman shared that she used Wikipedia as “her base” to understand her autoimmune condition before going to medical journals — and that “it would have been hard to do without Wikipedia.” That’s the essence of Wikipedia’s global reach: knowledge that crosses languages, borders, and lives.

### **We made it easier than ever to discover knowledge that interests you**

From Wikipedia’s beginning, we’ve helped users discover knowledge across the Wikimedia projects, as in the “blue links” that connect one article to another. #### **We made it easier than ever to discover knowledge that interests you**

[Share this story](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/#)

![Image 75](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/1280px-Search_suggestions_on_Italian_Wikipedia_4-3.png)

No typing needed. No thinking about what you might want to read. Automatically, you get the three suggested articles that relate directly to the article you’re viewing. This gives you an immediate way to find more knowledge across Wikipedia. A previous example of this kind of work: A decade ago, we implemented “Hovercards” (also called “link previews”), which gives users a visual preview when you hover over an article’s blue link. By introducing Hovercards, readers no longer needed to click the link to find out exactly what the article is about.

We know that users’ internet habits have changed in the past decade — and even in the past year as new AI tools have emerged and people have access to more ways to search for information. That’s why we’re now helping readers discover related content before they begin typing a search query. In the search bar on Wikipedia, in addition to ​​the three suggested articles that appear, users will also see “type-ahead search suggestions,” real-time suggestions that appear as you type, predicting what you might be looking for and surfacing relevant articles before you complete your search. We created these “type-ahead search suggestions” soon after we introduced Hovercards, and we’ve improved their functionality since then — another example of the continuing work that we’re doing to help you discover knowledge that interests you, whether it’s a medical article or something else entirely.

### **We strengthened the safety of volunteer editors and their ability to add knowledge for you across Wikipedia**

Volunteer contributors add the knowledge on Wikipedia, and they live in almost every country of the world, numbering more than 250,000. While many contributors have user pages with their biographical information, edits, and even how to contact them, a significant number of contributors make edits from countries where it can be dangerous to contribute knowledge on topics like politics or historic events. Understandably, some editors don’t want to reveal their real names or IP addresses, which can identify their approximate location, including country, city, and even a postal code.

[Read More](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/#)

#### **We strengthened the safety of volunteer editors and their ability to add knowledge for you across Wikipedia**

[Share this story](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/#)

![Image 77](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/1280px-Edit-a-thon_Les_Mysteres_de_Pascal_atelier_de_contribution.jpg)

That’s why we deployed “temporary accounts” last year across nearly every Wikipedia language edition. This protects the privacy of editors who are editing Wikipedia without a Wikimedia account or without logging in, and helps them contribute knowledge without fear of reprisal for their volunteer work on Wikipedia.

From Wikipedia’s beginning, IP addresses have played a major part in the protection of Wikipedia’s knowledge. They allow users to fight vandalism, abuse, and other disruptive behavior across our projects. But changing laws and regulation around internet privacy created urgency for the Wikimedia projects to protect personal data more strongly — which is what “temporary accounts” are doing. No longer are editors’ IP addresses publicly available. Protecting editors has always been of paramount importance. As the internet changes, we’re adapting ways to protect editors and their ability to continue expanding Wikipedia’s knowledge base without fear of reprisals.

### **We built and applied protective measures into our server systems that have helped us block billions of requests originating from unscrupulous AI scrapers**

The Wikimedia Foundation’s[Site Reliability Engineering team](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Site_Reliability_Engineering) ensures that Wikipedia and its sister projects are always available, rapidly and securely, so that you can find knowledge when you need it. #### ****We built and applied protective measures into our server systems that have helped us block billions of requests originating from unscrupulous AI scrapers****

[Share this story](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/#)

![Image 79](https://wikimediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/1280px-Wikimedia_Servers-0051_16.jpg)

Since January 2024, we have seen the bandwidth used for downloading multimedia content grow by 50 percent — an increase that’s not coming from human readers but largely from automated programs that scrape our [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Main_Page) image catalog of openly licensed images to feed images to AI models. Our infrastructure is built to sustain sudden traffic spikes from humans searching Wikipedia in moments of breaking news, but the amount of traffic generated by scraper bots was unprecedented and significantly increased Foundation costs and staff time.

While human readers tend to focus on specific, often similar topics, crawler bots tend to “bulk read” larger numbers of pages and visit the less popular pages. This means these types of requests are more likely to get forwarded to our core datacenter rather than the “cache” datacenter that’s closest to the user — which makes it much more expensive in terms of consumption of our resources.

To help mitigate these issues, we updated our bot detection systems this past year to better manage the increase in AI scrapers, including those that tried to appear human and evade detection. Our content is free but our infrastructure is not, and our actions this past year were designed to establish responsible use of our infrastructure and dedicate our engineering resources to supporting and prioritizing our human users and their access to knowledge.

Accountability and transparency are core values of the Wikimedia Foundation. We are committed to stewarding funds and resources responsibly so that every contribution advances our mission of expanding access to knowledge for all.

### Investment in Technology 45%

Nearly half of our budget goes toward supporting the technology that powers Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects. #### Jimmy Wales

[Jimmy Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/en:Jimmy_Wales)is an Internet entrepreneur and[wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/wiki)enthusiast, and founder of the Wikipedia project.

Jimmy was born in Huntsville,Alabama in 1966, and is a graduate of Auburn University and the University of Alabama. He worked as Research Director at Chicago Options Associates, a futures and options trading firm then located in Chicago. In the mid-1990s he started[Bomis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomis), a search portal focusing on aspects of pop culture, one of the first users of the freely licensed data of the[Open Directory Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Directory_Project).

In 1999, Jimmy had the concept of a freely distributable encyclopedia and founded[Nupedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nupedia), by hiring philosopher Larry Sanger as editor-in-chief and assigning two programmers to write software for it. Nupedia failed, perhaps due to being a top-down cathedral model, as opposed to Wikipedia, which is the ultimate bazaar. After two years of working with the Nupedia concept, that team opened Wikipedia to help channel content into Nupedia; Wikipedia became an instant success, but not in the envisioned way, and Nupedia was shut down. In 2003, Jimmy set up the Wikimedia Foundation, a non-profit organization, to support Wikipedia and its sister projects.

In 2004, Jimmy founded[Wikia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikia). He was appointed a fellow of Harvard Law School’s[Berkman Center for Internet and Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkman_Center_for_Internet_%26_Society)in mid-2005, and in October of 2005 joined the Board of Directors of[Socialtext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialtext), a provider of wiki technology to businesses.

Succeeded Maryana Iskander as Chief Executive Officer in January 2026, Executive Team

* * *

Bernadette Meehan is a global executive and former U.S. ambassador with deep experience leading complex organizations, navigating global policy challenges, building cross-sector partnerships, and driving innovation. She brings over two decades of leadership in geopolitics, technology, philanthropy, and global markets.

As U.S. Ambassador to Chile from 2022–2025, Bernadette advanced digital infrastructure and technology partnerships critical to global competitiveness. She played a pivotal role enabling Google’s investment in the Humboldt Cable, the first subsea cable linking South America with Asia, a project poised to transform digital connectivity. She secured ratification of a long-pending Bilateral Tax Treaty and negotiated agreements to expand access for U.S. agricultural exports. Her advocacy for international scientific cooperation, particularly in astronomy and Antarctic research, strengthened U.S. leadership in frontier science.

Previously, Bernadette served as Executive Vice President of Global Programs and a member of the Executive Committee of the Obama Foundation. She designed leadership development programs across Africa, the Asia-Pacific, and Europe, establishing the foundation’s international presence and cultivating a worldwide network of emerging innovators, entrepreneurs, and civic leaders.

Bernadette’s commitment to public service was shaped over more than a decade as a career Foreign Service Officer, serving in both Republican and Democratic administrations. At the White House National Security Council (NSC) she held several senior roles, including Special Assistant to the President & NSC Spokesperson. Additional diplomatic assignments included Special Assistant to the Secretary of State; postings in the United Arab Emirates, Iraq, and Colombia; and resident fellow and adjunct professor at Georgetown University’s School of Foreign Service. In 2007 the Department of State recognized her as one of its most promising future leaders, and she is the recipient of numerous performance awards.

Bernadette began her career in the private sector as a Vice President at J.P. Morgan Chase and Lehman Brothers, building a foundation in global markets, financial strategy, and technology.

Her civic leadership includes prior service on the Board of Advisors at Georgetown University’s Institute for the Study of Diplomacy and the Advisory Council of Hostage US. Bernadette holds a Bachelor of Arts from Boston College and speaks Spanish and Arabic. She is married and the mother of a young daughter.

### ****A story of friendship and philanthropy: How three donors from Houston have bolstered Wikipedia year after year****

Diana Strassmann, Anne Chao, and Winnie Scheuer all live in Houston, Texas, USA. They share a love of Wikipedia and its ability to offer knowledge that’s free of misinformation, commercial influence, and language barriers. That’s why Diana, Anne, and Winnie have supported the Wikimedia Foundation with donations and with something just as important: their time.

Diana has done it in the classroom — as a [professor of humanities at Rice University](https://profiles.rice.edu/faculty/diana-strassmann), where she taught students how to edit Wikipedia through in-class assignments and edit-a-thons that she helped organize. Diana shepherded Wikipedia editing for almost two decades, until she retired in June 2024, and it’s through Rice 20 years ago that Diana first met Anne, [who has also lectured there](https://profiles.rice.edu/faculty/anne-chao). Diana has also hosted fundraising events for Wikipedia, which is how she and Anne got to know Winnie, whose career has been in the art world. They’ve become fast friends — and easily laugh together, even as Anne teases Winnie that she’s from a younger generation.

“She’s like our younger sister,” says Anne, prompting Winnie to laugh with her.

Diana, Anne, and Winnie have distinct personal stories, but their backgrounds share an important theme: All three have a history of thriving in positions of leadership that involve helping others learn about ideas. In this fundamental way, their engagement with Wikipedia isn’t by accident. In Wikipedia, Diana, Anne, and Winnie see a knowledge source that reflects their own belief that knowledge should do good in the world.

An economist by training, Diana is the founding editor of the journal _Feminist Economics,_ which is the journal of the International Association for Feminist Economics — an organization she co-founded. Anne, who has a doctorate in Modern Chinese History from Rice University, is Program Manager of the Houston Asian American Archive. Winnie has two decades of experience in the art world, with roles in the modern and contemporary art departments at Christie’s and Phillips auction houses in New York, and in executive administration at the Museum of Fine Arts, Houston.

“I believe in a theory of knowledge that’s participatory, so making sure that the Wikimedia Foundation has the tools that it needs to function well is really important to me,” says Diana, who taught her first Wikipedia assignment in 2007. “I see the Wikimedia Foundation — especially with the advent of AI and other changes in technology — as being all the more needing of financial support. So I’m very happy to be helpful in that regard.”

Diana first became aware of Wikipedia through her son, who was a voracious reader of Wikipedia as a teen. “When I first became interested in Wikipedia, it was because of my son. He’s almost 36, but (back then) he spent so much time reading Wikipedia. And I thought, ‘I got to learn more about this.’ He loved learning from Wikipedia — far more interesting to him than fiction.”

All three say that their support of Wikipedia is unwavering. That’s why they now collaborate on Houston fundraising events for Wikipedia, essentially becoming volunteer ambassadors for a knowledge source they use as readily as anyone.

“I use Wikipedia quite a lot,” says Winnie, who once used Wikipedia to trace her family lineage for generations. “I find (its articles) to be the perfect amount of information that I can understand in a very clear way about what I’m missing or what I need to know.”

Winnie — whose husband, Kevin Bonebrake, is a [Board member of the Wikimedia Endowment](https://wikimediaendowment.org/#leadership), a permanent fund that safeguards Wikipedia’s future — uses the word “family” to describe her connection to the Wikimedia projects. “Kevin and I hope to be part of the Wikipedia and Wikimedia Foundation family for a long time,” she says. “This is something that is very dear to us and is truly at the top of our gifting philosophy. And there’s no other organization that we feel is more important to us. It’s really our priority, and will continue to be.”

This kind of continuation is paramount to Diana and Anne, too — as it is to many of the eight million people who donate to the Wikimedia Foundation every year, and as it is to the hundreds of thousands of volunteer Wikipedia editors who give their time willingly from around the world. Wikipedia’s supporters live in virtually every country and corner of the world. Houston — a city in southeast Texas where millions of people reside — is a microcosm of the global campaign that’s trying, as Anne says, to “see Wikipedia continue to grow.”
