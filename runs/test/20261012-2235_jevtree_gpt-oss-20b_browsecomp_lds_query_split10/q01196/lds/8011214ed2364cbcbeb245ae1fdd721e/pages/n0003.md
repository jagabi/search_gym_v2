Where your donation goes

 Accountability and transparency are core values at the Wikimedia Foundation. We manage funds and resources to ensure that every contribution supports our mission. We have earned the Platinum Seal of Transparency from Candid (formerly GuideStar), and Charity Navigator awarded us its highest rating. You can [read our most recent annual report](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/) for more information about our financial health. 

45% Investment in Technology 
Nearly half of our budget goes toward supporting the technology that powers Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects. We are constantly working to enhance the user experience for both contributors and readers, improve site security, and ensure reliable access to our websites globally. This infrastructure and product support sustain one of the top ten most visited websites in the world, all at a fraction of the cost of popular for-profit websites.

32% Support for Volunteers 
The global reach of Wikimedia projects is made possible by the hard work of volunteers from across the globe. We provide grants, legal support, and other resources to help build vibrant volunteer communities. Additionally, we promote community engagement through outreach initiatives and advocate for the growth and protection of free knowledge.

12% Allocation to Fundraising Efforts 
Donor support is crucial to sustaining Wikipedia and our other free knowledge endeavors. Our team is committed to efficient and effective fundraising throughout the year, ensuring that every contribution helps advance our mission.

11% General and Administrative Expenses 
Operational costs are essential for the smooth management and governance of the Wikimedia Foundation. These expenses help us recruit top talent and support staff around the world, empowering them to carry out the mission of the Wikimedia Foundation.

The **Padmanabhaswamy Temple** (Malayalam:[[pɐd̪mɐnaːbʰɐswaːmi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Malayalam "Help:IPA/Malayalam")) is a [Hindu temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_temple "Hindu temple") dedicated to [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu "Vishnu") in [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram "Thiruvananthapuram"), [Keralam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala"), India. It is one of the 108 [Divya Desams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Desam "Divya Desam") which are considered the sacred sites of the [Sri Vaishnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Vaishnavism "Sri Vaishnavism") tradition.

The temple's principal deity is [Anantha Padmanabhaswamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anantha_Padmanabhaswamy?action=edit&redlink=1 "Anantha Padmanabhaswamy (page does not exist)"), a form of Vishnu depicted in [yogic sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga-nidra "Yoga-nidra"), on his serpent mount named [Shesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shesha "Shesha"). Padmanabhaswamy is the [tutelary deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutelary_deity "Tutelary deity") of the [Travancore royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travancore_royal_family "Travancore royal family"). The titular [Maharaja of Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_of_Travancore "Maharaja of Travancore"), [Moolam Thirunal Rama Varma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moolam_Thirunal_Rama_Varma "Moolam Thirunal Rama Varma"), is the current trustee of the temple.

## Mythology, legends, and stories

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Padmanabhaswamy_Temple&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Mythology, legends, and stories")]

The Temple is referred to as 'Syanandoorapura' in the [Puranas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas "Puranas") and Hindu Epics.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-RK-1) Several extant Hindu texts including the [Vishnu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu_Purana "Vishnu Purana"),[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Ramesh_2000_137-2)[Brahma Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Purana "Brahma Purana"),[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Ramesh_2000_130-3)[Matsya Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsya_Purana "Matsya Purana"),[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Ramesh_2000_134-4)[Varaha Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varaha_Purana "Varaha Purana"),[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Ramesh_2000_134-4)[Skanda Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanda_Purana "Skanda Purana"),[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Ramesh_2000_130-3)[Padma Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Purana "Padma Purana"),[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Ramesh_2000_130-3)[Vayu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayu_Purana "Vayu Purana"), [Bhagavata Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavata_Purana "Bhagavata Purana") and [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata") mention the Padmanabhaswamy Temple.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Ramesh_2000_130-3)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-The_Kovils_of_Kerala-5)

It is believed that [Parashurama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parashurama "Parashurama") "purified and venerated" the idol of Sree Padmanabhaswamy in the _[Dvapara Yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvapara\_Yuga "Dvapara Yuga")_.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-6) As per the legend, Parashurama entrusted 'Kshethra Karyam' (administration of the Temple) to seven Potti families – Koopakkara Potti, Vanchiyoor Athiyara Potti, Kollur Athiyara Potti, Muttavila Potti, Karuva Potti, Neythasseri Potti and Sreekaryathu Potti. Then King Adithya Vikrama of Vanchi (Venad) was directed by Parashurama to do 'Paripalanam' (Protection) of the Temple. Parashurama is said to have given the 'Tantram' of the Temple to Tharananallur Namboothiripad. This legend is narrated in detail in the Kerala Mahathmyam which forms part of the [Brahmanda Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmanda_Purana "Brahmanda Purana").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-RK-1)

Another story, which is more recent and less regarded mythologically and legendarily, associates the Temple with the legendary sage [Vilwamangalam Swamiyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilwamangalam_Swamiyar "Vilwamangalam Swamiyar") (also called Divakara Muni in some versions).[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-7)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-The_Kovils_of_Kerala-5)

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Padmanabhaswamy_Temple&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")]

[![Image 26](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple_Gopuram.jpg/250px-Padmanabhaswamy_Temple_Gopuram.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Padmanabhaswamy_Temple_Gopuram.jpg)

[Gopuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopuram "Gopuram") of the Padmanabhaswamy Temple

The Temple has been referred to in (the only recorded) [Sangam period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangam_period "Sangam period")[literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature") several times including in the [Cilappatikaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilappatikaram "Cilappatikaram").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-rediff.com-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Seran_Vanji-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Silappadikaram-10) Many conventional historians and scholars believe that one of the names that the Temple had, "The Golden Temple", was in cognisance of the Temple's extreme wealth by that point (early Sangam period).[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-rediff.com-8) Many extant pieces of Tamil [Sangam literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangam_literature "Sangam literature") and poetry as well as later works of the 9th century of Tamil poet–saints like [Nammalwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nammalvar "Nammalvar") eulogise the temple as heaven and both the temple and the city as having walls and streets of pure gold.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-huffingtonpost.com-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-a.srivathsan-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-13)

The temple is one of the 108 principal [Divya Desams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Desam "Divya Desam") ("Holy Abodes") in [Vaishnavism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnavism "Vaishnavism") according to existing [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language") hymns from the seventh and eighth centuries C.E and is glorified in the _[Divya Prabandha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya\_Prabandha "Divya Prabandha")_. The _Divya Prabandha_ extols this shrine as being among the 13 [Divya Desams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Desam "Divya Desam") in Malai Nadu (corresponding to present-day Keralam with [Kanyakumari district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanyakumari_district "Kanyakumari district")). Nammalwar sang the glories of Padmanabha.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-108_Divya_Desams-14)

[Mukilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukilan's_invasion_of_Venad "Mukilan's invasion of Venad"), a [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "Muslim") marauder, temporarily invaded parts of Venad in 1680 AD [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-SMenon-15) and destroyed Budhapuram Bhaktadasa Perumal Temple which was owned by Neythasseri Potti. Mukilan intended to plunder Padmanabhaswamy Temple and destroy it, but he was dissuaded from doing so by local Muslims loyal to the royals of Venad, soon after which he was killed in the Battle of [Manacaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manacaud "Manacaud").

### Extant temple records

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Padmanabhaswamy_Temple&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Extant temple records")]

A pertinent event in the long history of the temple was the construction of a "granta-pura" (record-room) within the temple compound itself around 1425 AD by the then Venad King Veera Iravi Iravi Varma, to store the "Mathilakam" (within-the-walls) records, as the then existing temple records were known.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Frontline01-16)

A major portion of the records (over 3000 'Cadjan' leaf-records) from the Mathikalam had been donated later to the Archives Department in 1867 at the time of the formation of the latter. Each of these Cadjan leaf-records, which have been compiled over thousands of years, contains 10,000 documentations according to [R. Nagaswamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Nagaswamy "R. Nagaswamy"), noted archaeologist and historian, totalling over 30 crores of records.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-RN-17) Despite their cultural value, only a small portion of these grantas (bundles) of cadjan leaf records which are written mostly in ancient scripts of [Middle Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Tamil "Middle Tamil") and Malayalam, have been studied and deciphered.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Frontline01-16)

The rest of these Mathilakam documents – segregated under 70 "heads" – is still with the Archives Department. According to [Aswathi Thirunal Gowri Lakshmi Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswathi_Thirunal_Gowri_Lakshmi_Bayi "Aswathi Thirunal Gowri Lakshmi Bayi"), a member of the Travancore Royal Family and author of a book on the temple, from a very early period in recorded history the temple had employed two kinds of 'record writers'. One group was to record the proceedings and transactions of the [Ettara Yogam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettara_Yogam "Ettara Yogam"), a council of temple administrators, that included the then king. The other was to write and preserve the records of the day-to-day functioning of the temple, maintain correct accounts of the temple-treasury, and of temple-revenue-collections and of temple-expenditure, as well as to note down all the other records connected with the functioning of the temple.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-Frontline01-16)

### Main shrine

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Padmanabhaswamy_Temple&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Main shrine")]

[![Image 27](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/5/50/Interior_of_the_Padmanabhaswamy_Temple.jpg/250px-Interior_of_the_Padmanabhaswamy_Temple.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Interior_of_the_Padmanabhaswamy_Temple.jpg)

Interior of the Padmanabhaswamy Temple

In the _[Garbhagriha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbhagriha "Garbhagriha")_, Padmanabha reclines on the serpent, _[Anantha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ananta\_(infinite) "Ananta (infinite)")_ or _Adi Sesha_.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-18) The serpent has five hoods facing inwards intended to signify contemplation. The deity's right hand is placed over a [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva "Shiva")[lingam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingam "Lingam"). Sridevi-[Lakshmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmi "Lakshmi"), the Goddess of Prosperity and [Bhudevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhudevi "Bhudevi"), the Goddess of Earth, two consorts of Vishnu, are by his side. [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma "Brahma") is depicted as emerging on a [lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelumbo_nucifera "Nelumbo nucifera") which emanates from the navel of the deity. The deity is made from 12,008 [saligramams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sila_(murti) "Sila (murti)").[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-19) These saligrams are from the banks of the [Gandaki River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandaki_River "Gandaki River") in [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), and to commemorate this, certain rituals used to be performed at the [Pashupatinath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupatinath_Temple "Pashupatinath Temple"). The idol is covered with a special ayurvedic mix called "Katusarkara yogam".[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-20)

The platforms in front of the [vimanam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimanam_(tower) "Vimanam (tower)") and where the deity rests are both carved out of a single massive stone and hence called "Ottakkal-mandapam". On the orders of [Marthanda Varma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marthanda_Varma "Marthanda Varma") (1706–58), the Ottakkal-mandapam was cut out of a rock at Tirumala (a village in Thiruvananthapuram District), about 4 miles (6.4 km) north of the temple. It measured 20 square feet (1.9 m 2; 190 dm 2; 19,000 cm 2) in area by 2.5 feet (30 in; 7.6 dm; 76 cm) thick and was placed in front of the deity in the month of Edavom 906 M.E. (1731 CE). At the same time, Marthanda Varma also brought 12,000 shaligrams, [aniconic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aniconism "Aniconism") representations of [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu "Vishnu"), from the Gandaki River, north of [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi "Varanasi"). These were used in the re-consecration of Padmanabha.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-SMenon-15)

### Other shrines

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Padmanabhaswamy_Temple&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Other shrines")]

Inside the Temple, there are two other important shrines - Thekkedom and Thiruvambadi - for the deities [Ugra Narasimha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimha "Narasimha") and [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna "Krishna") Swami, respectively.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-SMenon-15) The Thiruvambadi shrine enjoys an independent status. Thiruvambadi has its own namaskara mandapam and bali stones and flagmast. The deity of Thiruvambadi is Parthasarathi, the "Divine Charioteer" of [Arjuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjuna "Arjuna"), who is the warrior prince and one of the main protagonists that appear in the epic of [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata"). The two-armed granite idol, with one hand holding the whip and the other resting on the left thigh holding the conch close to it, is in standing posture. The deity is dressed and adorned as [Mohini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohini "Mohini") on [Ekadashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekadasi "Ekadasi") days.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-SMenon-15)

There are also shrines for [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama "Rama") accompanied by his wife [Sita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sita "Sita"), half-brother [Lakshmana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmana "Lakshmana"), [Hanuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman "Hanuman"), [Vishvaksena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishvaksena "Vishvaksena") (the Nirmalyadhari of Vishnu and "Remover of Obstacles"), [Vyasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyasa "Vyasa") and [Ashwatthama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashwatthama "Ashwatthama"), [Ganapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganapati "Ganapati"), [Sasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasta "Sasta") and Kshetrapala (who guards the temple). Idols of Garuda and Hanuman stand with folded hands in the Valiya balikkal area.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabhaswamy_Temple#cite_note-SMenon-15)
