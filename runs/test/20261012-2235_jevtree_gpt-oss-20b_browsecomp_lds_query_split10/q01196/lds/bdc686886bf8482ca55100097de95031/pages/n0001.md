**Raja Ravi Varma** (Malayalam:[[ɾaːd͡ʒaː ɾɐʋi ʋɐrmːɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Malayalam "Help:IPA/Malayalam")) (29 April 1848 – 2 October 1906[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-4)) was an Indian painter. His works are one of the best examples of the fusion of European [academic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_art "Academic art") with a purely Indian sensibility and iconography. He greatly enhanced his reach and influence as a painter and public figure by making affordable [lithographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithography "Lithography") of his paintings available to the public. His lithographs increased the involvement of common people with fine arts and defined artistic tastes. His religious depictions of Hindu deities and works from [Indian epic poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_epic_poetry "Indian epic poetry") and [Puranas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas "Puranas") have received critical acclaim. He was part of the royal family of [Parappanad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parappanad "Parappanad"), [Malappuram district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malappuram_district "Malappuram district").

Raja Ravi Varma was closely related to the royal family of [Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travancore "Travancore") of present-day [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala") state in India. Later in his life, two of his granddaughters were adopted into the royal family.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-5)

## Personal life

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Raja_Ravi_Varma&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Personal life")]

Varma was born on 29 April 1848, as [M. R. Ry.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Raja_Rajya_Shri "Maha Raja Rajya Shri") Ravi Varma ([Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam_language "Malayalam language"): രവി വർമ്മ), [Koil Thampuran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koyi_Thampuran "Koyi Thampuran") of Kilimanoor at [Kilimanoor palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilimanoor_palace "Kilimanoor palace") in the erstwhile [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state "Princely state") of [Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travancore "Travancore") (present-day [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala"))[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-pal-6) into an aristocratic family that for over 200 years produced consorts for the princesses of the matrilineal Travancore royal family. The title [Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja "Raja") was conferred as a personal title by the Viceroy and Governor-General of India [Lord Curzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Curzon "Lord Curzon").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-pal-6)

Varma's sister-in-law, [Bharani Thirunal Lakshmi Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharani_Thirunal_Lakshmi_Bayi "Bharani Thirunal Lakshmi Bayi"), Senior Rani of [Attingal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attingal "Attingal") (or [Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Travancore "Kingdom of Travancore")), who adopted Varma's granddaughters in 1900

Ravi Varma was the son of Ezhumavil Neelakanthan Bhattatiripad and Uma Ambabayi Thampurratti. His mother Uma Ambabayi Thampuratty belonged to the baronial family which ruled the Kilimanoor [feudal estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief "Fief") within the kingdom of [Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travancore "Travancore"). She was a poet and writer, and her work _Parvati Swayamvaram_ was published by Varma after her death. Ravi Varma's father was a scholar of [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") and [Ayurveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda "Ayurveda") and hailed from the [Ernakulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernakulam "Ernakulam") district in Kerala. Ravi Varma had two siblings, a sister named [Mangala Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangala_Bayi "Mangala Bayi") and a brother named [Raja Varma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Raja_Raja_Varma "C. Raja Raja Varma") (born 1860). The last-named was also a painter and worked closely with Ravi Varma all his life.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

In 1866, at age 18, Varma was married to 12-year-old Bhageerthi Bayi (known formally as Pooruruttati Nal Bhageerathi Bayi Thampuratty) of the royal house of [Mavelikkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavelikkara "Mavelikkara"), another major [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief "Fief") of Travancore kingdom. Notably, the house of Mavellikara was a branch of the Royal House of Travancore. Bhageerthi was the youngest of three sisters; her two elder siblings were adopted into the royal family of Travancore in 1857 to perpetuate the lineage. Designated the Senior and Junior Rani of Attingal, their descendants were vested with the succession to the throne of Travancore. Therefore, Ravi Varma's connection to the royal family became very close due to his marriage with Bhageerthi. His children would be royal by birth, since they belonged to their mother's family. The marriage, which was arranged by the parents in the proper Indian manner, was considered successful. The couple had five children, two sons, and three daughters. Their elder son, Kerala Varma (b.1876) was of an excessively spiritual temperament. He never married and eventually renounced the world, leaving home for good in 1912. The younger son, Rama Varma (born 1879), inherited his father's artistic talent and studied at the [JJ School of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JJ_School_of_Arts "JJ School of Arts"), [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"). He was married to Gowri Kunjamma, sister of [Dewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewan "Dewan")[PGN Unnithan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGN_Unnithan "PGN Unnithan"), and became the father of seven children.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

The three daughters of Ravi Varma and Bhageerthi Bayi were Mahaprabha Amma (who features in two of Varma's most famous paintings), Uma Amma (named after Varma's mother) and Cheria Kochamma. In 1900 CE, the Royal House of Travancore once again faced a succession crisis. Bhageerthi's two elder sisters, who had been adopted in order to carry forward the lineage, had failed to produce the desired heirs. They had six children between them, but only two of those survived, and both were boys (who also, incidentally, later died childless). According to the [matrilineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilineal "Matrilineal") Marumakkathayam system, the succession to the throne could only progress through females, and therefore it was necessary to make an adoption. Tradition dictated that two girls belonging to branches of the Royal Family be adopted together. They would be designated the Senior and Junior Rani of Attingal, and the succession to the throne of Travancore would be vested in their progeny, in accordance with the unusual and unique [Marumakkathayam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marumakkathayam "Marumakkathayam") system of succession.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

Two of Varma's granddaughters were marked by destiny to receive this honour, the main reason being that they were the nearest matrilineal ([cognatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognatic "Cognatic")) kin to the incumbent Rani of Attingal. In August 1900, Mahaprabha's eldest daughter Lakshmi Bayi (aged 5 years) and Uma's eldest daughter Parvati Bayi (aged 4 years) were adopted into the Royal family of Travancore. It was [Bharani Thirunal Lakshmi Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharani_Thirunal_Lakshmi_Bayi "Bharani Thirunal Lakshmi Bayi"), their surviving grand-aunt, who formally adopted them. She died within one year of doing this, and the two girls were then installed as the Senior and Junior Ranis of [Attingal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attingal "Attingal") respectively. They were married while yet in their early teens to two gentlemen from suitable aristocratic families. It was the Junior Rani, [Sethu Parvathi Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sethu_Parvathi_Bayi "Sethu Parvathi Bayi"), who gave birth to the much-awaited heir in 1912, exactly a day after her sixteenth birthday. Incidentally, her husband was a grand-nephew of Raja Ravi Varma and belonged to [Kilimanoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilimanoor_Palace "Kilimanoor Palace"). The newborn child was the future Maharaja [Chithira Thirunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chithira_Thirunal_Balarama_Varma "Chithira Thirunal Balarama Varma"), the last ruling [Maharaja of Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_of_Travancore "Maharaja of Travancore"). He was followed by a brother (the future Maharaja [Marthanda Varma III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthradom_Thirunal_Marthanda_Varma "Uthradom Thirunal Marthanda Varma")) and a sister [Lakshmi Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karthika_Thirunal_Lakshmi_Bayi "Karthika Thirunal Lakshmi Bayi"), the mother of Maharaja [Rama Varma VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moolam_Thirunal_Rama_Varma "Moolam Thirunal Rama Varma") who is presently on the throne (since 2013). Meanwhile, the Senior Rani ([Sethu Lakshmi Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sethu_Lakshmi_Bayi "Sethu Lakshmi Bayi"), daughter of Mahaprabha Amma, and Regent from 1924 to 1931) also gave birth to two daughters later in life (in 1923 and 1926).[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

In this way, the entire present (existing) royal family of [Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travancore "Travancore") is descended from Raja Ravi Varma. Well known among his royal descendants are the writers [Aswathi Thirunal Gowri Lakshmi Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswathi_Thirunal_Gowri_Lakshmi_Bayi "Aswathi Thirunal Gowri Lakshmi Bayi") and [Shreekumar Varma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreekumar_Varma "Shreekumar Varma"), the artist [Rukmini Varma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rukmini_Varma "Rukmini Varma") and the classical musician [Aswathi Thirunal Rama Varma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswathi_Thirunal_Rama_Varma "Aswathi Thirunal Rama Varma").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

Around Ravi Varma's 57th birthday he announced his decision to accept [Sanyasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanyasa "Sanyasa"), and retire from all worldly life when he turned 60. In his final years he suffered from grief for the death of Raja Raja Varma, and also from diabetes, which contributed to his death on 2 October 1906.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

## Art career

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Raja_Ravi_Varma&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Art career")]

Varma was patronised by [Ayilyam Thirunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayilyam_Thirunal "Ayilyam Thirunal"), the next [Maharaja of Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_of_Travancore "Maharaja of Travancore") and began formal training thereafter.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-The_Diary_of_C._Rajaraja_Varma-7) He learned the basics of painting in [Madurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurai "Madurai"). Later, he was trained in water painting by Rama Swami Naidu and rather reluctantly in [oil painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_painting "Oil painting") by British portraitist Theodore Jenson.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-8)

[![Image 9](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Raja_Ravi_Verma_Studio_Vadodara.jpg/250px-Raja_Ravi_Verma_Studio_Vadodara.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Raja_Ravi_Verma_Studio_Vadodara.jpg)

The studio used by Varma during his stay at the [Laxmi Vilas Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laxmi_Vilas_Palace "Laxmi Vilas Palace")

The British administrator, [Edgar Thurston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Thurston "Edgar Thurston") was significant in promoting the careers of Varma and his brother.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-9) Varma received widespread acclaim after he won an award for an exhibition of his paintings at Vienna in 1873. Varma's paintings were also sent to the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition "World's Columbian Exposition") held in Chicago in 1893 and he was awarded three gold medals.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-10) He travelled throughout India in search of subjects. He often modelled Hindu [Goddesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddess "Goddess") on [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India "South India") women, whom he considered beautiful. Ravi Varma is particularly noted for his paintings depicting episodes from the story of [Dushyanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dushyanta "Dushyanta") and [Shakuntala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakuntala "Shakuntala"), and [Nala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nala "Nala") and [Damayanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damayanti "Damayanti"), from the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata"). Ravi Varma's representation of [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") characters has become a part of the Indian imagination of the epics. He is often criticized for being too showy and sentimental in his style but his work remains very popular in India. Many of his fabulous paintings are housed at [Laxmi Vilas Palace, Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laxmi_Vilas_Palace,_Vadodara "Laxmi Vilas Palace, Vadodara").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-11)

## Raja Ravi Varma Press

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Raja_Ravi_Varma&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Raja Ravi Varma Press")]

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Raja_Ravi_Varma%2C_Mrs._Ramanadha_Rao.jpg/250px-Raja_Ravi_Varma%2C_Mrs._Ramanadha_Rao.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Raja_Ravi_Varma,_Mrs._Ramanadha_Rao.jpg)

_Mrs. Ramanadha Rao and son_

Apparently on the advice of the then Dewan (Prime Minister) of [Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travancore "Travancore"), [T. Madhava Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Madhava_Rao "T. Madhava Rao"), Ravi Varma started a lithographic printing press in [Ghatkopar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghatkopar "Ghatkopar"), Mumbai in 1894 and later shifted it to Malavli near [Lonavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonavala "Lonavala"), Maharashtra in 1899. The [oleographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleographs "Oleographs") produced by the press were mostly of Hindu gods and goddesses in scenes adapted mainly from the _[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")_, the _[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")_ and the _[Puranas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas "Puranas")_. These oleographs were very popular and continued to be printed in thousands for many years, even after the 1906 death of Ravi Varma.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

The Ravi Varma press was the largest and most innovative press in India at that time. The press was managed by Varma's brother, Raja Varma, but under their management, it was a commercial failure. By 1899 the press was deeply in debt and in 1901, the press was sold to his printing technician from Germany, Fritz Schleicher. Schleicher continued to print Ravi Varma's prints but later employed other artists to create new designs. Schleicher also broadened the product of press to include commercial and advertisement labels. Under the management of Schleicher and his successors, the press continued successfully until a devastating fire destroyed the whole factory in 1972. Many of Ravi Varma's original lithographic prints were also lost in the fire.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-12)

## Legacy

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Raja_Ravi_Varma&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Legacy")]

Raja Ravi Varma is sometimes regarded as the first modern Indian artist due to his ability to reconcile Western aesthetics with Indian iconography. The Indian art historian and critic [Geeta Kapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geeta_Kapur "Geeta Kapur") wrote,

> Ravi Varma is the indisputable father figure of modern Indian art. Naive and ambitious at the same time, he opens up the debate for his later compatriots in the specific matter of defining individual genius through professional acumen, of testing modes of cultural adaptation with idiosyncratic effect, of attempting pictorial narration with its historic scope.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-15)

Similarly, Baroda School artist [Gulam Mohammed Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulam_Mohammed_Sheikh "Gulam Mohammed Sheikh") also wrote about Ravi Varma as a modern artist. In his essay "Ravi Varma in Baroda", Sheikh asserted that Varma was a key figure in the establishment of Indian modern art, claiming that "the story of contemporary Indian art was never the same after Ravi Varma had entered it. He left his imprint on almost every aspect of it." Like Kapur, Sheikh praised Ravi Varma's integration of Indian and Western aesthetics and techniques, comparing him favorably to Indian modernist [Nandalal Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandalal_Bose "Nandalal Bose").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-16)

However, Ravi Varma's legacy is controversial. Fellow Baroda School artist and art historian [Ratan Parimoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratan_Parimoo "Ratan Parimoo") saw Ravi Varma in a less favorable light, derogatorily referring to him as [kitsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsch "Kitsch") and claiming Varma's work was less spiritually authentic than folk art and tribal art. He argued that Ravi Varma was responsible for the "vulgarity" of popular art, comparing Varma's work to the lurid colors and sexuality of popular images in [calendar art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_art?action=edit&redlink=1 "Calendar art (page does not exist)") and films.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-17)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-18) Australian art critic [Christopher Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Allen_(critic) "Christopher Allen (critic)") made the same points in his review of the 2026 exhibition _The God of Small Things: Faith and Popular Culture_ at the [Queensland Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Art_Gallery "Queensland Art Gallery").[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-19)

Despite his controversial legacy, Ravi Varma continues to be an important figure for modern and contemporary Indian artists. For example, modern artist [Nalini Malani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalini_Malani "Nalini Malani") recreated Ravi Varma's _[Galaxy of Musicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy\_of\_Musicians "Galaxy of Musicians")_ in her video installation _Unity in Diversity_ to interrogate Ravi Varma's idealistic nationalism.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-20) Similarly, contemporary artist [Pushpamala N.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushpamala_N. "Pushpamala N.") recreated several Ravi Varma paintings with herself as the subject to deconstruct Ravi Varma's idealized depictions of goddesses and Indian women.

Many organizations do programs in his memory and give awards in his name. A two days festival of The Maharaja Ranjitsinh Gaekwad Festival of Arts is annually organized, in his memory at the [Durbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durbar_(court) "Durbar (court)") Hall in [Laxmi Vilas Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laxmi_Vilas_Palace "Laxmi Vilas Palace"), [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara "Vadodara"), [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-Sandesh2022-22) The Raja Ravi Varma Award for Excellence in the Field of Visual Arts is presented during this festival. [Jayant Parikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayant_Parikh "Jayant Parikh") was the first recipient.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-23)[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-24)

## In popular culture

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Raja_Ravi_Varma&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: In popular culture")]

[J. Sasikumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Sasikumar "J. Sasikumar") made _Raja Ravi Varma_, an Indian documentary television film on the artist in 1997. It was produced by the [Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India")'s [Films Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Films_Division_of_India "Films Division of India").[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-29)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-30)

_[Makaramanju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makaramanju "Makaramanju")_ (English: _The Mist of Capricorn_) is a 2011 Indian [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam "Malayalam")-language romantic drama film by [Lenin Rajendran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Rajendran "Lenin Rajendran") starring [Santosh Sivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santosh_Sivan "Santosh Sivan") as Varma, the film focuses on Varma's painting "Urvashi Pururavas".[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-31) The 2014 Indian Hindi-language film, _[Rang Rasiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rang\_Rasiya "Rang Rasiya")_ (English title: _Colours of Passion_) explores Varma's inspiration behind his paintings with [Randeep Hooda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randeep_Hooda "Randeep Hooda") in the role of the painter.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ravi_Varma#cite_note-hooda_paint-32)
