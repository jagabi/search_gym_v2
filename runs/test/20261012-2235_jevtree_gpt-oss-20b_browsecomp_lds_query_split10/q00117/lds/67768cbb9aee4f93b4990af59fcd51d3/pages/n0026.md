# Celebrating Thirty Years of the Internet Archive with the ‘Class of 1996’

 Posted on [May 7, 2026](https://blog.archive.org/2026/05/07/celebrating-thirty-years-of-the-internet-archive-with-the-class-of-1996/ "5:24 pm") by [Chris Freeland](https://blog.archive.org/author/chrisfreeland/ "View all posts by Chris Freeland")

[![Image 1](https://blog.archive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/HERO-827x1024.png)](https://blog.archive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/HERO.png)[![Image 2](https://blog.archive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CLASS-OF-1996_CONTACT-SHEET-OF-LOGOS-1080-827x1024.png)](https://blog.archive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CLASS-OF-1996_CONTACT-SHEET-OF-LOGOS-1080.png)
_Before feeds, before algorithms, there was the **Class of 1996**: websites & organizations founded (or expanded) in 1996, like the Internet Archive._

On the occasion of the **Internet Archive’s 30th anniversary**, we’re opening the internet’s yearbook to celebrate the sites, services & scrappy experiments that helped shape the web as we know it. From class leaders like **Center for Democracy and Technology** to cultural icons like **The Onion** to the **archivists** making sure none of it disappears, this is a reunion worth attending.

Some are still thriving. Some have changed beyond recognition. Some are already gone. All of them remind us: the early web wasn’t just built, it was lived in.

**THE MORE YOU KNOW:**Did you know that some publishers are **blocking the Wayback Machine from archiving their sites**, putting decades of reporting and cultural history at risk of disappearing from the public record? If the web’s past matters — _and the Class of 1996 reminds us that it does_ — **now is the time to speak up**. Add your name to the [petition calling on publishers to stop blocking the Wayback Machine](https://www.savethearchive.com/newsleaders/) and help ensure the internet’s history remains accessible for future generations.
