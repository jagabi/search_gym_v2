_**Slant Magazine**_ is an American online publication that features reviews of movies, music, TV, DVDs, theater, and video games, as well as interviews with actors, directors, and musicians. The site covers various film festivals like the New York Film Festival.

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slant_Magazine&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

_Slant Magazine_ was launched in 2001. On January 21, 2010, it was relaunched and absorbed the entertainment blog _The House Next Door_, founded by [Matt Zoller Seitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Zoller_Seitz "Matt Zoller Seitz"), a former _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ and _[New York Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New\_York\_Press "New York Press")_ writer, and maintained by Keith Uhlich, former _[Time Out New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time\_Out\_New\_York "Time Out New York")_ film critic, who was the blog's editor until 2012.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

## In the media

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slant_Magazine&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: In the media")]

_Slant Magazine_'s reviews, which [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") of _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ has described as "passionate and often prickly",[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine#cite_note-2) have occasionally been the source of debate and discourse online and in the media. Ed Gonzalez's review of Kevin Gage's 2005 film _[Chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos\_(2005\_horror\_film) "Chaos (2005 horror film)")_ sparked some controversy[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] when [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") quoted it in his review of the film for the _[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago\_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")_;[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine#cite_note-3)_The New York Press_ quoted another _Slant Magazine_ writer, Keith Uhlich, in a review of the [Michael Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bay "Michael Bay") film _[The Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Island\_(2005\_film) "The Island (2005 film)")_;[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine#cite_note-4) and Gonzalez, who wrote regularly for _[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Village\_Voice "The Village Voice")_ film section, was praised by former _Voice_ critic Nathan Lee for his attention to politics and pop culture in a lively and interesting way.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine#cite_note-5)

KillerStartups.com, a web community that reviews websites for both entrepreneurs and investors, called _Slant Magazine_ "one of the most influential online sources of news, comment, opinion and controversy in the world of indie, pop and mainstream entertainment."[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine#cite_note-6) On January 21, 2010, _[MovieMaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MovieMaker "MovieMaker")_ named _Slant Magazine_'s blog, The House Next Door, one of the "50 Best Blogs for Moviemakers",[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine#cite_note-7) and on January 26, 2010, The House Next Door was named one of "18 obsessive, cantankerous, and unstoppable Gotham blogs worth going ape over" by the _[Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village\_Voice "Village Voice")_.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine#cite_note-8)

## Rating system

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slant_Magazine&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Rating system")]

_Slant Magazine_ employs two different rating systems for its reviews:[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

*   Films and television programs are rated using the traditional four-star system.
*   Albums, DVDs and video games are rated using a five-star system.
