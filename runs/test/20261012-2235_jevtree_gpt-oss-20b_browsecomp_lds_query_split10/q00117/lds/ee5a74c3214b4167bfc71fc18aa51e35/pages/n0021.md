_**Slate**_ is an American [online magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_magazine "Online magazine") that covers current affairs, [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_United_States "Politics of the United States"), and [culture in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States "Culture of the United States"). It was created in 1996 by [Michael Kinsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kinsley "Michael Kinsley") with [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft")'s backing. In 2004, it was purchased by The Washington Post Company (later renamed the [Graham Holdings Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Holdings "Graham Holdings")), and since 2008 has been managed by [The Slate Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Slate_Group "The Slate Group"), an online publishing entity created by Graham Holdings. _Slate_ is based in New York City, with an additional office in Washington, D.C.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-1)

As of 2025,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit) the magazine is both ad-supported and has a membership model with a metered [paywall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paywall "Paywall"). It is known, and sometimes criticized, for adopting [contrarian](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/contrarian "wikt:contrarian") views, which gave rise to the term "Slate Pitches". It has a generally [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States "Modern liberalism in the United States") editorial stance.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-3)

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

### Origin

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Origin")]

_Slate_ was founded on June 24, 1996[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-6) by [Michael Kinsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kinsley "Michael Kinsley") under the ownership of [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft"). Kinsley wrote that one of the site's goals was proving that "the economies of cyberspace make it easier for our kind of journalism to pay for itself".[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Tracy_2022-7) The outlet was considered an early pioneer of [digital journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_journalism "Digital journalism") and popularized features such as [hyperlinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperlink "Hyperlink") and communications between writers and readers. The website received much user traffic from the [MSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSN "MSN") network through its relationship with Microsoft.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Carr_2004-8) In 1998, _Slate_ introduced a [paywall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paywall "Paywall")-based business model that attracted up to 20,000 subscribers but was later abandoned.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Kaufman_2014_1-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-10) The outlet achieved a profitable quarter in 2003 and broke even in 2004 with $6 million in revenue from six million monthly readers.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Carr_2004-8)

### Graham Holdings ownership

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Graham Holdings ownership")]

In 2004, the magazine was purchased by [The Washington Post Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post_Company "The Washington Post Company").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-NBC_2004-12) An agreement in the purchase stipulated that the MSN website would continue to redirect to _Slate_.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-NBC_2004-12) It introduced _Slate V_ in 2007, an online video magazine website.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Reuters_2008-13) Later, in 2008, The Washington Post Company launched The Slate Group, an online publishing entity, to manage _Slate_ and other online magazines.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-14) The same year, _Slate_ launched _[The Root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Root\_(magazine) "The Root (magazine)")_, an African American news site, and "The Big Money", a business news website.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Reuters_2008-13)

In 2011, _Slate_ switched their [content management system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_management_system "Content management system") from Gutenberg to [Adobe CQ5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Experience_Cloud "Adobe Experience Cloud").[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Phelps_2011-15) In the same year, the magazine laid off several high-profile journalists, including co-founder [Jack Shafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Shafer "Jack Shafer") and [Timothy Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Noah "Timothy Noah") (author of the _Chatterbox_ column). At the time, it had around 40 full-time editorial staff.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-16)

In 2012, then-editor David Plotz spoke at [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest "South by Southwest") on _Slate_'s efforts in running long-form journalism. Plotz discussed their commitment for all editors to spend four to six weeks each year to focus on a project, nicknamed "[Fresca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresca "Fresca")" projects, instead of working on news output.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Gandert_2010-17)_Slate_ also launched the "Slate Book Review", a monthly books section edited by Dan Kois,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-18) and a dedicated ad sales team.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-19)

In 2013, the magazine was redesigned under the guidance of design director [Vivian Selbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Selbo "Vivian Selbo").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-20) After The Washington Post Company sold [The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post") to Jeff Bezos in 2013, _Slate_'s parent company was renamed to [Graham Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Holdings "Graham Holdings"), which continued to own the magazine _Slate_.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Kaufman_2014_1-9)

In 2014, it was estimated that the website had 30 million visitors monthly.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Kaufman_2014_2-21) The same year, _Slate_ introduced a paywall system called "Slate Plus", offering ad-free podcasts and bonus materials.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Kaufman_2014_1-9)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-22) A year later, it had attracted 9,000 subscribers, generating about $500,000 in annual revenue.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-23) By September 2014, _Slate_ became profitable after preceding years had seen layoffs and falling ad revenue.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Levy_2014-24)_Slate_ then moved all content behind a metered paywall for international readers in June 2015, attributing the change to advertisers targeting domestic readers.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-25)

In 2018, _Slate_ staff members joined the [Writers Guild of America, East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America,_East "Writers Guild of America, East"). After union members authorized a [strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_action "Strike action"), _Slate_ agreed to a three-year [collective bargaining agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_agreement "Collective agreement") in January 2019.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Peiser_2019-26)

In 2021, [Mike Pesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pesca "Mike Pesca"), host of the podcast _[The Gist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Gist "The Gist")_, was suspended from _Slate_ after a [Slack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slack_(software) "Slack (software)") discussion on [Donald G. McNeil Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_G._McNeil_Jr. "Donald G. McNeil Jr.") and the usage of a racial slur,[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-27)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-28) ultimately parting ways and taking his podcast independent.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-29)

On September 16, 2025, _Slate_ filed a lawsuit against [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") over alleged antitrust violations.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-30)

## slate.fr

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: slate.fr")]

In February 2009, slate.fr, a French-language offshoot of _Slate_, was launched. Its five founders were: [Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde "Le Monde") editors [Jean-Marie Colombani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Colombani "Jean-Marie Colombani") and [Éric Leser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Leser?action=edit&redlink=1 "Éric Leser (page does not exist)")[[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric%20Leser "fr:Éric Leser")], [Libération](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lib%C3%A9ration "Libération") and [20 minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_minutes_(France) "20 minutes (France)") editor [Johan Hufnagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Hufnagel?action=edit&redlink=1 "Johan Hufnagel (page does not exist)")[[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan%20Hufnagel "fr:Johan Hufnagel")], [Les Echos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Echos_(France) "Les Echos (France)") editor [Éric Le Boucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Le_Boucher?action=edit&redlink=1 "Éric Le Boucher (page does not exist)")[[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric%20Le%20Boucher "fr:Éric Le Boucher")], and political advisor [Jacques Attali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Attali "Jacques Attali"). The founders held fifty percent in the publishing company, while The Washington Post Company held 15 percent.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-31)[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-32) In 2011, slate.fr started a separate site covering African news, _Slate Afrique_, with a Paris-based editorial staff.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-33)

### Reputation for counterintuitive arguments ("Slate pitches")

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Reputation for counterintuitive arguments (\"Slate pitches\")")]

Since 2006,[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-34)_Slate_ has been known for publishing [contrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrarian "Contrarian") pieces arguing against commonly held views about a subject, giving rise to the #slatepitches [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter")[hashtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashtag "Hashtag") in 2009.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Levy_2014-24) The _[Columbia Journalism Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia\_Journalism\_Review "Columbia Journalism Review")_ defined _Slate_[pitches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_Pitch "Sales Pitch") as "an idea that sounds wrong or counterintuitive proposed as though it were the tightest logic ever".[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-35) In 2014, _Slate_'s then editor-in-chief, Julia Turner, acknowledged that a reputation for counterintuitive arguments forms part of _Slate_'s "distinctive" brand, but argued that the hashtag misrepresents the site's journalism.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Levy_2014-24)

In 2019, _Slate_ editors such as David Plotz said that the _Slate_ pitch had become difficult due to the polarizing politics of the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "First presidency of Donald Trump").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-36) In 2022, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ reported that anonymous _Slate_ staffers felt that the publication's reputation for contrarian views had diminished over the years.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Robertson_2022-37) Former Slate writer [Matthew Yglesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Yglesias "Matthew Yglesias") concurred with this reporting, stating that the journalism industry had become homogeneous in recent times.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-38)

### Podcasts

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Podcasts")]

See also: [Panoply Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panoply_Media "Panoply Media")

According to the [Nieman Foundation for Journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieman_Foundation_for_Journalism "Nieman Foundation for Journalism"), _Slate_ has been involved in [podcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast "Podcast") "almost from the very beginning" of the medium. Its first podcast, _Political Gabfest_, was released in 2005 and was inspired by _Slate_'s editorial conference calls. _Slate_ podcasts have gotten longer over the years. The original _Gabfest_ ran 15 minutes; by 2012, most ran about 45 minutes. In 2012, it was reported that _Slate_'s podcasts had the highest advertisement and sell-through rates of their content on account of high user engagement.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Phelps_2012-39)

## Reception

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Reception")]

The Nieman Foundation for Journalism regarded the outlet as the "web's oldest living magazine".[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Phelps_2011-15) In 2004, _The New York Times_ opined that the publication produced "well-regarded journalism".[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Carr_2004-8) In 2010, the _Columbia Journalism Review_ commended _Slate_'s high-quality editorial standards and praised its usage of interactive and multimedia content, calling them "consistently innovative, and often a big hit with readers".[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Gandert_2010-17) In 2019, _The New York Times_ wrote that _Slate_ was known for "counterintuitive analysis and its many podcasts".[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Peiser_2019-26) In 2022, Katie Robertson of the _New York Times_ noted _Slate_ was known for its "smart analysis, interesting debate and top-tier journalistic talent".[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Robertson_2022-37)

### Accolades

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Accolades")]

#### "Monkeyfishing"

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: \"Monkeyfishing\"")]

On June 7, 2001, _Slate_ published an article by Jay Forman of a practice of fisherman from an island in the [Florida Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Keys "Florida Keys") of fishing for [rhesus monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhesus_macaque "Rhesus macaque").[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Kuczynski_2001-54)_[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Wall\_Street\_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-55) and _The New York Times_[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Kuczynski_2001-54) found the claim to be a hoax after investigation. Later, Forman admitted to his editor, [Jack Shafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Shafer "Jack Shafer"), that his claim had been fabricated.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-56)

## Staff

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Slate_(magazine)&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Staff")]

Michael Kinsley was _Slate_'s founder and served as its first [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief "Editor-in-chief") from 1996 to 2002. He was followed by [Jacob Weisberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Weisberg "Jacob Weisberg"), who held the position from 2002 to 2008. Afterward, [David Plotz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Plotz "David Plotz") became editor until July 2014, when he was replaced by [Julia Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Turner_(journalist) "Julia Turner (journalist)").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Kaufman_2014_2-21) After Turner resigned in 2019 to join the [Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times"), [Jared Hohlt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Hohlt "Jared Hohlt") became editor on April 1, 2019.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Peiser_2019-26)

Former [HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost "HuffPost") editor [Hillary Frey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Frey "Hillary Frey") was named as the magazine's new editor-in-chief in May 2022.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-57)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine)#cite_note-Fischer_2022-51)
