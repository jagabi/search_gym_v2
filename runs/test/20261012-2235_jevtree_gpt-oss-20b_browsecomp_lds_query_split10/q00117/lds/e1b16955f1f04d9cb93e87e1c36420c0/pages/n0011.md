One of the tallest tree-houses in the world has burned down in just under 10 minutes.

News outlets report the 97ft tall, 10-storey tree-house caught fire and quickly burned down Tuesday night in Crossville, Tennessee.

The famous tree-house had been vacant since 2012 and had been frequented by vandals, according to local news outlets.

However, the Cumberland County Fire and Rescue officials say the cause of the fire is under investigation.

David Sulcer, who visited the tree-house as a youngster, said: ‘The memories that scout pack 3213 had at this place. The great times and friendships.

‘This place was awesome from 10,000 sq ft tree-house to the swimming hole and even the horses waking us up at 6 in the morning, or hearing the Bob cat at night.’

Sharing pictures and videos of the fire on the Crossville Then and Now Facebook page, Charlie Lawson said: ‘Around 10:30 pm, the call came out that the tree-house was on fire, within minutes there were County Officers and the County Fire Dept. on scene.

‘Upon their arrival, they both said the structure was on the ground. In less than 10 minutes, it was to be no more. The cause of the fire is unknown.

‘According to reports you could see the fire as much as 50 feet above the tree line, they said it could easily be seen from Colinx on Genesis Rd, and also while traveling down I-40.

‘Of course that is no surprise, the tree-house was 100 feet tall, so, it lit the sky up.

‘Over the past 30 years the wood has seasoned very well, it is no surprise that it went up this fast. It set a record as being the Worlds Largest tree-house, now, someone else has the record, I don’t think this record will be broke anytime soon.’

The tree-house was built by Horace Burgess, an architect-turned-minister. He told news outlets that God commanded him to build the sprawling tree-house as a means of ministry.

It took him about 19 years to build it with salvaged woods scraps and recycled metal.

The structure spanned across several trees and was a tourist destination until the fire marshal deemed it a public safety hazard.
