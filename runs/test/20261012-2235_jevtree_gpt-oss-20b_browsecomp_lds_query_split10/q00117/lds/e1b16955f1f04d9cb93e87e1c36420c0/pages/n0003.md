# Horace Burgess’ Treehouse

Tennessee

Horace Burgess' Treehouse, also known as the Minister's Treehouse, was a treehouse and church in Crossville, Tennessee.

## History

Horace Burgess, a local minister and landscape architect in Crossville, constructed a small treehouse for his son, Kevin, in 1986. [9](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit9) The hideaway soon grew into a four-level, six-room home encompassing 2,300 square-feet, towering 50-feet above the ground and supported by two white oak trees and a few pines. The project cost about $3,000, with most of the wood coming from scraps from construction sites where he worked at and from neighbors.

The inspiration to build a more massive treehouse came to Burgess as he laid in bed in 1993. 2 11 Burgess, who had been going through a rough patch in life, stated that God had shown him the plans of the treehouse, which included art on the wall and an elevator.

> “If you build a tree house, I’ll see that you never run out of material.” [1](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit1)

Over the period of twelve years, Burgess erected a 97-foot-tall treehouse. 3 11 It was supported by an 80-foot-tall white oak tree and 15 other trees, 258,000 nails driven by a nail gun, and 500 penny nails driven by hand. [2](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit2) A belfry contained ten bells that were crafted out of ten oxygen-acetylene canisters weighing 5,700 pounds. [11](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit11) The treehouse doubled as a church where Burgess officiated 23 weddings. [2](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit2) When not used for service, the sanctuary doubled as a basketball court. 4 11

Because of its unique construction and its unofficial status as the world’s largest treehouse, Burgess’ wooden contraption became a tourist attraction [5](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit5) attracting up to 1,000 visitors per week. [10](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit10)

> “You’re above all your situations, circumstances, for a moment. Your eyes can see over all the stuff that’s under you.”  
> – Horace Burgess [2](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit2)

On August 7, 2012, a state fire marshal performed an inspection on Burgess’ treehouse and ordered it closed as a safety precaution at the end of the month. [10](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit10) The state noted that it was 60 feet over code, did not have a fire alarm or sprinkler system and was not designed by a licensed architect. 2 6 7 10

In March 2018, the treehouse and its accompanying 139 acres of land were put up for sale with an asking price of $1.5 million. [12](https://abandonedonline.net/location/horace-burgess-treehouse/#cit12)On October 22, 2019, the treehouse and its supporting trees were burned to the ground with no cause determined. 7 8 Because of the all-wood construction, the inferno lasted just 15 minutes.
