# Horace Burgess's Treehouse

Large treehouse in Tennessee, US (1993–2019)  From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

**Horace Burgess's Treehouse** (also known as the **Minister's Treehouse**) was a [treehouse](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Treehouse "Treehouse") and church in [Crossville, Tennessee](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Crossville,_Tennessee "Crossville, Tennessee"), United States. Construction began in 1993, mostly by Burgess, who says that, in a vision, God commanded him to build a treehouse. It became a popular local attraction and was unofficially called the largest tree house in the world.[[1]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-slate2013-1) It was closed by the state on August 30, 2012, for [fire code](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Fire_code "Fire code") violations.[[2]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-hazzard-2) On October 22, 2019, the tree house completely burned to the ground in less than 15 minutes under unknown circumstances.[[3]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyt-3)[[4]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-Jones-4)

## History and description

[![Image 1](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Horace_Burgess_Treehouse_Aug._27%2C_2011.jpg/500px-Horace_Burgess_Treehouse_Aug._27%2C_2011.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#/media/File:Horace_Burgess_Treehouse_Aug._27,_2011.jpg)

The exterior of the treehouse

Horace Burgess, a local minister, was praying in 1993 when he said God told him, "If you build a tree house, I'll see that you never run out of material."[[1]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-slate2013-1) Inspired by the vision, Burgess began building the treehouse and continued working on it for the next 12 years.[[3]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyt-3) The 97-foot-tall (30 m) tree house and church was supported by a still-living 80 ft (24 m)[white oak](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/White_oak "White oak") tree with a 12 ft-diameter (3.7 m) base, and relied on six other oak trees for support.[[5]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-usa2007-5) He built it using 258,000 nails with a nailgun, and about 500 pounds of penny nails driven by hand.[[3]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyt-3) The treehouse was located on Beehive Lane in [Crossville, Tennessee](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Crossville,_Tennessee "Crossville, Tennessee").[[6]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-6)

Over time, Burgess' treehouse became a popular tourist attraction.[[7]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyd2012-7) Burgess officiated 23 weddings in the treehouse church,[[3]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyt-3) and when not in service, the sanctuary doubled as a basketball court.[[8]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-independent2009-8) Although there is no [Guinness World Records](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records") category for largest treehouse, it was often referred to as the world's largest.[[9]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-hp2012-9)[Pete Nelson](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Pete_Nelson "Pete Nelson"), the host of _[Treehouse Masters](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Treehouse\_Masters "Treehouse Masters")_ on [Animal Planet](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Animal_Planet "Animal Planet"), who has written six books about treehouses and owns a business building them, said it was "by far" the largest in the world.[[3]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyt-3)

In August 2012, the structure was closed to public access by the state because it had become a public attraction but did not follow building and fire safety codes.[[10]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-10)[[2]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-hazzard-2) It was a "deathtrap", said one official, "it was very cool, but also very dangerous."[[4]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-Jones-4) The structure stood vacant and dilapidated for years, becoming a frequent target of vandalism.[[11]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-fire-11) On October 22, 2019, the treehouse and its supporting trees completely burned to the ground in an inferno lasting about 15 minutes.[[12]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-12) The fire department responded; however, by the time they arrived nothing was left to save.[[11]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-fire-11) "It was basically a pile of rubble when we pulled up. The fire was so intense we had to park 500 yards away", said an official.[[4]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-Jones-4) Fire officials said there was no electricity on site and no electric storms. The recent new owner of the treehouse, who had no insurance, declined to open an investigation.[[3]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyt-3) Burgess said most of the marriages he officiated there had not lasted, and that he was relieved after the fire; the treehouse had "always been a pain" he said.[[3]](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Horace_Burgess's_Treehouse#cite_note-nyt-3)
