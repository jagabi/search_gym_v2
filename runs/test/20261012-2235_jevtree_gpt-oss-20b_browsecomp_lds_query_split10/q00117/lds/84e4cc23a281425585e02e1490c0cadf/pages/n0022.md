# Two local wedding venues abruptly close, forcing couples to change plans

![Image 23: WTAE branding logo](https://kubrick.htvapps.com/htv-prod-media.s3.amazonaws.com/htv_default_image/site_branding/wtae.png)

* * *

Updated: 6:39 PM EST Feb 8, 2020

* * *

[Editorial Standards ⓘ](https://www.wtae.com/article/hearst-television-news-policy-statements/14471973)

Two popular event venues in the Pittsburgh area have abruptly shut down, forcing many couples to change their wedding plans.

Noah's Event Venue has more than 30 locations across the country. Two are in the Pittsburgh area, one in Cranberry Township and one in Canonsburg.

Advertisement

**_Watch the latest report from Pittsburgh's Action News 4 in the video player above._**

The closure has canceled about 2,800 events at 28 venues across the county at a cost of more than $7 million, according to the company's bankruptcy attorney.

Pittsburgh's Action News 4 spoke with Noah's bankruptcy attorney, Ken Cannon. He said the company filed for bankruptcy last year. On Friday, a bankruptcy judge ruled that the company should not be marketing for future events or accepting payments. The judge did not believe Noah's had a viable reorganization plan.

[![Image 24: Pittsburgh-area venues offering to help after sudden closure of two wedding venues ](https://kubrick.htvapps.com/htv-prod-media.s3.amazonaws.com/images/vlcsnap-2020-02-07-21h00m16s554-1581183661.png?crop=1.00xw%3A1.00xh%3B0%2C0&resize=136%3A*) LIST: Western Pennsylvania venues offering to help after sudden closure of two wedding venues](https://www.wtae.com/article/list-local-venues-offering-to-help-after-sudden-closure-of-two-wedding-venues/30823769)

With no income, Noah's decided to shut down. Cannon said Noah's case will move to Chapter 7 bankruptcy soon.

Cannon said the brides and grooms whose weddings were canceled will have to file bankruptcy court claims and, "Sadly, there's not very much in the way of assets or money." The company has not yet released a statement on the closures.

Pittsburgh's Action News 4 spoke with local couples who are now forced to change their wedding plans. They are furious and demanding answers from the company.

"I'm thinking that I got scammed. I'm thinking, 'What am I supposed to do in three months when all of my family and friends have booked flights to come here and celebrate what's supposed to be one of the happiest days of my life, where now I have to start from scratch when I've been planning this since last April?'" Dana Gregg, a local bride, said.

One bride said she found out through her caterer that her venue shut down.

"I blacked out. That's the only way to explain it. That's something that you have bad dreams about, or it's something you see in a movie or on TV and you think, 'Man, that sucks,' but you'd never think that it would happen to you," said Kelsey Wiltman.

Wiltman also said the company charger her more than what was stated in her contract. She has not been able to make contact with the company.

The owner of the building in Cranberry where Noah's was located reached out to Pittsburgh's Action News 4. In an e-mail they said:

_"We have not been paid rent for 8 months last year or any rent this year. We patiently waited for Noah's to try to reorganize but it was not possible. We are asking the public to respect our property and not take out their frustrations on our building, it does not belong to Noah's, they were just the tenant."_
