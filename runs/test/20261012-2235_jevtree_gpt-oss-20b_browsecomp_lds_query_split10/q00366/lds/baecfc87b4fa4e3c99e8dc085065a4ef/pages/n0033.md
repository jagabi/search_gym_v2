# Netflix announces second seasons for Untold (including Manti Te’o) and Last Chance U: Basketball, plus new Bad Sport series

> _Bad Sport_will cover the 2002 Salt Lake City Olympic figure skating scandal, Indycar driver Randy Lanier's marijuana-smuggling operation, Arizona State point-shaving, a "horse hitman," match-fixing in Italian soccer, and the downfall of South African cricket captain Hansie Cronje.

Sports documentaries and documentary series have been a growing focus for Netflix recently, including [_Untold_](https://awfulannouncing.com/films/way-brothers-netflix-untold-sports-documentaries.html),[_Blood Brothers_](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/muhammad-ali-malcolm-x-documentary-blood-brothers-netflix.html),[_Formula 1: Drive To Survive_](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/season-3-of-netflixs-superb-formula-1-drive-to-survive-is-out-tomorrow.html),[_Last Chance U: Basketball_](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/netflix-drops-full-trailer-for-last-chance-u-basketball-and-the-change-of-sport-looks-promising.html), and more. There are plenty of previously-announced sports documentary projects still on the way at the service, including[_Schumacher_](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/netflix-drops-trailer-for-schumacher-documentary.html), [a Bubba Wallace series](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/netflix-announces-bubba-wallace-docuseries.html), and[_Heart of Invictus_](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/archewell-productions-announces-first-netflix-series-in-partnership-with-the),but they’ve now announced three more; second seasons for _Untold_ and _Last Chance U: Basketball_, and a new _Bad Sport_ documentary series covering “the intersection of sports and crime.” Here’s more on _Bad Sport_ (which will premiere Oct. 6) from [a post](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/adding-to-our-roster-of-sports-doc-series-bad-sport-untold-last-chance-u-basketball) from Netflix VP Brandon Riegg:

> _Logline: Bad Sport looks at six unbelievable stories at the intersection of sports and crime, as told by the athletes, coaches and law enforcement officials at the center of the controversies. Episodes include the 2002 Salt Lake City Olympic figure skating scandal; superstar Indycar driver Randy Lanier’s marijuana-smuggling operation; the Arizona State University basketball point-shaving scheme; the horse hitman at the center of an insurance fraud ring; Calciopoli, the biggest match-fixing scandal in Italian football history; and South African cricket captain Hansie Cronje’s tragic fall from grace._

Those are certainly some promising stories. And it’s impressive that even with a growing interest in sports and crime crossovers (a few of the many other examples include USA Today’s [_The Sneak_](https://awfulannouncing.com/podcasts/the-sneak-true-crime-nascar-mario-rossi.html), ESPN’s[_Playing For The Mob_](https://awfulannouncing.com/2014/30-30-review-playing-mob.html), and _Untold_ Vol. 1’s[_Crime and Penalties_](https://decider.com/2021/08/31/untold-crime-and-penalties-netflix-review/)), there are still intriguing stories out there that haven’t received a lot of recent documentary or docuseries coverage. The sports and crime focus also seems like a great fit for Netflix, as they’ve done well with crime stories both real (including [_This Is A Robbery_](https://www.netflix.com/title/81032570), on the ahhht heist at da Gahdnah) and fake (including [_American Vandal_](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv/tv-reviews/american-vandal-season-2-review-718584/) and[_Lupin_](https://www.rogerebert.com/streaming/lupin-tv-review-2021-netflix)).

Beyond that, Riegg’s post also has some details on what to expect in the second seasons of _Untold_ and _Last Chance U: Basketball_. For _Untold_, Vol. 2 will feature “four new stories told over four weeks.” There’s only a subject listed for one of those stories, but it’s a doozy: the Manti Te’o fake girlfriend hoax. That will be directed by Ryan Duffy and Tony Vainuku, and it will feature interviews with both Te’o and “the catfisher behind the hoax” (Ronaiah Tuiasosopo).

Participation from Te’o and Tuiasosopo in an Netflix documentary was known [back in January](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/manti-teo-and-ronaiah-tuiasosopo-are-participating-in-a-planned-players-tribune-and-netflix-documentary-on-the-lennay-kekua-hoax.html), as Te’o cited that for why he declined to appear in [Don Van Natta’s _Backstory_ project on this at ESPN](https://awfulannouncing.com/espn/espns-backstory-the-tale-of-teo-looks-into-the-manti-teo-catfishing-scandal.html). But it’s new to have this as part of the _Untold_ series, and to have listed directors of [Duffy](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm2096169/) (he was an executive producer on _Untold_‘s first season and on Netflix’s previous[_Home Game_](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt12429046/?ref_=nm_flmg_prd_6) series exploring dangerous sports around the world, but also directed and executive produced Fox’s [_Truth Be Told: Rick Ankiel_,](https://awfulannouncing.com/mlb/fox-announces-five-new-mlb-documentaries.html) and got a lot of attention with his previous work for Vice, including [documenting Dennis Rodman’s 2013 North Korea trip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Duffy_(journalist)#North_Korea_trip)) and [Vainuku](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm4189470/bio) (he won an Emmy for 2015’s[_In Football We Trust_](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1780871/?ref_=nm_flmg_dr_2), on Pacific Islanders in the NFL).

As AA’s Ben Koo [noted in January](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/manti-teo-and-ronaiah-tuiasosopo-are-participating-in-a-planned-players-tribune-and-netflix-documentary-on-the-lennay-kekua-hoax.html), Te’o’s claims to Van Natta that in this project “I would be the star of the beginning, the middle and the finish” are certainly concerning for those of us who want to see a full exploration of the story rather than a lionization of Te’o. But _Untold_ so far has managed to do a good job of telling these stories and [incorporating athlete participation](https://awfulannouncing.com/nba/pistons-fan-and-pacers-fan-discuss-untold-malice-at-the-palace.html) without making it only those athletes’ sides. We’ll see how they do with this one. For an example of what they’ve done so far, here’s a clip from _Breaking Point_(the documentary that series executive producers Chapman and Maclain Way [told AA](https://awfulannouncing.com/films/way-brothers-netflix-untold-sports-documentaries.html) started this whole project), on tennis star Mardy Fish’s battles with mental health :

[Video 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tIKZIPG5iv8)

As for _Last Chance U_, the [shift to basketball](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/season-five-of-last-chance-u-will-premiere-on-july-28th-and-season-six-will-focus-on-basketball.html) with the East Los Angeles College Huskies after five seasons covering [three](https://awfulannouncing.com/ncaa/buddy-stephens-finds-self-awareness-sort-gripping-new-season-last-chance-u.html)[different](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/last-chance-u-season-4-review-sometimes-everything-goes-wrong-and-you-cant-stop-the-bleeding.html)[football programs](https://awfulannouncing.com/ncaa/last-chance-u-season-five-oakland-laney-eagles.html) drew some skepticism at first from some corners, but it [allowed for detailed coverage of more of the team](https://awfulannouncing.com/netflix/netflix-drops-full-trailer-for-last-chance-u-basketball-and-the-change-of-sport-looks-promising.html) (due to the smaller rosters) and their interactions with coach John Mosley. And _Last Chance U: Basketball_ was relatively well-received overall, and for season two, it will be sticking with the same team, which should allow for some building of stories like we saw from season one to season two of the football show. Here’s a clip of what this series did in its first season covering basketball:

[Video 5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EwvtO2Uq7c)

Overall, these are some notable announcements from Netflix, and it’s certainly interesting to see them continue their push into sports documentaries on such a wide level. And it’s also interesting that they’re willing to continue to put out notably different sports documentary series with different people involved, rather than try to combine everything into one overarching sports documentary brand the way ESPN has done with _30 for 30_. We’ll see how these new and returning series work out for them.
