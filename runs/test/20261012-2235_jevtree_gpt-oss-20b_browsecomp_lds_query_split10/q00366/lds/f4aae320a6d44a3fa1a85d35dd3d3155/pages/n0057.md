**Wikipedia**[[e]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-5) is a free online [encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia "Encyclopedia") written and maintained by a community of volunteer editors, known as [Wikipedians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedians "Wikipedians"), through [open collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_collaboration "Open collaboration") and the [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiki "Wiki") software [MediaWiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaWiki "MediaWiki"). Founded by [Jimmy Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wales "Jimmy Wales") and [Larry Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Sanger "Larry Sanger") in 2001, Wikipedia has been hosted since 2003 by the [Wikimedia Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation "Wikimedia Foundation"), an American [nonprofit organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/501(c)(3)_organization "501(c)(3) organization") funded mainly by donations from readers.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-WF10.23.23-6) Wikipedia is the largest and most read [reference work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_work "Reference work") in history.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Wiki20-7)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-8)

Initially available only in English, Wikipedia has grown to [over 300 languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wikipedias "List of Wikipedias") and is one of the world's [most visited websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites "List of most-visited websites")as of 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Wikipedia&action=edit). The [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Wikipedia "English Wikipedia"), with over 7 million [articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_(publishing) "Article (publishing)"), remains the largest of the editions, which together comprise more than 68 million articles and attract more than 1.5 billion unique device visits and 13 million edits per month (about five edits per second on average) as of April 2024.[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Wikipedia&action=edit)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Wikimedia_Stats-9)As of December 2025,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Wikipedia&action=edit) over 25% of Wikipedia's [traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_traffic "Web traffic") comes from the United States, while Japan accounts for nearly 7%, and the United Kingdom, Germany, and Russia each represent around 5%.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-10)

Wikipedia has been praised for its internal structure and culture, enabling the [democratization of knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization_of_knowledge "Democratization of knowledge"), and for its extensive coverage on various topics. The encyclopedia [has also been criticized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_Wikipedia "Criticism of Wikipedia") for [systemic bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_bias "Systemic bias"), such as [a gender bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_bias_on_Wikipedia "Gender bias on Wikipedia") against women and [a geographical bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_bias_on_Wikipedia "Geographical bias on Wikipedia") against the [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South "Global South").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-11)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-12) The [reliability of Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliability_of_Wikipedia "Reliability of Wikipedia") was frequently criticized in the 2000s, but has received greater praise from the late 2010s onward.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Wiki20-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Econ21-13)[Censorship of Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_of_Wikipedia "Censorship of Wikipedia") by national governments has ranged from censoring specific pages to the entire site, sometimes due to its criticism of the government, or for content considered blasphemous.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-14)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Last_best-15) Wikipedia articles on [breaking news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_news "Breaking news") are accessed as sources for up-to-date information by many.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-16)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-17)

### Nupedia

Main article: [Nupedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nupedia "Nupedia")

Various collaborative online encyclopedias were attempted before Wikipedia's launch, but with limited success.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-18) Wikipedia began as a complementary project for Nupedia, a free online English-language encyclopedia project whose articles were written by experts and reviewed under a formal process.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-KockJungSyn2016-19) It was founded on March 9, 2000, under the ownership of [Bomis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomis "Bomis"), a [web portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_portal "Web portal") company. Its main figures were Bomis CEO [Jimmy Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wales "Jimmy Wales") and [Larry Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Sanger "Larry Sanger"), editor-in-chief for Nupedia and later Wikipedia.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Sidener-2004-20)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Meyers-21) Nupedia was initially licensed under its own Nupedia [Open Content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_content "Free content") License. Before Wikipedia was founded, Nupedia switched to the [GNU Free Documentation License](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Free_Documentation_License "GNU Free Documentation License") at the urging of [Richard Stallman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Stallman "Richard Stallman").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-stallman1999-22) Wales is often credited with defining the goal of making a publicly editable encyclopedia,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-SangerMemoir-23) while Sanger is credited with the strategy of using a [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiki "Wiki") to reach that goal.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-24) On January 10, 2001, Sanger proposed on the Nupedia mailing list to create a wiki as a "feeder" project for Nupedia.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-nupedia_feeder_from_WP_1-25)

### Launch and growth

Wikipedia was launched on January 15, 2001 (sometimes referred to as "Wikipedia Day"),[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter20227-26) as a single English language edition with the domain name _www.wikipedia.com_,[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-WikipediaHome-27) and was announced by Sanger on the Nupedia mailing list.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-SangerMemoir-23) The name, proposed by Sanger to forestall any potential damage to the Nupedia name,[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-28) originated from a [blend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blend_word "Blend word") of the words _wiki_ and _encyclopedia_.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-MiliardWho-29)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-J_Sidener-30) Wales stated that the idea for Wikipedia was influenced by the [spontaneous order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_order "Spontaneous order") ideas associated with [Friedrich Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hayek "Friedrich Hayek") and the [Austrian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_School "Austrian School") of economics after being exposed to these ideas by the [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian "Libertarian") economist [Mark Thornton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Thornton "Mark Thornton").[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-31)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Mangu-Ward-2007-32) In particular, Wales was influenced by Hayek's essay [The Use of Knowledge in Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Use_of_Knowledge_in_Society "The Use of Knowledge in Society") stating it was "central" to his thinking about "how to manage the Wikipedia project".[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Mangu-Ward-2007-32) Its integral policy of "[neutral point of view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view "Wikipedia:Neutral point of view")" arose within its first year.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202229-33) Otherwise, there were initially relatively few rules, and it operated independently of Nupedia.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-SangerMemoir-23)[Bomis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomis "Bomis") originally intended for it to be a for-profit business.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Seth-Finkelstein-34)

The Wikipedia home page on December 20, 2001[[f]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-35)

Wikipedia gained early contributors from Nupedia, _[Slashdot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slashdot "Slashdot")_ postings, and web [search engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine "Search engine") indexing. Language editions were created beginning in March 2001, with a total of 161 in use by the end of 2004.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-36)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-WP_early_language_stats_1-37) Nupedia and Wikipedia coexisted until the former's servers were taken down permanently in 2003, and its text was incorporated into Wikipedia. The [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Wikipedia "English Wikipedia") passed the mark of 2 million articles on September 9, 2007, making it the largest encyclopedia ever assembled, surpassing the _[Yongle Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle\_Encyclopedia "Yongle Encyclopedia")_ made in China during the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") in 1408, which had held the record for almost 600 years.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-EB_encyclopedia-38)

Due to fears of commercial advertising and lack of control, users of the [Spanish Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Wikipedia "Spanish Wikipedia") forked from Wikipedia to create [Enciclopedia Libre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enciclopedia_Libre "Enciclopedia Libre") in February 2002.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-EL_fears_and_start_1-39) Wales then announced that Wikipedia would not display advertisements and changed Wikipedia's domain from _wikipedia.com_ to _wikipedia.org_.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Shirky-40)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-41)

After an early period of exponential growth,[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-wikisym_slowing_growth_1-42) the growth rate of the English Wikipedia in terms of the numbers of new articles and of editors appears to have peaked around early 2007.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-guardian_WP_user_peak_1-43) The edition reached 3 million articles in August 2009. Around 1,800 articles were added daily to the encyclopedia in 2006; by 2013 that average was roughly 800.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-44) A team at the [Palo Alto Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto_Research_Center "Palo Alto Research Center") attributed this slowing of growth to "increased coordination and overhead costs, exclusion of newcomers, and resistance to new edits".[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-wikisym_slowing_growth_1-42) Others suggested that the growth flattened naturally because articles that could be called "[low-hanging fruit](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/low-hanging%20fruit "wikt:low-hanging fruit")"—topics that clearly merit an article—had already been created and built up extensively.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-bostonreview_the_end_of_WP_1-45)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-46)[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-stanford_WP_lack_of_future_growth_1-47)

In November 2009, a researcher at the [Rey Juan Carlos University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey_Juan_Carlos_University "Rey Juan Carlos University") in Madrid, Spain, found that the English Wikipedia had lost 49,000 editors during the first three months of 2009; in comparison, it lost only 4,900 editors during the same period in 2008.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-guardian_editors_leaving_1-48)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-49)_The Wall Street Journal_ cited the array of rules applied to editing and disputes related to such content among the reasons for this trend.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-50) Wales disputed these claims in 2009, denying the decline and questioning the study's methodology.[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-telegraph_Wales_WP_not_losing_editors_1-51) Two years later, in 2011, he acknowledged a slight decline, noting a decrease from "a little more than 36,000 writers" in June 2010 to 35,800 in June 2011. In the same interview, he also claimed the number of editors was "stable and sustainable".[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-wiki-women-52) A 2013 _[MIT Technology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT\_Technology\_Review "MIT Technology Review")_ article, "The Decline of Wikipedia", questioned this claim, reporting that since 2007 Wikipedia had lost a third of its volunteer editors, and suggesting that those remaining had focused increasingly on minutiae.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Simonite-2013-53) In July 2012, _[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Atlantic "The Atlantic")_ reported that the number of administrators was also in decline.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Meyer-2012-54) In November 2013, _[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New\_York\_(magazine) "New York (magazine)")_ magazine stated, "Wikipedia, the sixth-most-used website, is facing an internal crisis."[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-55) The number of active English Wikipedia editors has since remained steady after a long period of decline.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-56)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-57)

On January 20, 2014, Subodh Varma reporting for _[The Economic Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Economic\_Times "The Economic Times")_ indicated that not only had Wikipedia's growth stalled, it "had lost nearly ten percent of its page views last year. There was a decline of about 2 billion between December 2012 and December 2013. Its most popular versions are leading the slide: page-views of the English Wikipedia declined by twelve percent, those of the German version slid by 17 percent and the Japanese version lost 9 percent."[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-economictimes.indiatimes.com-58) Varma added, "While Wikipedia's managers think that this could be due to errors in counting, other experts feel that Google's [Knowledge Graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_Graph_(Google) "Knowledge Graph (Google)") project launched last year may be gobbling up Wikipedia users."[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-economictimes.indiatimes.com-58) When contacted on this matter, [Clay Shirky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Shirky "Clay Shirky"), associate professor at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") and fellow at Harvard's [Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkman_Klein_Center_for_Internet_&_Society "Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society") said that he suspected much of the page-view decline was due to Knowledge Graphs, stating, "If you can get your question answered from the search page, you don't need to click [any further]."[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-economictimes.indiatimes.com-58) By the end of December 2016, Wikipedia was ranked the fifth most popular website globally.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Alexa-59) As of January 2023, 55,791 English Wikipedia articles have been cited 92,300 times in scholarly journals,[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-60) from which [cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing "Cloud computing") was the most cited page.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-61)

### Sister projects

Main article: [Wikimedia project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_project "Wikimedia project")

Wikipedia has spawned several sister projects, which are also wikis run by the [Wikimedia Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation "Wikimedia Foundation"). These other [Wikimedia projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_project "Wikimedia project") include [Wiktionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiktionary "Wiktionary"), a dictionary project launched in December 2002,[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-62)[Wikiquote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikiquote "Wikiquote"), a collection of quotations created a week after Wikimedia launched,[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-63)[Wikibooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikibooks "Wikibooks"), a collection of collaboratively written free textbooks and annotated texts,[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-64)[Wikimedia Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Commons "Wikimedia Commons"), a site devoted to free-knowledge multimedia,[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-65)[Wikinews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikinews "Wikinews"), for collaborative journalism[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-66) (which was later shut down in 2026[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-67)), and [Wikiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikiversity "Wikiversity"), a project for the creation of free learning materials and the provision of online learning activities.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-68) Another sister project of Wikipedia, [Wikispecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikispecies "Wikispecies"), is a catalog of all species, but is not open for public editing.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-69) In 2012, [Wikivoyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikivoyage "Wikivoyage"), an editable travel guide,[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-70) and [Wikidata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikidata "Wikidata"), an editable knowledge base, launched.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-71)

In January 2007, Wikipedia first became one of the ten [most popular websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites "List of most-visited websites") in the United States, according to [Comscore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comscore "Comscore") Networks.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Comscore-2007-73) With 42.9 million unique visitors, it was ranked ninth, surpassing _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ (No. 10) and [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") (No. 11).[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Comscore-2007-73) This marked a significant increase over January 2006, when Wikipedia ranked 33rd, with around 18.3 million unique visitors.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-74) In 2014, it received 8 billion page views every month.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-75) On February 9, 2014, _The New York Times_ reported that Wikipedia had 18 billion [page views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pageview "Pageview") and nearly 500 million [unique visitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unique_user#Unique_visitor "Unique user") a month, "according to the ratings firm comScore".[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-small_screen-76)As of March 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Wikipedia&action=edit), it ranked sixth in popularity, according to [Similarweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similarweb "Similarweb").[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-77) Jeff Loveland and [Joseph Reagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Reagle "Joseph Reagle") argue that, in process, Wikipedia follows a long tradition of historical encyclopedias that have accumulated improvements piecemeal through "[stigmergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stigmergy "Stigmergy") accumulation".[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-sagepub_WP_and_encyclopedic_production_1-78)[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-theatlantic_WP_actually_a_reversion_1-79)

On January 18, 2012, the English Wikipedia participated in a series of coordinated protests against two proposed laws in the [United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress")—the [Stop Online Piracy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_Online_Piracy_Act "Stop Online Piracy Act") (SOPA) and the [PROTECT IP Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROTECT_IP_Act "PROTECT IP Act") (PIPA)—by [blacking out its pages for 24 hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_against_SOPA_and_PIPA "Protests against SOPA and PIPA").[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-LA_Times_Jan_19-80) More than 162 million people viewed the blackout explanation page that temporarily replaced its content.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-BBC_WP_blackout_protest_1-81)[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-82)

In January 2013, [274301 Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/274301_Wikipedia "274301 Wikipedia"), an [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid"), was named after Wikipedia;[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-83) in October 2014, Wikipedia was honored with the _[Wikipedia Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia\_Monument "Wikipedia Monument")_;[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-84) and, in July 2015, 106 of the 7,473 700-page volumes of Wikipedia became available as [Print Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Print_Wikipedia "Print Wikipedia").[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-85) In April 2019, an Israeli [lunar lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_lander "Lunar lander"), [Beresheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beresheet "Beresheet"), crash landed on the surface of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") carrying a copy of nearly all of the English Wikipedia engraved on thin nickel plates; experts say the plates likely survived the crash.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-WRD-20190805-86)[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-VOX-20190806-87) In June 2019, scientists reported that all 16 GB of article text from the English Wikipedia had been encoded into [synthetic DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_genomics "Synthetic genomics").[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-CNET-20190629-88)

On January 18, 2023, Wikipedia debuted a new website redesign, called "[Vector 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_2022 "Vector 2022")".[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Pearl-2023-89)[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Tech_Desk-2023-90) It featured a redesigned [menu bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menu_bar "Menu bar"), moving the [table of contents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_contents "Table of contents") to the left as a [sidebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidebar_(computing) "Sidebar (computing)"), and numerous changes in the locations of buttons like the language selection tool.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Tech_Desk-2023-90)[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-91) The update initially received backlash, most notably when editors of the [Swahili Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_Wikipedia "Swahili Wikipedia") unanimously voted to revert the changes.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Pearl-2023-89)[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-92)

Both Sanger and Wales gave public interviews in late 2025 about their reflections about the status and state of Wikipedia, leading up to its 25 years of operation on January 15, 2026; Wales appeared on the PBS television news show GZERO World interviewed by [Ian Bremmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Bremmer "Ian Bremmer")[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-93) and Sanger has appeared on the Fox News Channel, interviewed by [Ashley Rindsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Rindsberg "Ashley Rindsberg").[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-94) Wales' book _[The Seven Rules of Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Seven\_Rules\_of\_Trust "The Seven Rules of Trust")_ was published in October 2025 by [Penguin Random House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Random_House "Penguin Random House"). It was described by the publisher as a "sweeping reflection on the global crisis of credibility and knowledge" with the book examining the "rules of trust" that enabled the growth and success of Wikipedia.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Penguin-95)

### Impacts of generative AI on Wikipedia views

Further information: [Artificial intelligence in Wikimedia projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_in_Wikimedia_projects "Artificial intelligence in Wikimedia projects")

Since January 2024, the Wikimedia Foundation has reported a roughly 50 percent increase in bandwidth use from downloads of multimedia content across its projects. According to the foundation, this growth is largely attributed to automated programs, or "[scraper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_scraping "Web scraping")" bots, that collect large volumes of data from Wikimedia sites for use in training large language models and related applications.[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-96)

In October 2025, the Wikimedia Foundation reported an estimated 8 percent decline in [traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_traffic "Internet traffic") as compared to the same month in 2024 in human page views. They speculated that it reflects the growing use of [generative AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI") and social media for finding information.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-97)[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-98)

### Restrictions

Due to Wikipedia's increasing popularity, some editions, including the English version, have introduced editing restrictions for certain cases. For instance, on the English Wikipedia and some other language editions, only users with 10 edits that have an account that is four days old may create a new article.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-99) On the English Wikipedia, among others, particularly controversial, sensitive, or vandalism-prone pages have been protected to varying degrees.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-100) A frequently vandalized article can be "semi-protected" or "extended confirmed protected", meaning that only "autoconfirmed" or "extended confirmed" editors can modify it.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202212%E2%80%9313-101) A particularly contentious article may be locked so that only [administrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_administrators "Wikipedia administrators") can make changes.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-102) A 2021 article in the _[Columbia Journalism Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia\_Journalism\_Review "Columbia Journalism Review")_ identified Wikipedia's page-protection policies as "perhaps the most important" means at its disposal to "regulate its market of ideas".[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-103) Wikipedia has delegated some functions to [bots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_agent "Intelligent agent"). Such [algorithmic governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_by_algorithm "Government by algorithm") has an ease of implementation and scaling, though the automated rejection of edits may have contributed to a downturn in active Wikipedia editors.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Ren-2024-104) Bots must be approved by the community before their tasks are implemented.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202236-105)

In certain cases, all editors are allowed to submit modifications, but review is required for some editors, depending on certain conditions. For example, the [German Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Wikipedia "German Wikipedia") maintains "stable versions" of articles which have passed certain reviews.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-WP_some_sites_stable_versions_1-106) Following protracted trials and community discussion, the English Wikipedia introduced the "pending changes" system in December 2012.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-BInsider_pending_changes_intro_1-107) Under this system, new and unregistered users' edits to certain controversial or vandalism-prone articles are reviewed by established users before they are published.[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-108) However, restrictions on editing may reduce editor engagement as well as efforts to diversify the editing community.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-109)

Articles related to the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict "Israeli–Palestinian conflict") are placed under extended-confirmed protection.[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Harrison-2023-110) Editors also can make only one revert per day across the entire field and can be banned from editing related articles. These restrictions were introduced in 2008.[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Benjakob-2020a-111) In January 2025, the [Arbitration Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikipedia) "Arbitration Committee (Wikipedia)") introduced the "balanced editing restriction", which requires sanctioned users to devote only a third of their edits to articles related to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict even when no misconduct rules have been violated.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Harrison-2025-112)[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Elia-Shalev-2025-113)

### Review of changes

[![Image 11](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Wikipedia_editing_interface.png/250px-Wikipedia_editing_interface.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wikipedia_editing_interface.png)

Wikipedia's editing interface

Although changes are not systematically reviewed, Wikipedia's software provides tools allowing anyone to review changes made by others. Each article's History page links to each revision.[[h]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-114)[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Torsten_Kleinz-115) On most articles, anyone can view the latest changes and undo others' revisions by clicking a link on the article's History page. Registered users may maintain a "watchlist" of articles that interest them so they can be notified of changes.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-116) There are a variety of different 'patrols' on Wikipedia,[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-117) including "New pages patrol" a patrol where newly created articles are checked for glaring issues.[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-118)

In 2003, economics PhD student Andrea Ciffolilli argued that the low [transaction costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_cost "Transaction cost") of participating in a [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiki "Wiki") created a catalyst for collaborative development, and that features such as allowing easy access to past versions of a page favored "creative construction" over "creative destruction".[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FMonday_collaborative_effort_1-119)

### Vandalism

Main article: [Vandalism on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandalism_on_Wikipedia "Vandalism on Wikipedia")

Any change that deliberately compromises Wikipedia's integrity is considered vandalism. The most common and obvious types of vandalism include additions of obscenities and crude humor, but it can also include advertising and other types of spam.[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-upenn_link_spamming_1-120) Sometimes editors commit vandalism by removing content or entirely blanking a given page. Less common types of vandalism, such as the deliberate addition of plausible but [false information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_information "False information"), can be more difficult to detect. Vandals can introduce irrelevant formatting, modify page semantics such as the page's title or categorization, manipulate the article's underlying code, or use images disruptively.[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-WP_vandalism_manipulation_1-121)

[![Image 12: White-haired elderly gentleman in suit and tie speaks at a podium.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/John_Seigenthaler_Sr._speaking.jpg/250px-John_Seigenthaler_Sr._speaking.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_Seigenthaler_Sr._speaking.jpg)

American journalist [John Seigenthaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Seigenthaler "John Seigenthaler") (1927–2014), subject of the [Seigenthaler biography incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seigenthaler_biography_incident "Seigenthaler biography incident")

Obvious vandalism is generally easy to remove from Wikipedia articles; the median time to detect and fix it is a few minutes.[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-MIT_IBM_study-122)[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-CreatingDestroyingAndRestoringValue-123) However, some vandalism takes much longer to detect and repair.[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Seigenthaler-124)

In the [Seigenthaler biography incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seigenthaler_biography_incident "Seigenthaler biography incident"), an anonymous editor introduced false information into the biography of American political figure [John Seigenthaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Seigenthaler "John Seigenthaler") in May 2005, falsely presenting him as a suspect in the [assassination of John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy "Assassination of John F. Kennedy").[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Seigenthaler-124) It remained uncorrected for four months.[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Seigenthaler-124) Seigenthaler, the founding editorial director of _[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA\_Today "USA Today")_ and founder of the Freedom Forum [First Amendment Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_Center "First Amendment Center") at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University "Vanderbilt University"), called Wikipedia co-founder Jimmy Wales and asked whether he had any way of knowing who contributed the misinformation. Wales said he did not, although the perpetrator was eventually traced.[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-book_The_World_is_Flat_1-125)[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-126) After the incident, Seigenthaler described Wikipedia as "a flawed and irresponsible research tool".[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Seigenthaler-124) The incident led to policy changes at Wikipedia for tightening up the verifiability of biographical articles of living people.[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-127)

### Disputes and edit warring

Main article: [Disputes on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disputes_on_Wikipedia "Disputes on Wikipedia")

Wikipedia editors often have disagreements regarding content, which can be discussed on article Talk pages. Disputes may result in repeated competing changes to an article, known as "edit warring".[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Wikipedia:Dispute_resolution_%E2%80%93_Wikipedia-128)[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-NBC_WP_editorial_warzone_12-129) It is widely seen as a resource-consuming scenario where no useful knowledge is added,[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-130) and criticized as creating a competitive[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-131) and conflict-based editing culture associated with traditional masculine [gender roles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_role "Gender role").[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-132)[[125]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-133) Research has focused on, for example, impoliteness of disputes,[[126]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-134)[[127]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-135) the influence of rival editing camps,[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-136)[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-137) the conversational structure,[[130]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-138) and the shift in conflicts to a focus on sources.[[131]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-139)[[132]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Ruprechter-140)

[Taha Yasseri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha_Yasseri "Taha Yasseri") of the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") examined editing conflicts and their resolution in a 2013 study.[[133]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-141)[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Yasseri-2014-142) Yasseri contended that simple reverts or "undo" operations were not the most significant measure of [counterproductive work behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterproductive_work_behavior "Counterproductive work behavior") at Wikipedia. He relied instead on "mutually reverting edit pairs", where one editor reverts the edit of another editor who then, in sequence, returns to revert the first editor. The results were tabulated for several language versions of Wikipedia. The English Wikipedia's three largest conflict rates belonged to the articles [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush"), [anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism "Anarchism"), and [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad "Muhammad").[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Yasseri-2014-142) By comparison, for the German Wikipedia, the three largest conflict rates at the time of the study were for the articles covering [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia "Croatia"), [Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientology "Scientology"), and [9/11 conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_conspiracy_theories "9/11 conspiracy theories").[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Yasseri-2014-142) In 2020, researchers identified other measures of editor behaviors, beyond mutual reverts, to identify editing conflicts across Wikipedia.[[132]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Ruprechter-140)

Editors also debate the [deletion of articles on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deletion_of_articles_on_Wikipedia "Deletion of articles on Wikipedia"), with roughly 500,000 such debates since Wikipedia's inception as of 2019. Once an article is nominated for deletion, the dispute is typically determined by initial votes (to keep or delete) and by reference to topic-specific notability policies.[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-143)

Wikipedia is composed of 11 different [namespaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namespace "Namespace"),[_[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual\_of\_Style/Dates\_and\_numbers#Chronological\_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_] with its articles being present in _mainspace_. Other namespaces have a prefix before their page title and fulfill various purposes. For example, the project namespace uses the _Wikipedia_ prefix and is used for self-governance-related discussions. Most readers are not aware of these other namespaces.[[136]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter20223-144)

The fundamental principles of the Wikipedia community are embodied in the "Five Pillars", while the detailed editorial principles are expressed in numerous policies and guidelines intended to appropriately shape content.[[137]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-145) The five pillars are:

*   Wikipedia is an encyclopedia
*   Wikipedia is written from a neutral point of view
*   Wikipedia is free content that anyone can use, edit, and distribute
*   Wikipedia's editors should treat each other with respect and civility
*   Wikipedia has no firm rules

The rules developed by the community are stored in the Wikipedia namespace, and Wikipedia editors write and revise the website's policies and guidelines in accordance with community consensus.[[138]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-pcworld_who's_behind_WP-146) Originally, rules on the non-English editions of Wikipedia were based on a translation of the rules for the English Wikipedia. They have since diverged to some extent.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-WP_some_sites_stable_versions_1-106)

### Content policies and guidelines

"No original research" redirects here. For the Wikipedia policy, see [Wikipedia:No original research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research "Wikipedia:No original research").

According to the rules on the English Wikipedia community, each entry in Wikipedia must be about a topic that is [encyclopedic](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/encyclopedic "wikt:encyclopedic") and is not a dictionary entry or dictionary-style.[[139]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-:5-147) A topic should also meet [Wikipedia's standards of "notability"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notability_in_the_English_Wikipedia "Notability in the English Wikipedia"), which generally means that the topic has been covered extensively in reliable sources that are independent of the article's subject.[[140]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202253%E2%80%9354-148) Wikipedia intends to convey only knowledge that is already established and recognized and therefore must not present original research.[[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202232-149) Some subjects, such as politicians and academics, have specialized notability requirements.[[140]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202253%E2%80%9354-148) Finally, Wikipedia must reflect a neutral point of view. This is accomplished through summarizing reliable sources, using impartial language, and ensuring that multiple points of view are presented based on their prominence. Information must also be verifiable.[[142]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter20225%E2%80%937-150) Information without citations may be [tagged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_needed_(Wikipedia) "Citation needed (Wikipedia)") or removed entirely.[[143]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202233-151) This can at times lead to the removal of information which, though valid, is not properly sourced.[[144]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-IHT_WP_valid_info_wrong_removable_1-152) As Wikipedia policies changed over time, and became more complex, their number has grown. In 2008, there were 44 policy pages and 248 guideline pages; by 2013, scholars counted 383 policy pages and 449 guideline pages.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Ren-2024-104)

## Governance

Further information: [Wikipedia:Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administration "Wikipedia:Administration")

Wikipedia's initial anarchy integrated democratic and [hierarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy "Hierarchy") elements over time.[[145]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-153)[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-154) An article is not considered to be owned by its creator or any other editor, nor by the subject of the article.[[147]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-155) Editors in good standing in the community can request extra user rights, granting them the technical ability to perform certain special actions. Some user rights are granted automatically, such as the _autoconfirmed_ and _extended confirmed_ groups, when thresholds for account age and edits are met.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202212%E2%80%9313-101)

### Administrators

Main article: [Wikipedia administrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_administrators "Wikipedia administrators")

Experienced editors can choose to run for "[adminship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrators_(Wikipedia) "Administrators (Wikipedia)")",[[148]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-David_Mehegan-156) which includes the ability to delete pages or prevent them from being changed in cases of severe vandalism or editorial disputes.[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-:6-157) Administrators are not supposed to enjoy any special privilege in decision-making; instead, their powers are mostly limited to making edits that have project-wide effects and thus are disallowed to ordinary editors, and to implement restrictions intended to prevent disruptive editors from making unproductive edits.[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-:6-157)

By 2012, fewer editors were becoming administrators compared to Wikipedia's earlier years, in part because the process of vetting potential administrators had become more rigorous.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Meyer-2012-54) In 2022, there was a particularly contentious request for adminship over the candidate's anti-Trump views; ultimately, they were granted adminship.[[150]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-158)

### Dispute resolution

"!vote" redirects here. For the entry in Wikipedia's internal glossary, see [Wikipedia:Glossary §!vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Glossary#!vote "Wikipedia:Glossary"). For the internal essay, see [Wikipedia:Polling is not a substitute for discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Polling_is_not_a_substitute_for_discussion "Wikipedia:Polling is not a substitute for discussion").

Over time, Wikipedia has developed a semi-formal dispute resolution process. To determine community consensus, editors can raise issues at appropriate community forums, seek outside input through third opinion requests, or initiate a more general community discussion known as a "request for comment",[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Wikipedia:Dispute_resolution_%E2%80%93_Wikipedia-128) in which bots add the discussion to a centralized list of discussions, invite editors to participate, and remove the discussion from the list after 30 days.[[151]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-159) However, editors have the discretion to close (and delist) the discussion early or late. If the result of a discussion is not obvious, a closer—an uninvolved editor usually in good standing—may render a verdict from the strength of the arguments presented and then the number of arguers on each side.[[152]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Bandler-2024-160) Wikipedians emphasize that the process is not a vote by referring to statements of opinion in such discussions as "!vote"s, in which the exclamation mark is the symbol for [logical negation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negation#Usage_in_colloquial_language "Negation") and pronounced "not".[[153]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-161)

Wikipedia encourages local resolutions of conflicts, which Jemielniak argues is quite unique in organization studies, though there has been some recent interest in [consensus building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making "Consensus decision-making") in the field.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Jemielniak-162) Reagle and [Sue Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sue_Gardner "Sue Gardner") argue that the approaches to consensus building are similar to those used by [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers "Quakers").[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Jemielniak-162):62 A difference from Quaker meetings is the absence of a [facilitator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facilitator "Facilitator") in the presence of disagreement, a role played by the clerk in Quaker meetings.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Jemielniak-162):83

#### Arbitration Committee

Main article: [Arbitration Committee (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikipedia) "Arbitration Committee (Wikipedia)")

The Arbitration Committee presides over the ultimate dispute resolution process. Although disputes usually arise from a disagreement between two opposing views on how an article should read, the Arbitration Committee explicitly refuses to directly rule on the specific view that should be adopted.[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Hoffman-2009-163)

Statistical analyses suggest that the English Wikipedia committee ignores the content of disputes and rather focuses on the way disputes are conducted,[[156]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-emory_p_181-164) functioning not so much to resolve disputes and make peace between conflicting editors, but to weed out problematic editors while allowing potentially productive editors back in to participate.[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Hoffman-2009-163) Therefore, the committee does not dictate the content of articles, although it sometimes condemns content changes when it deems the new content violates Wikipedia policies (for example, if the new content is considered biased).[[i]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-165) Commonly used solutions include cautions and probations (used in 63% of cases) and banning editors from articles (43%), subject matters (23%), or Wikipedia (16%).[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Hoffman-2009-163) Complete bans from Wikipedia are generally limited to instances of [impersonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impersonator "Impersonator") and [anti-social behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-social_behavior "Anti-social behavior").[[157]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-166) When conduct is not impersonation or anti-social, but rather edit warring and other violations of editing policies, solutions tend to be limited to warnings.[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Hoffman-2009-163)

## Community

Main article: [Wikipedia community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_community "Wikipedia community")

[Video 1](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/61/Wikimania_-_the_Wikimentary.webm?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original#t=4)

Video of [Wikimania 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimania#2005 "Wikimania")–an annual conference for users of Wikipedia and other projects operated by the [Wikimedia Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation "Wikimedia Foundation"), was held in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main "Frankfurt am Main"), Germany, August 4–8.

Each article and each user of Wikipedia has an associated and dedicated "talk" page. These form the primary communication channel for editors to discuss, coordinate, and debate.[[158]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-167) Wikipedia's community has been described as [cultlike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult "Cult"),[[159]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-168) although not always with entirely negative connotations.[[160]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-169) Its preference for cohesiveness, even if it requires compromise that includes disregard of [credentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credential "Credential"), has been referred to as "[anti-elitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-elitism "Anti-elitism")".[[161]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-170)

[Video 2](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/27/Editing_Hoxne_Hoard_at_the_British_Museum.ogv?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original)

Wikipedians and [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum "British Museum") curators collaborate on the article [Hoxne Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoxne_Hoard "Hoxne Hoard") in June 2010.

Wikipedia does not require that its editors and contributors provide identification.[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-user_identification-171) As Wikipedia grew, "Who writes Wikipedia?" became one of the questions frequently asked there.[[163]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-172)[Jimmy Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wales "Jimmy Wales") once argued that only "a community... a dedicated group of a few hundred volunteers" makes the bulk of contributions to Wikipedia and that the project is therefore "much like any traditional organization".[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-blodget-173) Since Wikipedia relies on volunteer labour, editors frequently focus on topics that interest them.[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202261%E2%80%9362-174)

The English Wikipedia has [7,251,841](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:Statistics "Special:Statistics") articles, [54,933,403](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:ListUsers "Special:ListUsers") registered editors, and [271,802](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:ActiveUsers "Special:ActiveUsers") active editors. An editor is considered active if they have made one or more edits in the past 30 days.[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-175) Editors who fail to comply with Wikipedia cultural rituals, such as signing talk page comments, may implicitly signal that they are Wikipedia outsiders, increasing the odds that Wikipedia insiders may target or discount their contributions. Becoming a Wikipedia insider involves non-trivial costs: the contributor is expected to learn Wikipedia-specific technological codes, submit to a sometimes convoluted dispute resolution process, and learn a "baffling culture rich with in-jokes and insider references".[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-labor_squeeze_on_WP_1-176) Editors who do not log in are in some sense "[second-class citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-class_citizen "Second-class citizen")" on Wikipedia,[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-labor_squeeze_on_WP_1-176) as "participants are accredited by members of the wiki community, who have a vested interest in preserving the quality of the work product, on the basis of their ongoing participation",[[168]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-legal_edu_and_WP_1-177) but the contribution histories of anonymous unregistered editors recognized only by their [IP addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address "IP address") cannot be attributed to a particular editor with certainty.[[168]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-legal_edu_and_WP_1-177) New editors often struggle to understand Wikipedia's complexity. Experienced editors are encouraged not to "bite" the newcomers in order to create a more welcoming atmosphere.[[169]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDowellVetter202210%E2%80%9311-178)

### Research

A 2007 study by researchers from [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College "Dartmouth College") found that "anonymous and infrequent contributors to Wikipedia... are as reliable a source of knowledge as those contributors who register with the site".[[170]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-sciam_good_samaritans_1-179)[Jimmy Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wales "Jimmy Wales") stated in 2009 that "[I]t turns out over 50% of all the edits are done by just 0.7% of the users... 524 people... And in fact, the most active 2%, which is 1400 people, have done 73.4% of all the edits."[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-blodget-173) However, _[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business\_Insider "Business Insider")_ editor and journalist [Henry Blodget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Blodget "Henry Blodget") showed in 2009 that in a random sample of articles, most Wikipedia content (measured by the amount of contributed text that survives to the latest sampled edit) is created by "outsiders", while most editing and formatting is done by "insiders".[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-blodget-173)

In 2008, a _Slate_ magazine article reported that "one percent of Wikipedia users are responsible for about half of the site's edits."[[171]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-180) This method of evaluating contributions was later disputed by [Aaron Swartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Swartz "Aaron Swartz"), who noted that several articles he sampled had large portions of their content (measured by number of characters) contributed by users with low edit counts.[[172]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-181) A 2008 study found that Wikipedians were less agreeable, open, and conscientious than others,[[173]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-liebertonline_view_on_WP_users_1-182) although a later commentary pointed out serious flaws, including that the data showed higher openness and that the differences with the control group and the samples were small.[[174]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-183) According to a 2009 study, there is "evidence of growing resistance from the Wikipedia community to new content".[[175]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-newscientist_WP_boom_to_bust_1-184)

### Diversity

Several studies have shown that most volunteer Wikipedia contributors are male. The results of a [Wikimedia Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation "Wikimedia Foundation") survey in 2008 showed that only 13 percent of Wikipedia editors were female.[[176]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-185) Because of this, universities throughout the United States tried to encourage women to become Wikipedia contributors.[[177]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-CBC_News-2013-186) Similarly, many of these universities, including [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") and [Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University "Brown University"), gave college credit to students who create or edit an article relating to women in science or technology.[[177]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-CBC_News-2013-186)[Andrew Lih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lih "Andrew Lih"), a professor and scientist, said that the reason he thought the number of male contributors outnumbered the number of females so greatly was because identifying as a woman may expose oneself to "ugly, intimidating behavior".[[178]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-Bloomberg_2016-187) Data has shown that Africans are underrepresented among Wikipedia editors.[[179]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia#cite_note-memeb-188)
