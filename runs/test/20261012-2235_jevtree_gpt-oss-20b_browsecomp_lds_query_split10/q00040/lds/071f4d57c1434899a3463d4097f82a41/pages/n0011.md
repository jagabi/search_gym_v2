The **10,000 metres** or the **10,000-metre run** is a common [long-distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_running "Long-distance running") track running event. The event is part of the [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_(sport) "Athletics (sport)") programme at the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games") and the [World Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Championships "World Athletics Championships"), and is common at championship-level events. The race consists of 25 laps around an Olympic-sized 400 m track. It is less commonly held at track and field meetings due to its duration. The 10,000-metre track race is usually distinguished from its [road running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_running "Road running") counterpart, the [10K run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10K_run "10K run"), by referring to the distance in metres rather than kilometres.

The 10,000 metres is the longest standard track event, approximately equivalent to 6 miles 376 yards or 32,808 feet 5 inches.

[![Image 13](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Taisto_M%C3%A4ki_sets_10%2C000_m_world_record.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Taisto_M%C3%A4ki_sets_10,000_m_world_record.jpeg)

[Taisto Mäki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taisto_M%C3%A4ki "Taisto Mäki") from [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") breaks the 30-minute barrier in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki "Helsinki") on 17 September 1939.

Added to the Olympic programme in 1912, athletes from Finland, nicknamed the "[Flying Finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Finn "Flying Finn")", dominated the event until the late 1940s. In the 1960s, African runners began to come to the fore.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_meters#cite_note-1) In 1988, the women's competition debuted in the Olympic Games.

Official records are kept for outdoor 10,000-metre track events. The world record for men is held by [Joshua Cheptegei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Cheptegei "Joshua Cheptegei") of [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda "Uganda") in 26:11.00, set in [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia "Valencia"), Spain on 7 October 2020. For women, the world record is held by [Beatrice Chebet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Chebet "Beatrice Chebet") of [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya"), in 28:54.14, set in Eugene, Oregon, on 25 May 2024.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_meters#cite_note-2)

The 10,000 metres demands exceptional levels of aerobic endurance, and elite athletes typically train in excess of 160 km (100 miles) a week.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_meters#cite_note-3)

## 6 miles

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=10,000_metres&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: 6 miles")]

10,000 metres is the slightly longer metric derivative of the 6-mile (9,656.1-metre) run, an event common in countries when they were using the [imperial measurement system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_units "Imperial units"). 6 miles was used in the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_Commonwealth_Games "Athletics at the Commonwealth Games") until 1966 and was a [championship in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships "USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships") in non-Olympic years from 1953 to 1973. It is 24 laps around a 1⁄4-mile (402 m; 440 yd; 1,320 ft) track.
