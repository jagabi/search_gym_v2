# [Is This a Classic Chicago Novel?](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2018/06/06/is-this-a-classic-chicago-novel/)

By [Kathleen Rooney](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/author/krooney/ "Posts by Kathleen Rooney")
[![Image 1](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/1ihp2zcok19ulslrlgqlg-a-1024x491.jpeg)](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/1ihp2zcok19ulslrlgqlg-a.jpeg)

The newly established publishing arm of the _Chicago Review of Books_ identifies itself as “a small press to republish classic Chicago literature in beautiful new editions.” But of what can a classic be said to consist? Looking at the etymology of the term, one finds that the meaning “of or belonging to the highest class; approved as a model” dates to the seventeenth century and derives from the Latin _classicus_, “relating to the highest classes of the Roman people”—in other words, superior. The obvious questions arise: superior to what and according to whom? Like so many highly subjective designations, the clearest definitions of _classic_ are usually ostensive. The definer simply points to examples and says,That—that’s what we mean.

The text toward which Chicago Review of Books Press points to inaugurate their new series is Henry Blake Fuller’s _The Cliff-Dwellers_, which they declare to be “the first great ‘Chicago novel’ ” and cite as having been listed by _Chicago_ magazine as number six in their 2010 list of “The Top 40 Chicago Novels.” In his introduction to the reissue, the _Chicago Review of Books_’s editor in chief, Adam Morgan, quotes Dr. Joseph Dimuro of UCLA as calling _The_ _Cliff-Dwellers_“arguably the first important novel of the American city.”[Read More](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2018/06/06/is-this-a-classic-chicago-novel/#more-126215)
