**Lorain** ([/l ɔː ˈ r eɪ n/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"))[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-8) is a city in [Lorain County, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain_County,_Ohio "Lorain County, Ohio"), United States. It is located in [Northeast Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Ohio "Northeast Ohio") on [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie "Lake Erie") at the mouth of the [Black River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_(Ohio) "Black River (Ohio)"), about 25 miles (40 km) west of [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland "Cleveland"). With a population of 65,211 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census "2020 United States census"), Lorain is the [ninth-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Ohio "List of cities in Ohio") in Ohio, the most populous in Lorain County and the third-most populous in [Greater Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Cleveland "Greater Cleveland").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-9)

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

See also: [Black River Township, Lorain County, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_Township,_Lorain_County,_Ohio "Black River Township, Lorain County, Ohio")

[![Image 12](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/PostcardLorainOHAtTheLoop1913.jpg/250px-PostcardLorainOHAtTheLoop1913.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PostcardLorainOHAtTheLoop1913.jpg)

"At the Loop", Lorain, _c._ 1913

According to local government records, the city began as an unincorporated village established before 1834 as “Black River Village”, and was renamed in 1837 as "Charleston." According to 19th-century historians, the new name was rejected by its own citizens, who continued to use Black River Village. The village was incorporated as Lorain in 1874 and became a city in 1896.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-10) The first mayor was [Conrad Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Lorain,_Ohio "List of mayors of Lorain, Ohio"), who took office on April 6, 1874. The municipal boundaries incorporated most of the former [Black River Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_Township,_Lorain_County,_Ohio "Black River Township, Lorain County, Ohio") judicial boundaries, and portions of the [Sheffield Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Township,_Lorain_County,_Ohio "Sheffield Township, Lorain County, Ohio"), [Amherst Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_Township,_Lorain_County,_Ohio "Amherst Township, Lorain County, Ohio"), and [Brownhelm Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownhelm_Township,_Ohio "Brownhelm Township, Ohio") judicial borders.

The [1924 Lorain–Sandusky tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Lorain%E2%80%93Sandusky_tornado "1924 Lorain–Sandusky tornado") hit the city on Saturday, June 28, 1924. The tornado formed over the [Sandusky Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandusky_Bay "Sandusky Bay") during the late afternoon hours and hit [Sandusky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandusky,_Ohio "Sandusky, Ohio"), where it killed eight people and destroyed 100 homes and 25 businesses.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-lorain1-11) After moving east over [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie "Lake Erie") for several miles, the tornado then struck Lorain, killing 72. Among the dead were 15 people inside a collapsed theater, which makes it the worst tornado-related death toll from a single building in Ohio. Eight people were also killed inside the Bath House near the location where the tornado came onshore.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-lorain1-11)

## Economy

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Economy")]

[![Image 13](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Launching_of_the_SS_Greater_Detroit.jpg/250px-Launching_of_the_SS_Greater_Detroit.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Launching_of_the_SS_Greater_Detroit.jpg)

Launching of the SS _[Greater Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS\_Greater\_Detroit "SS Greater Detroit")_ in Lorain, 1923

Lorain has a [deindustrialized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialization "Deindustrialization") economy and was home to the [American Ship Building Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Ship_Building_Company "American Ship Building Company") Lorain Yard, [Ford Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company")[Lorain Assembly Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain_Assembly "Lorain Assembly"), and [United States Steel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Steel_Corporation "United States Steel Corporation")'s steel mill on the city's south side. Smaller industries included [Thew Shovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thew_Shovel "Thew Shovel") and [Lorain Products Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain_Products_Corporation "Lorain Products Corporation"). The city faces many similar issues to other [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt "Rust Belt") cities, including [population decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_decline "Population decline") and [urban decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_decay "Urban decay"). Poverty in the city is above the national average at 26.2%,[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-Lorain_poverty-19) lower than Cleveland's 36%.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-Cleveland_poverty-20) but higher than neighboring [Elyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elyria,_Ohio "Elyria, Ohio")'s 22.2%[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-Elyria_poverty-21)

[CenturyTel of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CenturyTel_of_Ohio "CenturyTel of Ohio") is based in Lorain.

## Parks and recreation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Parks and recreation")]

There are 51 parks managed by the city parks and recreation department, a total of 583 acres.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-27)

[Lakeview Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeview_Park_(Lorain,_Ohio) "Lakeview Park (Lorain, Ohio)") is bisected by West Erie Avenue, with the northern section being managed by the [Lorain County Metro Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain_County_Metro_Parks "Lorain County Metro Parks") and the southern by the city. The park was established in 1917 under Mayor Leonard M. Moore as a way of providing more publicly-accessible space on the lakefront. The park features a beach, rose garden, various recreational facilities, bathhouse, concession stand, several gazebos and picnic shelters, and lawn bowling. There is a sculpture shaped as an [Easter basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_basket "Easter basket") built in 1935. Traditionally, families in Lorain, in celebration of [Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter "Easter"), take an annual photo at the basket.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-29)

The rose garden was dedicated in 1932, and has 2,500 roses in 48 beds. The shape of the garden, a wheel with eight spokes, is the [Rotary International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotary_International "Rotary International") emblem in honor of the 17 community organizations that funded the garden initially, including the Lorain Rotary. The garden was restored in 2005 and roses are planted to honor and commemorate those that had ties to the community or garden itself in city history.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-30)

## Government

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Government")]

See also: [List of mayors of Lorain, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Lorain,_Ohio "List of mayors of Lorain, Ohio")

[![Image 15](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Lorain%2C_Ohio_City_Hall.jpg/250px-Lorain%2C_Ohio_City_Hall.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lorain,_Ohio_City_Hall.jpg)

Lorain City Hall

The Lorain municipal government is a [Mayor-Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-Council "Mayor-Council") structure, and operates as a statutory city under the laws and regulations set by the [Ohio Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Constitution "Ohio Constitution"), making it one of the largest Ohio cities to operate without a charter. The City of Lorain operates on a [ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_(electoral_subdivision) "Ward (electoral subdivision)")-based system. Elected positions include the mayor, eleven City Council members, the Council President, Auditor, Treasurer, Law Director, Clerk of Courts, and two judges.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-:0-31)

The mayor functions as the chief of the executive branch, with job duties including: determining city laws, spurring economic development, planning and administering city projects, delivering city services, negotiating city contracts, and budgeting.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-32) As of January 1, 2020, Jack Bradley is mayor.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-33)

The City Council consists of 11 members; eight members are elected by ward and three members are elected at-large, with one council member presiding as the President of Council. The Lorain City Council responsibilities include: determining the salary of city officials and employees, enacting ordinances and resolutions of city services, enacts tax levies, appropriating and borrowing money, licensing, regulating business, commerce, and other municipal duties. Council members serve two-year terms.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-34) Through the City Budget, the City Council directly controls the operation of the planning, zoning, street construction, maintenance and repair, water and sewer services, municipal court services, and general administrative services.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-:0-31)

### Politics

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Politics")]

Politics in the city have traditionally been closely tied to the local [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"). At the state level, Lorain is represented by [Nathan Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Manning "Nathan Manning") (R-[North Ridgeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ridgeville,_Ohio "North Ridgeville, Ohio")) of Ohio Senate District 13, and [Joe Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Miller_(Ohio_politician) "Joe Miller (Ohio politician)") (D-Amherst) of Ohio House District 53. At the federal level, all of Lorain is represented by Republican U.S. Representative [Bob Latta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Latta "Bob Latta") of [Ohio's 5th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio's_5th_congressional_district "Ohio's 5th congressional district"). Lorain is represented in the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") by Ohio Republicans [Bernie Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Moreno "Bernie Moreno") and [Jon Husted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Husted "Jon Husted").

Voter turnout for the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election") in Lorain was 24,198 out of a registered 40,885 voters, a voter [turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout_in_United_States_presidential_elections "Voter turnout in United States presidential elections") rate of 59.19%. Democratic candidate [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton_2016_presidential_campaign "Hillary Clinton 2016 presidential campaign") captured 15,192 votes, or 62.78%; Republican candidate [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2016_presidential_campaign "Donald Trump 2016 presidential campaign") captured 7,584 votes, or 31.34%; Independent candidate [Gary Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Johnson_2016_presidential_campaign "Gary Johnson 2016 presidential campaign") captured 613 votes, or 2.53%; [Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_the_United_States "Green Party of the United States") candidate [Jill Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Stein_2016_presidential_campaign "Jill Stein 2016 presidential campaign") captured 222 votes, or 0.92%. Other candidates had marginal amounts of write-in votes; additionally, it is possible that some voters did not select a presidential candidate when casting their ballot.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-36)

Lorain primarily has a local street network with four state highways maintained by the [Ohio Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Department_of_Transportation "Ohio Department of Transportation") and one [U.S. route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Numbered_Highway_System "United States Numbered Highway System"). There are no [interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System "Interstate Highway System") that pass through the city limits. Public transit is provided by [Lorain County Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain_County_Transit "Lorain County Transit"), which operates two fixed-route bus lines. [Norfolk Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Southern_Railway "Norfolk Southern Railway") operates a freight railroad running parallel to the Lake Erie shoreline.

#### Public transit

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Public transit")]

Lorain County Transit operates two fixed-route bus lines in Lorain, designated as 1 and 2. 1 runs along Washington Avenue to and from Downtown Commons (formerly Meridian Plaza), in downtown Lorain, and the LifeSkills Center in Elyria, operating one bus in each direction bi-hourly on weekdays, while route 2 likewise operates with the same points, but operates along Broadway.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] Additionally, Lorain is also served by "Via LC", a demand-responsive microtransit service operating under partnership with Via Transportation.

LCT additionally formerly operated bus routes 31, 41, 42, and 43 in Lorain. 3 of which connected as a terminus point at the now-defunct Super Kmart. 31 operated along North Ridge and Cooper Foster Park Road to and from Elyria, 41 originally operated along West Erie and Oberlin Avenues in Lorain, which eventually split into two routes; designating 42 for the latter; the former additionally operated to and from the Lorain Hospital. 43 operated as a loop service along the central and west sides of Lorain on a loop on weekdays. The four routes were shut down in January 2010 as a result of funding shortfalls.

#### Highways

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Highways")]

*   [U.S. Route 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_6#Ohio "U.S. Route 6") runs east–west along Erie Avenue, crossing the northern section of the City along the Lake Erie shoreline. U.S.-6 enters the city in the east from Sheffield Lake and continues west to Vermilion.
*   [Ohio State Route 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Route_2 "Ohio State Route 2") briefly runs east–west through city limits at the Broadway Avenue/Middle Ridge Road [Diamond interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_interchange "Diamond interchange") (exit 166).
*   [Ohio State Route 57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Route_57 "Ohio State Route 57") generally runs north–south, starting in the north at the intersection of Erie Avenue and Broadway Avenue. SR-57 runs south along Broadway until 28th Street, where the route then turns east and crosses South Lorain along the southern border of the steel mill. SR-57 turns south on Grove Avenue and continues south toward Elyria.
*   [Ohio State Route 58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Route_58 "Ohio State Route 58") runs north–south, starting in the north at the intersection of Erie Avenue and North Leavitt Road and continuing south toward Amherst.
*   [Ohio State Route 611](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Route_611 "Ohio State Route 611") runs east–west, starting in the west at the partial interchange of Erie Avenue and West 21st Street and continuing east until Colorado Avenue, where it turns east and continues toward Sheffield Village.

#### Bridges

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Bridges")]

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Joseph_H._Thompson.jpg/250px-Joseph_H._Thompson.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Joseph_H._Thompson.jpg)

[Charles Berry Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Berry_Bridge "Charles Berry Bridge")

There are three bridges that cross the Black River in the Lorain Harbor; two of these bridges are for motor vehicles and pedestrians and one is for rail transport only. The two motor vehicle/pedestrian bridges are the [Charles Berry Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Berry_Bridge "Charles Berry Bridge") and the [Lofton Henderson Memorial Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lofton_Henderson_Memorial_Bridge "Lofton Henderson Memorial Bridge").These two bridges, formerly known as the Erie Avenue Bridge and 21st Street Bridge, respectively, opened on October 12, 1940.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-:2-39) At the time of opening, they were coined the "Twin Bridges."

The Charles Berry Bridge is a double-leaf 1,052-foot (321-meter)[bascule bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bascule_bridge "Bascule bridge"); of the total length, 333 feet (101 meters) are the bascule span. At the time of construction, the bridge was the largest bascule bridge in the world and is now often credited as the second-largest in the world. Annually, the Charles Berry Bridge has an average of 700 openings.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-:2-39)

The rail bridge, historically known as the 11th Street Bridge,[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-40) is a single-track [vertical-lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical-lift_bridge "Vertical-lift bridge")[truss bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truss_bridge "Truss bridge") operated by the Norfolk-Southern Railway and constructed in 1974.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-41)

### Police

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Police")]

The Lorain Police Department was established in 1853 and has 113 police officers and 34 civilian employees.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-42)

On July 23, 2025 an ambush-like conflict occurred where three Lorain Police officers were shot by an armed gunman. Two police officers were parked eating lunch when the shooter opened fire. A third police officer responding to the call was also shot. The suspect was pronounced dead at the scene. Lorain Police Officer Philip C. Wagner was killed by injuries sustained during the incident.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-43)[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-44)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-46)

## In popular culture

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lorain,_Ohio&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: In popular culture")]

[![Image 18](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/PostcardLorainOHBroadwayLookingNorth1908.jpg/250px-PostcardLorainOHBroadwayLookingNorth1908.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PostcardLorainOHBroadwayLookingNorth1908.jpg)

Broadway, looking north, _c._ 1908

Lorain is the setting for Lorain-born [Toni Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Morrison "Toni Morrison")'s first novel, _[The Bluest Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Bluest\_Eye "The Bluest Eye")_,[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain,_Ohio#cite_note-47) where she writes:

> In that young and growing Ohio town whose side streets, even, were paved with concrete, which sat on the edge of a calm blue lake, which boasted an affinity with Oberlin, the underground railroad station, just thirteen miles away, this melting pot on the lip of America facing the cold but receptive Canada—What could go wrong?
