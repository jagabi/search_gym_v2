Unless ...](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3439/the-art-of-fiction-no-71-joan-didion)[![Image 11: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # J. P. Donleavy 1975 “So I knew I had to write a book that would be the best work in the world. It was that simple.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3809/the-art-of-fiction-no-53-j-p-donleavy)[![Image 12: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Margaret Drabble 1978 “I’ve often worried about this—that if one got really very happy in life, one might not want to write at all.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3440/the-art-of-fiction-no-70-margaret-drabble)[![Image 13: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Stanley Elkin 1976 “We all die, yes? We suffer, correct? The score keeps changing, is it not so? art hunts for avenues to life.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3394/the-art-of-fiction-no-73-john-gardner)[![Image 15: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # William Gass 1977 “Getting even is one great reason for writing.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3576/the-art-of-fiction-no-65-william-gass)[![Image 16: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # William Goyen 1976 “It is _enraging_ to work in words, sometimes; You cannot help seeing the curtain as you peek into the intimate rooms behind.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4036/the-art-of-fiction-no-46-jerzy-kosinski)[![Image 21: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Peter Levi 1979 “It’s a natural activity, you see, writing. More people do it than will admit to it…”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3379/the-art-of-poetry-no-24-peter-levi)[![Image 22: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Archibald MacLeish 1974 “The subject of art is life. You learn life by living it. And you don’t live it alone—even on Walden Pond—as _Walden_ proves on every page.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3944/the-art-of-poetry-no-18-archibald-macleish)[![Image 23: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Bernard Malamud 1975 “Comedy, I imagine, is harder to do consistently than tragedy, but I like it spiced in the wine of sadness.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3869/the-art-of-fiction-no-52-bernard-malamud)[![Image 24: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Pablo Neruda 1971 “My house has been burned; I have been detained more than once; I have been exiled; they have declared me incommunicado . . . Very well then. I'm ...](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4091/the-art-of-poetry-no-14-pablo-neruda)[![Image 25: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Joyce Carol Oates 1978 “I have beliefs, of course, like everyone—but I don’t always believe in them.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3441/the-art-of-fiction-no-72-joyce-carol-oates)[![Image 26: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Charles Olson 1970 “I've been very lucky, very lucky. I'm sorry, but I was born with a towel on my head.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4134/the-art-of-poetry-no-12-charles-olson)[![Image 27: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Anthony Powell 1978 “A n awful lot of bad writing is due to people trying to write like great writers and not really seeing that the outer covering has nothing to do with it at all ...](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3475/the-art-of-fiction-no-68-anthony-powell)[![Image 28: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Jean Rhys 1979 “It seems as if I was fated to write… which is horrible.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3380/the-art-of-fiction-no-64-jean-rhys)[![Image 29: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # George Seferis 1970 “In English the expression ‘ancient Greece’ includes the meaning of ‘finished,’ whereas for us Greece goes on living, for better or for worse; it is in ...](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4112/the-art-of-poetry-no-13-george-seferis)[![Image 30: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Irwin Shaw 1979 “The most brilliant example [of good editing] in our time . . . was Ezra Pound’s editing of _The Waste Land,_ which made the poem ...](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3395/the-art-of-fiction-no-4-continued-irwin-shaw)[![Image 31: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # John Steinbeck 1975 “A book is like a man—clever and dull, brave and cowardly, beautiful and ugly.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4156/the-art-of-fiction-no-45-continued-john-steinbeck)[![Image 32: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Gore Vidal 1974 “First coffee. Then a bowel movement. Then the muse joins me.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3917/the-art-of-fiction-no-50-gore-vidal)[![Image 33: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Kurt Vonnegut 1977 "Literature should not disappear up its own asshole, so to speak."](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3605/the-art-of-fiction-no-64-kurt-vonnegut)[![Image 34: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Eudora Welty 1972 “Once you're into a story everything seems to apply—what you overhear on a city bus is exactly what your character would say on the page you're writing ...](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4013/the-art-of-fiction-no-47-eudora-welty)[![Image 35: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Jessamyn West 1977 “I go to sleep reading and I wake up reading. Somehow I have the feeling that in some book is the great treasure I’ve been looking for all my life.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3546/the-art-of-fiction-no-67-jessamyn-west)[![Image 36: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # John Hall Wheelock 1976 “If a poet is really good he can give you a moment of reconcilement to the tragic nature of things.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3668/the-art-of-poetry-no-21-john-hall-wheelock)[![Image 37: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # Richard Wilbur 1977 “One of the jobs of poetry is to make the unbearable bearable, not by falsehood but by clear, precise confrontation.”](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/3509/the-art-of-poetry-no-22-richard-wilbur)[![Image 38: Marguerite Young](https://www.theparisreview.org/images/placeholder-interviews.gif) # P.
