**Ohio** ([/oʊ ˈ h aɪ.oʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")[](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e4/En-us-Ohio.ogg/En-us-Ohio.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:En-us-Ohio.ogg "File:En-us-Ohio.ogg")[_oh-HY-oh_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"))[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-16) is a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") in the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States") region of the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). It borders the Canadian [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_and_territories_of_Canada "Provinces and territories of Canada") of [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario "Ontario") to the north (through [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie "Lake Erie")), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania") to the east, [West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia "West Virginia") to the southeast, [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky "Kentucky") to the southwest, [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana "Indiana") to the west, and [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan") to the northwest. Ohio is the [34th-largest state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area "List of U.S. states and territories by area"), at 44,825 sq mi (116,100 km 2), and the [seventh-most populous state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population "List of U.S. states and territories by population"), with a population of nearly 11.9 million. Its capital and [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Ohio "List of cities in Ohio") is [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio"), with other major metropolitan cities including [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland "Cleveland"), [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati "Cincinnati"), [Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio "Dayton, Ohio"), [Akron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron,_Ohio "Akron, Ohio"), and [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Ohio "Toledo, Ohio").

Ohio derives its name from the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River "Ohio River") that forms its southern border, which, in turn, originated from the [Seneca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca_language "Seneca language") word _\_ohiːyo'\__, meaning "good river", "great river", or "large creek".[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-17)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-18) Its geography is varied, with rolling glaciated plains across most of the state, rugged unglaciated hills in the southeastern [Allegheny Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Plateau "Allegheny Plateau"), and Lake Erie coastline to the north. At the time of European contact, the area known as the [Ohio Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Country "Ohio Country") was inhabited by [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_peoples "Algonquian peoples") and [Iroquoian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haudenosaunee "Haudenosaunee") nations, and was contested among Native American tribes, [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America "British America"), and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France "New France"). Following American independence, Ohio was organized as part of the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory "Northwest Territory") and achieved statehood in 1803. Ohio was the first state admitted under the [Northwest Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance "Northwest Ordinance") and the first post-colonial [free state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states "Slave states and free states") admitted to the union.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Mary_Stockwell-2006-3)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-19)

Historically known as the heart of [American industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_the_United_States "Manufacturing in the United States"), Ohio has the third-largest manufacturing sector and second-largest automobile production in the U.S.; it also has a large agriculture sector.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-20) The state has shifted to a more [information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_economy "Knowledge economy") and [service-based economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_economy "Service economy") in the 21st century, ranking [seventh in GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_GDP "List of U.S. states and territories by GDP")as of 2025[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ohio&action=edit).[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-21) Tourism in Ohio is driven by attractions such as [Cedar Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Point "Cedar Point"), the [National Museum of the United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_United_States_Air_Force "National Museum of the United States Air Force") and the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame"), as well as recreation areas including [Cuyahoga Valley National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_Valley_National_Park "Cuyahoga Valley National Park") and [76 state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protected_areas_of_Ohio "List of protected areas of Ohio"). Ohio is nicknamed the "Buckeye State" after its [Ohio buckeye trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesculus_glabra "Aesculus glabra").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Why_is_Ohio_known_as_the_Buckeye_State_and_why_are_Ohioans_known_as_Buckeyes-1998-13) It is also known as the "Mother of Presidents", having been the [birthplace of seven U.S. presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_United_States_by_home_state "List of presidents of the United States by home state"),[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-22) and the "Birthplace of Aviation", as the [Wright brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers "Wright brothers") designed and built [the world's first successful airplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Flyer "Wright Flyer") in Ohio before its first flight in North Carolina.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-OH-23)

## History

Main articles: [Prehistory of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Ohio "Prehistory of Ohio") and [History of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ohio "History of Ohio")

### Indigenous settlement

[![Image 14](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Sunwatch_Aerial_illustration_HRoe_2018_400px.jpg/250px-Sunwatch_Aerial_illustration_HRoe_2018_400px.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sunwatch_Aerial_illustration_HRoe_2018_400px.jpg)

Artist's conception of the [Fort Ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ancient "Fort Ancient") culture [SunWatch Indian Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunWatch_Indian_Village "SunWatch Indian Village") in present-day [Dayton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio "Dayton, Ohio")

Archeological evidence of spear points of both the Folsom and Clovis types indicate that the Ohio Valley was inhabited by [nomadic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad "Nomad") as early as 13,000 BC.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Knepper-24) These early nomads disappeared from Ohio by 1,000 BC.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Knepper-24) Between 1,000 and 800 BC, the sedentary [Adena culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adena_culture "Adena culture") emerged. The Adena established "semi-permanent" villages because they domesticated plants, including sunflowers, and "grew squash and possibly corn"; with hunting and gathering, this cultivation supported more settled, complex villages.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Knepper-2-25) The most notable remnant of the Adena culture is the [Great Serpent Mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Serpent_Mound "Great Serpent Mound"), located in [Adams County, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_County,_Ohio "Adams County, Ohio").[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Knepper-2-25)

Around 100 BC, the Adena evolved into the [Hopewell tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_tradition "Hopewell tradition"), who were also mound builders. Their complex, large and technologically sophisticated [earthworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthworks_(archaeology) "Earthworks (archaeology)") can be found in modern-day [Marietta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta,_Ohio "Marietta, Ohio"), [Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_Ohio "Newark, Ohio"), and [Circleville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circleville,_Ohio "Circleville, Ohio").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-26) They were also a prolific trading society, their trading network spanning a third of the continent.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-27) The Hopewell disappeared from the Ohio Valley about 600 AD. The [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture "Mississippian culture") rose as the Hopewell culture declined. Many Siouan-speaking peoples from the plains and east coast claim them as ancestors and say they lived throughout the Ohio region until approximately the 13th century.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Knepper-3-28)

There were three other cultures contemporaneous with the Mississippians: the [Fort Ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ancient "Fort Ancient") people, the [Whittlesey culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittlesey_culture "Whittlesey culture")[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Knepper-3-28) and the [Monongahela Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela_Culture "Monongahela Culture").[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-29) All three disappeared in the 17th century. Their origins are unknown. The Shawnees may have absorbed the Fort Ancient people.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Knepper-3-28) It is also possible that the Monongahela held no land in Ohio during the Colonial Era. The Mississippian culture was close to and traded extensively with the Fort Ancient people.

[![Image 15](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/5NationsExpansion.jpg/250px-5NationsExpansion.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:5NationsExpansion.jpg)

[Iroquois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquois "Iroquois") conquests during the [Beaver Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_Wars "Beaver Wars") (mid-1600s), which largely depopulated the upper and mid-[Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River "Ohio River") valley

Indians in the Ohio Valley were significantly affected by the aggressive tactics of the [Iroquois Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquois_Confederation "Iroquois Confederation"), based in central and western New York.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-30) After the [Beaver Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_Wars "Beaver Wars") in the mid-17th century, the Iroquois claimed much of the Ohio country as hunting and, more importantly, beaver-trapping ground. The land gradually became repopulated by [Algonquian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_people "Algonquian people") following a series of epidemics and war in the mid-17th century. Many of these nations were multi-ethnic (sometimes multi-linguistic) societies born out of the earlier devastation brought about by disease,[_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please\_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_] war, and subsequent social instability. They subsisted on agriculture supplemented by seasonal hunts. By the 18th century, they were part of a larger global economy brought about by European entry into the [fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade "Fur trade").[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-31)

Some of the Indigenous nations that historically inhabited Ohio include the Iroquoian,[[c]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-32) the Algonquian,[[d]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-33) and the Siouan.[[e]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-34)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-35)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-36) The region was also the site of Indian massacres, such as the [Yellow Creek massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Creek_massacre "Yellow Creek massacre") and the [Gnadenhutten massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnadenhutten_massacre "Gnadenhutten massacre").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-37) After the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812 "War of 1812"), when Natives suffered serious losses such as the [Battle of Tippecanoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tippecanoe "Battle of Tippecanoe"), most Native tribes either left Ohio or had to live on only limited reservations. By 1842, all remaining Natives were forced out of the state.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-38)

### Colonial and Revolutionary eras

See also: [Ohio Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Country "Ohio Country") and [Western theater of the American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_theater_of_the_American_Revolutionary_War "Western theater of the American Revolutionary War")

During the 18th century, the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonization_of_the_Americas "French colonization of the Americas") set up a system of [trading posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_post "Trading post") to control the fur trade in the region, which was known as the [Ohio Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Country "Ohio Country"). Beginning in 1754, France and [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America "British America") fought the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War "French and Indian War"), with various Native American tribes on each side. As a result of the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763) "Treaty of Paris (1763)"), the French ceded control of Ohio and the remainder of the [Old Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Northwest "Old Northwest") to Britain in 1763.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-39)

During the 1760s and 70s, Britain thinly exercised sovereignty over the Ohio Country by stationing small garrisons in the region.[[f]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-40) Just beyond Ohio Country was the [Miami people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_people "Miami people") capital of [Kekionga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kekionga "Kekionga"), which became the center of British trade and influence in Ohio Country and throughout the future Northwest Territory. By the [Royal Proclamation of 1763](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Proclamation_of_1763 "Royal Proclamation of 1763"), lands west of [Appalachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia "Appalachia") were closed to settlement by Anglo-American colonists.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-41) The [Treaty of Fort Stanwix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fort_Stanwix "Treaty of Fort Stanwix") in 1768 explicitly reserved lands north and west of the Ohio as Native lands.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-42) A new set of British policies towards the region's tribes led to the outbreak of [Pontiac's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_War "Pontiac's War") in 1763.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-43)

Ohio tribes participated in the war until an armed expedition in Ohio led by [Brigadier General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_General "Brigadier General")[Henry Bouquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bouquet "Henry Bouquet") brought about a truce. Another colonial military expedition into the Ohio Country in 1774 brought [Lord Dunmore's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Dunmore's_War "Lord Dunmore's War"), kicked off by the [Yellow Creek massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Creek_massacre "Yellow Creek massacre") in Ohio, to a conclusion. In 1774, Britain passed the [Quebec Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Act "Quebec Act"), which formally annexed Ohio and other western lands to the [Province of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec "Province of Quebec") in order to provide a civil government and to centralize British administration of the [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal")-based fur trade.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-44) The prohibition of settlement west of the Appalachians remained, contributing to the American Revolution.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-45)

By the start of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War"), the movement of Natives and Americans between the Ohio Country and [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies "Thirteen Colonies") had resulted in tension. [Fort Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pitt_(Pennsylvania) "Fort Pitt (Pennsylvania)") in Pennsylvania had become the main fort where expeditions into Ohio started. Intrusions into the area included General [Edward Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Hand "Edward Hand")'s 1778 movement of 500 Pennsylvania [militiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_(United_States) "Militia (United States)") from Fort Pitt towards Mingo towns on the [Cuyahoga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_River "Cuyahoga River"), where the British stored military supplies which they distributed to Indian raiding parties;[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-46) Colonel [Daniel Brodhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Brodhead "Daniel Brodhead")'s invasion in 1780 and [destruction of the Lenape capital of Coshocton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brodhead's_Coshocton_expedition "Brodhead's Coshocton expedition");[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-47) a detachment of one hundred of [George Rogers Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Rogers_Clark "George Rogers Clark")'s troops that were [ambushed near the Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lochry's_Defeat "Lochry's Defeat") by Indians led by [Joseph Brant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Brant "Joseph Brant") in the same year; a British and Native American attack on the U.S.' [Fort Laurens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Laurens "Fort Laurens");[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-48) and the 1782 detainment and murder of 96 [Moravian Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Munsee "Christian Munsee") pacifists by Pennsylvania militiamen in the [Gnadenhutten massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnadenhutten_massacre "Gnadenhutten massacre").[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-49)[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-50)

The western theatre never had a decisive victor. In the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783) "Treaty of Paris (1783)") in 1783, Britain ceded all claims to Ohio Country to the new [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") after its victory in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-51)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-52)

### Northwest Territory

See also: [Northwest Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance "Northwest Ordinance") and [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory "Northwest Territory")

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Ohio_Country_en.png/250px-Ohio_Country_en.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ohio_Country_en.png)

The [Ohio Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Country "Ohio Country") indicating battle sites between American settlers and Indigenous tribes, 1775–1794

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/LandingOfThePioneers.jpg/250px-LandingOfThePioneers.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LandingOfThePioneers.jpg)

The landing of [Rufus Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Putnam "Rufus Putnam") and the first settlers at [Marietta, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta,_Ohio "Marietta, Ohio"), 1788

The United States created the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory "Northwest Territory") under the [Northwest Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance "Northwest Ordinance") of 1787.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-53)[Slavery was not permitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states "Slave states and free states") in the new territory. Settlement began with the founding of [Marietta, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta,_Ohio "Marietta, Ohio"), by the [Ohio Company of Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Company_of_Associates "Ohio Company of Associates"), which had been formed by a group of American Revolutionary War veterans. Following the Ohio Company, the [Miami Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Purchase "Miami Purchase") (also referred to as the "[Symmes Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmes_Purchase "Symmes Purchase")") claimed the southwestern section, and the [Connecticut Land Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Land_Company "Connecticut Land Company") surveyed and settled the [Connecticut Western Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Western_Reserve "Connecticut Western Reserve") in present-day [Northeast Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Ohio "Northeast Ohio"). Territorial surveyors from Fort Steuben began surveying an area of eastern Ohio called the [Seven Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Ranges "Seven Ranges") at about the same time.

The old Northwest Territory originally included areas previously known as Ohio Country and [Illinois Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Country "Illinois Country"). As Ohio prepared for statehood, the [Indiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Territory "Indiana Territory") was created, reducing the Northwest Territory to approximately the size of present-day Ohio plus the eastern half of the [Lower Peninsula of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Peninsula_of_Michigan "Lower Peninsula of Michigan") and the eastern tip of the [Upper Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Peninsula_of_Michigan "Upper Peninsula of Michigan") and a sliver of southeastern Indiana called "The Gore". The coalition of Native American tribes, known as the [Western Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Confederacy "Western Confederacy"), was forced to cede extensive territory, including much of present-day Ohio, in the [Treaty of Greenville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Greenville "Treaty of Greenville") in 1795.

Under the Northwest Ordinance, areas could be defined and admitted as states once their population reached 60,000. Although Ohio's population was only 45,000 in December 1801, the [United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") determined that it was growing rapidly enough and accelerated the process via the [Enabling Act of 1802](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1802 "Enabling Act of 1802"). In regard to the [Leni Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leni_Lenape "Leni Lenape") natives, Congress decided that 10,000 acres on the [Muskingum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskingum_River "Muskingum River") in the present state of Ohio would "be set apart and the property thereof be vested in the [Moravian Brethren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Brethren "Moravian Brethren")... or a society of the said Brethren for civilizing the Indians and promoting Christianity".[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-54)

[Rufus Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Putnam "Rufus Putnam") has been referred to as the "father of Ohio". He and [Manasseh Cutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manasseh_Cutler "Manasseh Cutler") were instrumental in creating the Northwest Ordinance, which opened up the Northwest Territory for settlement.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-55) The land was used to serve as compensation for what was owed to Revolutionary War veterans. Putnam organized and led the Ohio Company of Associates, who settled at Marietta, Ohio, where they built [Campus Martius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_Martius_(Ohio) "Campus Martius (Ohio)").[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-56)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-57)[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-58) Putnam set substantial amounts of land aside for schools, and in 1798, he created the plan for the construction of Muskingum Academy, now [Marietta College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta_College "Marietta College"). After being appointed superintendent of the Ohio Company relating to the settlement north of the Ohio River, he was later commissioned as Surveyor-General of United States Lands and served as a judge in the Northwest Territory's first court. In 1802, he served in the [Ohio Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Constitutional_Convention_(1802) "Ohio Constitutional Convention (1802)").[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-59)[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-60)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-61)

### Statehood and early years

See also: [Ohio in the War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_in_the_War_of_1812 "Ohio in the War of 1812")

On February 19, 1803, President [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson") signed an act of Congress approving Ohio's boundaries and constitution.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-62) Congress, however, did not formally admit Ohio as the 17th state. After the oversight was discovered in 1953 during preparations for Ohio's sesquicentennial, Congressman [George H. Bender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._Bender "George H. Bender") introduced legislation retroactively admitting Ohio effective March 1, 1803, the date the [Ohio General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_General_Assembly "Ohio General Assembly") first convened.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Blue-2002-63) At a special session in [Chillicothe, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chillicothe,_Ohio "Chillicothe, Ohio"), the state legislature approved a new petition for statehood, which was delivered to [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), on horseback and approved that August.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Blue-2002-63)[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-64)[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-65)

Ohio has had three capital cities: Chillicothe, [Zanesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanesville,_Ohio "Zanesville, Ohio"), and [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio"). Chillicothe served as the capital from 1803 to 1810, before the seat of government moved to Zanesville for two years as part of a legislative compromise. The capital returned to Chillicothe in 1812 and was permanently relocated to Columbus in 1816 because of its central location within the state.

[![Image 18](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Battle_of_Lake_Erie.jpg/250px-Battle_of_Lake_Erie.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Battle_of_Lake_Erie.jpg)

_[Battle of Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle\_of\_Lake\_Erie "Battle of Lake Erie")_ by [William Henry Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Powell_(artist) "William Henry Powell (artist)")

Although many Native Americans migrated west to evade encroachment, others remained in Ohio and partially assimilated. Around 1809, the [Shawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee "Shawnee") renewed resistance to further American expansion, leading to [Tecumseh's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh's_War "Tecumseh's War") between the United States and [Tecumseh's confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh's_confederacy "Tecumseh's confederacy"). During the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812 "War of 1812"), the British invaded Ohio from [Upper Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada "Upper Canada") and allied with the Shawnee. [Tecumseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh "Tecumseh") was killed at the [Battle of the Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Thames "Battle of the Thames") in 1813, and most of the Shawnee, excluding the [Pekowi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekowi "Pekowi") in southwest Ohio, were forcibly removed westward.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-66)

Ohio was on the front line of the western theater during the War of 1812. One of the largest naval engagements of the war was the [Battle of Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lake_Erie "Battle of Lake Erie") that took place near [South Bass Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bass_Island "South Bass Island"), where Commodore [Oliver Hazard Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hazard_Perry "Oliver Hazard Perry") lead a U.S. Navy squadron to victory over a Royal Navy force under [Robert Heriot Barclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Heriot_Barclay "Robert Heriot Barclay").[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-67) The victory and subsequent peace are commemorated by [Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry's_Victory_and_International_Peace_Memorial "Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial").[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-68)

After the war, most remaining Native American groups in Ohio were forcibly removed westward under federal removal policies, particularly following the [Indian Removal Act of 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Removal_Act_of_1830 "Indian Removal Act of 1830").[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-69)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-70) In 1835, Ohio and the [Michigan Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Territory "Michigan Territory") fought the mostly bloodless [Toledo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo_War "Toledo War") over control of the Toledo Strip, which included the mouth of the [Maumee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maumee_River "Maumee River") and surrounding farmland. Only one person was injured in the conflict. Congress later made Michigan's statehood conditional on ending the dispute; in exchange for relinquishing its claim to the Toledo Strip, Michigan received the western two-thirds of the [Upper Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Peninsula_of_Michigan "Upper Peninsula of Michigan").[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-71)

### Civil War and industrialization

See also: [Underground Railroad in Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Railroad_in_Ohio "Underground Railroad in Ohio"), [Ohio in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_in_the_American_Civil_War "Ohio in the American Civil War"), and [Petroleum industry in Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Ohio "Petroleum industry in Ohio")

[![Image 19](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/MorganWashington.jpg/250px-MorganWashington.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MorganWashington.jpg)

[Morgan's Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan's_Raid "Morgan's Raid") enters [Old Washington, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Washington,_Ohio "Old Washington, Ohio"), during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War")

[![Image 20](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Standard_Oil_Company_1889_CP04381_-_DPLA_-_032e381bb16b1a45abecfd1a6a86ae3a_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Standard_Oil_Company_1889_CP04381_-_DPLA_-_032e381bb16b1a45abecfd1a6a86ae3a_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Standard_Oil_Company_1889_CP04381_-_DPLA_-_032e381bb16b1a45abecfd1a6a86ae3a_(cropped).jpg)

The first [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil "Standard Oil") refinery was opened in Cleveland by businessman [John D. Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller "John D. Rockefeller").

During much of the 19th century, industry was rapidly introduced to complement an existing agricultural economy. One of the first iron [blast furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_furnace "Blast furnace") opened near [Youngstown, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngstown,_Ohio "Youngstown, Ohio"), in 1804.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-OhioSteel.org-72) By the mid-19th century, 48 blast furnaces were operating in Ohio, mostly in the southern part of the state.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-OhioSteel.org-72) The discovery of coal deposits in the state aided further development of Ohio's steel industry, and by 1853, [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland "Cleveland") was the nation's third-largest iron and steel producer.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-OhioSteel.org-72)

Ohio played a significant role in the [Underground Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Railroad "Underground Railroad") due to its location on the [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky "Kentucky") border, the northernmost state of the [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South "Upland South"), and its anti-slavery sentiments.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-73)[Abolitionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism "Abolitionism") serving the Underground Raildroad worked with enslaved people to organize escape routes to the North, and many of these routes intersected and passed through the state of Ohio.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-74)

Ohio's central location and large population gave it an important place in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"), as the Ohio River and the state's railroads were vital arteries for troop and supply movements. Ohio's industry made it one of the most important states in the Union during the war. It contributed more soldiers per capita than any other state in the Union. In 1862, the state's morale was badly shaken in the aftermath of the [Battle of Shiloh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shiloh "Battle of Shiloh"), a costly victory in which Ohio forces suffered 2,000 casualties.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-75) Later that year, when the [Confederate States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army "Confederate States Army") under the leadership of [Stonewall Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_Jackson "Stonewall Jackson") threatened Washington, D.C., Ohio governor [David Tod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Tod "David Tod") recruited 5,000 volunteers to provide three months of service.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-76)

In July 1863, towns along the Ohio River were attacked and ransacked in [Morgan's Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan's_Raid "Morgan's Raid"), starting in [Harrison, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison,_Ohio "Harrison, Ohio"), in the west and culminating in the [Battle of Salineville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Salineville "Battle of Salineville") in the far east. Although the raid had little strategic impact on the Confederacy, it alarmed the populations of Ohio and [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana "Indiana") by marking the deepest penetration of Confederate forces into Union territory during the war.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-77) Almost 35,000 Ohioans died in the conflict, and 30,000 were physically wounded.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-78) By the end of the Civil War, the Union's top three generals—[Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant "Ulysses S. Grant"), [William Tecumseh Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tecumseh_Sherman "William Tecumseh Sherman"), and [Philip Sheridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sheridan "Philip Sheridan")—were all from Ohio.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-79)

Before the Civil War, the state's largest city was [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati "Cincinnati"), but by the 1880s, the proximity of cities in [Northeast Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Ohio "Northeast Ohio") to coal and iron ore resources led to the rapid urban growth of Cleveland, [Akron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron,_Ohio "Akron, Ohio"), [Canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton,_Ohio "Canton, Ohio") and Youngstown.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-OhioSteel.org-72) The first [Bessemer converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessemer_converter "Bessemer converter") was purchased by the [Cleveland Rolling Mill Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Rolling_Mill "Cleveland Rolling Mill"), which became part of the [U.S. Steel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Steel_Corporation "U.S. Steel Corporation") after the merger of [Federal Steel Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Steel_Company "Federal Steel Company") and [Carnegie Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Steel "Carnegie Steel"), the first billion-dollar American corporation.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-OhioSteel.org-72) The first open-hearth furnace used for steel production was constructed by the Otis Steel Company in Cleveland, and by 1892, Ohio was the second-largest steel-producing state, behind Pennsylvania.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-OhioSteel.org-72)[Republic Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Steel "Republic Steel") was founded in Youngstown in 1899 and was at one point the nation's third-largest producer. Another important producer, [Armco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK_Steel "AK Steel"), was founded in [Middletown, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middletown,_Ohio "Middletown, Ohio"), in 1899.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-80)

### 20th century

The [flag of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ohio "Flag of Ohio") was adopted in 1902.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-81) After building the _[Wright Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright\_Flyer "Wright Flyer")_ at their workshop in [Dayton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio "Dayton, Ohio"), brothers [Orville and Wilbur Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orville_and_Wilbur_Wright "Orville and Wilbur Wright") made four brief flights at [Kitty Hawk, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Hawk,_North_Carolina "Kitty Hawk, North Carolina"), on December 17, 1903, inventing the first successful airplane.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-82) Ohio was hit by its greatest natural disaster in the [Great Flood of 1913](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood_of_1913 "Great Flood of 1913"), resulting in at least 428 fatalities and hundreds of millions of dollars in property damage, particularly around the [Great Miami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Miami_River "Great Miami River") basin.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-83) The [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") was founded in [Canton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton,_Ohio "Canton, Ohio") in 1920, later being honored as the home of the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame "Pro Football Hall of Fame") in 1963.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Timeline_Detail_|_Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame_Official_Site-84)[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Maroon-2006-85)

[![Image 21](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Halftrack-production-3.jpg/250px-Halftrack-production-3.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Halftrack-production-3.jpg)

A factory in [Canton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton,_Ohio "Canton, Ohio"), formerly used to manufacture safes and locks, converted to produce [half-tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-track "Half-track") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). Many Ohio factories shifted to the production of military equipment for the war effort.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-f545-86)

In the 1930s, the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States "Great Depression in the United States") struck the state hard. By 1933, more than 40% of factory workers and 67% of construction workers were unemployed in Ohio.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Ohio_History_Central-87) Approximately 50% of industrial workers in Cleveland and 80% in Toledo became unemployed, with the state unemployment rate reaching a high of 37.3%.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Ohio_History_Central-87) During this era, Cleveland residents [Jerry Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Siegel "Jerry Siegel") and [Joe Shuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Shuster "Joe Shuster") created [Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman "Superman"), partly inspired by the Jewish [golem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golem "Golem") tradition and used to portray the defeat of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism").[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-88)[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-89) Ohio went on to contribute roughly 839,000 servicemembers to [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-90) During the war, many Ohio manufacturers converted their facilities to produce military equipment and other war materials in support of the [Allied powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II").[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-f545-86) The [Dayton Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Project "Dayton Project") was a branch of the larger [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") to develop [polonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonium "Polonium") triggers used in early atomic bombs.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-f545-86)

Ohio played a significant role in American popular culture during the 20th century, producing [notable contributors to music, film, and visual art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Ohio "List of people from Ohio"). Disc jockey [Alan Freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Freed "Alan Freed") hosted the [first live rock and roll concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moondog_Coronation_Ball "Moondog Coronation Ball") in Cleveland in 1952.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-BBCFreed-91) Ohioans also made history in the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race "Space Race"), with [John Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn "John Glenn") becoming the first American to orbit Earth in 1962, and [Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong "Neil Armstrong") becoming the first human to walk on the Moon in 1969. [Carl Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Stokes "Carl Stokes") was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Cleveland_mayoral_election "1967 Cleveland mayoral election") mayor of Cleveland in 1967 and became the first African American mayor of one of the nation's 10 most populous cities.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-92)

In 1970, [Ohio Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Army_National_Guard "Ohio Army National Guard") troops killed four students during an antiwar protest at [Kent State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_University "Kent State University"), an event known as the [Kent State shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_shootings "Kent State shootings").[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-93) The shootings and the subsequent rise in student strikes affected public opinion at an already socially contentious time over the role of the [United States in the Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_in_the_Vietnam_War "United States in the Vietnam War").[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-94)

Ohio was an important state in developing [China–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93United_States_relations "China–United States relations") in the late 1970s and early 1980s.[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Lampton-2024-95):59 Relations between the two countries normalized in 1979, during the second term of Ohio governor [Jim Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Rhodes "Jim Rhodes").[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Lampton-2024-95):112 Rhodes sought to encourage economic ties, viewing China as a potential market for Ohio machinery exports, and led a [trade mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_mission "Trade mission") to China in July 1979.[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Lampton-2024-95):112 The trip resulted in developing economic ties, a sister state-province relationship with [Hubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubei "Hubei") province, long-running Chinese exhibitions at the [Ohio State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Fair "Ohio State Fair"), and major academic exchanges between Ohio State University and [Wuhan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_University "Wuhan University").[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Lampton-2024-95):113 Beginning in the 1980s, the state entered into international economic and resource cooperation treaties and organizations with other governments in the Great Lakes region, including the [Great Lakes Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Charter "Great Lakes Charter"), [Great Lakes Compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Compact "Great Lakes Compact"), and the [Council of Great Lakes Governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Great_Lakes_Governors "Council of Great Lakes Governors"). In 1995, the [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement "Dayton Agreement") ending the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War "Bosnian War") was signed at [Wright-Patterson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright-Patterson_Air_Force_Base "Wright-Patterson Air Force Base").[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-96)

### 21st century

The trend of [deindustrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialization "Deindustrialization") has continued to impact the state's economy and others within the [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt "Rust Belt") region in the 21st century. Manufacturing in the Midwestern United States experienced a stark decline during the first quarter of the century,[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Arden-2021-97) a trend that greatly impacted Ohio. From 1990 to 2019, it lost over 300,000 manufacturing jobs, but added over 1,000,000 non-manufacturing jobs.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Arden-2021-97) Coinciding with this decline, Ohio has seen a large decline in union membership: 17.4% of Ohioan workers were union members in 2000, while 12.8% were union members in 2022.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-98)

In the wake of these economic changes, the state government moved to promote new industries to offset manufacturing losses, such as the production of [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy "Solar energy") and [electric vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle "Electric vehicle").[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-99) In 2002, Governor [Bob Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Taft "Bob Taft") launched the "Third Frontier" program, which aimed to increase investment in Ohio and boost its technology sector.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-100) Much of northern Ohio was subject to the [Northeast blackout of 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_blackout_of_2003 "Northeast blackout of 2003"), which was traced back to a [FirstEnergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FirstEnergy "FirstEnergy") generating plant that went offline in [Eastlake, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastlake,_Ohio "Eastlake, Ohio").[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-101) Ohio's economy was heavily afflicted by the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"), as the state's [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment "Unemployment") rose from 5.6% in the first two months of 2008 up to a peak of 11.1% in December 2009 and January 2010.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-U.S._Bureau_of_Labor_Statistics-1976-102) It took until August 2014 for the unemployment rate to return to 5.6%.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-U.S._Bureau_of_Labor_Statistics-1976-102)

Politically, Ohio had been long regarded as a [swing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state "Swing state"),[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Fahey-2021-103) but the success of many [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") candidates in Ohio since the late 2000s has led many to question whether Ohio remains an electoral battleground.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-Fahey-2021-103)[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-104)[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-105) On March 9, 2020, the [COVID-19 pandemic reached Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Ohio "COVID-19 pandemic in Ohio"), which resulted in millions of cases.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-The_New_York_Times-2020-106)[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-107) Ohio's economy was also impacted by the pandemic, as the state saw large job losses in 2020, as well as large amounts of subsequent [stimulus spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulus_(economics) "Stimulus (economics)").[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-108) In 2023, the [Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_Ceremonial_Earthworks "Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks") sites, which preserve monumental earthworks constructed by the [Hopewell tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_tradition "Hopewell tradition") between approximately 1 and 400 AD, were designated as a [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site") by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO").[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-:1-109)

## Geography

[Put-in-Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put-in-Bay,_Ohio "Put-in-Bay, Ohio") is located on [South Bass Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bass_Island "South Bass Island"), one of Ohio's [Lake Erie Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lake_Erie_Islands "List of Lake Erie Islands").

[![Image 24](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/The_Ohio_river_running_between_Ohio_and_West_Virginia.jpg/250px-The_Ohio_river_running_between_Ohio_and_West_Virginia.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Ohio_river_running_between_Ohio_and_West_Virginia.jpg)

The [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River "Ohio River") marks the state's southern and eastern borders with [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky "Kentucky") and [West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia "West Virginia").

Ohio's location has proven to be an asset for economic growth and expansion. Because it links the Northeast to the Midwest, much cargo and business traffic passes through its borders along its well-developed highways. Ohio has the nation's 10th-largest highway network and is within a one-day drive of 50% of North America's population and 70% of North America's manufacturing capacity.[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-110) To the north, Ohio has 312 miles (502 km) of coastline with Lake Erie,[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-111) which allows for numerous cargo ports such as Cleveland and Toledo. Ohio's southern border is defined by the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River "Ohio River"). Ohio's neighbors are [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania") to the east, [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan") to the northwest, [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie "Lake Erie") to the north, [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana "Indiana") to the west, [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky "Kentucky") on the south, and [West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia "West Virginia") on the southeast. Ohio's borders were defined by [metes and bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metes_and_bounds "Metes and bounds") in the [Enabling Act of 1802](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1802 "Enabling Act of 1802") as follows:

> Bounded on the east by the Pennsylvania line, on the south by the Ohio River, to the mouth of the [Great Miami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Miami_River "Great Miami River"), on the west by the line drawn due north from the mouth of the Great Miami aforesaid, and on the north by an east and west line drawn through the southerly extreme of [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan "Lake Michigan"), running east after intersecting the due north line aforesaid, from the mouth of the Great Miami until it shall intersect Lake Erie or the territorial line, and thence with the same through Lake Erie to the Pennsylvania line aforesaid.

Ohio is bounded by the Ohio River, but nearly all of the river belongs to Kentucky and West Virginia. In 1980, the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Supreme_Court "U.S. Supreme Court") held that, based on the wording of the cessation of territory by [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia "Virginia") (which at the time included what is now Kentucky and West Virginia), the boundary between Ohio and Kentucky (and, by implication, West Virginia) is the northern low-water mark of the river as it existed in 1792.[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-112) Ohio has only that portion of the river between the river's 1792 low-water mark and the present high-water mark. The border with Michigan has also changed, as a result of the [Toledo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo_War "Toledo War"), to angle slightly northeast to the north shore of the mouth of the Maumee River.

Much of Ohio features [glaciated till plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_till_plains_(Ohio) "Glacial till plains (Ohio)"), with an exceptionally flat area in the northwest being known as the [Great Black Swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Black_Swamp "Great Black Swamp"). This glaciated region in the northwest and central state is bordered to the east and southeast first by a belt known as the [glaciated Allegheny Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaciated_Allegheny_Plateau "Glaciated Allegheny Plateau"), and then by another belt known as the [unglaciated Allegheny Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unglaciated_Allegheny_Plateau "Unglaciated Allegheny Plateau"). Most of Ohio is of low relief, but the unglaciated Allegheny Plateau [features rugged hills and forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Ohio "Appalachian Ohio").

Ohio's rugged southeastern quadrant, stretching in an outward bow-like arc along the Ohio River from the West [Virginia Panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Panhandle_of_West_Virginia "Northern Panhandle of West Virginia") to the outskirts of Cincinnati, forms a distinct [socioeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomics "Socioeconomics") unit. Geologically similar to parts of West Virginia and southwestern Pennsylvania, this area's coal mining legacy, dependence on small pockets of old manufacturing establishments, and distinctive regional dialect set this section off from the rest of the state. In 1965, Congress passed the [Appalachian Regional Development Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Regional_Development_Act "Appalachian Regional Development Act"), an attempt to "address the persistent poverty and growing economic despair of the Appalachian Region".[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-113) It defines 29 Ohio counties as part of Appalachia.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-114) While 1/3 of Ohio's land mass is part of the federally defined Appalachian region, only 12.8% of Ohioans live there (1.476 million people.)[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-115)

Significant Ohio rivers include the [Cuyahoga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_River "Cuyahoga River"), [Great Miami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Miami_River "Great Miami River"), [Maumee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maumee_River "Maumee River"), [Muskingum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskingum_River "Muskingum River"), and [Scioto River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scioto_River "Scioto River"). The rivers in northern Ohio drain into the northern Atlantic Ocean via [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie "Lake Erie") and the [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River "St. Lawrence River"), and those in southern Ohio drain into the [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico") via the Ohio River and the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River"). Ohio also includes [Bass Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_Islands "Bass Islands") and [Kelleys Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelleys_Island,_Ohio "Kelleys Island, Ohio").[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-116)[Grand Lake St. Marys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Lake_St._Marys "Grand Lake St. Marys") in the west-central part of the state was constructed as a supply of water for [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal "Canal") in the canal-building era of 1820–1850. This body of water, over 20 square miles (52 km 2), was the largest artificial lake in the world when completed in 1845.[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-117)[Ohio's canal-building projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Canals_in_Ohio "Category:Canals in Ohio") were not the economic fiasco that similar efforts were in other states. Some cities, such as Dayton, owe their industrial emergence to their location on canals, and as late as 1910 interior canals carried much of the bulk freight of the state.

Areas under the protection of the [National Park Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Park_Service "National Park Service") include [Cuyahoga Valley National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_Valley_National_Park "Cuyahoga Valley National Park"), [Hopewell Culture National Historical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_Culture_National_Historical_Park "Hopewell Culture National Historical Park"), [Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Aviation_Heritage_National_Historical_Park "Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park"), [First Ladies National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ladies_National_Historic_Site "First Ladies National Historic Site"), [James A. Garfield National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Garfield_National_Historic_Site "James A. Garfield National Historic Site"), [William Howard Taft National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft_National_Historic_Site "William Howard Taft National Historic Site"), [Charles Young Buffalo Soldiers National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Young_Buffalo_Soldiers_National_Monument "Charles Young Buffalo Soldiers National Monument"), and [Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry's_Victory_and_International_Peace_Memorial "Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial").[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-118)

### Fauna and flora

[![Image 25](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Hellbender_Cryptobranchus.jpg/250px-Hellbender_Cryptobranchus.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hellbender_Cryptobranchus.jpg)

[Eastern Hellbender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellbender "Hellbender") in captivity

Ohio has wide variety of unique animal species. Rare and endangered species include the [Eastern Hellbender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellbender "Hellbender"), which is found in the Southeastern Appalachian region of Ohio and is classified as state endangered.[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-119) The Eastern Hellbender is the 3rd largest [amphibian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian "Amphibian") in the world, and can grow up to 27 inches in length. It is fully aquatic and breathes almost entirely through its skin. Due to this, it is only found in pristine, cool, clear, fast flowing streams and rivers. It is highly threatened by [habitat loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_destruction "Habitat destruction"), [water pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution_in_the_United_States "Water pollution in the United States"), and [sedimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentation "Sedimentation") due to logging and other human activities.[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-120)

Although predominantly not in a subtropical climate, some warmer-climate flora and fauna reach well into Ohio. For instance, some trees with more southern ranges, such as the [blackjack oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackjack_oak "Blackjack oak"), _Quercus marilandica_, are found at their northernmost in Ohio just north of the Ohio River. Also evidencing this climatic transition from a subtropical to a continental climate, several plants such as the southern magnolia _([Magnolia grandiflora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia\_grandiflora "Magnolia grandiflora"))_, [Albizia julibrissin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albizia_julibrissin "Albizia julibrissin") (mimosa), [Crape Myrtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crape_Myrtle "Crape Myrtle"), and even the occasional [needle palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needle_palm "Needle palm") are hardy landscape materials regularly used as street, yard, and garden plantings in the [Bluegrass region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_region "Bluegrass region") of Ohio. These same plants will simply not thrive in much of the rest of the state. This interesting change may be observed while traveling through Ohio on [Interstate 75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_75_in_Ohio "Interstate 75 in Ohio") from [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati "Cincinnati") to [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Ohio "Toledo, Ohio"). The observant traveler of this diverse state may even catch a glimpse of Cincinnati's [common wall lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_wall_lizard "Common wall lizard"), one of the few examples of permanent "subtropical" fauna in Ohio.

### Climate

See also: [Climate change in Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Ohio "Climate change in Ohio")

[![Image 26](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/K%C3%B6ppen_Climate_Types_Ohio.png/250px-K%C3%B6ppen_Climate_Types_Ohio.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:K%C3%B6ppen_Climate_Types_Ohio.png)

[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification") of Ohio, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatological_normal "Climatological normal").

The climate of Ohio is a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate "Humid continental climate") ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification")_Dfa/Dfb_) throughout most of the state, except in the extreme southern counties of Ohio's [Bluegrass region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_region "Bluegrass region") section, which are located on the northern periphery of the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate "Humid subtropical climate") (_Cfa_) and [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South "Upland South") region of the United States. Summers are typically hot and humid throughout Ohio. Winters generally range from cool to cold. Precipitation in Ohio is moderate year-round. Severe weather is not uncommon in the state, although there are typically fewer [tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado "Tornado") reports in Ohio than in states located in what is known as the [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley "Tornado Alley"). Severe [lake effect snowstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_effect_snow "Lake effect snow") are not uncommon on the southeast shore of [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie "Lake Erie"), which is located in an area designated as the [Snowbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowbelt "Snowbelt").

The highest recorded temperature was 113[°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit "Fahrenheit") (45[°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius "Celsius")), near [Gallipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipolis,_Ohio "Gallipolis, Ohio") on July 21, 1934.[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-121) The lowest recorded temperature was −39°F (−39°C), at [Milligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milligan,_Ohio "Milligan, Ohio") on February 10, 1899,[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-122) during the [Great Blizzard of 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Blizzard_of_1899 "Great Blizzard of 1899").[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-123)

The worst weather disaster in Ohio history occurred along the Great Miami River in 1913. Known as the [Great Dayton Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Dayton_Flood "Great Dayton Flood"), the entire [Miami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Miami_River "Great Miami River") watershed flooded, including the downtown business district of [Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio "Dayton, Ohio"). As a result, the [Miami Conservancy District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Conservancy_District "Miami Conservancy District") was created as the first major floodplain engineering project in Ohio and the United States.[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio#cite_note-125)
