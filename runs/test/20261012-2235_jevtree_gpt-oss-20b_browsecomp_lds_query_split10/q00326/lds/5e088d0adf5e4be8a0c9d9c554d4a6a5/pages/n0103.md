# Meet Spanish Teacher Francisco Fuentes of TANDEM Madrid

![Image 3: Francisco Fuentes stands by a banner sign inside the TANDEM building. Meet Spanish Teacher Francisco Fuentes of TANDEM Madrid. Photo by David Corral.](https://www.dreamsabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/photo-by-david-corral-francisco-fuentes-stands-by-a-banner-sign-for-tandem-meet-francisco-fuentes-of-tandem-madrid-scaled.jpg)

When Francisco Fuentes studied at university, he primarily focused on English. In addition to undergraduate degrees in English studies and translation and interpreting, he earned a master’s in European comparative literature. But a one-day introductory course in teaching Spanish as a foreign language sparked his curiosity and changed his career.

Today, Fuentes teaches Spanish at TANDEM Madrid, a language school in the city’s historic Centro district. Most teachers there take on a three- to five-year term as head of studies, a role that Fuentes recently concluded.

As head of studies, Fuentes organized which teachers taught which classes while also teaching all levels of Spanish himself.

“I strived to create a supportive and inclusive environment where both teachers and students felt comfortable and valued,” he says. Though managing a 20-person team presented challenges, he found it rewarding and motivating. “It allowed me to build strong relationships within the school community.”

Francisco Fuentes looks forward to focusing on teaching full time again. He enjoys meeting people from all over the world and learning about their cultures, languages, and life experiences.

“I rely on my sociable nature, my sense of humour, and a commitment to continuous learning,” he says. “I always try to reflect on my teaching experiences and improve whenever something does not go as planned.”

## Connecting Cultures

Francisco Fuentes began teaching at [TANDEM Madrid](https://www.tandemmadrid.com/) in 2015. Many of his classes focus on business Spanish, with companies arranging the lessons for employees.

As he progressed in his career, Fuentes became more efficient and confident when lesson planning. He also developed a deeper understanding of student needs and teaching strategies.

“I particularly value the opportunity to connect with all students across different groups and to design lessons that align with their interests,” he says.

It can be challenging to manage expectations and motivate disengaged students who may be less comfortable in a group setting. But his years in the field keep him focused.

“I have become more composed and reflective,” says Fuentes. “While I remain enthusiastic and energetic, I now approach education with greater perspective and maturity compared to earlier stages in my career.”

## The TANDEM Experience

Operating since 1982, TANDEM Madrid offers unique experiences to students, combining language learning with cultural activities. The Spanish department also offers unique experiences known as PASIONES, explains Fuentes. In customized, one-on-one sessions, language learning is paired with wine tastings and museum visits, for example. This appeals to students seeking personal, in-depth cultural experiences to help them learn Spanish.

The individualized approach and personal attention for each student sets TANDEM apart from other Spanish language schools in Madrid, he says. Students can better thrive in a welcoming environment where they build meaningful connections.

“We take a student-centred approach by considering language needs and personal interests,” he adds. “Students are encouraged to express their preferences, and we design activities that are both enriching and engaging, ensuring that each experience aligns with their goals and motivations.”

Courses at TANDEM actively integrate Madrid into lessons. Students go outside the classroom whenever possible to experience all the capital city has to offer. “Teachers also guide students by recommending places to eat, socialize, and explore, enriching their cultural immersion,” says Fuentes.

TANDEM Madrid earned accreditation through Instituto Cervantes, an official recognition granted to institutions that meet rigorous standards for teaching Spanish as a foreign language.

“It ensures quality, consistency, and professionalism in educational practices,” Fuentes says. “For me personally, it signifies being part of a broader community united by a shared passion for teaching Spanish and promoting the language worldwide.”

## Beyond the Classroom

When he’s not teaching, Francisco Fuentes enjoys learning other languages. He studied French and speaks Italian fluently. He tried German for a few years, but decided to focus on Dutch.

“I lived in the Netherlands and in Belgium a few years ago, and I have this kind of romantic relationship with the countries and their culture,” he says. “I find it easy enough to continue with my work, and as a hobby, it helps me wind down a bit.”

Fuentes also enjoys travel. While he’s already been to Japan, he hopes to travel to Singapore and Vietnam, where he has friends, as well as Cambodia and South Korea. Time in the United States is on the list, too. California, New York, and Boston interest him the most.

During his free time in Madrid, Fuentes enjoys going to the cinema. “One of my favourite places is [Cines Verdi](https://madrid.cines-verdi.com/), a classic cinema located in the Chamberí district, which offers a peaceful and affordable experience,” he says. He also enjoys spending time with friends at traditional _castizo_ taverns such as [Casa Macareno](https://casamacareno.com/) and [Celso y Manolo](https://celsoymanolo.es/), dining on updated Spanish classics.

## Sights and Tastes of Madrid

Since the city is so intertwined with TANDEM students’ cultural experiences, they get the opportunity to really engage with locals. For students and visitors alike, Fuentes suggests less tourist-oriented spots: hidden cafés, cinemas, and peaceful squares.

“These environments offer more authentic opportunities for linguistic and cultural immersion, as Spanish is more naturally and consistently spoken,” he says.

If you’re in Madrid in August, Francisco recommends attending the traditional festivities of San Cayetano, San Lorenzo, and La Paloma.

“These celebrations offer a unique opportunity to experience the authentic castizo atmosphere, surrounded by locals,” he says. You’ll hear traditional music, see old-style clothing, and have a chance to eat classic foods.

“My favourite local dish is, without a doubt, _cocido madrileño_,” Fuentes says. The beloved stew of slow-simmered chickpeas, vegetables, and meats takes many hours to prepare.

Traditionally, restaurants serve the stew in three courses, called _vuelcos_: a bowl of broth followed by chickpeas and vegetables, then various meats. Fuentes recommends trying it at [Malacatín](https://malacatin.com/), one of the most renowned restaurants for cocido madrileño. Their version features noodle soup, chickpeas with cabbage, meat, and a whole chicken with a cumin tomato sauce.

## TANDEM Madrid’s Future and Francisco Fuentes’s Advice

“Looking ahead, the department faces new challenges shaped by global developments and technological advancements, particularly the rise of artificial intelligence,” says Francisco Fuentes of TANDEM Madrid’s future.

He views AI as a tool to create images or help translate, but he doesn’t see it as a threat that could replace the experience of in-person language learning. “Technology will never master the sensitive character of a person,” he says. “A human is a human.”

For those interested in teaching, Fuentes advises people to observe and learn from other teachers, look at their practices with a critical eye, and note effective and ineffective practices. “Most importantly, I believe that teaching as soon as possible is the best way to gain experience and improve,” he says.

As his career progresses, he also sees continuous professional development as essential. Whether exploring new methodologies, reading, or integrating modern tools and applications into teaching, keeping up with current developments is key to evolving with the times.

He also attends conferences, preferably in person, to interact with people face to face: “It’s more human in a way, and every time I have the opportunity to do that, I do.”

Francisco also emphasizes the importance of empathy. Individuals face different circumstances. “My advice to other professionals is to remain humble, generous, and lead by example, even in challenging situations,” he says.
