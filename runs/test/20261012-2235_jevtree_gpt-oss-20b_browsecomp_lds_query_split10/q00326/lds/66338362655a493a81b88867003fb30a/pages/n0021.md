Dear Colleagues

I applaud your initiative: thank you.

At the same time, might I also suggest that you investigate the counterpart? There are, for instance, many Japanese scholars who, at retirement (or further on, their heirs), find no home for their wonderful collections: their (former) universities already own most of the books they have, the used book market is saturated, and their students live in tiny housing with no space.

At the same moment, it is highly likely that a well-stocked scholar's library in Japan contains innumerable books not held anywhere in any academic library in Europe

### [The Reincarnation of Dharma Libraries Project](https://networks.h-net.org/group/announcements/20165294/reincarnation-dharma-libraries-project)

Guttorm Norberg Gundersen•10/10/2026  Announcement 

Announcement Type

Call for Volunteers

Subject Fields

Religious Studies and Theology

On behalf of the Woodenfish Foundation, we are pleased to introduce a new initiative:**The Reincarnation of Dharma Libraries**.

Over decades of research and teaching, scholars in Buddhist Studies build extensive, highly specialized personal libraries. Upon retirement, finding an enduring home for these collections can be challenging, as many Western university libraries currently face space constraints and restrictive acquisition policies. By contrast, many universities and monastic colleges across Asia are actively seeking to expand their research holdings.
