**Stephen Edwin King** (born September 21, 1947) is an American author. Dubbed the "**King of Horror**",[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-te-2) he is widely known for his [horror fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_fiction "Horror fiction") and has also explored other genres, among them [suspense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_(genre) "Thriller (genre)"), [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_fiction "Crime fiction"), [science-fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction"), [fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy "Fantasy"), and [mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_fiction "Mystery fiction").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-3) His career spans six decades, and he has written approximately [200 short stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King_short_fiction_bibliography "Stephen King short fiction bibliography"), most of which have been published in collections.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-Thrillist-4)

His [debut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debut_novel "Debut novel"), _[Carrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie\_(novel) "Carrie (novel)")_ (1974), established him in horror, and _[Different Seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Different\_Seasons "Different Seasons")_ (1982), a collection of four novellas, was his first major departure from the genre. Among the films adapted from King's fiction are [_Carrie_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_(1976_film) "Carrie (1976 film)") (1976), [_The Shining_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shining_(film) "The Shining (film)") (1980), [_The Dead Zone_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dead_Zone_(film) "The Dead Zone (film)") (1983), [_Stand by Me_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_by_Me_(film) "Stand by Me (film)") (1986), [_Misery_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misery_(film) "Misery (film)") (1990), _[The Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shawshank\_Redemption "The Shawshank Redemption")_ (1994), [_Dolores Claiborne_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolores_Claiborne_(film) "Dolores Claiborne (film)") (1995), [_The Green Mile_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Green_Mile_(film) "The Green Mile (film)") (1999), [_It_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_(2017_film) "It (2017 film)") (2017), _[The Life of Chuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Life\_of\_Chuck "The Life of Chuck")_ (2024) and [_The Long Walk_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Walk_(2025_film) "The Long Walk (2025 film)") (2025). He has published under the pseudonym [Richard Bachman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bachman "Richard Bachman") and has co-written works with other authors, notably his friend [Peter Straub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Straub "Peter Straub") and sons [Joe Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Hill_(writer) "Joe Hill (writer)") and [Owen King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_King "Owen King"). He has also written nonfiction, notably _[Danse Macabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danse\_Macabre\_(King\_book) "Danse Macabre (King book)")_ (1981) and _[On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On\_Writing:\_A\_Memoir\_of\_the\_Craft "On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft")_ (2000).

Among [other awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Stephen_King "List of awards and nominations received by Stephen King"), King has won the [O. Henry Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Henry_Award "O. Henry Award") for "[The Man in the Black Suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Black_Suit "The Man in the Black Suit")" (1994) and the [_Los Angeles Times_ Book Prize for Mystery/Thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Book_Prize_for_Mystery/Thriller "Los Angeles Times Book Prize for Mystery/Thriller") for _[11/22/63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11/22/63 "11/22/63")_ (2011). He has also won honors for his overall contributions to literature, including the 2003 [Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award "National Book Award"),[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-natbook-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-NBA-6) the 2007 Grand Master Award from the [Mystery Writers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_Writers_of_America "Mystery Writers of America")[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-7) and the 2014 [National Medal of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Arts "National Medal of Arts").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-auto4-8)[Joyce Carol Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Carol_Oates "Joyce Carol Oates") called King "a brilliantly rooted, psychologically 'realistic' writer for whom the American scene has been a continuous source of inspiration, and American popular culture a vast cornucopia of possibilities."[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Oates-9)

## Early life and education

King was born in [Portland, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Maine "Portland, Maine"), on September 21, 1947. His father, Donald Edwin King, a traveling vacuum salesman after returning from [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), was born in Indiana with the surname Pollock, changing it to King as an adult.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-10) King's mother was Nellie Ruth King (née Pillsbury).[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-11) His parents were married in [Scarborough, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_Maine "Scarborough, Maine"), on July 23, 1939. They lived with Donald's family in Chicago before moving to [Croton-on-Hudson, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croton-on-Hudson,_New_York "Croton-on-Hudson, New York").[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-12) King's parents returned to Maine towards the end of World War II, living in a modest house in Scarborough. He is of [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch-Irish_Americans "Scotch-Irish Americans") descent.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-13)

When King was two, his father left the family. His mother raised him and his older brother David by herself, sometimes under great financial strain. They moved from Scarborough and depended on relatives in Chicago, Illinois; Croton-on-Hudson; [West De Pere, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_De_Pere,_Wisconsin "West De Pere, Wisconsin"); [Fort Wayne, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne,_Indiana "Fort Wayne, Indiana"); [Malden, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malden,_Massachusetts "Malden, Massachusetts"); and [Stratford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford,_Connecticut "Stratford, Connecticut").[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-14) When King was 11, his family moved to [Durham, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_Maine "Durham, Maine"), where his mother cared for her parents until their deaths. After that, she became a caregiver in a local residential facility for the mentally challenged.

King says he started writing when he was "about six or seven, just copying panels out of comic books and then making up my own stories ... Film was also a major influence. I loved the movies from the start. So when I started to write, I had a tendency to write in images because that was all I knew at the time."[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ParisReview-15) Regarding his interest in horror, he says "It’s built in. That’s all. The first movie I ever saw was a horror movie. It was _[Bambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambi "Bambi")_. When that little deer gets caught in a forest fire, I was terrified, but I was also exhilarated."[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-RS-16) He recalls showing his mother a story he copied out of a comic book. She responded: "I bet you could do better. Write one of your own." He recalls "an immense feeling of _possibility_ at the idea, as if I had been ushered into a vast building filled with closed doors and had been given the key to open any I liked."[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-17) King was a voracious reader in his youth: "I read everything from [Nancy Drew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Drew "Nancy Drew") to _[Psycho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psycho\_(novel) "Psycho (novel)")_. My favorite was _[The Shrinking Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shrinking\_Man "The Shrinking Man")_, by [Richard Matheson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Matheson "Richard Matheson")—I was 8 when I found that."[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ByTheBook-18)

King asked a [bookmobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookmobile "Bookmobile") driver, "Do you have any stories about how kids really are?" She gave him a copy of _[Lord of the Flies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord\_of\_the\_Flies "Lord of the Flies")_, which proved formative: "It was, so far as I can remember, the first book with hands—strong ones that reached out of the pages and seized me by the throat. It said to me, 'This is not just entertainment; it's life or death.'... To me, _Lord of the Flies_ has always represented what novels are _for,_ why they are indispensable."[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:LordOfTheFlies-19) He attended Durham Elementary School and entered [Lisbon High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_High_School_(Maine) "Lisbon High School (Maine)") in [Lisbon Falls, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Falls,_Maine "Lisbon Falls, Maine"), in 1962.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-OfficialBio-1) He contributed to _Dave's Rag_, the newspaper his brother printed with a [mimeograph machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimeograph_machine "Mimeograph machine"), and later sold stories to his friends. His first independently published story was "[I Was a Teenage Grave Robber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Was_a_Teenage_Grave_Robber "I Was a Teenage Grave Robber")", serialized over four issues of the [fanzine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanzine "Fanzine")_Comics Review_ in 1965. He was a sports reporter for Lisbon's _Weekly Enterprise_.

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Hampden_Academy.jpg/250px-Hampden_Academy.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hampden_Academy.jpg)

In 1971, King worked as a teacher at [Hampden Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden_Academy "Hampden Academy").

In 1966, King entered the [University of Maine at Orono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Orono "University of Maine at Orono") on a scholarship. While there, he wrote for the student newspaper, _The Maine Campus_, and found mentors in the professors Edward Holmes and [Burton Hatlen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Hatlen "Burton Hatlen").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-20)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-bn-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Singer-22) King participated in a writing workshop organized by Hatlen, where he fell in love with [Tabitha Spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabitha_King "Tabitha King").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-bn-21) King graduated in 1970 with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts#United_States "Bachelor of Arts") in English, and his daughter Naomi Rachel was born that year. King and Spruce wed in 1971.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-OfficialBio-1) He paid tribute to Hatlen: "Burt was the greatest English teacher I ever had. It was he who first showed me the way to the pool, which he called 'the language pool, the myth-pool, where we all go down to drink.' That was in 1968. I have trod the path that leads there often in the years since, and I can think of no better place to spend one's days; the water is still sweet, and the fish still swim."[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-bn-21)

## Career

### Beginnings

King sold his first professional short story, "[The Glass Floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Glass_Floor "The Glass Floor")", to _Startling Mystery Stories_ in 1967.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-OfficialBio-1) After graduating from the University of Maine, King earned a certificate to teach high school but was unable to find a teaching post immediately. He sold short stories to magazines like _[Cavalier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalier\_(magazine) "Cavalier (magazine)")_. Many of these early stories were republished in _[Night Shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night\_Shift\_(short\_story\_collection) "Night Shift (short story collection)")_ (1978). In 1971, King was hired as an English teacher at [Hampden Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden_Academy "Hampden Academy") in [Hampden, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampden,_Maine "Hampden, Maine").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-OfficialBio-1) He continued to contribute short stories to magazines and worked on ideas for novels, including the [anti-war novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-war_novel "Anti-war novel")_[Sword in the Darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword\_in\_the\_Darkness "Sword in the Darkness")_, still unpublished.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-23)

### 1970s: _Carrie_ to _The Dead Zone_

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Portrait_photograph_of_Stephen_King_by_Alex_Gotfryd%2C_c._1977.jpg/250px-Portrait_photograph_of_Stephen_King_by_Alex_Gotfryd%2C_c._1977.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Portrait_photograph_of_Stephen_King_by_Alex_Gotfryd,_c._1977.jpg)

Portraits from the first edition of _[Carrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie\_(novel) "Carrie (novel)")_ (1974) (left) and _[The Shining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shining\_(novel) "The Shining (novel)")_ (1977) (right)

King recalls the origin of his [debut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debut_novel "Debut novel"), _[Carrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie\_(novel) "Carrie (novel)")_: "Two unrelated ideas, adolescent cruelty and telekinesis, came together." It began as a short story intended for _Cavalier_; King tossed the first three pages in the trash but his wife, [Tabitha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabitha_King "Tabitha King"), recovered them, saying she wanted to know what happened next. She told him: "You've got something here. I really think you do."[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-24) He followed her advice and expanded it into a novel.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-25) Per _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Guardian "The Guardian")_, _Carrie_ "is the story of Carrie White, a high-school student with latent—and then, as the novel progresses, developing—telekinetic powers. It's brutal in places, affecting in others (Carrie's relationship with her almost hysterically religious mother being a particularly damaged one), and gory in even more."[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-26)_[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ noted that "King does more than tell a story. He is a schoolteacher himself, and he gets into Carrie's mind as well as into the minds of her classmates. He also knows a thing or two about symbolism — blood symbolism especially."[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-27)

King was teaching _[Dracula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracula "Dracula")_ to high school students and wondered what would happen if Old World [vampires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampires "Vampires") came to a small New England town. This was the germ of _['Salem's Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Salem's\_Lot "'Salem's Lot")_, which King called "_[Peyton Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton\_Place\_(novel) "Peyton Place (novel)")_ meets _Dracula_".[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Deaver-28) King's mother died from uterine cancer around the time '_Salem's Lot_ was published.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-OfficialBio-1) After his mother's death, King and his family moved to [Boulder, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulder,_Colorado "Boulder, Colorado"). He paid a visit to the [Stanley Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Hotel "The Stanley Hotel") in [Estes Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estes_Park,_Colorado "Estes Park, Colorado") which provided the basis for _[The Shining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shining\_(novel) "The Shining (novel)")_, about an alcoholic writer and his family taking care of a hotel for the winter.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ParisReview-15) It was around this time that King suspected he was an alcoholic.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-RS-16)

King's family returned to [Auburn, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn,_Maine "Auburn, Maine") in 1975, where he completed _[The Stand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Stand "The Stand")_, an apocalyptic novel about a pandemic and its aftermath. King recalls that it was the novel that took him the longest to write, and that it was "also the one my longtime readers still seem to like the best".[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-29) In 1977, the Kings, with the addition of [Owen Philip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_King "Owen King"), their third and youngest child, traveled briefly to England. They returned to Maine that fall, and King began teaching creative writing at the [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine "University of Maine").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-OfficialBio-1) The courses he taught on horror provided the basis for his first nonfiction book, _[Danse Macabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danse\_Macabre\_(King\_book) "Danse Macabre (King book)")_. In 1979, he published _[The Dead Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dead\_Zone\_(novel) "The Dead Zone (novel)")_, about an ordinary man gifted with [second sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_sight "Second sight"). It was the first of his novels to take place in [Castle Rock, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Rock_(Stephen_King) "Castle Rock (Stephen King)"). King later reflected that with _The Dead Zone_, "I really hit my stride."[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-30)

### 1980s: _Different Seasons_ to _The Dark Half_

In 1982, King published _[Different Seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Different\_Seasons "Different Seasons")_, a collection of four novellas with a more serious dramatic bent than the horror fiction for which he had become famous.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-31) Three of the four novellas were adapted as films: _[The Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Body\_(King\_novella) "The Body (King novella)")_ as [_Stand by Me_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_by_Me_(film) "Stand by Me (film)") (1986);[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-32)_[Rita Hayworth and Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita\_Hayworth\_and\_Shawshank\_Redemption "Rita Hayworth and Shawshank Redemption")_ as _[The Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shawshank\_Redemption "The Shawshank Redemption")_ (1994);[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-33) and _[Apt Pupil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apt\_Pupil "Apt Pupil")_ as the [film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apt_Pupil_(film) "Apt Pupil (film)") (1998).[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-34) The fourth, _[The Breathing Method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Breathing\_Method "The Breathing Method")_, won the [British Fantasy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Fantasy_Award "British Fantasy Award") for Best Short Fiction.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-auto1-35)[Alan Cheuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Cheuse "Alan Cheuse") wrote "Each of the first three novellas has its hypnotic moments, and the last one is a horrifying little gem."[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-36)

King struggled with addiction throughout the decade and often wrote under the influence; he says he "barely remembers writing" _[Cujo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cujo "Cujo")_ (1981).[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-37) In 1983, he published _[Christine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine\_(1983\_novel) "Christine (1983 novel)")_, "A love triangle involving 17-year-old misfit Arnie Cunningham, his new girlfriend and a haunted 1958 [Plymouth Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Fury "Plymouth Fury")."[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-38) Later that year, he published _[Pet Sematary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet\_Sematary "Pet Sematary")_, which he had written in the late 1970s, when his family was living near a highway that "used up a lot of animals" as a neighbor put it. His daughter's cat was killed, and they buried it in a pet cemetery built by the local children. King imagined a burial ground beyond it that could raise the dead, albeit imperfectly. He initially found it too disturbing to publish, but resurrected it to fulfill his contract with [Doubleday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubleday_(publisher) "Doubleday (publisher)").[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-39)

In 1985, King published _[Skeleton Crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton\_Crew\_(short\_story\_collection) "Skeleton Crew (short story collection)")_, a book of short fiction including "[The Reach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reach "The Reach")" and _[The Mist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Mist\_(novella) "The Mist (novella)")_. He recalls: "I would be asked, 'What happened in your childhood that makes you want to write those terrible things?' I couldn't think of any real answer to that. And I thought to myself, 'Why don't you write a final exam on horror, and put in all the monsters that everyone was afraid of as a kid? Put in Frankenstein, the werewolf, the vampire, the mummy, the giant creatures that ate up New York in the old B movies. Put 'em all in there."[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-40) These influences coalesced into _[It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It\_(novel) "It (novel)")_, about a group of seven children who are terrorized by the eponymous entity, which takes the form of its victims' fears and haunts the town of [Derry, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derry_(Stephen_King) "Derry (Stephen King)"). He said he thought he was done writing about monsters, and wanted to "bring on all the monsters one last time…and call it It."[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-41)_It_ won the [August Derleth Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Fantasy_Award_for_Best_Novel "British Fantasy Award for Best Novel") in 1987.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-auto-42)

1987 was an unusually productive year for King. He published _[The Eyes of the Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Eyes\_of\_the\_Dragon "The Eyes of the Dragon")_, a [high fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_fantasy "High fantasy") novel which he originally wrote for his daughter.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-43) The book was well received by critics (_The New York Times_ praised its "pellucid" prose) but not some of King's fans, who resented his departure from horror.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-44) He followed it with _[Misery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misery\_(novel) "Misery (novel)")_, about a popular writer who is injured in a car wreck and held captive by his self-described "number-one fan". _Misery_ shared the inaugural [Bram Stoker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bram_Stoker_Award "Bram Stoker Award") with _[Swan Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan\_Song\_(McCammon\_novel) "Swan Song (McCammon novel)")_ by [Robert R. McCammon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_R._McCammon "Robert R. McCammon").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-auto2-45) King says the novel's central conflict was a metaphor for addiction.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ParisReview-15) He published _[The Tommyknockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Tommyknockers "The Tommyknockers")_, a [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") novel filled, he says, with metaphors for addiction. After its publication, King's wife staged an intervention, and he agreed to seek treatment.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:OnWriting-46) Two years later, he published _[The Dark Half](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dark\_Half "The Dark Half")_, about an author who kills off his literary alter-ego, only to find he's taken on a life of his own.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-47) In the author's note, King writes that "I am indebted to the late [Richard Bachman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bachman "Richard Bachman")."[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-48)

### 1990s: _Four Past Midnight_ to _Hearts in Atlantis_

In 1990, King published _[Four Past Midnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four\_Past\_Midnight "Four Past Midnight")_, a collection of four novellas with the common theme of time. In 1991, he published _[Needful Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needful\_Things "Needful Things")_, his first novel since achieving sobriety, billed as "The Last Castle Rock Story".[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ParisReview-15) In 1992, he published _[Gerald's Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald's\_Game "Gerald's Game")_ and _[Dolores Claiborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolores\_Claiborne "Dolores Claiborne")_, two novels about women loosely linked by a solar eclipse.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-49) The latter novel is narrated by the title character in an unbroken monologue; [Mark Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Singer_(journalist) "Mark Singer (journalist)") described it as "a morally riveting confession from the earthy mouth of a sixty-six-year-old Maine coastal-island native with a granite-hard life but not a grain of self-pity". King said he based the character of Claiborne on his mother.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Singer-22)

In 1994, King's story "[The Man in the Black Suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Black_Suit "The Man in the Black Suit")" was published in the [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween "Halloween") issue of _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_Yorker "The New Yorker")_.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-50) The story went on to win the 1996 [O. Henry Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Henry_Award "O. Henry Award"). In 1996, King published _[The Green Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Green\_Mile\_(novel) "The Green Mile (novel)"),_ the story of a death row inmate, as a [serial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_(literature) "Serial (literature)") novel in six parts. It had the distinction of holding the first, fourth, tenth, twelfth, fourteenth, and fifteenth positions on the _New York Times_ paperback-best-seller list at the same time.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Singer-22) In 1998, he published _[Bag of Bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bag\_of\_Bones "Bag of Bones")_, his first book with [Scribner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Scribner's_Sons "Charles Scribner's Sons"), about a recently widowed novelist. Several reviewers said that it showed King's maturation as a writer; [Charles de Lint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Lint "Charles de Lint") wrote "He hasn't forsaken the spookiness and scares that have made him a brand name, but he uses them more judiciously now... The present-day King has far more insight into the human condition than did his younger self, and better yet, all the skills required to share it with us."[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-51)_Bag of Bones_ won the [Bram Stoker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bram_Stoker_Award "Bram Stoker Award") and [August Derleth Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Fantasy_Award_for_Best_Novel "British Fantasy Award for Best Novel").[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-sfadb-52)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-53)

In 1999, he published _[The Girl Who Loved Tom Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Girl\_Who\_Loved\_Tom\_Gordon "The Girl Who Loved Tom Gordon")_, about a girl who gets lost in the woods and finds solace in listening to broadcasts of [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox "Boston Red Sox") games, and _[Hearts in Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearts\_in\_Atlantis "Hearts in Atlantis")_, a book of linked novellas and short stories about coming of age in the 1960s. Later that year, King was hospitalized after being hit by a van. Reflecting on the incident, he said "it occurs to me that I have nearly been killed by a character out of one of my own novels. It's almost funny." He said his nurses were "told in no uncertain terms, don't make any _Misery_ jokes".[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Craft-54)

### 2000s: _On Writing_ to _Under the Dome_

[![Image 11](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/StephenKingGFDL.PNG/250px-StephenKingGFDL.PNG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:StephenKingGFDL.PNG)

King at the [Harvard Book Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Book_Store "Harvard Book Store"), June 6, 2005

In 2000, King published _[On Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On\_Writing:\_A\_Memoir\_of\_the\_Craft "On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft")_, a mix of memoir and style manual which _[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Wall\_Street\_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ called "a one-of-a-kind classic".[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-55) Later that year he published _[Riding the Bullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riding\_the\_Bullet "Riding the Bullet")_, "the world's first mass e-book, with more than 500,000 downloads". Inspired by its success, he began publishing an [epistolary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistolary_novel "Epistolary novel") horror novel, _[The Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Plant\_(novel) "The Plant (novel)")_, in online installments using the [pay what you want](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_what_you_want "Pay what you want") method provided by [Amazon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com "Amazon.com")'s Honor System.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-56) He suggested readers pay $1 per installment, and said he'd only continue publishing if 75% of readers paid.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Dubner-57) When _The Plant_ folded, the public assumed that King had abandoned the project because sales were unsuccessful, but King later said he had simply run out of stories.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-58) The unfinished novel is still available from King's official site, now free.

In 2002, King published _[From a Buick 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From\_a\_Buick\_8 "From a Buick 8")_, a return to the territory of _Christine_.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-59) In 2005, he published the mystery _[The Colorado Kid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Colorado\_Kid "The Colorado Kid")_ for the [Hard Case Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Case_Crime "Hard Case Crime") imprint.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-60) In 2006, he published _[Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell\_(novel) "Cell (novel)"),_ in which a mysterious signal broadcast over cell phones turns users into mindless killers. That same year, he published _[Lisey's Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisey's\_Story "Lisey's Story")_, about the widow of a novelist. He calls it his favorite of his novels, because "I've always felt that marriage creates its own secret world, and only in a long marriage can two people at least approach real knowledge about each other. I wanted to write about that, and felt that I actually got close to what I really wanted to say."[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ByTheBook-18) In 2007, King served as guest editor for the annual anthology _[The Best American Short Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Best\_American\_Short\_Stories "The Best American Short Stories")_.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-61)

[![Image 12](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Stephen_King%2C_Comicon.jpg/250px-Stephen_King%2C_Comicon.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stephen_King,_Comicon.jpg)

King in 2007

In 2008, King published _[Duma Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duma\_Key "Duma Key")_, his first novel set in Florida,[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-62) and the collection _[Just After Sunset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just\_After\_Sunset "Just After Sunset")_.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-63) In 2009, it was announced he would serve as a writer for _[Fangoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangoria "Fangoria")_.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-64) King's novel _[Under the Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under\_the\_Dome\_(novel) "Under the Dome (novel)")_ was published later that year, and debuted at No. 1 on [_The New York Times_ Bestseller List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Bestseller_List "The New York Times Bestseller List").[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-65)[Janet Maslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Maslin "Janet Maslin") said of it, "Hard as this thing is to hoist, it's even harder to put down."[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-66)

### 2010s: _Full Dark, No Stars_ to _The Institute_

In 2010, King published _[Full Dark, No Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full\_Dark,\_No\_Stars "Full Dark, No Stars")_, a collection of four novellas with the common theme of retribution. In 2011, he published _[11/22/63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11/22/63 "11/22/63")_, about a time portal leading to 1958, and an English teacher who travels through it to try to prevent the [Kennedy assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy "Assassination of John F. Kennedy"). [Errol Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errol_Morris "Errol Morris") called it "one of the best time travel stories since [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells "H. G. Wells")".[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-67) In 2013, he published _[Joyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyland\_(King\_novel) "Joyland (King novel)")_, his second book for Hard Case Crime.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-68) Later that year, he published _[Doctor Sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor\_Sleep\_(novel) "Doctor Sleep (novel)")_, a sequel to _The Shining._

During his Chancellor's Speaker Series talk at [University of Massachusetts Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Lowell "University of Massachusetts Lowell") on December 7, 2012, King said that he was writing a crime novel about a retired policeman being taunted by a murderer, with the working title _[Mr. Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr.\_Mercedes "Mr. Mercedes")_.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-69) In an interview with _[Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade\_(magazine) "Parade (magazine)")_, he confirmed that the novel was "more or less" completed.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-70) It was published in 2014 and won the [Edgar Allan Poe Award for Best Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Award_for_Best_Novel "Edgar Allan Poe Award for Best Novel").[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-71) He returned to horror with _[Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival\_(novel) "Revival (novel)")_, which he called "a nasty, dark piece of work".[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-72) King announced in June 2014 that _Mr. Mercedes_ was part of a trilogy; the sequel, _[Finders Keepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finders\_Keepers\_(King\_novel) "Finders Keepers (King novel)")_, was published in 2015.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-73) The third book of the trilogy, _[End of Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End\_of\_Watch\_(novel) "End of Watch (novel)")_, was released in 2016.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-74) In 2018, he released _[The Outsider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Outsider\_(King\_novel) "The Outsider (King novel)")_, which features the character [Holly Gibney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Gibney "Holly Gibney"), and the novella _[Elevation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevation\_(novella) "Elevation (novella)")_.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-75) In 2019, he released _[The Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Institute\_(King\_novel) "The Institute (King novel)")_.

### 2020s: _If It Bleeds_ to present

In 2020, King released _[If It Bleeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If\_It\_Bleeds "If It Bleeds")_, a collection of four novellas. In 2021, he published _[Later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later\_(novel) "Later (novel)")_, his third book for Hard Case Crime.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-76) In 2022, King released the novel _[Fairy Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy\_Tale\_(novel) "Fairy Tale (novel)")_. _[Holly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly\_(novel) "Holly (novel)")_, about Holly Gibney was released in September 2023.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-77) In November 2023, the short story collection _[You Like It Darker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You\_Like\_It\_Darker "You Like It Darker")_, featuring twelve stories (seven previously published and five unreleased) was published by [Scribner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Scribner's_Sons "Charles Scribner's Sons") in May 2024.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-philippa-78) The book debuted at No. 1 on [_The New York Times_ fiction best-seller list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Best_Seller_list "The New York Times Best Seller list") for the week ending May 25, 2024.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-Combined_Print_&_E-Book_Fiction-79) King released _[Never Flinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never\_Flinch "Never Flinch")_, featuring Holly Gibney, in 2025.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-80)

### Pseudonyms

Main article: [Richard Bachman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bachman "Richard Bachman")

King published five short novels—_[Rage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rage\_(King\_novel) "Rage (King novel)")_ (1977), [_The Long Walk_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Walk_(novel) "The Long Walk (novel)") (1979), _[Roadwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadwork\_(novel) "Roadwork (novel)")_ (1981), _[The Running Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Running\_Man\_(King\_novel) "The Running Man (King novel)")_ (1982) and _[Thinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinner\_(novel) "Thinner (novel)")_ (1984)—under the pseudonym Richard Bachman. He explains: "I did that because back in the early days of my career there was a feeling in the publishing business that one book a year was all the public would accept...eventually the public got wise to this because you can change your name but you can't really disguise your style."[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-kingbachman-81) Bachman's surname is derived from the band [Bachman–Turner Overdrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachman%E2%80%93Turner_Overdrive "Bachman–Turner Overdrive") and his first name is a nod to Richard Stark, the pseudonym [Donald E. Westlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_E._Westlake "Donald E. Westlake") used to publish his darker work.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-82) The Bachman books are grittier than King's usual fare; King called his alter-ego "Dark-toned, despairing...not a very nice guy." A [Literary Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_Guild "Literary Guild") member praised _Thinner_ as "what Stephen King would write like if Stephen King could really write."[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Singer-22)

Bachman was exposed as King's pseudonym in 1985 by Steve Brown, a Washington, D.C. bookstore clerk who noticed stylistic similarities between King and Bachman and located publisher's records at the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") that named King as the author of _Rage_.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Brown-83) King announced Bachman's death from "cancer of the pseudonym". King reflected that "Richard Bachman began his career not as a delusion but as a sheltered place where I could publish a few early books which I felt readers might like. Then he began to grow and come alive, as the creatures of a writer's imagination so frequently do... He took on his own reality, that's all, and when his cover was blown, he died."[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-84) Originally, King planned _Misery_ to be released under the pseudonym before his identity was discovered.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-85)

When _[Desperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desperation\_(novel) "Desperation (novel)")_ (1996) was released, the companion novel _[The Regulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Regulators\_(novel) "The Regulators (novel)")_ was published as a "discovered manuscript" by Bachman. In 2006, King announced that he had discovered another Bachman novel, _[Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaze\_(novel) "Blaze (novel)")_, which was published the following year. The original manuscript had been held at the [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine "University of Maine") for many years and had been covered by numerous King experts. King rewrote the original 1973 manuscript for its publication.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-86)

King has used other pseudonyms. In 1972, the short story "[The Fifth Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fifth_Quarter_(short_story) "The Fifth Quarter (short story)")" was published under the name John Swithen (a _Carrie_ character) in _Cavalier_.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-87)_[Charlie the Choo-Choo: From the World of The Dark Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie\_the\_Choo-Choo "Charlie the Choo-Choo")_ was published in 2016 under the pseudonym Beryl Evans and illustrated by [Ned Dameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Dameron "Ned Dameron").[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-88) It is adapted from a fictional book central to the plot of King's _[The Dark Tower III: The Waste Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dark\_Tower\_III:\_The\_Waste\_Lands "The Dark Tower III: The Waste Lands")_.[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-89)

### _The Dark Tower_

Main article: [The Dark Tower (series)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Tower_(series) "The Dark Tower (series)")

In the late 1970s, King began a series about a lone gunslinger, [Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Deschain "Roland Deschain"), who pursues the "[Man in Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randall_Flagg "Randall Flagg")" in an alternate universe that is a cross between [J. R. R. Tolkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._R._Tolkien "J. R. R. Tolkien")'s [Middle-earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth "Middle-earth") and the American [Wild West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_West "Wild West") as depicted by [Clint Eastwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Eastwood "Clint Eastwood") and [Sergio Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Leone "Sergio Leone") in their [spaghetti Westerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaghetti_Western "Spaghetti Western"). The first story, _[The Dark Tower: The Gunslinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dark\_Tower:\_The\_Gunslinger "The Dark Tower: The Gunslinger")_, was initially published in five installments in _[The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Magazine\_of\_Fantasy\_&\_Science\_Fiction "The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction")_ under the editorship of [Edward L. Ferman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_L._Ferman "Edward L. Ferman"), from 1977 to 1981. It grew into an eight-volume epic, _[The Dark Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dark\_Tower\_(series) "The Dark Tower (series)")_, published between 1978 and 2012.

## Style, themes, and influences

### Style

[![Image 13](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Stephen_King_-_2011.jpg/250px-Stephen_King_-_2011.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stephen_King_-_2011.jpg)

King in 2011

In _[On Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On\_Writing:\_A\_Memoir\_of\_the\_Craft "On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft")_, King recalls:

> When, during the course of an interview for _The New Yorker_, I told the interviewer (Mark Singer) that I believed stories are found things, like fossils in the ground, he said that he didn't believe me. I replied that that was fine, as long as he believed that _I_ believe it. And I do. Stories aren't souvenir tee-shirts or GameBoys. Stories are relics, part of an undiscovered pre-existing world. The writer's job is to use the tools in his or her toolbox to get as much of each one out of the ground intact as possible. Sometimes the fossil you uncover is small, a seashell. Sometimes it's enormous, a _Tyrannosaurus Rex_ with all those gigantic ribs and grinning teeth. Either way, short story or thousand-page whopper of a novel, the techniques of excavation remain basically the same.[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-115)

King often starts with a "what-if" scenario, asking what would happen if an alcoholic writer was stranded with his family in a haunted hotel (_[The Shining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shining\_(novel) "The Shining (novel)")_), or if one could see the outcome of future events (_[The Dead Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dead\_Zone\_(novel) "The Dead Zone (novel)")_), or if one could travel in time to alter the course of history (_[11/22/63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11/22/63 "11/22/63")_).[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-JennaBlum6-116) He writes that "The situation comes first. The characters—always flat and unfeatured, to begin with—come next. Once these things are fixed in my mind, I begin to narrate. I often have an idea of what the outcome may be, but I have never demanded a set of characters that they do things my way. On the contrary, I want them to do things _their_ way. In some instances, the outcome is what I visualized. In most, however, it's something I never expected."[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-117)

[Joyce Carol Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Carol_Oates "Joyce Carol Oates") called King "both a storyteller and an inventor of startling images and metaphors, which linger long in the memory."[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Oates-9) An example of King's imagery is seen in _[The Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Body\_(King\_novella) "The Body (King novella)")_ when the narrator recalls a childhood clubhouse with a tin roof and rusty screen door: "No matter what time of day you looked out that screen door, it looked like sunset... When it rained, being inside the club was like being inside a Jamaican steel drum."[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-118) King writes that "The use of simile and other figurative language is one of the chief delights of fiction—reading it and writing it, as well. [...] By comparing two seemingly unrelated objects—a restaurant bar and a cave, a mirror and a mirage—we are sometimes able to see an old thing in a new and vivid way. Even if the result is mere clarity instead of beauty, I think writer and reader are participating together in a kind of miracle. Maybe that's drawing it a little strong, but yeah—it's what I believe."[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-119)

### Themes

When asked if fear was his main subject, King said "In every life you get to a point where you have to deal with something that's inexplicable to you, whether it's the doctor saying you have cancer or a prank phone call. So whether you talk about ghosts or vampires or Nazi war criminals living down the block, we're still talking about the same thing, which is an intrusion of the extraordinary into ordinary life and how we deal with it. What that shows about our character and our interactions with others and the society we live in interests me a lot more than monsters and vampires and ghouls and ghosts."[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ParisReview-15)

Joyce Carol Oates said that "Stephen King's characteristic subject is small-town American life, often set in fictitious Derry, Maine; tales of family life, marital life, the lives of children banded together by age, circumstance, and urgency, where parents prove oblivious or helpless. The human heart in conflict with itself—in the guise of the malevolent Other. The '[gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_fiction "Gothic fiction")' imagination magnifies the vicissitudes of 'real life' in order to bring it into a sharper and clearer focus."[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Oates-9) King's _The Body_ is about [coming of age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming_of_age "Coming of age"), a theme he has returned to several times, for example in _[Joyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyland\_(King\_novel) "Joyland (King novel)")_.[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-120)

King often uses authors as characters, such as Ben Mears in _['Salem's Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Salem's\_Lot "'Salem's Lot")_, Jack Torrance in _[The Shining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shining\_(novel) "The Shining (novel)")_, adult [Bill Denbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Denbrough "Bill Denbrough") in _[It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It\_(novel) "It (novel)")_ and Mike Noonan in _[Bag of Bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bag\_of\_Bones "Bag of Bones")_. He has extended this to breaking the [fourth wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_wall "Fourth wall") by including himself as a character in three novels of [_The Dark Tower_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Tower_(series) "The Dark Tower (series)"). Among other things, this allows King to explore themes of authorship; [George Stade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stade "George Stade") writes that _[Misery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misery\_(novel) "Misery (novel)")_ "is a parable in chiller form of the popular writer's relation to his audience, which holds him prisoner and dictates what he writes, on pain of death" while _[The Dark Half](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dark\_Half "The Dark Half")_ "is a parable in chiller form of the popular writer's relation to his creative genius, the vampire within him, the part of him that only awakes to raise Cain when he writes."[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-121)

Introducing King at the [National Book Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Awards "National Book Awards"), [Walter Mosley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mosley "Walter Mosley") said "Stephen King once said that daily life is the frame that makes the picture. His commitment, as I see it, is to celebrate and empower the everyday man and woman as they buy aspirin and cope with cancer. He takes our daily lives and makes them into something heroic. He takes our world, validates our distrust of it and then helps us to see that there's a chance to transcend the muck. He tells us that even if we fail in our struggles, we are still worthy enough to pass on our energies in the survival of humanity."[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-NBA-6) In his acceptance speech for the Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters, King said:

> [Frank Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Norris "Frank Norris"), the author of _[McTeague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McTeague "McTeague")_, said something like this: "What should I care if they, i.e., the critics, single me out for sneers and laughter? I never truckled, I never lied. I told the truth." And that's always been the bottom line for me. The story and the people in it may be make believe but I need to ask myself over and over if I've told the truth about the way real people would behave in a similar situation... We understand that fiction is a lie to begin with. To ignore the truth inside the lie is to sin against the craft, in general, and one's own work in particular.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-NBA-6)

### Influences

In _On Writing_, King says "If you want to be a writer, you must do two things above all: read a lot and write a lot."[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-122) He emphasizes the importance of good description, which "begins with clear seeing and ends with clear writing, the kind of writing that employs fresh images and simple vocabulary. I began learning my lessons in this regard by reading [Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Chandler "Raymond Chandler"), [Hammett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashiell_Hammett "Dashiell Hammett"), and [Ross Macdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Macdonald "Ross Macdonald"); I gained perhaps even more respect for the power of compact, descriptive language from reading [T. S. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._S._Eliot "T. S. Eliot") (those ragged claws scuttling across the ocean floor; those coffee spoons), and [William Carlos Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Carlos_Williams "William Carlos Williams") (white chickens, red wheelbarrow, the plums that were in the ice box, so sweet and so cold)."[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-123)

King has called [Richard Matheson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Matheson "Richard Matheson") "the author who influenced me most".[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-io9-124) Other influences include [Ray Bradbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Bradbury "Ray Bradbury"),[[125]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-nokingquote-125)[Joseph Payne Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Payne_Brennan "Joseph Payne Brennan"),[[126]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-126)[James M. Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_M._Cain "James M. Cain"),[[127]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-127)[Jack Finney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Finney "Jack Finney"),[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-128)[Graham Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Greene "Graham Greene"),[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:ParisReview-15)[Elmore Leonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmore_Leonard "Elmore Leonard"),[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-129)[John D. MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._MacDonald "John D. MacDonald"),[[130]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-130)[Don Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Robertson_(author) "Don Robertson (author)")[[131]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-131) and [Thomas Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Williams_(writer) "Thomas Williams (writer)").[[132]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-132) He often pays homage to classic horror stories by retelling them in a modern context. He recalls that while writing _['Salem's Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Salem's\_Lot "'Salem's Lot")_, "I decided I wanted to try to use the book partially as a form of literary homage (as [Peter Straub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Straub "Peter Straub") had done in _[Ghost Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost\_Story\_(Straub\_novel) "Ghost Story (Straub novel)")_, working in the tradition of such 'classical' ghost story writers as [Henry James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_James "Henry James"), [M. R. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._R._James "M. R. James"), and [Nathaniel Hawthorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Hawthorne "Nathaniel Hawthorne")). So my novel bears an intentional similarity to [Bram Stoker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bram_Stoker "Bram Stoker")'s _[Dracula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracula "Dracula")_, and after a while it began to seem I was playing an interesting—to me, at least—game of literary racquet-ball: _'Salem's Lot_ itself was the ball and _Dracula_ was the wall I kept hitting it against, watching to see how and where it could bounce, so I could hit it again. As a matter of fact, it took some pretty interesting bounces, and I ascribe this mostly to the fact that, while my ball existed in the twentieth century, the wall was very much a product of the nineteenth."[[133]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-133) Similarly, King's _[Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival\_(novel) "Revival (novel)")_ is a modern riff on [Mary Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Shelley "Mary Shelley")'s _[Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenstein "Frankenstein")_.[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-134) King dedicated it to "the people who built my house": Shelley, Stoker, [H. P. Lovecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._P._Lovecraft "H. P. Lovecraft"), [Clark Ashton Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Ashton_Smith "Clark Ashton Smith"), [Donald Wandrei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Wandrei "Donald Wandrei"), [Fritz Leiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Leiber "Fritz Leiber"), [August Derleth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Derleth "August Derleth"), [Shirley Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Jackson "Shirley Jackson"), [Robert Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bloch "Robert Bloch"), Straub and [Arthur Machen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Machen "Arthur Machen"), "whose short novel _[The Great God Pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Great\_God\_Pan "The Great God Pan")_ has haunted me all my life".[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-135)

He provided an appreciation for [_The Golden Argosy_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Golden_Argosy_(book) "The Golden Argosy (book)"), a collection of short stories featuring [Cather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willa_Cather "Willa Cather"), [Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway "Ernest Hemingway"), [Faulkner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Faulkner "William Faulkner"), [Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Scott_Fitzgerald "F. Scott Fitzgerald") and others: "I first found _The Golden Argosy_ in a Lisbon Falls (Maine) bargain barn called the Jolly White Elephant, where it was on offer for $2.25. At that time I only had four dollars, and spending over half of it on one book, even a hardcover, was a tough decision. I've never regretted it... _The Golden Argosy_ taught me more about good writing than all the writing classes I've ever taken. It was the best $2.25 I ever spent."[[136]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-Zane-136)

## Reception and influence

### Critical reception

King has been praised for his use of realistic detail. In _A Century of Great Suspense Stories_, editor [Jeffery Deaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffery_Deaver "Jeffery Deaver") wrote that "While there were many good best-selling writers before him, King, more than anybody since [John D. MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._MacDonald "John D. MacDonald"), brought reality to genre novels. He has often remarked that _['Salem's Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Salem's\_Lot "'Salem's Lot")_ was '_[Peyton Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton\_Place\_(novel) "Peyton Place (novel)")_ meets _[Dracula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracula "Dracula")_'. And so it was. The rich characterization, the careful and caring social eye, the interplay of story line and character development announced that writers could take worn themes such as vampirism and make them fresh again. Before King, many popular writers found their efforts to make their books serious blue-penciled by their editors. 'Stuff like that gets in the way of the story,' they were told. Well, it's stuff like that that has made King so popular, and helped free the popular name from the shackles of simple genre writing. He is a master of masters."[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Deaver-28)[Daniel Mendelsohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Mendelsohn "Daniel Mendelsohn"), reviewing _[Bag of Bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bag\_of\_Bones "Bag of Bones")_, wrote that "Stephen King is so widely accepted as America's master of paranormal terrors that you can forget his real genius is for the everyday... This is a book about reanimation: the ghosts', of course, but also Mike's, his desire to re-embrace love and work after a long bereavement that King depicts with an eye for the kind of small but moving details that don't typically distinguish blockbuster horror novels."[[137]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-137)

Many critics argue that King has matured as a writer. In his analysis of post–World War II horror fiction, _The Modern Weird Tale_ (2001), [S. T. Joshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._T._Joshi "S. T. Joshi") devotes a chapter to King's work. Joshi argues that King's best-known works are his worst, describing them as mostly bloated, illogical, maudlin and prone to _[deus ex machina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deus\_ex\_machina "Deus ex machina")_ endings. Despite these criticisms, Joshi argues that since _[Gerald's Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald's\_Game "Gerald's Game")_ (1992), King has been tempering the worst of his writing faults, producing books that are leaner, more believable and generally better written.[[138]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-Joshi-138)

In 2003, King was honored by the [National Book Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award "National Book Award") with a lifetime achievement award, the Medal of Distinguished Contribution to American Letters. Some in the literary community expressed disapproval of the award: [Richard E. Snyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Snyder "Richard E. Snyder"), the former CEO of [Simon & Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_&_Schuster "Simon & Schuster"), described King's work as "non-literature" and critic [Harold Bloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Bloom "Harold Bloom") denounced the choice: "The decision to give the National Book Foundation's annual award for 'distinguished contribution' to Stephen King is extraordinary, another low in the shocking process of [dumbing down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumbing_down "Dumbing down") our cultural life. I've described King in the past as a writer of [penny dreadfuls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_dreadful "Penny dreadful"), but perhaps even that is too kind. He shares nothing with [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe "Edgar Allan Poe"). What he is is an immensely inadequate writer on a sentence-by-sentence, paragraph-by-paragraph, book-by-book basis."[[139]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-139)

King acknowledged the controversy in his acceptance speech: "There are some people who have spoken out passionately about giving me this medal. There are some people who think it's an extraordinarily bad idea. There have been some people who have spoken out who think it's an extraordinarily good idea. You know who you are and where you stand and most of you who are here tonight are on my side. I'm glad for that. But I want to say it doesn't matter in a sense which side you were on. The people who speak out, speak out because they are passionate about the book, about the word, about the page and, in that sense, we're all brothers and sisters. Give yourself a hand."[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-NBA-6)[Shirley Hazzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Hazzard "Shirley Hazzard"), whose novel _[The Great Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Great\_Fire\_(Hazzard\_novel) "The Great Fire (Hazzard novel)")_ was that year's National Book Award winner, responded by criticizing King; she later said that she had never read him.[[140]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-140)

[Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") wrote that "A lot people were outraged when he was honored at the National Book Awards, as if a popular writer couldn't be taken seriously. But after finding that his book _On Writing_ has more useful and observant things to say about the craft than any book since [Strunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Strunk_Jr. "William Strunk Jr.") and [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._B._White "E. B. White")'s _[The Elements of Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Elements\_of\_Style "The Elements of Style")_, I have gotten over my own snobbery. King has, after all, been responsible for the movies _[The Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shawshank\_Redemption "The Shawshank Redemption")_, [_The Green Mile_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Green_Mile_(film) "The Green Mile (film)"), _[The Dead Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dead\_Zone\_(film) "The Dead Zone (film)")_, _[Misery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misery\_(film) "Misery (film)")_, _[Apt Pupil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apt\_Pupil\_(film) "Apt Pupil (film)")_, _[Christine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine\_(1983\_film) "Christine (1983 film)")_, _[Hearts in Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearts\_in\_Atlantis\_(film) "Hearts in Atlantis (film)")_, _[Stand By Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand\_by\_Me\_(film) "Stand by Me (film)")_ and _[Carrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie\_(1976\_film) "Carrie (1976 film)")_... And we must not be ungrateful for _[Silver Bullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver\_Bullet\_(film) "Silver Bullet (film)")_, which I awarded three stars because it was 'either the worst movie made from a Stephen King story, or the funniest', and you know which side of that I'm gonna come down on."[[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-141)

### Appraisal by other authors

[Cynthia Ozick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Ozick "Cynthia Ozick") said that, upon giving a reading with King, "It dawned on me as I listened to him that, never mind all the best sellers and all the stereotypes -- this man is a genuine, true-born _writer_, and that was a revelation. He is not [Tom Clancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy "Tom Clancy"). He writes sentences, and he has a literary focus, and his writing is filled with literary history. It's not glib, it's not just contemporary chatter and it's not stupid -- that's a bad way to say that something's smart, but that's what I mean."[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Dubner-57)

[Joyce Carol Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Carol_Oates "Joyce Carol Oates") praised King's [sense of place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_of_place "Sense of place"): "His fiction is famously saturated with the atmosphere of Maine; much of his mostly vividly imagined work—_[Salem's Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem's\_Lot "Salem's Lot")_, _[Dolores Claiborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolores\_Claiborne "Dolores Claiborne")_, the elegantly composed story '[The Reach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reach "The Reach")', for instance—is a poetic evocation of that landscape, its history and its inhabitants."[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Oates-9) Oates included the latter story in the second edition of _The Oxford Book of American Short Stories_.[[142]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-142)

[Peter Straub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Straub "Peter Straub") compared King favorably to [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens "Charles Dickens"): "Both are novelists of vast popularity and enormous bibliographies, both are beloved writers with a pronounced taste for the morbid and grotesque, both display a deep interest in the underclass."[[143]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-143) Straub included King's short story "[That Feeling, You Can Only Say What It Is in French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Feeling,_You_Can_Only_Say_What_It_Is_in_French "That Feeling, You Can Only Say What It Is in French")" in the [Library of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_America "Library of America") anthology _[American Fantastic Tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American\_Fantastic\_Tales "American Fantastic Tales")_.[[144]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-144)

[David Foster Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Foster_Wallace "David Foster Wallace") assigned _[Carrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie\_(novel) "Carrie (novel)")_ and _[The Stand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Stand "The Stand")_ while teaching at [Illinois State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_State_University "Illinois State University"). Wallace praised King's ear for dialogue: "He's one of the first people to talk about real Americans and how they live, to capture real American dialogue in all its, like, foulmouthed grandeur... He has a deadly ear for the way people speak... Students come to me and a lot of them have been led to believe that there's good stuff and bad stuff, literary books and popular books, stuff that's redemptive and commercial shit—with a sharp line drawn between the two categories. It's good to show them that there's a certain amount of blurring. Surface-wise, King's work is a bit televisual, but there's really a lot going on."[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King#cite_note-:Singer-22)
