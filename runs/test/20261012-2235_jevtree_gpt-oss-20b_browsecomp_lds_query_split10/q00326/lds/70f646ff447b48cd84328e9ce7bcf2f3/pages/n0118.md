# On my radar: Audrey Niffenegger’s cultural highlights

This article is more than 8 years old

The writer on the latest Laurie Anderson release, enjoying the Syfy channel and the London gallery she’s fallen in love with

Audrey Niffenegger

Sun 4 Mar 2018 05.00 EST Last modified on Wed 21 Mar 2018 19.48 EDT

Share

[9 9](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/mar/04/on-my-radar-audrey-niffenegger-cultural-hghlights#comments)

[Prefer the Guardian on Google](https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=theguardian.com)

[**Audrey Niffenegger**](https://www.theguardian.com/books/audrey-niffenegger)**is an American writer and artist born in Michigan in 1963 and now living in Chicago. In 1997, she had an idea for a sci-fi romance graphic novel about involuntary time travel. It became**[**_The Time Traveler’s Wife_**](https://www.theguardian.com/books/the-time-travelers-wife)**, her bestselling 2003 debut novel (and subsequently a film). Her books since include**[**_Her Fearful Symmetry_**](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2009/oct/04/her-fearful-symmetry-audrey-niffenegger)**,**[**_The Night Bookmobile_**](https://www.theguardian.com/books/series/nightbookmobile)**, and _Raven Girl_.**[**_Bizarre Romance_**](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/1112263/bizarre-romance/)**, a collaboration with her husband,**[**Eddie Campbell**](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2014/apr/29/weekend-comics-special-audrey-niffenegger-eddie-campbell)**, is out now (Jonathan Cape).**

## 1. Exhibition

[**Charlotte Salomon. Life? Or Theatre?**](https://jck.nl/en/exhibition/charlotte-salomon-life-or-theatre)

![Image 3: Charlotte Salomon painting in the garden about 1939.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/953db24b0948e295579041b2af08cca3deeab839/0_290_1369_821/master/1369.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)[View image in fullscreen](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/mar/04/on-my-radar-audrey-niffenegger-cultural-hghlights#img-2)

“An extraordinary graphic memoir’: Charlotte Salomon painting in her garden, circa 1939. Photograph: Alamy Stock Photo

Charlotte Salomon made her extraordinary graphic memoir, _Life? Or Theatre?_, to stave off despair during her exile from her native Berlin to her grandparents’ home in France during the Nazi occupation. [In hundreds of paintings and drawings] she recounts the suicides of family members, the disastrous effect of the Nazis on her life, and the philosophical education she receives from her stepmother’s singing teacher. I have seen this several times, most recently at the Royal Academy. It is now at the Jewish Historical Museum in Amsterdam, with extra, revelatory pages that have been in her family’s collection.

## 2. Documentary

[**Jazz (Ken Burns, 2000)**](http://www.pbs.org/kenburns/jazz/home/)

![Image 4: Duke Ellington at the piano and Louis Armstrong on trumpet in the Ken Burns’ documentary, Jazz, broadcast on PBS.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/c479eed568f6b4182d34168dae2caefa3d6e4182/0_77_2000_1355/master/2000.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)[View image in fullscreen](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/mar/04/on-my-radar-audrey-niffenegger-cultural-hghlights#img-3)

‘Watching the series intensified my respect’: Duke Ellington and Louis Armstrong in Ken Burns’s series, Jazz. Photograph: AP

I’ve always liked jazz, especially early New Orleans and Chicago style jazz, but I never felt knowledgeable about it, not in the deep way I know about rock’n’roll. My collaborator and husband, Eddie Campbell, loves it, knows about it and is always playing it at our house, and so it seemed desirable to improve my sense of jazz history. Ken Burns’s documentary TV series did the trick. It’s not light-hearted: racism, drug abuse and poverty have informed this divine music. Watching the series has intensified my respect for jazz musicians. It’s a place to begin to listen.

## 3. Place

[**House of Illustration, Granary Square, London N1**](https://www.houseofillustration.org.uk/)

![Image 5: Illustrator Sir Quentin Blake poses in front of an illustration of his study at the House of Illustration in King’s Cross.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/a9be5b6066e9a75e33c8bc8accb43a30548ab692/0_116_4000_2400/master/4000.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)[View image in fullscreen](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/mar/04/on-my-radar-audrey-niffenegger-cultural-hghlights#img-4)

‘They want you to draw’: Quentin Blake at the House of Illustration. Photograph: Alamy Stock Photo

I have fallen in love with the House of Illustration, near King’s Cross in London. This beautiful new gallery, founded by Quentin Blake in 2014, is dedicated to illustration and the graphic arts. At the moment they have a quirky exhibit of North Korean graphics – until 13 May. They have a charming bookshop, and they want you to draw. To help, they have loads of fun workshops: Medical Illustration, Creating Graphic Novels, Illustrating the Animal Kingdom. It’s easy to be happy there.

## 4. Television

[**The Magicians, Syfy**](http://www.syfy.com/themagicians)

![Image 6: The Magicians TV series](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/211d33efe26b76b64782db7d6d327331e97189bd/0_214_1200_720/master/1200.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)[View image in fullscreen](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/mar/04/on-my-radar-audrey-niffenegger-cultural-hghlights#img-5)

‘Fun, occasionally brilliant’: The Magicians on Syfy. Photograph: Syfy

I did not own a TV until two years ago and so I never had a clue about television shows – and I was pretty snobby about that. Now I watch TV and some of it is delightful. Anyway, I am a devoted fan of author [Lev Grossman’s beautiful Magicians Trilogy](http://levgrossman.com/magicians-trilogy/) – he gets at the yearning confusion of late adolescence, the need to transcend our human limitations and the certainty that we will fail at this. But, oh, wait: magic is real. The TV show – _The Magicians_, on Syfy – is fun, occasionally brilliant, and diverges from the books in interesting ways.

## 5. Library

[**The London Library, St James’s Square, SW1**](http://www.londonlibrary.co.uk/)

![Image 7: The London Library](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/a51d0d84811be49ab00a7e754451336045abcba2/0_0_2464_1617/master/2464.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)[View image in fullscreen](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/mar/04/on-my-radar-audrey-niffenegger-cultural-hghlights#img-6)

‘More than 1 million books’: the London Library Photograph: David Sillitoe/The Guardian

It is difficult to choose only one library, because all libraries are manifestations of the continuous library that includes every library that is, was or will be. But I’m choosing a library of my aspiration, one I have wandered through but not yet used: the storied, glorious London Library. It was founded by Thomas Carlyle, opened in 1841, possesses more than 1 million books and will lend almost any of them if one becomes a member and pays a yearly fee. I think that very soon I will succumb to its charms.

## 6. Music

[**Landfall by Laurie Anderson and Kronos Quartet**](http://www.nonesuch.com/journal/laurie-anderson-kronos-quartet-landfall-first-collaboration-out-now-2018-02-16)

![Image 8: Laurie Anderson](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/6ee06d3b139ea852cb799b4ebf4e5b0c2a7f969a/154_0_1304_783/master/1304.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)[View image in fullscreen](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/mar/04/on-my-radar-audrey-niffenegger-cultural-hghlights#img-7)

‘A mournful and wonderful elegy’: Laurie Anderson

_Landfall_ is the first collaboration between Laurie Anderson and the Kronos Quartet, both longtime favourites of mine, released on Nonesuch Records. It is a mournful and wondering elegy for things lost in Hurricane Sandy and a lush evocation of the events of that storm. Another thing I’m excited about musically is the [Royal Opera production of Coraline](http://www.roh.org.uk/productions/coraline-by-aletta-collins) at the Barbican. It’s a new opera based on Neil Gaiman’s spooky, lovely children’s book, with music by Mark-Anthony Turnage and directed by Aletta Collins, and looks quite otherworldly.

## In 22 days, our democracy faces a critical test…

With the midterm elections approaching, voters need fact-based reporting to decide the future of our country. Facing declining popularity, Donald Trump is accelerating his attacks on journalists’ First Amendment rights. Before you close this tab, can we ask you to support the Guardian at this critical time for journalism in the US?

The assault on the free press makes it more difficult to report on top election issues like the war in Iran, immigration enforcement, and AI’s potentially existential threat to humanity.

Most recently, Trump banned CNN, MS Now and Politico from the White House, in retaliation for “negative” coverage. While a federal judge has restored White House access for now, saying the ban is likely unconstitutional, Trump is still blocking CNN from Air Force One.

At the same time, Trump is using the FCC – which is supposed to be an independent regulator in the public interest – to settle political scores: The FCC targeted ABC after Jimmy Kimmel made a joke about the First Lady, and Trump suggested that the agency should punish the host of Meet the Press for making a factual statement the president disliked.

The Pentagon, meanwhile, fired the editor of Stars and Stripes newspaper for “insubordination” shortly after they reported on a suicide attempt aboard the USS Lincoln, the Navy aircraft carrier reported to be in dire straits after 10 months at sea.

Making matters worse, many of the US’s largest news organizations are now owned by billionaires whose personal and business interests may imperil the outlets’ editorial independence. Media critics have observed how CBS News, now owned by a Trump ally, has softened its coverage; CNN could be taken over by the same billionaire; and Jeff Bezos has imposed extreme staff cuts and editorial interventions at the Washington Post.

When freedom is under attack, it falls to a determined few news organizations to ensure the full truth still reaches the public. The Guardian is owned neither by a billionaire nor a corporation and remains dedicated to keeping trustworthy journalism paywall-free for the world.
