_**City of God**_ ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language"): _**Cidade de Deus**_) is a 2002 Brazilian [epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_film "Epic film")[crime film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_film "Crime film") directed by [Fernando Meirelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Meirelles "Fernando Meirelles") and co-directed by [Kátia Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A1tia_Lund "Kátia Lund"). The screenplay, written by [Bráulio Mantovani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%A1ulio_Mantovani "Bráulio Mantovani"), is adapted from the [1997 novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(Lins_novel) "City of God (Lins novel)") by [Paulo Lins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Lins "Paulo Lins"), though the plot is also loosely based on real events. The film portrays the rise of [organized crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_crime "Organized crime") in the [Cidade de Deus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cidade_de_Deus,_Rio_de_Janeiro "Cidade de Deus, Rio de Janeiro") suburb of [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro "Rio de Janeiro") between the late 1960s and early 1980s, culminating in a war between drug dealer Li'l Zé and vigilante-turned-criminal Knockout Ned. Its tagline is, "If you run, the beast catches you; if you stay, the beast eats you."

The film features a cast including [Alexandre Rodrigues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Rodrigues_(actor) "Alexandre Rodrigues (actor)"), [Leandro Firmino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Firmino "Leandro Firmino"), [Jonathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Haagensen "Jonathan Haagensen") and [Phellipe Haagensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phellipe_Haagensen "Phellipe Haagensen"), [Douglas Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Silva "Douglas Silva"), Daniel Zettel, [Seu Jorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seu_Jorge "Seu Jorge"), and the film debut of [Alice Braga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Braga "Alice Braga"). Most of the actors were actual residents of [favelas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favela "Favela") such as [Vidigal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidigal_(favela) "Vidigal (favela)") and Cidade de Deus itself.

_City of God_ received widespread critical acclaim and earned four nominations at the [76th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76th_Academy_Awards "76th Academy Awards"): [Best Cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Cinematography "Academy Award for Best Cinematography") ([César Charlone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Charlone_(cinematographer) "César Charlone (cinematographer)")), [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director "Academy Award for Best Director") (Meirelles), [Best Film Editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Film_Editing "Academy Award for Best Film Editing") (Daniel Rezende), and [Best Adapted Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay") (Mantovani). At the previous [75th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Academy_Awards "75th Academy Awards"), it was [Brazil's submission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Brazilian_submissions_for_the_Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film "List of Brazilian submissions for the Academy Award for Best International Feature Film") for the [Academy Award for Best International Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film "Academy Award for Best International Feature Film") but was not selected as a finalist. In 2015, the Brazilian Film Critics Association (Abraccine) ranked _City of God_ 8th on its list of the 100 greatest Brazilian films.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-8)

Following the success of _City of God_, Meirelles and Lund created the _[City of Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City\_of\_Men "City of Men")_ TV series and its [2007 film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Men_(film) "City of Men (film)"). Both projects share some actors (notably leads Silva and [Darlan Cunha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlan_Cunha "Darlan Cunha")) and the same setting as _City of God_.

## Plot

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Plot")]

An armed gang chases after an escaped chicken in a _[favela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favela "Favela")_ called the _City of God_. The chicken stops between the gang and a young man nicknamed Rocket.

In the 1960s, three impoverished, amateur thieves known as the "Tender Trio"—Shaggy, Clipper, and Rocket's older brother, Goose—rob business owners and share the money with the community who, in turn, hide them from the police.

Li'l Dice, a young boy, convinces them to hold up a motel and rob its occupants. The gang resolves not to kill anyone and tells Li'l Dice to be a lookout. Instead, Li'l Dice guns down the motel occupants after falsely warning the trio that the police are coming. The massacre attracts so much police attention that the trio is forced to split up: Clipper joins the Church, Shaggy is shot by the police while trying to escape the _favela_, and Goose is shot by Li'l Dice after taking his money while Li'l Dice's friend Benny, Shaggy's brother, watches.

In the 1970s, Rocket has joined a group of young [hippies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie "Hippie"). He enjoys [photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography "Photography") and likes one girl, Angélica, but his attempt to get close to her is ruined by a gang of petty criminal kids known as "The Runts". Li'l Dice, who now calls himself "Li'l Zé", has established a drug empire with Benny by eliminating all of the competition, except for Carrot, who is a good friend of Benny's. Rocket witnesses Li'l Zé take over 'the apartment', a known drug distribution center, and forces Carrot's underboss Blacky, to work for him instead.

Because of this monopoly, a relative peace comes over the City of God under the reign of Li'l Zé, who manages to avoid police attention by executing petty criminals, including a member of The Runts. Benny decides to branch out of the drug dealer crowd and befriends Tiago, Angélica's ex-boyfriend, who introduces him to his friend group. Benny and Angélica begin dating. Together, they decide to leave the city and the drug trade.

During Benny's farewell party, Zé and Benny get into an argument about Benny leaving; the argument is interrupted by Blacky accidentally killing Benny while trying to shoot Li'l Zé. Benny's death leaves Li'l Zé unchecked. Carrot kills Blacky for endangering his life. Li'l Zé and a group of his soldiers start to make their way to Carrot's hideout to kill him.

On the way, Zé follows a girl who dismissed his advances at Benny's party. He beats up her boyfriend, a peaceful ex-soldier named Knockout Ned, and rapes her. After Ned's brother stabs Li'l Zé, his gang retaliates by shooting into his house, killing his brother and uncle in the process. A gang war breaks out between Carrot and Li'l Zé. A vengeful Ned sides with Carrot, initially trying to stay true to his ideals of only harming criminals, but he quickly loses his morals after having to shoot a policeman during a bank robbery. Tiago also is drawn into the conflict to support his drug addiction, siding with Li'l Zé.

The war is still ongoing a year later, in 1981, the origin forgotten. Both sides enlist more "soldiers" and Li'l Zé gives the Runts weapons. One day, Li'l Zé has Rocket take photos of him and his gang. A reporter publishes the photos, a significant scoop since no outsiders can safely enter the City of God anymore. Rocket believes Li'l Zé will kill him for publishing the photo of him and his gang. The reporter takes Rocket in for the night, and he loses his virginity to her. Unbeknownst to him, Li'l Zé, jealous of Ned's media fame, is pleased with the photos and with his own increased notoriety.

Rocket returns to the city for more photographs. Rocket finds himself caught between Zé's gang and the arriving police, who quickly withdraw when they realize they are outnumbered and outgunned. Rocket is surprised that Zé asks him to take pictures, but as he prepares to take the photo, Carrot's gang arrives. In the ensuing gunfight, Ned kills Tiago but is then killed by a boy who has infiltrated Carrot's gang to avenge his father, the policeman whom Ned shot during the bank robbery. The police capture Li'l Zé and Carrot and plan to show Carrot off to the media. Since Li'l Zé has been bribing the police, they take all of Li'l Zé's money and let him go, but Rocket secretly photographs the scene. The Runts meet with Zé, seeming to want to talk, but tackle him to the floor, mock him, and shoot him dozens of times in the head, back and legs in retaliation for Li'l Zé murdering their friend a year prior to set an example for the Runts previous petty crimes spree. Rocket approaches Zé's mangled body, and takes a final photograph.

Rocket contemplates whether to publish the cops' photo, expose corruption, and become famous, or the picture of Li'l Zé's dead body, which will get him an internship at the newspaper. He decides on the latter, and the Runts walk around the City of God, making a hit list of the dealers they plan to kill to take over the drug business.

## Production

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Production")]

_City of God_ was filmed on [16mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16_mm_film "16 mm film") film stock.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-10)

On the bonus DVD, it is revealed that the only professional actor in the film was [Matheus Nachtergaele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matheus_Nachtergaele "Matheus Nachtergaele"), who played the supporting role of Carrot.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-dvdcom-11) Most of the remaining cast were from real-life favelas, and in some cases, even the real-life City of God favela itself. According to Meirelles, amateur actors were used for two reasons: the lack of available professional black actors, and the desire for authenticity. Meirelles explained: "Today I can open a casting call and have 500 black actors, but just ten years ago this possibility did not exist. In Brazil, there were three or four young black actors and at the same time I felt that actors from the middle class could not make the film. I needed authenticity."[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-Bessa/iG-12)

Beginning around 2000, about a hundred children and young people were hand-picked and placed into an "actors' workshop" for several months. In contrast to more traditional methods (e.g. studying theatre and rehearsing), it focused on simulating authentic street war scenes, such as a hold-up, scuffle, and shoot-out. Much came from [improvisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisation "Improvisation"), as it was thought better to create an authentic, gritty atmosphere. This way, the inexperienced cast soon learned to move and act naturally. After filming, the crew could not leave the cast to return to their old lives in the favelas. Assistance groups were set up to help those involved in the production to build more promising futures.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-:0-13):135–139

Meirelles went into the film with the intention of staying true to the "casual nature" of the violence in [the novel by Lins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(Lins_novel) "City of God (Lins novel)"). Critic Jean Oppenheimer wrote on the production of the film saying that: "A second guiding principle was to avoid glamorising the violence" and that "many of the killings are either shown indistinctly or kept out of frame."[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-:0-13):26–32

Because the real Cidade de Deus favela was in the middle of a conflict, a large majority of the film was shot in Cidade Alta, a different favela within Rio. During the production, slumlords did not allow for the production company to have their own security, so local security guards were hired for the safety of the set.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-14)

Lund and Meirelles filmed the [short film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film "Short film")_Golden Gate_ as a test run[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-dvdcom-11) while casting for _City of God_ was in the initial stages.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-15)

## Reception

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Reception")]

### Box office

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Box office")]

The film was screened out of competition at the [2002 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Cannes_Film_Festival "2002 Cannes Film Festival").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-festival-cannes.com-16) The film could not be part of the official selection because [Walter Salles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Salles "Walter Salles"), one of the executive producers, was in the festival jury.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-17) In Brazil, _City of God_ garnered the largest audience for a domestic film in 2002, with over 3.1 million tickets sold, and a gross of [R$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_real "Brazilian real")18.6 million ($10.3 million).[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-18) The film grossed over $7.5 million in the U.S. and over US$30.5 million worldwide.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-19)

### Critical response

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Critical response")]

On [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), _City of God_ has an approval rating of 91% based on reviews from 162 critics, with an average rating of 8.6/10. The website's consensus reads, "_City of God_ offers a shocking and disturbing—but always compelling—look at life in the slums of Rio de Janeiro."[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-20) On [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), the film holds a score of 79 out of 100 based on 33 critic reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-21)

Colin Kennedy from _[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire\_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")_ awarded the film a full 5 out of 5 stars, comparing it favorably to _[Goodfellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodfellas "Goodfellas")_ and writing in his review, "At once a laboratory for cinema technique and a victory for raw heart, this is a snot-nosed, blood-stained masterpiece. If you see even one or two better movies this year, you will be very lucky indeed."[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-22) In 2008, the magazine chose _City of God_ as the 177th-best film of all time.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-23)

[Film critic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_critic "Film critic")[Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") awarded the film 4 stars out of 4, writing in his review, "_City of God_ churns with furious energy as it plunges into the story of the slum gangs of Rio de Janeiro. Breathtaking and terrifying, urgently involved with its characters, it announces a new director of great gifts and passions: Fernando Meirelles. Remember the name."[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-24)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-25)

Filmmaker [Robert Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Altman "Robert Altman") stated, "I don't know how Fernando Meirelles made _City of God_. It's so courageous, so truthful. I think it's the best picture I've ever seen".[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-26) Meirelles himself cited Altman's work as an influence on his own career.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-27)

The film was not without criticism. Peter Rainer of _[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New\_York\_(magazine) "New York (magazine)")_ magazine stated that while the film was "powerful", it was also "rather numbing".[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-28)[John Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Powers_(film_critic) "John Powers (film critic)") of _[LA Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA\_Weekly "LA Weekly")_ wrote that although "[the film] whirs with energy for nearly its full 130-minute running time, it is oddly lacking in emotional heft for a work that aspires to be so epic—it is essentially a tarted up exploitation picture whose business is to make ghastly things fun".[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-29)

Ivana Bentes, a Brazilian film critic, criticised the film for its depiction of the favela and her view that it glorified issues of poverty and violence as means of "domestication of the most radical themes of [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Brazil "Culture of Brazil") and [Brazilian cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Brazil "Cinema of Brazil")... as products for export."[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-30) Bentes targets the film specifically in saying that: "_City of God_ promotes tourism in hell".[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-Freire-31)

_City of God_ was ranked No. 3 in [Film4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film4 "Film4")'s "[50 Films to See Before You Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50_Films_to_See_Before_You_Die "50 Films to See Before You Die")", and No. 7 in _[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire\_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")_ magazine's "The 100 Best Films of World Cinema" in 2010.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-32) It was also ranked No. 6 on _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Guardian "The Guardian")_'s list of "the 25 Best Action Movies Ever".[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-33)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-34) It was ranked No. 1 in _[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste\_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")_ magazine's 50 best movies of the decade of the 2000s.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-35)_[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time\_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ listed it as one of the [100 greatest films of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time's_All-Time_100_Movies "Time's All-Time 100 Movies").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-37)

In 2012, the [Motion Picture Editors Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Editors_Guild "Motion Picture Editors Guild") listed _City of God_ as the 17th-best-edited film of all time based on a survey of its members.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-38)

## Legacy

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Legacy")]

In an interview with _[Slant Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant\_Magazine "Slant Magazine")_, Meirelles states he had met with Brazil's former and current president [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_In%C3%A1cio_Lula_da_Silva "Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva") who told him about the impact the film has had on both policies and public security within the country. The film has also sparked major increase in film productions, with over 45 being done during 2002. Films such as [_The Motorcycle Diaries_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Motorcycle_Diaries_(film) "The Motorcycle Diaries (film)") and _The Intruder_ are some of the films which have used Brazil for film production.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-Gonzalez-72)

The 2013 documentary _[City of God – 10 Years Later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City\_of\_God\_%E2%80%93\_10\_Years\_Later "City of God – 10 Years Later")_ reunites the cast and crew of _City of God_ and takes a look at how their lives have changed after the original film's release. In a _[BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC")_ article written at the time of the documentary's release, Firmino mentions that the cast had mixed careers after the film's release. Firmino says that Jefechander Suplino, who played Clipper, could not be found by the documentary producers. His mother, however, believes him to still be alive, but is unaware of his whereabouts. [Seu Jorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seu_Jorge "Seu Jorge"), who played Knockout Ned, had a better career after the film and became a major musician, performing at the [London 2012 Olympic Games closing ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_closing_ceremony "2012 Summer Olympics closing ceremony").[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-Bowater-73)

_[City of God: The Fight Rages On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City\_of\_God:\_The\_Fight\_Rages\_On "City of God: The Fight Rages On")_, a miniseries set two decades after the events of the film, premiered in August 2024.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-74)

## Home media

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Home media")]

### North America

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=City_of_God_(2002_film)&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: North America")]

The film's international distributor [Miramax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramax "Miramax") released the film on DVD in the United States on June 8, 2004.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-75)

In 2010, Miramax was sold by [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") (their owners since 1993), with the studio being taken over by private equity firm [Filmyard Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmyard_Holdings "Filmyard Holdings") that same year.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-76) Filmyard sublicensed the home video rights for several Miramax titles to [Lionsgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionsgate_Films "Lionsgate Films"), who released _City of God_ on [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray") on December 13, 2011.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-77)

Filmyard sold Miramax to Qatari company [beIN Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeIN_Media_Group "BeIN Media Group") during March 2016.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-78) In April 2020, [ViacomCBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ViacomCBS "ViacomCBS") (now known as [Paramount Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance")) acquired the rights to Miramax's library, after buying a 49% stake in the studio from beIN.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-79) The film was made available on their streaming service [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+ "Paramount+"),[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-80) and in 2021, [Paramount Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Home_Entertainment "Paramount Home Entertainment") reissued _City of God_ on Blu-ray, along with many other Miramax titles.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-81) Paramount Home Entertainment also issued the film on a double-feature Blu-ray with _[City of Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City\_of\_Men\_(film) "City of Men (film)")_, another Miramax title they had acquired in 2020.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_(2002_film)#cite_note-82)
