_**Iracema: Uma Transa Amazônica**_ ([[iʁaˈsemɐ ˈumɐ ˈtɾɐ̃zamaˈzonikɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese "Help:IPA/Portuguese"), "Iracema: An Amazonian Transaction") is a 1974 Brazilian [Cinema Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_Novo "Cinema Novo") film directed by Jorge Bodanzky and Orlando Senna and very loosely inspired by _[Iracema: The Legend of Ceará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema "Iracema")_, an 1865 novel by [José de Alencar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_Alencar "José de Alencar"). The film takes place in the state of [Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par%C3%A1 "Pará").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-4) The title can refer to a deal or transaction, to the [Trans-Amazonian Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Amazonian_Highway "Trans-Amazonian Highway"), but also to [sexual intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_intercourse "Sexual intercourse").

## Production

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Production")]

[![Image 7](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Jorge_Bodanzky_cropped.jpg/250px-Jorge_Bodanzky_cropped.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jorge_Bodanzky_cropped.jpg)

The two directors, Jorge Bodanzky (_left_) and Orlando Senna (_right_)

The film combines scripted scenes with [Paulo César Pereio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_C%C3%A9sar_Pereio "Paulo César Pereio") (the only professional actor) and documentary footage of forests being cut down, and peasants, workers and prostitutes speaking to the camera. It was filmed in [16 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16_mm "16 mm") to allow filming in remote locations and to convey a chaotic, out-of-control sense of the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rainforest "Amazon rainforest").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-5)

## Plot

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Plot")]

A [White Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Brazilian "White Brazilian") truck driver, Tião, and a 15-year-old [_cabocla_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cabocla "wikt:cabocla") (mixed-race) prostitute, Iracema, drive through an Amazon that is facing rapid changes.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-auto-6)

## Release and reception

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Release and reception")]

_Iracema_ was banned in Brazil (then [under military rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_in_Brazil "Military dictatorship in Brazil")) due to its explicit sexual content and critique of society.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-7) Its first showing abroad was at the 1975 [Taormina Film Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taormina_Film_Fest "Taormina Film Fest") in [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily"), where it was nominated for the Golden Charybdis award.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-8) It showed at the [International Critics' Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Critics'_Week "International Critics' Week") at the [1976 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Cannes_Film_Festival "1976 Cannes Film Festival").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-9)

_Iracema_ was screened at the 1980 [Festival de Brasília](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_de_Bras%C3%ADlia "Festival de Brasília"), winning prizes for Best Film, Best Actress (Edna de Cássia), Best Supporting Actress (Conceição Senna) and Best Editing (Eva Grundman and Jorge Bodanzky).[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-12)

The _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New\_York\_Times "New York Times")_ wrote that _Iracema_ "is a jaundiced view of the great Brazilian dream as represented by the trans-Amazon highway and the journey to the junk heap by its title character."[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-auto-6)

In 2015, _Iracema_ was named as the 21st best Brazilian film of all time on the [Abraccine Top 100 Brazilian films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraccine_Top_100_Brazilian_films "Abraccine Top 100 Brazilian films") list.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iracema:_Uma_Transa_Amaz%C3%B4nica#cite_note-13)
