# Khavn

[Patrick F. Campos](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/author/patrick-f-campos/ "Posts by Patrick F. Campos")

July 2026

[Great Directors](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/category/great-directors/ "View all posts in Great Directors")

[Issue 118](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/issues/issue-118)

### **b. 23 December 1973, Quezon City, Philippines**

## **This Is Not an Essay about Khavn**

### **I.**

In the summer of 2008, at the artist-run Mag:net Café in Quezon City, a band called The Brockas set up in front of a screen and played loud. Behind them ran _M_ (Fritz Lang, 1931), set in the underworld of Weimar Berlin. The band’s three principals are filmmakers. Lav Diaz, who had directed low-budget industry pictures in the 1990s, was by then exploring long-duration cinema. Roxlee, the eldest of the three, had been making animation by hand since the early 1980s. Khavn, whose first feature-length film of 1999 was nearing its tenth anniversary, was already, possibly, the most prolific filmmaker in the country. Between the three of them stood the past and future of Philippine alternative film practice. All were steeped in cultural history and nourished by Filipino folk and popular traditions: Diaz regards these with a Dostoevskian sense of gloom, Roxlee with the satiric humour of Saul Steinberg, and Khavn with the unironic reverence of a Dadaist. The three keep to varied temporalities too. Diaz waits, holding a take until duration becomes the subject. Roxlee draws frame after sly, unruly frame. Khavn finishes before the impulse cools, treating speed itself as discipline.

The band’s name pluralises the sturdy, guttural surname of Lino Brocka, all hard consonants and street-corner force. Brocka (1939–1991), director of _Maynila sa mga Kuko ng Liwanag_ (_Manila in the Claws of Light_, 1975) and _Insiang_ (1976), the first Filipino film shown at Cannes, remains the canonical social realist of the Martial Law years, the director who hammered out the tragedies of the urban poor onscreen. The Brockas’ sound is raw, improvised, fractured, holding a different time from the projected image. The allusive name also sketches the three careers behind it: rooted but restless artists alert to the political charge of making work in the Philippines, anarchic in temper, and independent in their stance toward the industry. Khavn is arguably the most punk of the three, not least because when he is not making noise and assaulting the senses, he plays classical piano and turns earnest lovesick poems into hooky pop-romance videos, epitomised by his early work, _Paalam aking bulalakaw_ (_Goodbye My Shooting Star_, 2006).

The set that night moved from Lang to Khavn’s works in progress. After _M_, they played to a short film that staged the many ways one might kill the revered martyr, José Rizal. Then came _Iskwaterpangk_ (_Squatterpunk_, 2007), which moves through the garbage dumps of Manila where children cavort and careen in the waste, the band supplying its overdriven, clattering sonic energy in real time.The camera keeps moving, and the music keeps going, so that nothing holds still long enough to ask for the pity the image might otherwise draw.[1](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-1) The refusal of pity is a formal decision, a constant posture in Khavn’s work.Philippine cinema had settled into two broad ways of filming poverty.[2](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-2) The social-realist way, which Brocka spent a career refining, treats the slum as a matter of political urgency and holds a measured distance from the bodies on screen. The other way turns poverty into texture for export. _Squatterpunk_ sits in both, or in neither, and the discomfort of that position is the work’s charge.[3](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-3) Whether a formal refusal can be an ethical guarantee is a question the film declines to answer.

[![Image 53](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Brockas-MOV-2008-Ledh.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Brockas-MOV-2008-Ledh.jpg)
The Brockas with Kidlat Tahimik ca. 2008. Courtesy of Khavn.

[![Image 54](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rotterdam.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rotterdam.jpg)
Khavn playing to his film in Rotterdam, 2016. Courtesy of Khavn.

The evening pictures a conjuncture in film history. A movement was cohering around digital, its premise half-stated in the wordplay of Khavn’s production company, Filmless Films. The excitement around this movement sat near its peak that year. Within a few years, so-called independent cinema would harden into a category and a brand, and the sense that anything might happen would narrow. Khavn would mark the moment in the documentary and edited book he titled _Philippine New Wave: This Is Not a Film Movement_ (2010), the subtitle winking at _Movement_, the magazine Nick Deocampo had edited a generation earlier.[4](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-4) The wink concedes what the denial pretends to refuse. Something was moving, alright. In 2008, tomorrow was still wide open.

The most-reproduced sentence in Khavn’s cinema is a negation. Hundreds of his films open with the credit _this is not a film by Khavn_. At the close, where old Filipino movies were punctuated with _wakas_, or ‘finished’, he opts for the abrasive _wazak_, an anagrammatic pun of _wasak_, which in Tagalog means shattered and, in the street, wasted, as in drink- or drug-addled. The substitution runs across his body of work, a catchphrase and a slogan and a mantra he is wont to utter.The opening credit refuses the signature. The closing credit refuses the finished form. Between the two negations sits everything Khavn has made. This essay tries to say what that everything amounts to, and to argue that the double negation is one form a cinema takes when its industry has gone moribund and the only terms on offer for survival are a neoliberal demand for ceaseless commodity and sameness.

### **II.**

The Philippine film industry ran on celluloid for most of the 20th century, and by the late 1990s it was in spiralling decline. The industrial structure that had anchored production since the 1930s had contracted, and the exhibition infrastructure that made a mass cinema possible had fragmented. The conditions inhabited by Brocka and his equally influential and friendly rival Ishmael Bernal (1938–1996) – director of the canonical _Manila by Night_ (1980) and _Himala_ (_Miracle_, 1982) – no longer existed. Into that vacancy came the mini-DV camera in the early 2000s, arriving without institutional planning and ahead of the institutions that would later organise around it. Cinemalaya, the festival that inaugurated a digital indie ecosystem from 2005 onward and that came to valorise and emblematise the new cinema, arrived after the fact. Khavn had been around since 1994, in the peripheries, tinkering at home with a Video-8, his practice preceding and exceeding DV.

His first feature, _Kamias: Alaala ng Paglimot_ (1999), a nonsequitur interweaving of home movies with family and friends, opens before dawn with the slaughter of a pig, shot lo-fi against every rule of proper filmmaking. The title translates as _Memory of Forgetting_, a productive contradiction that doubles as Khavn’s _modus vivendi_, since you cannot remember the act of forgetting without that act having failed. He gave the film an alternate title, _Sisipo_, after Camus’ Sisyphus, suggesting he had an inkling of the repetition he was to commence. Kamias was his home, his territorial base between 2002 and 2011, where he ran the artist space Oracafe, organised the multisite .MOV Film, Music and Literature Festival, and established Avant-Pop, instigating the diverse art he digs. By 2008, Olaf Möller was describing him as “one of underground digital cinema’s best-kept secrets,” and Jonathan Beller baptised him “the father of digital cinema in the Philippines.”[5](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-5)

The credit “not a film by Khavn” first appeared in the year of Cinemalaya’s founding, introducing _Ang pamilyang kumakain ng lupa_ (_The Family That Eats Soil_, 2005), an early provocation that pushed abject imagery toward grotesque excess. Violent claymation opens the film, which then follows the topsy-turvy lives of a family that ate soil instead of food, their meals intercut with excrement, bodily fluids, and perverse behaviour.The “not” refers to the medium that gave Khavn his material truth. Digital production costs a tiny fraction of celluloid; calling something “not a film” in the traditional sense gave the truest account of what was being made.

Khavn counts Kidlat Tahimik his forebear. Kidlat made the iconic _Mababangong Bangungot_ (_Perfumed Nightmare_, 1977) with a handheld Bolex, expired celluloid and years of patience, and he works by holding onto inherited categories and turning their charge inside out, keeping “Third World” as a badge against its own démodé hierarchy and titling his decades-long Magellan saga _Memories of Overdevelopment_(1984), loading the word “development” until it means damage.[6](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-6) Khavn’s gesture is the determinate negation, the “not” that cancels the category and builds inside the clearing. Kidlat’s defiance of the system is to take all the time to finish a work. Khavn’s is to finish _ahora mismo_. The contrast surfaces in their respective nightmares. Where Kidlat’s is scented with cosmopolitan wonder, the romance of travel, and anticolonial heroism, Khavn’s _Bangungot na Bangag_ (_Overdosed Nightmare_, 2008) lets the perfume curdle into a drugged and rancid film centred on an unsympathetic, repellent antihero, an antithetical allusion to Kidlat lodged in Manila’s yet-unresolved present.

[![Image 55](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Main-Image-Alipato-Slaughterhouse-5000.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Main-Image-Alipato-Slaughterhouse-5000.jpg)
Behind the scenes of _Alipato._ Courtesy of Khavn.

The credit line “This is not a film by Khavn” is not a renunciation of authorship but a shift toward collaboration, formation, practice, with each film becoming, formally, a first film. The oeuvre accumulates without becoming a monument, because the credit that would consolidate it into one is withheld. Every film keeps the door open.The late-1990s collapse of Philippine celluloid production wiped out the funding infrastructure and the archival one with it. A cinema working in the wake of these losses finds in the digital medium not simply a liberation but a particular accountability to a history that industrial and institutional preservation has failed. To produce under those conditions, and to mark the production “not a film,” is to refuse the terms under which films were once made and then lost.

The early Philippine digital generation’s anti-institutional energy was, in time, converted into festival-brand logic, a vaunted category that rewarded a recognisable type.[7](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-7) Khavn’s response, with a film like _The Family That Eats Soil_, which dared one to watch images middle-brow spectators would find revolting, was to embed the refusal in the credit line, so that every screening re-performs the institutional challenge as a condition of projection. The “not” is not a position taken outside institutions. It renews itself inside them.Well before the institutional infrastructure assembled around it, the practice held its own integrity. Alexis Tioseco’s conversation with Khavn draws out his sense of the oeuvre as accumulation without goal, of the work as one continuous act, of each film as a tangible version of the same restlessness.[8](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-8)

### **III.**

Film manifestos share a structure once you recognise it. A _we_ constituted against an opponent, a _now_ that doubles as a deadline, the vow that takes the form of sacred law into profane hands. Above all, the announcement of death. You declare the existing cinema finished to clear room for the one that will take its place. The Oberhausen Manifesto of 1962, signed by 26 filmmakers including Alexander Kluge, pronounced German cinema dead and claimed the right to make it new.[9](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-9) Glauber Rocha placed hunger at the centre of a continent’s aesthetics. Jonas Mekas wrote open letters to a cinema that refused to exist. The form recurs across decades and geographies. Something has died, we are the survivors, here are the commandments of the new dispensation.The form is local too. Roxlee, Khavn’s forebear alongside Kidlat, had also set down his commandments for indie filmmakers.[10](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-10) They prize originality and poverty of means, urge filmmakers to lend their tools to anyone who needs them and abandon the star system, and counsel patience with the small audience, which may turn out to be the new future.

[![Image 56](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Khavn-Kluge-Munich-2024-Villamor.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Khavn-Kluge-Munich-2024-Villamor.jpg)
Kluge and Khavn, Munich, 2024. Courtesy of Khavn.

Khavn has been writing in the same form since at least 2000 and knows its conventions well enough to work them from inside.[11](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-11) The first of his manifestos, the _12 Bowowows of Impurity_, signed on behalf of DOGMAN2000, opens with an epigraph: “God spelled backwards is dog, & vice versa.” The line works as an irreverent joke in a Catholic country and, beneath the joke, as a theorem about negation, the claim that apparent inversion is the same object read from the other end, that the impure and the pure, the filmless and the filmed, share a single structure distinguished only by the direction it is read from. The _Bowowows_ is a pledge of impurity against the declared purity rites of Dogme 95. Lars von Trier and Thomas Vinterberg had issued their Vow of Chastity years earlier, sealing a room around a closed list of renunciations, no music outside the diegesis, no genre, no superficial action, no temporal alienation, and signing it against a European art cinema they found domesticated. The _Bowowows_ takes the Vow and punctures it with filth.

Where the _Bowowows_ pledges, the _Digital Dekalogo: A Manifesto for a Filmless Philippines_ legislates. Written in 2003, it opens with the death announcement and attaches an instruction for the funeral. “Film is dead. Please omit flowers.” The instruction to skip the flowers turns solemnity to impatience before the sentence is done. The ten commandments that follow keep the form’s gravity and undercut it at every turn. Shoot whenever you can and never delay. If a film can be finished in a day, finish it in a day. Make first and judge later, mind the quantity and leave the quality to God, if you believe in God. The filmmaker it addresses has run out of excuses, the one for whom digital was not an aesthetic preference, only the material fact left once celluloid had been priced out of existence.

The _Day-Old Flick Manifesto_ of 2012 codified a practice underway. A film shot and delivered between one sunset and the next, the deadline built into the form. Prolificacy as method. The day-old flick is perishable on purpose. _Bahag Kings_ (2006) had already given the practice one early comic iteration. Seven artist-friends, on half quest and half dare, don homespun _bahag_ (or g-strings, associated with indigenous regalia) and move through the city, into malls, streets, and establishments. The randomness becomes premise. The costume becomes event. Antics become structure. The unplanned climax arrives when public decorum retorts: they are brought to the police station and castigated. The day then closes, as days do, with a meal and laughter and conversation about what happened. The day-old process would later make a rule of this: start before the spark fades, let the world interrupt, call the interruption form. By now Khavn’s films run into the several hundreds, the count itself unsettled, his own estimates passing “300-plus” titles with no figure final.[12](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-12) The slippage is deliberate. A practice that produces faster than anyone can tally, it answers the era’s demand for commodity while withholding the sameness that demand extracts, and leaves no settled sum for an institution to file.

Then 2023. In the _bawal_ manifesto, _bawal_ being Tagalog for “forbidden,” he yells before a congress of alternative filmmakers, runs through a list of corporations. Vivamax, ABS-CBN, GMA, Regal Films, Netflix, Amazon, HBO. Then the bodies that had hardened indie into a brand. Cinemalaya, QCinema, FDCP, NCCA. It forbids working for any of them. It forbids the use of CG and AI. It forbids shooting for less than a single frame and for longer than a lifetime. Each prohibition cancels the one before it, every rule undoing the next, since the use of _bawal_ is itself ironic. Then the close. _Bawal ang bawal. Bawal magbawal. Bawal maniwala sa mga manifesto._ Forbidden to forbid. Forbidden to make a forbidding. Forbidden to believe in manifestos.

The genre dismantles its authority in the same breath that authority lets it speak. The rule against rules is still a rule. It has also stopped being one. Every manifesto tradition supplies the death announcement; Khavn’s adds the recursion, the document folded back on itself until the authority it needed to speak is the authority its speaking takes apart. The structure repeats the credit lines. The opening negation refuses authorship. The closing negation refuses law. Neither lets the thing close.

### **IV.**

Kluge came to the same tradition from its opposite end. The Oberhausen signatory who in 1962 pronounced German cinema dead reached his negation through the institutions of European modernism, where Khavn reached his through their absence. The two first met in _Happy Lamento_ (2018), Kluge’s return to cinema after more than two decades, assembled in cooperation with Khavn from footage of _Alipato: The Very Brief Life of an Ember_ (2016) and built around electric light, the circus, the song ‘Blue Moon’, and the street wars of children’s gangs in Manila. Kluge supplied the essayistic apparatus of interviews, quotations, and historical fragments. Khavn supplied the bodies.

_Orphea_ (Kluge and Khavn, 2020), shown at the Berlinale two years later, made the collaboration its subject. The pair inverts the Orpheus myth. Orpheus becomes Orphea, Eurydice becomes Euridiko, and the descent into the underworld becomes a wager to recover all the dead rather than only one beloved. The underworld is Manila, and also Silicon Valley’s afterlife research, Russian immortality projects, European migration, Purcell, Tchaikovsky, Adorno, Rilke. The film does not reconcile these. Kluge’s montage works by intellectual adjacency, an idea set beside an image so that the interval between them does the thinking. Khavn’s works by overload, movement and screams and cheap instruments and slum texture pushed past the point where adjacency can be read. In _Orphea_ the two methods run at once without dissolving into each other, and the want of synthesis is the most exact account of what the collaboration is.The disordering runs both ways. The collaboration risks casting Manila as the wildness beyond European order, a familiar avant-garde appetite for non-Western excess. Khavn brought an already-made cinema of the city, _Squatterpunk_ and _The Family That Eats Soil_ and the slum-grotesque behind them; that cinema disorders Kluge’s European archive as much as the archive frames it. Manila stops being the underside of the myth and becomes the place where the myth is tested against poverty, spectacle, and noise.

[![Image 57](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Orphea.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Orphea.jpg)
Lilith Stangenberg on the set of _Orphea._ Courtesy of Khavn.

The film after _Orphea_ was made from its leavings. _Love Is a Dog from Hell_ (2022) keeps the same two leads, the same reversed myth, the same Manila, much of the same material, and turns it into a different object, unitary where _Orphea_ was dispersed, driven by Orphea’s grief through an underworld rendered as a single descent. A rock opera for one voice. Khavn took the shared myth-machine and re-noised it alone. The post-Kluge move begins not as a departure from Kluge but as a counter-edit of him.The wound moved from the street and burrowed into the sediment of history.Before Kluge, the negation came from the punk score and the child gang and the body in extremis. After Kluge, the archive moved closer to the centre. The films turn toward lost cinema, dead masters, national memory, and they handle these the way the early work handled the slum, as material to be wrecked and made to sing.

_Nitrate: To the Ghosts of the 75 Lost Philippine Silent Films (1912–1933)_ (2023) mourns lost silent films and reconstructs from found footage and surrogates what that vanished cinema might have been. The archive cannot be recovered, so it is fabricated._National Anarchist: Lino Brocka_ (2023) does the same to a person. Brocka survives in damaged transfers, old VHS textures, and circulating fragments, and Khavn drives these remains through editing and grading until they howl. The result is a séance rather than a tribute, the social-realist returning in intertitles, speaking through the wreck of his own imperfect preservation. _Rizal’s Makamisa: Phantasm of Revenge_ (2024) goes furthest. Rizal, the polymath executed by Spain in 1896 and installed as the national hero, left a third novel unfinished. Khavn shot an adaptation on expired stock, hand-painted and scratched the frames, and presented the result as a silent film recovered from the 1920s, the work of an invented Filipino surrealist, supposedly unearthed at a Swiss cinémathèque and slipped into the historical record as though it had always belonged there. The provenance is Khavn’s fiction, written and published as his account of the film.[13](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-13) You do not inherit the archive. You forge it, and you sign the forgery with another man’s name._Bomba Bernal_ (2025) assembles found footage from Philippine _bomba_, the sexploitation cinema that flared in the 1970s, and runs it under an AI-cloned voice of Bernal reading Bernal’s writings. Bernal, dead since 1996, was a critic as well as a director, and the technology returns his voice to his prose. Two years earlier the _bawal_ manifesto had forbidden the use of AI. The film arrives in breach of it, and the only cover available is the manifesto’s final clause, the one that forbids belief in manifestos.

Across these films the negation keeps its shape and changes its object. The early work broke cinema by refusing its proper form. The late work breaks cinema by refusing its proper dead, declining to let the silent films stay lost, Brocka stay monumental, Bernal stay archived, Rizal stay literary, Orpheus stay male, or the canon stay sacrosanct. _Wazak_ was a wrecking aimed at the present. Aimed instead at the archive, it becomes a way of keeping the buried in circulation, scratched and singing and unwilling to stay where history filed them.

### **V.**

_Wakas_, like ‘fin’ or ‘the end’, is archaic. Old films flashed it at the close as a printed seal, the curtain drawn, order restored. By driving _wasak_ into the body of that word, Khavn turns the end into breakage. _Wazak_ gathers _wasak_’s range: the broken object and the ruined surface; the mind blown by excess; the state of being wasted on noise, speed, or sensation. The word is also praise and riff at once — cool as a cracked mirror, a bad trip made luminous, the aesthetic of deliberate damage naming itself. Politically, it abrades the polished surfaces of national cinema, of taste, of cultural respectability, working as a word that refuses to land immaculately rather than a position argued.Two negations bracket every film. “This is not a film by Khavn” at the head. _Wazak_ at the foot. Between them the film stays unfinished by design, never settling into the object that a signature and an ending would consolidate.

[![Image 58](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Ruined-Heart-scaled.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Ruined-Heart-scaled.jpg)
Khavn and Christopher Doyle behind the scenes of _Ruined Heart._ Courtesy of Khavn.

_Kristo_ (2004) opened one such long-running cycle, a one-armed, one-legged bet-taker at the centre of the _sabungan_ (or the cockpit), the sacred term bleeding into the gambler’s trade. _The Middle Mystery of Kristo Negro_ (2009) carried the figure into Ilocano country as a Black Christ hauling a dead goat where the cross should be. _Misericordia: The Last Mystery of Kristo Vampiro_ (2013) turned the cycle into travel-shockumentary, its guide a vampire Christ moving through _pabasa_(chanted passion), self-flagellation and, again, the cockpit. The Mondo tradition of Gualtiero Jacopetti had filmed the non-Western world as spectacle for the metropolitan eye. Khavn shoots the same penitential body from inside the devotion, so that the question of who looks, and from where, becomes a misprision of the rite rather than a frame around it.

Return rarely clarifies an origin in Khavn. The Catholic apocalypse appears first as _Three Days of Darkness Zero_(2007), half an hour of radio play in the dark, then returns restaged as _Three Days of Darkness_ (2008), the “Zero” standing for a beginning that declines to be first. _Mondomanila: How I Fixed My Hair After a Rather Long Journey_ (2012) is a Manila film avowedly made after Brocka’s and Bernal’s own hearts, but as a slum opera. _Alipato_ returned to that world as its official anti-sequel, the same streets after fire and revenge had moved through them, before becoming Kluge’s material. When Tadanobu Asano arrived to shoot _Pusong Wazak_(_Ruined Heart: Another Lovestory Between a Criminal and a Whore_, 2014), aware of _Mondomanila_, Khavn pivoted to something the first film had not been. The series moves forward by withholding the film it promises.

Brocka returns often. He stands behind the slum of _Squatterpunk_ as precedent, then his iconic tragic hero Julio Madiaga fuses with his despicable villain Kumander Kontra as the damned Kontra Madiaga in _Maynila sa mga pangil ng dilim_ (_Manila in the Fangs of Darkness_, 2008). _Mondomanila_ had taken the measure of Brocka and Bernal. The thread gathers, finally, into _National Anarchist_, where the dead director becomes the whole occasion instead of its allusion. Like always, Khavn claims the inheritance as disturbance, never as imitation.Rizal recurs too. _Ultimo: Different Ways of Killing a National Hero_ (2008) staged the hero’s last hours, spliced with travel footage, at once killing him endlessly and denying him death. Years later, _Rizal’s Makamisa_ manufactured its own fictive archive. The hero is killed, then made apocryphal. Each return of a hundred others carries its mark, scratched or overdosed or redubbed or sung, and the cycle declines to resolve.

[![Image 59](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Main-Image-Makamisa-Albert-Banzon-scaled.jpeg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Main-Image-Makamisa-Albert-Banzon-scaled.jpeg)
Khavn posing with the cast of _Makamisa,_ including Lav Diaz, Roxlee, John Lloyd Cruz, Bituin Escalante, and Lilith Stangenberg. Courtesy of Khavn.

Sound in Khavn works against the image as often as with it. _Desaparadiso_ (2015) carries its history of the forcibly disappeared in song, speech withdrawn because political disappearance is itself a crisis of speech. _Ruined Heart_ strips out verbal exposition and rebuilds from music, gesture, and colour. _EDSA XXX: Nothing Ever Changes in the Ever-Changing Republic_ (2014) sings the republic’s stalled history. _Simulacrum Tremendum_ (2016) makes the arrangement total. A diary of over 22 years of footage, gathered from Video-8 through mini-DV to iPhone, family and friends and the late-nineties Manila underground threaded with 36 of his poems, ran close to 13 hours while Khavn played live piano nonstop, fruit and eggs stacked on the lid, stealing bites with one hand as the other played on. Author, performer, and instrument occupy one body, and the film survives as the record of a single endurance performance. A practice organised this way cannot arrive anywhere. A catalogue made entirely of returns has no last film, only a most recent one.

### **VI.**

_Squatterpunk_ planted a refusal of pity as a formal decision. That formal decision travels through the same festival infrastructure that converts the aesthetics of refusal into currency. _Ruined Heart_ entered that infrastructure, Christopher Doyle behind the camera and Tadanobu Asano in front of it, set about ruining the smoothness it was being asked to supply. William Brown reads the films’ chaotic surfaces as chaosmopolitanism, the refusal of cosmopolitan art cinema’s remorseless consumption of Manila’s poverty.[14](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-14) The child with the mohawk in the garbage remains available as image for that consumption even as the work around it withholds the sentiment consumption needs. The instability is the practice’s condition of possibility as much as its cost, and Khavn has shown no appetite for settling it.

The question shifts register in the properly scripted _Balangiga: Howling Wilderness_ (2017). The massacre of 1901, when a local attack on American troops was followed by a brutal U.S. retaliatory campaign, reaches the screen through a boy, an old man, a toddler, and a carabao, in whose hollowed body a boy finds a hiding place. José B. Capino reads the film as hallucinatory history, trauma from the colonial past that is neither archived nor resolved but held open as sensory landscape.[15](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2026/great-directors/khavn/#fn-52217-15) The history in the title howls; atrocity filmed this way evades the museum. Whether evasion is an ethical guarantee the film does not decide.

[![Image 60](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Balangiga-03.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Balangiga-03.jpg)
_Balangiga_

[![Image 61](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Squatterpunk-lead.jpg)](http://www.sensesofcinema.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Squatterpunk-lead.jpg)
Behind the scenes of _Squatterpunk._ Courtesy of Khavn.

The _Digital Dekalogo_ was written against scarcity. Celluloid cost a thousand times digital. _Bomba Bernal_ meets the opposite condition. Generative tools make production frictionless, and friction was what the early ethic took as given, the resistance the world puts up against anyone making something from almost nothing. Whether anchoring the AI-cloned voice in Bernal’s writing and the degraded _bomba_ archive resists the manufacture of synthetic cultural authority, or joins it with slop under cover of irony, the film declines to settle. The no-excuses ethos was forged from scarcity. Abundance dissolves the scarcity, and with it the ground the ethic stood on.

More than three hundred films. The day-old flick. The quality left to God. A body of work at that scale accumulates into a monument through sheer mass, even as each withheld credit line refuses the monumental gesture. The practice that holds double negation open risks, by its volume, closing what it was built to keep open. The _bawal_ manifesto includes a prohibition on taking orders from the auteur, _bawal magpautos sa auteur_, a clause that turns the demand back on Khavn’s accumulated authority along with everyone else’s. Another clause forbids buying your own bullshit, _bawal bilhin ang sariling bullshit_.

The consequence is practical as well as conceptual. A filmography past three hundred and still growing offers no stable vantage from which a curator can claim a representative view. Every retrospective approximates; every account, this one included, works from a selection the body of work was designed to exceed. _EDSA XXX_ imagined the republic in 2030, thirty years past the uprising that ended the Marcos dictatorship, and found it unchanged—every rising absorbed back into the order it had set out to dismantle. The practice runs the other way, moving faster than any single frame can contain so as to refuse the sameness the system rewards. The fact it has succeeded produces its own enclosure. The filmmaker who made himself uncountable has made himself impossible to see whole, and whether that impossibility sustains the same productive suspension the two negations were built to maintain, or whether it is the monument’s inverse, a mass too large to apprehend, inaccessibility produced by surplus where the monument produces it by enclosure, the work leaves open.

In 2008, at Mag:net, the sense that digital cinema could still become anything had not yet narrowed into a category and a brand. Khavn refused the plateau that category would offer, staying unfinished by design. Today, somewhere a film of his is ending. The screen reads _wazak_. He is working with someone on seven more.
