**IMDb**, formerly known as the **Internet Movie Database**, is an [online database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_database "Online database") of information related to films, television series, podcasts, video games, and streaming content online – including cast, production crew and biographies, plot summaries, trivia, ratings, and fan and critical reviews. As of September 2025, IMDb ranks as the 40th most visited website in the world and the 35th in the United States.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-3)

The database contained some 25.9 million titles, and 14.8 million person records as of September 2025.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-stats_2022.12-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-5)

## Features

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Features")]

User profile pages show a user's registration date and, optionally, their personal ratings of titles. Since 2015, "badges" can be added showing a count of contributions. These badges range from total contributions made to independent categories such as photos, trivia, and biographies. If a registered user or visitor is in the entertainment industry and has an IMDb page, they can add photos through IMDbPRO.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-6)

#### Rankings

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Rankings")]

The IMDb Top 250 is a [list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List "List") of the top-rated 250 films, based on ratings by registered users of the website using the methods described. As of June 23,2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit), _[The Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Shawshank\_Redemption "The Shawshank Redemption")_ (1994), directed by [Frank Darabont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Darabont "Frank Darabont"), is the highest-ranked film on the list, and has been since 2008.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-AVFilm14-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-vanityfair-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-9) The "Top 250" rating is based on only the ratings of "regular voters". The number of votes a registered user would have to make to be considered as a user who votes regularly has been kept secret. IMDb has stated that to maintain the effectiveness of the Top 250 list they "_deliberately do not disclose the criteria used for a person to be counted as a regular voter_".[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-10) In addition to other weightings, the Top 250 films are also based on a weighted rating formula referred to in [actuarial science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuarial_science "Actuarial science") as a _credibility formula_.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-11) This label arises because a statistic is taken to be more credible the greater the number of individual pieces of information; in this case from eligible users who submit ratings. Although the current formula is not disclosed, IMDb originally used the following formula to calculate their weighted rating:[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-12)

The IMDb also has a Bottom 100 feature which is assembled through a similar process although only 10,000 votes must be received to qualify for the list.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-13)

The Top 250 list comprises a wide range of feature films, including major releases, cult films, independent films, critically acclaimed films, silent films, and non-English-language films. Documentaries, short films and TV episodes are not currently included.

Since 2015, there has been a Top 250 list devoted to ranking television shows.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-14)

### Reference view

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Reference view")]

IMDb originally used a more sidebar/list-based view on title pages. However, in 2010 the site updated pages to more free-flowing layouts, and offered logged-in users an "advanced view" site preference setting called "Combined view", or this could be done on an ad-hoc basis by simply adding `/combined` to the end of the [URL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/URL "URL") (e.g. https://www.imdb.com/title/tt2358891/combined), for users to choose should they prefer the older page view display method or to aid the editing of data.

In 2017, some alterations were made to this advanced view, and the setting was renamed "Reference view", again also able to be accessed ad-hoc by simply adding `/reference` to the end of the URL (e.g. https://www.imdb.com/title/tt2358891/reference), with the former `/combined` URLs made to link to the newer `/reference` ones.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_ref_view_1-15)

### Message boards

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Message boards")]

By February 20, 2017, all the message boards and their content were permanently removed. According to the website, the decision was made because the boards were "no longer providing a positive, useful experience for the vast majority of our more than 250 million monthly users worldwide".[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-16) Others have mentioned its susceptibility to [trolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troll_(slang) "Troll (slang)") and disagreeable behavior.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-17)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-19) Needham also mentioned in a post some months earlier that the boards received less income from ads, and that their members only made up a very small part of the website's visitors. The boards were costly to run due to the system's age and dated design, which did not make business sense.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-20) The decision to remove the message boards was met with outspoken backlash from some of its users, and sparked an online petition garnering over 8,000 signatures.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-21) In the days leading up to February 20, 2017, both Archive.org[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-22) and MovieChat.org[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-23) preserved the entire contents of the IMDb message boards using [web scraping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_scraping "Web scraping"). Archive.org and MovieChat.org have published IMDb message board archives.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-24)

### IMDbPro

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: IMDbPro")]

Actors, crew, and industry executives can post their own resume and upload photos of themselves for a yearly membership fee to IMDbPro. IMDbPro can be accessed by anyone willing to pay the annual fee of US$149.99. Membership enables a user to access the rank order of each industry personality, as well as agent contact information for any actor, producer, director etc. that has an IMDb page. IMDbPro also allows existing actors to claim their name page, as well as production companies to claim titles they own/manage. Enrolling in IMDbPro enables members who are industry personnel to upload a head shot to open their page, as well as to upload hundreds of photos to accompany their page. Anyone can register as an IMDb user and contribute to the site as well as view its content; however, those users enrolled in IMDbPro have greater access and privileges.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-25)

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: History")]

IMDb began as a fan-operated movie database on the [Usenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usenet "Usenet") group "rec.arts.movies" in 1990, and moved to the World Wide Web (www. system) in 1993.

### Pre-website

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Pre-website")]

IMDb originated from a personal film database that English film enthusiast [Col Needham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_Needham "Col Needham") had maintained as a hobby since 1987.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-ebsco-26) Needham, who worked as an engineer at [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard "Hewlett-Packard") in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol"), had been recording details of every film he watched since his teenage years.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-latimes-27)

In the late 1980s, Needham joined the [Usenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usenet "Usenet") newsgroup [rec.arts.movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rec.arts.movies?action=edit&redlink=1 "Rec.arts.movies (page does not exist)"), where film enthusiasts exchanged information about movies. He made a posting entitled "Those Eyes" about actresses with beautiful eyes, which prompted other users to respond with additions and their own lists.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-time-28) Needham subsequently started an "Actors List", while Dave Knight began a "Directors List", and Andy Krieg took over "THE LIST" (which would later be renamed the "Actress List") from Hank Driskill.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-techcrunch-29) Both the actors and actresses lists were initially restricted to people who were alive and working, but as the project grew, retired performers were added, leading Needham to create a separate "Dead Actors/Actresses List".[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-historyofdomains-30) Steve Hammond later started collecting and merging character names for both the actors and actresses lists; when these achieved popularity, they were merged back into the main lists.

By late 1990, the lists included almost 10,000 films and television series, correlated with actors and actresses appearing therein. On October 17, 1990, Needham developed and posted a collection of [Unix shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix_shell "Unix shell") scripts to the newsgroup that could be used to search the four lists, and thus the database that would become IMDb was born.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-techcrunch-29) At the time, it was known as the "rec.arts.movies movie database".

By 1992, the database had been expanded to include additional categories of filmmakers and other demographic material, as well as trivia, biographies, and plot summaries. The movie ratings had been properly integrated with the list data, and a centralized email interface for querying the database had been created by Alan Jay.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-historyofdomains-30) The database continued to be maintained by volunteer "section managers" who received contributions from users around the world.

### On the Web

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: On the Web")]

The database had been expanded to include additional categories of filmmakers and other demographic material as well as trivia, biographies, and plot summaries. The movie ratings had been properly integrated with the list data, and a centralized email interface for querying the database had been created by Alan Jay. Later, on August 5, 1993,[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-31) it moved onto the fledgling [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web") under the name of _Cardiff Internet Movie Database_.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-32) The database resided on the servers of the computer science department of [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University "Cardiff University") in Wales. [Rob Hartill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Hartill "Rob Hartill") was the original web interface author. In 1994, the email interface was revised to accept the submission of all information, which enabled people to email the specific list maintainer with their updates. However, the structure remained so that information received on a single film was divided among multiple section managers, the sections being defined and determined by categories of film personnel and the individual filmographies contained therein. Over the next few years, the database was run on a network of [mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_mirror "Web mirror") across the world with donated bandwidth.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-33)

### As an independent company

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: As an independent company")]

In 1996, IMDb was incorporated in the United Kingdom, becoming the Internet Movie Database Ltd. Founder Col Needham became the primary owner. General revenue for site operations was generated through advertising, licensing and partnerships.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_inc._info_1-34)

### As Amazon.com subsidiary (1998–present)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: As Amazon.com subsidiary (1998–present)")]

In April 1998, [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos "Jeff Bezos"), founder, owner, and [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer "Chief executive officer") of Amazon.com, struck a deal with Needham and other principal shareholders to buy IMDb outright; Amazon paid $55 million for IMDb and two other companies.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_amazon_buy_1-35) Bezos attached it to Amazon as a subsidiary, private company.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_prnw_info_1-36) This gave IMDb the ability to pay the shareholders salaries for their work.[_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please\_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_] In the process of expanding its product line, Amazon.com intended to use IMDb as an advertising resource for selling DVDs and videotapes.

From 1996 onwards, an annual newsletter email (archived on the website) has been sent from Col Needham to contributors on the first day of each calendar year. The annual newsletter lists various information about the past year on the site, including stats, top contributors tally for the year (the top 300 users, currently; fewer in previous years), and a perspective on the site's progress and future.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_newsletter_1-37)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_newsletter_2022.01.01-38)[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_newsletter_email_2022.01.01-39)

As an additional incentive for users, as of 2003[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit), users identified as one of the "top 100 contributors" of hard data received complimentary free access to IMDbPro for the following calendar year; for 2006 this was increased to the top 150 contributors, and for 2010 to the top 250.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_top_2010-40)

In 2008, IMDb launched their first official foreign-language version with IMDb.de, in German. Also in 2008, IMDb acquired two other companies: [Withoutabox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withoutabox "Withoutabox")[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_latimes_WAB_1-41) and [Box Office Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_Office_Mojo "Box Office Mojo").[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_BOM_1-42)

The website was originally [Perl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perl "Perl")-based, but IMDb [no longer discloses what software it uses for reasons of security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_through_obscurity "Security through obscurity"), apart from mentioning [The Apache Software Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Apache_Software_Foundation "The Apache Software Foundation").[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_perl_1-43) In 2010, the site was [filtered in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_China "Internet censorship in China").[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_TR_China_info_1-44)

In 2016, _The IMDb Studio at Sundance_ was launched, a talk show that is presented on _IMDb_ and [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_sundance_1-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_yt_info_1-46)

The site's message boards were disabled in February 2017. In April 2017, IMDb celebrated its 25th anniversary. As of that year, Needham was still managing IMDb from its main office in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol") in the [Castlemead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlemead "Castlemead") office tower.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-Jones-47)

In January 2019, IMDb launched an [ad-supported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad-supported "Ad-supported") streaming service called [Freedive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Freevee "Amazon Freevee").[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-48) This was the company's second attempt at a streaming service; it launched a similar service in 2008.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_cnet_2008-49)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_reg_info_1-50) In June 2019, Freedive was rebranded as IMDb TV.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_amz_trippled-51) In April 2022, the service was rebranded again as Amazon Freevee.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-imdb_freevee_1-52) On December 30, 2024, it was closed as an independent site and its content was merged into [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video").[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-53)

## Content and format

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Content and format")]

### Data provided by subjects

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Data provided by subjects")]

In 2006, IMDb introduced its "Résumé Subscription Service", where an actor or crew member can post their [résumé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9sum%C3%A9 "Résumé") and upload photos[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-54) for a yearly fee.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-55) IMDb résumé pages are kept on a sub-page of the regular entry about that person, with a regular entry automatically created for each résumé subscriber who does not already have one.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-56)

### Copyright, vandalism and error issues

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Copyright, vandalism and error issues")]

Volunteers who contribute content to the database technically retain copyright on their contributions, but the compilation of the content becomes the exclusive property of IMDb with the full right to copy, modify, and sublicense it, and they are verified before posting.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-57) However, credit is not given on specific title or filmography pages to the contributor(s) who have provided information. Conversely, a credited text entry, such as a plot summary, may be corrected for content, grammar, sentence structure, perceived omission or error, by other contributors without having to add their names as co-authors. Due to the time required for processing submitted data or text before it is displayed, IMDb is different from user-contributed projects like [Discogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discogs "Discogs"), or [OpenStreetMap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenStreetMap "OpenStreetMap"), or Wikipedia, in that contributors cannot add, delete, or modify the data or text on impulse, and the manipulation of data is controlled by IMDb technology and salaried staff.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-58)

IMDb has been subject to deliberate additions of false information; in 2012 a spokesperson said: "We make it easy for users and professionals to update much of our content, which is why we have an 'edit page'. The data that is submitted goes through a series of consistency checks before it goes live. Given the sheer volume of the information, occasional mistakes are inevitable, and, when reported, they are promptly fixed. We always welcome corrections."[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-59)

The Java Movie Database (JMDB)[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-jmdb-60) is reportedly creating an IMDb_Error.log file that lists all the errors found while processing the IMDb plain text files. A Wiki alternative to IMDb is Open Media Database[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-61) the content of which is also contributed by users but licensed under a [Creative Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Commons "Creative Commons") license (CC BY) and the [GFDL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Free_Documentation_License "GNU Free Documentation License"). Since 2007, IMDb has been experimenting with wiki-programmed sections for complete film synopses, parental guides, and FAQs about titles as determined by (and answered by) individual contributors.

### Data format and access

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Data format and access")]

IMDb, unlike other AI-automated queries, does not provide an [API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_API "Web API") for automated queries. However, most of the data can be downloaded as [compressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_compression "Data compression") plain text files and the information can be extracted using the [command-line interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface "Command-line interface") tools provided.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-62) There is also a Java-based [graphical user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_user_interface "Graphical user interface") (GUI) application available that is able to process the compressed plain text files, which allows a search and a display of the information.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-jmdb-60) This GUI application supports different languages, but the movie related data are in English, as made available by IMDb. A [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_(programming_language) "Python (programming language)") package called IMDbPY (since renamed cinemagoer) can also be used to process the compressed plain text files into a number of different [SQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL "SQL") databases, enabling easier access to the entire dataset for searching or data mining.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-63)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-64)[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-65)

## IMDb STARmeter Awards

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: IMDb STARmeter Awards")]

Annually, **IMDb STARmeter Awards** are presented to industry professionals in various categories. Professionals who have appeared in its annual "top 10 lists" are considered for this award. "IMDb determines its definitive top 10 lists using data from IMDbPro STARmeter rankings, which are based on the actual page views of the more than 200 million monthly visitors to the site."[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-67) Initially IMDb STARmeter Awards were given in two categories, **IMDb Fan favorite STARmeter Award** and **IMDb Breakout STARmeter Award**.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-68) Celebrating 20th anniversary of IMDbPro, it launched **IMDb Icon STARmeter Award**, which is given to prominent artists of the industry who have appeared in the top 10 positions throughout the year.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-:0-69)[Salma Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salma_Hayek "Salma Hayek") received the inaugural award.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-:0-69)

## Legal and policy issues

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=IMDb&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Legal and policy issues")]

In 2011, in the case of _[Hoang v. Amazon.com, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoang\_v.\_Amazon.com,\_Inc. "Hoang v. Amazon.com, Inc.")_, IMDb was sued by an anonymous actress for at least [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")1,075,000 because the movie website publicly disclosed her age (40, at the time) without her consent.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-70) The actress claimed that revealing her age could cause her to lose acting opportunities.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-71) Judge [Marsha J. Pechman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsha_J._Pechman "Marsha J. Pechman"), a US district judge in Seattle, dismissed the lawsuit, saying the actress had no grounds to proceed with an anonymous complaint. The actress re-filed and so revealed that she was Huong Hoang of Texas, who uses the stage name Junie Hoang.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-72) In 2013, Pechman dismissed all causes of action except for a [breach of contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breach_of_contract "Breach of contract") claim against IMDb; a jury then sided with IMDb on that claim.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-73) The [Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Ninth_Circuit "Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit") affirmed the district court judgment in March 2015.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-74)

Also in 2011, in the case of _United Video Properties Inc., et al. v. Amazon.Com Inc. et al._,[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-75) IMDb and Amazon were sued by [Rovi Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovi_Corporation "Rovi Corporation") and others for patent infringement over their various program listing offerings.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-76) The patent claims were ultimately construed in a way favorable to IMDb, and Rovi / United Video Properties lost the case.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-77) In April 2014, the decision was affirmed by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-78)

In January 2017, the State of California enacted state bill AB-1687, a [SAG-AFTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAG-AFTRA "SAG-AFTRA")-backed anti-ageism statute which requires "commercial online entertainment employment services" to honor requests by their subscribers for their ages and birthdays to be hidden.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-thr-imdblaw-79) By the beginning of 2017, IMDb had received more than 2,300 requests from individuals to remove their date of birth from the site. Included in this group were 10 [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") winners and another 71 nominated for Oscars, [Emmys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award"), or [Golden Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award").[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-80) On February 23, 2017, Judge [Vince Girdhari Chhabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Chhabria "Vince Chhabria") issued a stay on the bill pending a further trial, on the ground that it possibly violated the [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution") because it inhibited the public consumption of information. He also questioned the intent of the bill, as it was ostensibly meant to target IMDb.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-thr-lawstruck-81) In February 2018, Chhabria struck down the statute,[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-82) and in June 2020, the [Ninth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Circuit "Ninth Circuit") affirmed Chhabria's judgement, holding that the statute was an unconstitutional content-based restriction that violated the First Amendment.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-83)[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-84)

IMDb had long maintained that it would keep all valid information, but changed that policy related to birth names in 2019, instead removing birth names that are not widely and publicly known, of persons who no longer use their birth names.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-Variety_20190813-85) This was done in response to pressure from [LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ "LGBTQ") groups against the publication of the birth names of [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") people without their consent ([deadnaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadnaming "Deadnaming")). Any name a person had previously been credited under, however, continues to be maintained in the credits section, mentioning both the name the person uses and the name appearing in the credits.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb_(identifier)#cite_note-Variety_20190813-85)
