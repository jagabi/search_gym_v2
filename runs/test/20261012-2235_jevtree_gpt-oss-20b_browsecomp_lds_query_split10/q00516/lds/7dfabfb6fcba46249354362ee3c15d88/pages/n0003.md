**Daniel César Martín Brühl González**[[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-2)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-berliner2013-3) (German:[[ˈdaːni̯eːl ˈbʁyːl,-ni̯ɛl-]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/3b/De-Daniel_Br%C3%BChl2.ogg/De-Daniel_Br%C3%BChl2.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Daniel_Br%C3%BChl2.ogg "File:De-Daniel Brühl2.ogg"); Spanish:[[daˈnjel ˈbɾul ɡonˈθaleθ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish "Help:IPA/Spanish"); born 16 June 1978)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-dob-4) is a German and Spanish actor. He has received various accolades, including three [European Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Awards "European Film Awards") and three [German Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Film_Awards "German Film Awards"), along with nominations for two [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards") and a [BAFTA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award "BAFTA Award"). He received his first [German Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Film_Award "German Film Award") for [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_Filmpreis "Deutscher Filmpreis") for his roles in _[Das Weisse Rauschen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_White\_Sound "The White Sound")_ (2001), _Nichts Bereuen_ (2001), and _Vaya con Dios_ (2002). His starring role in the German film _[Good Bye, Lenin!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good\_Bye,\_Lenin! "Good Bye, Lenin!")_ (2003) received widespread recognition and critical acclaim, and garnered him the [European Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Awards "European Film Awards") for [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Award_for_Best_Actor "European Film Award for Best Actor") and another German Film Award for Best Actor.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:1-5)

He was introduced to mainstream international audiences through his breakthrough performance as Fredrick Zoller, a Nazi German war hero in [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino")'s _[Inglourious Basterds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inglourious\_Basterds "Inglourious Basterds")_ (2009), and appearances in films like _[The Bourne Ultimatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Bourne\_Ultimatum\_(film) "The Bourne Ultimatum (film)")_ (2007), _[The Fifth Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Fifth\_Estate\_(film) "The Fifth Estate (film)")_ (2013), and _[A Most Wanted Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A\_Most\_Wanted\_Man\_(film) "A Most Wanted Man (film)")_ (2014). Brühl received widespread critical acclaim and further recognition for his portrayal of former Formula 1 driver [Niki Lauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Lauda "Niki Lauda") in the biographical film _[Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush\_(2013\_film) "Rush (2013 film)")_ (2013), for which he earned nominations including the [Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture#2010s "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture"), the [Critic's Choice Award for Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Film_Critics_Association_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Broadcast Film Critics Association Award for Best Supporting Actor"), the [Screen Actors Guild Award for Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Supporting Role") and the [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role").

In the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe"), Brühl portrays [Helmut Zemo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Zemo_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Helmut Zemo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in _[Captain America: Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain\_America:\_Civil\_War "Captain America: Civil War")_ (2016) and the [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+ "Disney+") series _[The Falcon and the Winter Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Falcon\_and\_the\_Winter\_Soldier "The Falcon and the Winter Soldier")_ (2021). He also starred as Dr. Laszlo Kreizler in the [Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards "Primetime Emmy Awards")- and [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Miniseries_or_Television_Film "Golden Globe Award for Best Miniseries or Television Film")-nominated period drama television series _[The Alienist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Alienist\_(TV\_series) "The Alienist (TV series)")_ (2018–2020), for which he earned a nomination for the [Golden Globe Award as Best Actor in a Television Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Miniseries_or_Television_Film "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Miniseries or Television Film") at the [76th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76th_Golden_Globe_Awards "76th Golden Globe Awards") in 2018.

## Early life

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Br%C3%BChl&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Brühl was born on 16 June 1978 in [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-6) His father was the German television director and documentarian Hanno Brühl, who was born in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo "São Paulo"), Brazil.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-7)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-8) His mother is Marisa González Domingo, a Spanish teacher.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-9) Shortly after his birth, the family moved from Spain to [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne "Cologne"), Germany, where he grew up. He was raised speaking Spanish, German, Catalan, Portuguese and French.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-10)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:2-11) He attended secondary school at the [Dreikönigsgymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreik%C3%B6nigsgymnasium "Dreikönigsgymnasium"), the oldest school in Cologne.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:0-12)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:1-5)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-13)

## Career

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Br%C3%BChl&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]

### 1995–2002: Early work

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Br%C3%BChl&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 1995–2002: Early work")]

Brühl began acting at a young age despite not having any formal training as an actor. He participated in children's theater at school and first earned money at age 8 doing radio plays, followed by work in a dubbing studio leading to one of the dubbing actors to recommend the teenager to a talent agency. At age 15, Brühl landed a small part in the TV film _[Sven's Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven's\_Secret?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sven's Secret (page does not exist)")[[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svens%20Geheimnis "de:Svens Geheimnis")]_, played the street kid Benji in the soap opera _[Verbotene Liebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbotene\_Liebe "Verbotene Liebe") (Forbidden Love)_(1995), and continued to feature in television series in the following years.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-14) In 1999, he appeared in his film debut as Checo in _Paradise Mall_ (_Schlaraffenland_) and voiced Kom in the German version of _[Le château des singes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le\_Ch%C3%A2teau\_des\_singes "Le Château des singes")._ In 2000, he starred in his first main role as Markus Baasweiler in _[No More School](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schule%20(Film) "de:Schule (Film)")_ (_Schule_), and was cast as Jay in _[Deeply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deeply "Deeply")_.

In 2001, he continued to play main roles as the schizophrenic Lukas in [Hans Weingartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Weingartner "Hans Weingartner")'s critically acclaimed debut film [_Das Weisse Rauschen_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_wei%C3%9Fe_Rauschen "Das weiße Rauschen")_(The White Sound),_ as Daniel in _Nichts Bereuen (No Regrets)_, and as Marek in _Honolulu_. In 2002, he starred as Arbo in _[Vaya con Dios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaya\_con\_Dios\_(film) "Vaya con Dios (film)")_ and as boxer Marko Stemper in _Elefantenherz (Elephant Heart)_. He won the [German Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Film_Award "German Film Award") (2002) for Best Actor, the [Bavarian Film Award (2001)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Film_Awards_(Best_Acting)#Best_New_Actor "Bavarian Film Awards (Best Acting)") for Best New Actor, and the [New Faces Award (2002)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/New%20Faces%20Award "de:New Faces Award") for Best Actor for his performance in _Das Weisse Rauschen_, _Nichts Bereuen_, and _Vaya con Dios_. He won the [German Film Critics Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Film_Critics_Award "German Film Critics Award") (2003) for Best Actor for _Das Weisse Rauschen_ and _Vaya con Dios._ For _Das Weisse Rauschen_, Brühl insisted on meeting someone with paranoid schizophrenia to avoid the risk of making the character look clichéd. Two decades later, he still considers it his most difficult character to portray and comments that "It was important for me to explore my own madness, to believe in what I was doing and to convince myself that I was suffering from that disease, so it was quite difficult."[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-15) The film has been widely cited by the academic community in discussing and understanding schizophrenia due to its realistic portrayal.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-16)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-17)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-18)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-19)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:0-12)

### 2003–2008: Breakthrough and early success

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Br%C3%BChl&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 2003–2008: Breakthrough and early success")]

[![Image 5](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/11/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl_%282004%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Daniel_Br%C3%BChl_(2004).jpg)

Brühl in 2004

Brühl's international breakthrough role came in 2003 as Alex Kerner in [Wolfgang Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Becker_(director,_born_1954) "Wolfgang Becker (director, born 1954)")'s German tragicomedy _[Good Bye, Lenin!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good\_Bye,\_Lenin! "Good Bye, Lenin!")_ which tells about a German family that lived during the unification of Germany.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-20) The film became one of the most successful German films to date, receiving nominations at the [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/67th_Golden_Globe_Awards "67th Golden Globe Awards") and the [BAFTA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards") and winning at the [European Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Awards "European Film Awards"), [German Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Film_Award "German Film Award"), [Cesar Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Awards "César Awards"), and [London Film Critics' Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Film_Critics'_Circle "London Film Critics' Circle") among others. It was sold to more than 65 countries, reaching an estimated six million cinema-goers worldwide. In that year, Brühl won awards including the European Film Awards Peoples's Choice Award for Best European Actor and the [European Film Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Award_for_Best_Actor "European Film Award for Best Actor") for the role. He also won his second German Film Award as Best Actor for both his performance in the film and in _Elefantenherz_. He then voiced Kenai in the German version of [_Brother Bear_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brother_Bear_2 "Brother Bear 2").

Brühl reached further recognition in 2004 reuniting with filmmaker Hans Weingartner and starring as the anti-capitalist activist Jan in the internationally successful film [_The Edukators (Die Fetten Jahre sind vorbei)_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Edukators "The Edukators"). The film became a [cult film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_film "Cult film") as part of a "German New Wave" and received a 10-minute standing ovation at its premier at the 2004 [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival") where it was nominated for the [Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palme_d'Or "Palme d'Or"). Brühl earned a nomination for the [European Film Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Award_for_Best_Actor "European Film Award for Best Actor") for his role. At the same time, he won the European Film Awards Peoples's Choice Award for Best European Actor for his role as Paul in _[Love in Thoughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love\_in\_Thoughts "Love in Thoughts")_ of which plot is based on the Steglitz student tragedy involving two teenagers who created a suicide club.

In the same year, Brühl made his English-speaking film debut in _[Ladies in Lavender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies\_in\_Lavender "Ladies in Lavender")_, starring alongside English actresses [Judi Dench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judi_Dench "Judi Dench") and [Maggie Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Smith "Maggie Smith") as Andrea Marowski, and met [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II "Queen Elizabeth II") who attended its premiere.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:0-12) He also played Frank in _[Farland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farland\_(film)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Farland (film) (page does not exist)")[[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farland "de:Farland")]_. Brühl featured as Lieutenant Horstmayer of the German 93rd Infantry Regiment, a central character in the 2005 film _[Joyeux Noël](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyeux\_No%C3%ABl "Joyeux Noël")_, a trilingual World War I film based on the experiences of French, German and Scottish soldiers during the [Christmas truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_truce "Christmas truce") of 1914. The film shows Brühl's linguistic ability as he ably communicates in German, French and English throughout.

In 2006, he was invited to be part of the short film and Cinéfondation juries of the Cannes Film Festival. He starred as Chris in [_Cargo_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_(2006_film) "Cargo (2006 film)") and Karl in [_A Friend of Mine_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Friend_of_Mine_(2006_film) "A Friend of Mine (2006 film)"), voiced [Lightning McQueen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_McQueen "Lightning McQueen") in the German version of [_Cars_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cars_(film) "Cars (film)") and reprised his voice-over role as Kenai in the German version of _[Brother Bear 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brother\_Bear\_2 "Brother Bear 2")_. In Cannes-nominated film _[Salvador (Puig Antich)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador\_(Puig\_Antich) "Salvador (Puig Antich)"),_ he played [Salvador Puig Antich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Puig_Antich "Salvador Puig Antich"), a Spanish anarchist executed during the [Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco "Francisco Franco") era, marking his first time acting in his second language.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:0-12) In 2007, Brühl made a cameo appearance in _[2 Days in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2\_Days\_in\_Paris "2 Days in Paris")_, a romantic comedy film directed by French actress [Julie Delpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Delpy "Julie Delpy"). He appeared in a small role as Martin Kreutz in the film _[The Bourne Ultimatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Bourne\_Ultimatum\_(film) "The Bourne Ultimatum (film)")._ In 2008, he starred in the British-Russian production _[In Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In\_Transit\_(2008\_film) "In Transit (2008 film)")_, in which he played a young Nazi soldier named Klaus opposite [John Malkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Malkovich "John Malkovich"). He also played Tonda in _[Krabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Satanic\_Mill#Live-action\_movie "The Satanic Mill"),_ which was based on a popular German children's story, and Marcos in _A Bit of Chocolate_.

## Reception and acting style

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Br%C3%BChl&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Reception and acting style")]

> "If Daniel Brühl were a natural American or British talent, without a doubt he'd be a household name, based on the level of his acting ability but also on who he is, he's here for the long haul, he's highly intelligent, balanced, sensible...He's complex enough as a human being to be able to do justice to the most complex parts."

—[Peter Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Morgan "Peter Morgan") on Brühl[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-43)

The roles Brühl takes on are often morally complex characters, men who are suffering with a deep-seated darkness that threatens to weigh down their inherent humanity.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:2-11) He has utilized [method acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_acting "Method acting") since the beginning of his career.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-44) A German and Spanish national, he has played at least ten different nationalities (Polish, Spanish, French, Hungarian, German, Austrian, British, American, Argentine and a fictional Sokovian) and in his films has spoken English, Spanish, German, and French comfortably, as well as Polish, Russian, and Chinese. Writing for _[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Hollywood\_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ in 2018, Katie Kilkenny said: "If Hollywood executives have a global, and particularly European, male part to cast, there's a good chance Bruhl will make the shortlist. How did Bruhl become Hollywood's answer to a global everyman? The answer, collaborators say, lies not only in Bruhl's multilingualism, but also his choice of collaborators and Hollywood's welcoming of an influx of German talent in the last two decades."[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:1-5) In a 2017 interview with _Gentleman's Journal_, Brühl attributed his success to finding his niche as an actor.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:3-45)

On being typecast as a villain after taking on an increasing number of antagonistic roles, Brühl said that though it was a privilege to have made _Good Bye, Lenin!_, it was also a "curse" because he "was always offered the part of the sympathetic nice guy" and was refreshed and relieved when offered villain roles outside of Germany. [_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] The _[Hollywood Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood\_Insider "Hollywood Insider")_ noted his atypical career arc, "He went from consistently playing the love interest in European Cinema to playing the villain in Hollywood". Due to Brühl's performance, Baron Zemo has become one of the most iconic villains in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe"), despite having no superpowers. _[Film School Rejects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film\_School\_Rejects "Film School Rejects")_ remarks, "Even when embodying the role of a Nazi or another nefarious villain, Brühl manages to bring out the character's humanity — no matter how little of it there actually is".[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:2-11)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-46)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-47)[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:3-45)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:4-29) Brühl has also been described as having an "innate likability and charm".[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-48) The [_LA Times_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times") deemed his openness as such that "even his irony sounds straightforward". He has also been lauded for his work ethic. He befriended activist Daniel Domscheit-Berg and racing driver Niki Lauda after meeting them in preparation for his roles.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-LA-49)

Brühl has worked in both European and American film productions in several languages (English, Catalan, Spanish, German, French, Polish, and Chinese) and played at least ten different nationalities, including Polish (_[Ladies in Lavender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies\_in\_Lavender "Ladies in Lavender")_), Catalan (_[Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador\_(2006\_film) "Salvador (2006 film)")_), Spanish (_[7 Days in Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7\_Days\_in\_Havana "7 Days in Havana")_), French (_[2 Days in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2\_Days\_in\_Paris "2 Days in Paris")_), Hungarian (_[The Countess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Countess\_(film) "The Countess (film)")_), German (_Inglourious Basterds_, _[Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe\_(film) "Entebbe (film)")_, and most German productions), Austrian (_[Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush\_(2013\_film) "Rush (2013 film)")_, _[Woman in Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman\_in\_Gold\_(film) "Woman in Gold (film)")_), English (_[Burnt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnt\_(film) "Burnt (film)")_), American (_The Alienist_) and Sokovian (a fictional Eastern European country in _Captain America: Civil War_).[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-50)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:1-5)

## Public image

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Br%C3%BChl&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Public image")]

Brühl is referred to as the "golden boy of German film" and the "undisputed ruler of the European acting elite" by _The Gentleman's Journal_ on its May/June 2017 cover. He is also regarded as the most recognizable German actor to emerge since the country's reunification.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-:3-45)[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-55) He graced the _[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ "GQ")_ _Germany_ cover issue in December 2015 and was the recipient of the _GQ_ Film National Award at the 2015 [GQ Men of the Year Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ_Men_of_the_Year_Awards "GQ Men of the Year Awards").[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-56) He also headlined [_Esquire_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")_Spain_, _Esquire_ _Germany_, and [_Men's_ _Health_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_Health "Men's Health")_Best Fashion_ magazine, and featured in _Esquire_ _UK_.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-57)[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-58)[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-59)[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-60) He shot a [_Vogue_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_(magazine) "Vogue (magazine)") and _GQ_ _Germany_ advert with his wife too.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-61)

In February 2017, he was honoured by the French government at the Berlin International Film Festival and awarded the [Chevalier de l'Ordre des Arts et des Lettres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalier_de_l'ordre_des_Arts_et_des_Lettres "Chevalier de l'ordre des Arts et des Lettres") for his _'major contribution to the influence of film culture in France, Germany, Europe and the world'_, making him a Knight of the Order of Arts and Literature.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-62) He was offered membership in the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") in June 2017 to vote for the Oscars. In October 2018, Brühl accompanied Germany's Federal President [Frank-Walter Steinmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank-Walter_Steinmeier "Frank-Walter Steinmeier") and his wife [Elke Büdenbender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elke_B%C3%BCdenbender "Elke Büdenbender") during their visit to Spain.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Br%C3%BChl#cite_note-63)[_[unreliable source?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable\_sources "Wikipedia:Reliable sources")_]
