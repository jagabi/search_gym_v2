The **European Film Academy** is a non-profit association promoting the interests of the European film industry.

The European Film Academy honors films and filmmakers annually with the [European Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Awards "European Film Awards"). The ceremony takes place in various European cities every even year and in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") every odd year.

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=European_Film_Academy&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

The European Film Academy was formed by a group of European [filmmakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_director "Film director") who came together in Berlin on the occasion of the first presentation of the European Film Awards in November 1988.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-:0-2)

The European Film Academy was founded in 1989 under the name "European Cinema Society" by Swedish director [Ingmar Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingmar_Bergman "Ingmar Bergman"), along with forty other filmmakers from across Europe, including [Bernardo Bertolucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Bertolucci "Bernardo Bertolucci"), [Claude Chabrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Chabrol "Claude Chabrol"), [Dušan Makavejev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Makavejev "Dušan Makavejev"), [István Szabó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istv%C3%A1n_Szab%C3%B3 "István Szabó"), and [Wim Wenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wim_Wenders "Wim Wenders"). Bergman served as the first president, and Wenders was appointed chairman.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-EFAstory-3)

In 1990, the organization was renamed the European Film Academy and registered as a non-profit association.

In 1996, Wim Wenders succeeded Bergman as president,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-4) and [Nik Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nik_Powell "Nik Powell"), a British producer, was elected chairman.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-EFAstory-3)

Mike Downey, an Irish-British film producer, was elected chairman in 2020.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-new_president-5)

In 2021, Polish filmmaker [Agnieszka Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnieszka_Holland "Agnieszka Holland") became the first woman to serve as president of the Academy.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-new_president-5) That same year, the Academy discontinued use of the abbreviation "EFA" and began referring to itself either by its full name or simply as "the Academy." A new logo was introduced in August 2021, designed by the Polish agency Huncwot, as part of a broader update to its digital and visual identity.

In May 2024, French actress [Juliette Binoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliette_Binoche "Juliette Binoche") was elected president, succeeding Holland.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-new_president-5)

## Organization

### Board

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=European_Film_Academy&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Board")]

The board of the academy consists of members representing various parts of Europe. Board members are elected for a 2 year term, with a limit of 3 terms. Beginning in 2024, each of the 15 organization's board members were selected from 15 distinct geographical or linguistic regions in Europe, each comprising different countries. In addition, a board seat has been set aside for a transnational ethnic representative from either the Sámi or Roma populations in Europe.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-7)

Board meetings take place 3 times a year, one of which is held in Berlin, where the academy is headquartered.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-EFAstory-3)

### Funding

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=European_Film_Academy&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Funding")]

The European Film Academy is mainly funded by the Stiftung Deutsche Klassenlotterie Berlin (German National Lottery), the German State Minister for Culture and the Media, and Medienboard Berlin-Brandenburg. The presentation of the European Film Awards is financed independently from the Academy. Founded in 2006 to produce the European Film Awards ceremony for television, European Film Academy Productions (formerly EFA Productions) gGmbH is the in-house production team and is a subsidiary company of the European Film Academy e.V.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-:0-2) The European Film Awards are supported by patrons from the international film industry, including national organizations such as the British Film Institute, the Danish Film Institute, EURIMAGES, and the Swedish Film Institute.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-:0-2)

Throughout the year, the European Film Academy initiates and participates in a series of activities dealing with film politics as well as economic, artistic, and training aspects. The program includes conferences, seminars, and workshops, with a common goal is to build a bridge between creativity and the industry. Some of the academy's events have become an institution for meetups within the European film community. Some of these events are:

The Short Film Initiative An initiative by the European Film Academy in co-operation with fifteen festivals throughout Europe. At each of these festivals, an independent jury presents one of the European short films in competition with a nomination in the short film category of the European Film Awards.A Sunday in the Country A special weekend encounter between approximately 10 young European filmmakers and some established European Film Academy members. The private atmosphere of these gatherings guarantees an exchange of ideas and experience which goes far beyond the results of usual workshops.Conferences and Seminars A series of conferences initiated and/or supported by the European Film Academy to discuss what European film is, how it is changing, and where it is going.Master Classes These classes offer training opportunities for young talent, combining theoretical and practical training. The high-profile list of former masters includes film professionals such as [Jean-Jacques Annaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Annaud "Jean-Jacques Annaud"), [Jan De Bont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_De_Bont "Jan De Bont"), [Henning Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henning_Carlsen "Henning Carlsen"), [André Delvaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Delvaux "André Delvaux"), [Bernd Eichinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Eichinger "Bernd Eichinger"), [Krzysztof Kieślowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Kie%C5%9Blowski "Krzysztof Kieślowski"), [Jiří Menzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Menzel "Jiří Menzel"), [Tilda Swinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilda_Swinton "Tilda Swinton"), [István Szabó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istv%C3%A1n_Szab%C3%B3 "István Szabó"), [Marc Weigert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Weigert "Marc Weigert"), [Mike Newell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Newell_(director) "Mike Newell (director)"), [Tsui Hark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsui_Hark "Tsui Hark"), [Allan Starski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Starski "Allan Starski") and [Anthony Dod Mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Dod_Mantle "Anthony Dod Mantle").

## European Film Awards

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=European_Film_Academy&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: European Film Awards")]

Main article: [European Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Awards "European Film Awards")

[![Image 18](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Theater_des_Westens_Berlin_SaschaV.jpg/250px-Theater_des_Westens_Berlin_SaschaV.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Theater_des_Westens_Berlin_SaschaV.jpg)

The [Theater des Westens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_des_Westens "Theater des Westens") in Berlin was the first venue of the European Film Awards in 1988

The annual European Film Awards ceremony (known as FELIX until 1997) is the most high-profile activity of the European Film Academy. The academy pursues the following aims with the awards: attracting the interest of the audience in [European cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_cinema "European cinema"), promoting its cultural and artistic qualities, and regaining the public's confidence in its entertainment value. To put these ideas into practice, the People's Choice Award was added as a new category in 1997. As of 2020, the People's Choice Award merged with the Lux Award of the European Parliament into the joint initiative LUX Audience Award. The nominees for the LUX Audience Award are announced during the ceremony of the European Film Awards. After this, screenings of the nominated films are organised for the public in several European cities.

The members of the European Film Academy actively participate in the selection, nomination and awarding procedures for the European Film Awards.

Taking place in the second weekend of December, the European Film Awards are the first in the international awards season. Most of the nominees and winners of the European Film Awards are found in the following months among the nominees and winners of the [Golden Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe "Golden Globe") or the [Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"). In the past years, European producers and distributors repeatedly stressed that a nomination for or win at the European Film Awards had a positive impact on the chances for their films to win further international awards, such as a Golden Globe or an Oscar.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy#cite_note-12)

Throughout the year, the academy organizes a side program on the occasion of the [European Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Awards "European Film Awards"). Originally, this was a weekend with panel discussions and conferences in the city where the Awards ceremony would take place. At these events, production methods for the new millennium were discussed at the conference (Berlin 1999), or European filmmakers of international reputation (among them, Wim Wenders, [Liv Ullmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liv_Ullmann "Liv Ullmann"), [Tom Tykwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Tykwer "Tom Tykwer"), [Dominik Moll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Moll "Dominik Moll"), [Pavel Lungin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Lungin "Pavel Lungin"), [Maria de Medeiros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_de_Medeiros "Maria de Medeiros")) as well as the then EU commissioner [Viviane Reding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviane_Reding "Viviane Reding") made very personal and visionary speeches on the artistic, cultural, and social role of cinema in front of 800 guests at Theâtre de l'Odéon in Paris, where the conference "E LA NAVE VA - For a New Energy in European Cinema" was held (2000). Significant changes to the annual program were introduced in 2021.

### Month of European Film

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=European_Film_Academy&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Month of European Film")]

[![Image 20: icon](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/60px-Question_book-new.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Question_book-new.svg)This section **does not [cite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources") any [sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")**. As of 2022, the European Film Academy organizes an annual "Month of European Film". This program highlights European films in the month prior to the European Film Awards, both in European cinemas, on television, as well as in cooperation with streaming platforms. A pilot edition of the Month of European Film took place in Berlin in 2021. The first official edition of the Month of European Film was launched in November 2022 at the [Seville European Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville_European_Film_Festival "Seville European Film Festival") and took place simultaneously in a range of European cities.
