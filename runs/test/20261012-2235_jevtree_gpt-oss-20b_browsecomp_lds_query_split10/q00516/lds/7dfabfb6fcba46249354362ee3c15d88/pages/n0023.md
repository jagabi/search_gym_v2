The **Golden Globes** are American awards presented for excellence in international film and television. It is an annual award with an [award ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Award_ceremony "Award ceremony") held [since 1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Golden_Globes "1st Golden Globes") to honor artists, professionals, and their work. The ceremony is normally held every January, and has been a major part of the film industry's [awards season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awards_season "Awards season"), which culminates each year in the [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"). The eligibility period for Golden Globes extends from January 1 through December 31. The Golden Globes were not televised [from 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_Golden_Globes "26th Golden Globes")[to 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/29th_Golden_Globes "29th Golden Globes"), [in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36th_Golden_Globes "36th Golden Globes"), or [in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_Golden_Globes "79th Golden Globes"). The [2008 ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65th_Golden_Globes "65th Golden Globes") was canceled due to the [2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Writers_Guild_of_America_strike "2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike"), while the 2022 ceremony was downsized to a non-televised gala due to a lack of broadcasters.

The ceremony was originally established and organized by the [Hollywood Foreign Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Foreign_Press_Association "Hollywood Foreign Press Association") (HFPA), a group representing international journalists covering the United States' entertainment industry. In 2022, amid criticism and boycotts of the HFPA over aspects of its operations (including a lack of diversity in its membership), interim CEO [Todd Boehly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Boehly "Todd Boehly") announced that the Golden Globe Awards would be reformed as a for-profit entity under his investment firm [Eldridge Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldridge_Industries "Eldridge Industries") (the parent company of [Dick Clark Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark_Productions "Dick Clark Productions"), which had produced the Golden Globes since 1993), and that a new non-profit entity would be formed to continue the HFPA's philanthropic activities. The restructuring took effect on June 12, 2023, with the Golden Globes being acquired by Dick Clark Productions, and the HFPA's philanthropic activities transitioned to the [Golden Globe Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Foundation "Golden Globe Foundation").

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

The [Hollywood Foreign Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Foreign_Press_Association "Hollywood Foreign Press Association") (HFPA) was founded in 1943 as the Hollywood Foreign Correspondent Association (HFCA) by [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles")–based foreign [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist "Journalist") seeking to develop a better-organized process of gathering and distributing cinema news to non-U.S. markets.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Henrietta_Awards-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-vox-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-books.google.co.in-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-5) One of the organization's first major endeavors was to establish a ceremony similar to the [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") to honor film achievements. The [1st Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Golden_Globe_Awards "1st Golden Globe Awards") ceremony, honoring the best achievements in [1943 filmmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1943_in_film "1943 in film"), was held on January 20, 1944, at the [20th Century-Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century-Fox "20th Century-Fox") studios. Subsequent ceremonies were held at various venues throughout the next decade, including [the Beverly Hills Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beverly_Hills_Hotel "The Beverly Hills Hotel") and the [Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Roosevelt_Hotel "Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel").

### Foreign Press Association of Hollywood's Henrietta Awards

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Foreign Press Association of Hollywood's Henrietta Awards")]

In 1950, some of the journalists in the HFCA broke away to form the Foreign Press Association of Hollywood (FPAH). It was the FPAH that instituted the Henrietta Award for World Film Favorite, which was subsequently given out by the Hollywood Foreign Press Association, the new name for the organization when the FPAH and HFCA merged in 1955, minus the "Henrietta" name through 1980 (for the 1979 movie year).[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Henrietta_Awards-2)

The FPAH held its first World Film Favorite Festival on January 27, 1951, giving out Henrietta Awards in various categories. The award, an angel above a globe raised on four tall pillars, was named for the president of the FPAH, Henry Gris. Winners of the Henrietta for World Film Favorite were [Gregory Peck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Peck "Gregory Peck") and [Jane Wyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Wyman "Jane Wyman"). At the FPAH's second World Film Favorite Festival held January 26, 1952, the Henrietta was a large statuette of a naked woman holding a flower. Based on an international poll of 900 newspapers, magazines and radio stations, [Alan Ladd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Ladd "Alan Ladd") and [Esther Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Williams "Esther Williams") were presented the gold Henrietta for World Favorites. while silver Henriettas for "Best Young Box Office Personality" were presented to [Leslie Caron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Caron "Leslie Caron"), [Tony Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Curtis "Tony Curtis"), [John Derek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Derek "John Derek"), [Mitzi Gaynor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitzi_Gaynor "Mitzi Gaynor"), [Marilyn Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Monroe "Marilyn Monroe") and [Patrice Wymore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Wymore "Patrice Wymore"). At the third festival held on February 14, 1953, [John Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne "John Wayne") and [Susan Hayward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Hayward "Susan Hayward") won the gold Henriettas.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Henrietta_Awards-2)

The HFCA continued to hold their Golden Globe Awards. In 1952, the HFCA had established a special honorary award to recognize outstanding contributions to the entertainment industry. Recognizing its subject as an international figure within the entertainment industry, the first award was presented to director and producer [Cecil B. DeMille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_B._DeMille "Cecil B. DeMille"). The official name of the award thus became the [Cecil B. DeMille Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_B._DeMille_Award "Cecil B. DeMille Award").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Cecil_B._deMille_Award-6)

In January 1954, the two organizations held a joint ceremony, and the following year, they merged under the new Hollywood Foreign Press Association name. The Henrietta Award was terminated, but the HFPA instituted a special award called World Film Favorite, a Golden Globe surmounted by an angel. Similar to the Henrietta Award for World Film Favorite, the winner of the new HFPA Golden Globe was determined by a worldwide poll conducted by [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters"). This award, sometimes referred to as The Henrietta Award, was given out through 1980 for the 1979, movie year, when it was terminated.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Henrietta_Awards-2)

### Post-merger

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Post-merger")]

The [13th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Golden_Globe_Awards "13th Golden Globe Awards") held in February 1956 saw the first Golden Globe in Television Achievement. The first three permanent television award categories, [Best TV Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Television_Series_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Television Series – Drama"), [Best TV Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Television_Series_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Television Series Drama"), and [Best TV Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Television_Series_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Television Series Drama"), then made their debuts during the [19th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Golden_Globe_Awards "19th Golden Globe Awards") held in March 1962.

Beginning in 1963, the trophies commenced to be handed out by one or more persons referred to as "Miss Golden Globe", a title renamed on January 5, 2018, to "[Golden Globe Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Ambassador "Golden Globe Ambassador")". The holders of the position were, traditionally, the daughters or sometimes the sons of a celebrity, and as a point of pride, these often continued to be contested among celebrity parents.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-New_York_Times_5_January_2018-7)

In 2009, the Golden Globe statuette was redesigned (but not for the first time in its history). The New York firm [Society Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_Awards "Society Awards") collaborated for a year with the HFPA to produce a statuette that included a unique marble and enhanced the statuette's quality and gold content. It was unveiled at a press conference at the [Beverly Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hilton "Beverly Hilton") prior to the show.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Golden_Globe_Statuette-8)

The [Carol Burnett Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Burnett_Award "Carol Burnett Award") was created as a television counterpart to the Cecil B. DeMille Award, named after its first recipient in 2019, actress and comedian [Carol Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Burnett "Carol Burnett").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-9)

### Revenues from award broadcast

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Revenues from award broadcast")]

Revenues generated from the annual ceremony have enabled the HFPA to donate millions of dollars to entertainment-related charities, as well as funding scholarships and other programs for future film and television professionals. The most prominent beneficiary is the [Young Artist Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Artist_Award "Young Artist Award"), presented annually by the [Young Artist Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Artist_Foundation "Young Artist Foundation"), established in 1978 by Hollywood Foreign Press member [Maureen Dragone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_Dragone "Maureen Dragone"), to recognize and award excellence of young [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_(film_industry) "Hollywood (film industry)") performers under the age of 21 and to provide scholarships for young artists who may be physically or financially challenged.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Golden_Globes2-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-ABC_Local-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Young_Artist_Awards_1-12)

### 2022 boycott, acquisition by Dick Clark Productions

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 2022 boycott, acquisition by Dick Clark Productions")]

Main article: [Hollywood Foreign Press Association §Black representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Foreign_Press_Association#Black_representation "Hollywood Foreign Press Association")

In 2021, the HFPA faced criticism for the lack of [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people "Black people") representation among its members.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-13) On May 3, 2021, the HFPA announced plans for a reform package, including a 50% increase in members over the next 18 months, as well as new positions, term limits, and practices to improve its accountability.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-14) However, [Time's Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time's_Up_(organization) "Time's Up (organization)") and a group of 100 PR firms criticized the lack of given timelines for filling some of the new management positions, arguing that they would not be completed soon enough to have any material impact on the cycle of the upcoming [79th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_Golden_Globe_Awards "79th Golden Globe Awards") in January 2022. Time's Up further argued that the package "largely contains no specifics" nor "commitments to real accountability or change".[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-15)

On May 7, 2021, both [Amazon Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Studios "Amazon Studios") and [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") announced that they would stop their activities with the HFPA until sufficient actions on reforms are made.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-17) Other media companies followed suit on May 10,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-https-18) including [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC"), who announced that it would not televise the [79th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_Golden_Globe_Awards "79th Golden Globe Awards"), but that it would be open to televising the ceremony in 2023 if the HFPA were successful in its efforts to reform.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-tvline052021-19) WarnerMedia also boycotted the HFPA,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-https-18) while [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise") returned the awards he had won for _[Born on the Fourth of July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born\_on\_the\_Fourth\_of\_July\_(film) "Born on the Fourth of July (film)")_, _[Jerry Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry\_Maguire "Jerry Maguire")_ and _[Magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia\_(film) "Magnolia (film)")_ in solidarity.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-20)

Following these events, the HFPA released a timeline for its reforms, which would see the process completed by the week of August 2.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-21) On October 1, the HFPA released a list of 21 new members that it had recruited under these reforms, and named [Todd Boehly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Boehly "Todd Boehly") (owner of ceremony producer Dick Clark Productions) as its "interim CEO".[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-22)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-23) The HFPA then announced on October 15 that it still planned to hold the 79th Golden Globe Awards on January 9, 2022, with or without another media partner.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-24) With the televised absence of the Golden Globe Awards from NBC, the [Critics Choice Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics_Choice_Association "Critics Choice Association") attempted to shift their [Critics' Choice Movie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Choice_Movie_Awards "Critics' Choice Movie Awards") ceremony up a week to fill the void and increase their overall prestige, though it was later delayed due to [SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Omicron_variant "SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-26) The 79th ceremony was conducted as a non-televised, private presentation, with limited guests (particularly beneficiaries of the HFPA's philanthropic activities) and strict COVID-19 protocol due to Omicron variant.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-thr202216-27)

In July 2022, the HFPA approved a major restructuring, under which Boehly would establish a for-profit entity via his holding company [Eldridge Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldridge_Industries "Eldridge Industries") (owner of [Dick Clark Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark_Productions "Dick Clark Productions")—which has produced the Golden Globes' telecast since 1993,[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-:1-28) as well as the entertainment trade publication _[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Hollywood\_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-29) that will hold the Golden Globe Awards' intellectual property and oversee the "professionalization and modernization" of the ceremony, including "[increasing] the size and diversity of the available voters for the annual awards". The HFPA's philanthropic activities were to continue separately as a non-profit entity.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-30)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-31) NBC subsequently agreed to a one-year contract to air the [80th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80th_Golden_Globe_Awards "80th Golden Globe Awards") on January 10, 2023,[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-32) which were moved to a Tuesday evening to avoid conflicting with the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") (whose regular season was recently extended into January) and the [College Football Playoff National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_College_Football_Playoff_National_Championship "2023 College Football Playoff National Championship") (which was being hosted at [SoFi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoFi_Stadium "SoFi Stadium") in Inglewood).[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-variety0922-33)

On June 12, 2023, the Golden Globe Awards' assets and intellectual property were acquired by DCP (whose ownership includes [Penske Media Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penske_Media_Corporation "Penske Media Corporation"), owner of fellow entertainment publications _[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline\_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")_ and _[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety\_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")_) and Eldridge; the financial details of the purchase were not disclosed. The HFPA's philanthropic activities were transferred to a new entity known as the [Golden Globe Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Foundation "Golden Globe Foundation").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-ewJune2023-1)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-cnbc2023-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-deadline2023-35)

#### Subsequent lawsuit

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Subsequent lawsuit")]

Penske Media's ownership of the Golden Globe Awards–as well as its ownership of Hollywood publications including _The Hollywood Reporter_, _Variety_ and _Deadline_–became the source of a lawsuit several years after Penske Media acquired the Golden Globes. In July 2026, Penske was sued by the [Hollywood Foreign Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Foreign_Press_Association "Hollywood Foreign Press Association") (HFPA), which presented the Golden Globes prior to its acquisition by Penske-owned [Dick Clark Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark_Productions "Dick Clark Productions"). The federal suite alleges violations of federal and state antitrust laws. It is alleged that this acquisition was done by Penske to influence awards season races. The HFPA is seeking $150 million in damages and the invalidation of the agreement that transferred ownership of the Golden Globes to Penske Media.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-37)

### Podcasts added in 2026

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Podcasts added in 2026")]

For the 2026 awards the Golden Globes added a new category for Best Podcast, citing the 'seismic' growth of the medium. The inaugural award was won by [Amy Poehler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Poehler "Amy Poehler") for her weekly show, [_Good Hang with Amy Poehler._](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Hang_with_Amy_Poehler "Good Hang with Amy Poehler")[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-38)

## Rules

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Rules")]

### Eligibility

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Eligibility")]

The qualifying eligibility period for all nominations is the calendar year from January 1 through December 31.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

Voice-over performances and cameo appearances in which persons play themselves are not eligible from all film and TV acting categories.

Films must be at least 70 minutes and released for at least a seven-day run in the [Greater Los Angeles area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_area "Greater Los Angeles area"), starting prior to midnight on December 31. Films can be released in theaters, on [pay-per-view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay-per-view "Pay-per-view"), or by digital delivery.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

For the [Motion Picture – Non-English Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Non-English_Language "Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Non-English Language") category, films do not need to be released in the United States. At least 51 percent of the dialogue must be in a language other than English, and they must first be released in their country of origin during a 14-month period from November 1 to December 31 prior to the Awards. However, if a film was not released in its country of origin due to censorship, it can still qualify if it had a one-week release in the United States during the qualifying calendar year. There is no limit to the number of submitted films from a given country.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

A TV program must air in the United States between the [prime time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_time "Prime time") hours of 8 p.m. and 11 p.m. (or 7 p.m. and 11 p.m. on Sundays). A show can air on [broadcast television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_television "Broadcast television"), on basic or premium cable, or by digital delivery; it does not qualify if it is only on pay-per-view or via digital delivery of film. Also, a TV show must either be made in the United States or be a co-production financially and creatively between an American and a foreign production company. Furthermore, [reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_television "Reality television") and non-scripted shows are disqualified.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

A film cannot be entered in both the film and TV categories, and instead should be entered based on its original release format. If it was first aired on American television, then it can be entered into the TV categories. If it was released in theaters or on pay-per-view, then it should instead be entered into the film categories. A film festival showing does not count towards disqualifying what would otherwise be a TV program.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

Actors in a TV series must appear in at least six episodes during the qualifying calendar year. Actors in a TV film or miniseries must appear in at least five percent of the time in that TV film or miniseries.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

### Nominations and voting

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Nominations and voting")]

Entry forms for films need to be received by the deadline which varies each year, but is typically at the end of October. As of 2024, all films must be uploaded to the Golden Globes screening platform for viewing by all voters. TV programs should be submitted "as early as possible" before the deadline.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39) For TV programs, they too must be made available to be seen by voters by being uploaded to the Golden Globes screening platform. The minimum number of eligible minutes required must be uploaded. For Podcast programs, they must be uploaded to the Golden Globes screening platform by the deadline for viewers to watch/listen.

As of the 2023 ceremony, the HFPA removed exclusive press conferences hosted by the organization—a practice that had contributed to transparency issues.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-40)

Ballots to select the nominations are sent to voters in November, along with a "Reminder List" of eligible film and TV programs.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGTime-41) Each Golden Globes voter then votes for their top six (or eight in the case of Cinematic & Box Office Achievement) choices in each category, numbering them 6 to 1, with 6 being their top choice. The nominees in each category are then the five selections that receive the most votes. The [ranked voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_voting "Ranked voting") is only used to break ties, with number 6 worth 6 points, number 5 worth 5 points, and so on.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

After the nominations are announced on the first or second Monday of December, voters receive the final ballots.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGTime-41) The winner in each category is selected from among the nominees by [plurality voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting "Plurality voting"). In case of a tie, the winner is the one that had the most votes on the nomination ballot.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-GGRules-39)

As of the 2024 ceremony, the voting body consisted of 310 individuals, including representatives of 76 countries' journalists, and 95 members that were members of the HFPA.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-42)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-43)

## Ceremony

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Ceremony")]

Main article: [List of Golden Globe Awards ceremonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Golden_Globe_Awards_ceremonies "List of Golden Globe Awards ceremonies")

The broadcast of the Golden Globe Awards, broadcast to 167 countries worldwide, generally ranks as the third most-watched awards show each year, behind only the [Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscars "Oscars") and the [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award"). Since 2010, it was televised live in all United States time zones. Until [Ricky Gervais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Gervais "Ricky Gervais") hosted in 2010, the award ceremony was one of two major [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_(film_industry) "Hollywood (film industry)") award ceremonies (the other being the [Screen Actors Guild Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award "Screen Actors Guild Award")) that did not have a regular host; every year a different presenter introduced the ceremony at the beginning of the broadcast. Gervais returned to host the 68th and 69th Golden Globe Awards the next two years.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-auto-44)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-45)[Tina Fey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Fey "Tina Fey") and [Amy Poehler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Poehler "Amy Poehler") hosted the 70th, 71st and 72nd Golden Globe Awards in 2013 through 2015.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-auto-44) The Golden Globe Awards' theme song, which debuted in 2012, was written by Japanese musician and composer [Yoshiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshiki_(musician) "Yoshiki (musician)").[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-46)

### Broadcasting

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Broadcasting")]

The HFPA had previously held a lucrative broadcasting agreement with [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC"),[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-47) which began televising the Golden Globes ceremony locally in Los Angeles in 1958, then nationally in 1964. However, in 1968, the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission "Federal Communications Commission") (FCC) claimed the show "misled the public as to how the winners were determined" (allegations included that winners were determined by lobby; to motivate winners to show up to the awards ceremony winners were informed if they did not attend another winner would be named). The FCC admonished NBC for participating in the scandal. Subsequently, NBC refused to broadcast the ceremony from 1968 until after 1974.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-48)[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-49)

Since 1993, [Dick Clark Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark_Productions "Dick Clark Productions") (DCP) had produced the ceremony with NBC as a broadcaster; DCP's involvement came at a time of instability for the Golden Globes, including reduced credibility and having lost its contract with [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") (the interim period saw it contract with cable network [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_(U.S._TV_channel) "TBS (U.S. TV channel)") to air the ceremony).[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-:1-28) Enthusiastic over [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark "Dick Clark")'s commitment, the HFPA's contract contained an unusual provision granting DCP the role of producer in perpetuity, provided that it continued to maintain a broadcast rights agreement with NBC.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-thr-dcpsuit-50)

Due to threats of writers picketing the event as part of the ongoing [Writers Guild of America strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%932008_Writers_Guild_of_America_strike "2007–2008 Writers Guild of America strike"), the [65th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65th_Golden_Globe_Awards "65th Golden Globe Awards") ceremony was cancelled and replaced by an hour-long press conference to announce the winners. While NBC sought to be the exclusive broadcaster of the press conference, NBC faced conflicts with the HFPA and Dick Clark Productions over the proposed coverage, including a demand for a rights fee, and concerns over plans to have [Billy Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bush "Billy Bush") and [Nancy O'Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_O'Dell "Nancy O'Dell") of NBC's [syndicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_syndication "Broadcast syndication") entertainment news program _[Access Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access\_Hollywood "Access Hollywood")_ serve as presenters. The HFPA subsequently announced that it would not restrict coverage of the press conference by other broadcasters.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-:0-51)[E!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E! "E!") and [TV Guide Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Guide_Network "TV Guide Network") (who were typically known for red carpet coverage from major awards shows) both aired coverage of the press conference, as well as [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN").[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-52)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-53) NBC declined to air the conference itself; the ceremony timeslot was filled by a _[Dateline NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dateline\_NBC "Dateline NBC")_ preview special, an hour-long results special hosted by Bush and O'Dell, and an _Access Hollywood_ post-show also hosted by Bush and O'Dell.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-CTribune-54)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-55)[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-56)

In 2010, Dick Clark Productions reached an extension with NBC through 2018. However, the deal was negotiated without the HFPA's knowledge. The HFPA sued DCP over the deal, as well as claims that the company was attempting to sell digital rights that it did not hold; the HFPA had wanted a new contract that would grant them a larger share of revenue from the telecast.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-thr-dcpsuit-50) In April 2012, judge [Howard Matz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Matz "Howard Matz") upheld the NBC perpetuity clause and ruled in favor of DCP, noting that the HFPA had a history of "unbusinesslike display[s] of misplaced priorities" and "[succumbing] to bouts of pronounced turmoil and personal feuds", in contrast to DCP, which had been "represented by one experienced executive who was adept at dealing fairly and effectively with the often amateurish conduct of HFPA." Matz pointed out examples of the HFPA's enthusiasm over the relationship and their desire to "not get cancelled", such as having disregarded its own bylaws by approving an extension in 2001 without a formal vote. The case was taken to the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-thr-dcpsuit-50)

In 2014, Dick Clark Productions and the HFPA reached a settlement; details were not released, but DCP committed to continue its role as producer through at least the end of its current contract with NBC, and to work with the HFPA to "expand the brand with unique and exciting entertainment experiences". NBC held a right of first refusal to renew its contract beyond 2018, but bidding was to be open to other broadcasters;[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-variety-settlehfpa-57)[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-58) in September 2018, NBC agreed to renew its rights to the Golden Globes through 2027, maintaining the current broadcast arrangement and the involvement of Dick Clark Productions.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-59)[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-60)

In 2019 and 2020, NBC televised the late Sunday afternoon [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") (NFL) playoff game (which had historically gone to another NFL broadcaster) as a lead-in to the Golden Globes. Because of the large viewership of NFL playoff games, this was intended to boost the Golden Globes' television ratings, which dropped 11% between 2017 and 2018.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-61) If the game ever went long, NBC planned to still air the Golden Globes in its entirety on a [broadcast delay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_delay "Broadcast delay").[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-62) The 2021 ceremony was then postponed to February 28 due to the [impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_cinema "Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema") and [on television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_television "Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on television"), avoiding the NFL season altogether.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Postponement-63)

Per the aforementioned [2022 boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#2022_boycott,_acquisition_by_Dick_Clark_Productions), NBC declined to air the 2022 ceremony,[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-tvline052021-19) then signed a one-year deal to televise the 2023 ceremony, moving it to a Tuesday evening to avoid conflicting with _[Sunday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC\_Sunday\_Night\_Football "NBC Sunday Night Football")_ and the [2023 College Football Playoff National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_College_Football_Playoff_National_Championship "2023 College Football Playoff National Championship").[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-variety0922-33) In November 2023, DCP announced a one-year agreement to air the 81st ceremony on CBS and streaming on [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+ "Paramount+"), with the ceremony following CBS's afternoon [NFL coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_CBS "NFL on CBS").[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-64) In March 2024, DCP announced a five-year agreement with CBS to continue airing the Golden Globes; the agreement also includes rights to the DCP-produced [American Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Awards "American Music Awards") beginning in 2025, which had gone on hiatus after [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Awards_of_2022 "American Music Awards of 2022") when its contract with [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") expired.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-65) In September 2025, it was announced that CBS would air an additional Golden Globes special—_Golden Eve_— on the Thursday prior to the ceremony, which will highlight the recipients of the Cecil B. DeMille and Carol Burnett lifetime achievement awards, and feature highlights from the previously non-televised gala.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-66)

## Scandals and criticism

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Golden_Globes&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Scandals and criticism")]

Since the late 1950s, the HFPA had been racked by scandals and controversies. The organization had been criticized for the small size of its membership, the quality of the members, its exclusion of serious cinema journalists, and their closeness to the movie industry and stars. The Golden Globes under the HFPA were also accused of being bought or bartered, with the HFPA's members having been easily swayed by high-priced gifts and [access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access_journalism "Access journalism") offered by studios, production companies, and stars.[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-ewJan2023-166)[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Project_Casting-167)[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-:12-168)[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards#cite_note-Post_Bribe-169)
