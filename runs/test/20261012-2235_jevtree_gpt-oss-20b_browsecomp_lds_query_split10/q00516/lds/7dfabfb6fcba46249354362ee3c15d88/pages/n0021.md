**Barcelona**[[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-8) is a city on the northeastern coast of [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain"). It is the capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Catalonia "Municipalities of Catalonia") of the [autonomous community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_communities_of_Spain "Autonomous communities of Spain") of [Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalonia "Catalonia"), as well as the [second-most populous municipality of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish_municipalities_by_population "List of Spanish municipalities by population") after [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid"). With a population of 1.7 million within city limits,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-population-3) its urban area extends to numerous neighbouring municipalities within the [province of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Barcelona "Province of Barcelona") and is home to around 5.7 million people,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-demographia-4) making it the [fifth most populous urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_European_Union "List of urban areas in the European Union") of the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") after [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr "Ruhr") area, Madrid, and [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan "Milan").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-demographia-4) It is one of the largest metropolises on the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea"), located on the coast between the mouths of the rivers [Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llobregat "Llobregat") and [Besòs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bes%C3%B2s_(river) "Besòs (river)"), and bounded to the west by the [Serra de Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Collserola "Serra de Collserola") mountain range.

According to tradition, Barcelona was founded by either the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia") or the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginians "Carthaginians"), who had trading posts along the Catalonian coast.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-britannica.com-9) In the Middle Ages, Barcelona became the capital of the [County of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Barcelona "County of Barcelona"). After joining with the [Kingdom of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aragon "Kingdom of Aragon") to form the composite monarchy of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon"), Barcelona, which continued to be the capital of the [Principality of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Catalonia "Principality of Catalonia"), became the most important city in the Crown of Aragon and its main economic and administrative centre, only to be overtaken by [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia "Valencia"), wrested from [Moorish control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus "Al-Andalus") by the Catalans, shortly before the dynastic union between the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile") and the Crown of Aragon in 1516. Barcelona became the centre of [Catalan separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_separatism "Catalan separatism"), briefly becoming part of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") during the 17th century [Reapers' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reapers'_War "Reapers' War") and again from 1812 to 1814 under [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon").

Experiencing industrialization and several [workers movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_movement "Workers movement") during the 19th and early 20th century, it became the capital of [autonomous Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_of_Catalonia_(1931%E2%80%931939) "Autonomous Region of Catalonia (1931–1939)") in 1931 and was the epicenter of the [revolution experienced by Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Catalonia "Revolutionary Catalonia") during the [Spanish Revolution of 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Revolution_of_1936 "Spanish Revolution of 1936"), until its [capture by the fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Barcelona "Fall of Barcelona") in 1939. After the [Spanish transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy "Spanish transition to democracy") in the 1970s, Barcelona once again became the capital of an autonomous Catalonia. Barcelona has a rich cultural heritage and is today an important cultural centre and a major tourist destination. Particularly renowned are the architectural works of [Antoni Gaudí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Gaud%C3%AD "Antoni Gaudí") and [Lluís Domènech i Montaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llu%C3%ADs_Dom%C3%A8nech_i_Montaner "Lluís Domènech i Montaner"), which have been designated UNESCO [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site"). The city is home to two of the most prestigious universities in Spain: the [University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Barcelona "University of Barcelona") and [Pompeu Fabra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeu_Fabra_University "Pompeu Fabra University"). The headquarters of the [Union for the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Mediterranean "Union for the Mediterranean") are located in Barcelona. The city is known for hosting the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics "1992 Summer Olympics") as well as world-class [conferences and expositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#Trade_fair_and_exhibitions). In addition, many [international sport tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Barcelona#International_tournaments "Sport in Barcelona") have been played here. Barcelona is also the home of the [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football")[Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona").

Barcelona is a major cultural, economic, and financial centre in [southwestern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula"),[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-10) as well as the main [biotech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotech "Biotech") hub in Spain.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-11) As a leading world city, Barcelona's influence in global socio-economic affairs qualifies it for [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city "Global city") status (Beta +).[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-12) Barcelona is a [transport hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub "Transport hub"), with the [Port of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Barcelona "Port of Barcelona"), one of Europe's principal seaports and the busiest European passenger port,[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-StatServ2014-13) an international airport, [Barcelona–El Prat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona%E2%80%93El_Prat_Airport "Barcelona–El Prat Airport"), which handles over 50 million passengers per year,[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-14) an [extensive motorway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#Roads_and_highways), and a [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan%E2%80%93Barcelona_high-speed_rail_line "Perpignan–Barcelona high-speed rail line") line with links to France and the rest of Europe.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-15)

## Names

The name _Barcelona_ comes from the ancient Iberian _Baŕkeno_, attested in an ancient coin inscription found on the right side of the coin in [Iberian script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_scripts "Iberian scripts") as [![Image 24: Barkeno in Levantine Iberian script](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/Barkeno.png/40px-Barkeno.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Barkeno.png "Barkeno in Levantine Iberian script"),[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-16) in [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") sources as Βαρκινών, _Barkinṓn_;[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-17)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Sch%C3%BCtte1917-18) and in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") as _Barcino_,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-19)_Barcilonum_[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-20) and _Barcenona_.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Parthey1848-21)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-WesselingGrammarian1735-22)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Cook1910-23)

Other sources suggest that the city may have been named after the [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage") general [Hamilcar Barca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilcar_Barca "Hamilcar Barca"), who was supposed to have founded the city in the 3rd century BC,[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-britannica.com-9)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-24) but there is no evidence that its name in antiquity, _Barcino_, was connected with the [Barcid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcid "Barcid") family of Hamilcar.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Corporation1957-25) During [the Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages"), the city was variously known as _Barchinona_, _Barçalona_, _Barchelonaa_, and _Barchenona_.

An abbreviated form sometimes used by locals for the city is _Barna_. _Barça_ is only applied to the local football club [FC Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona"), not to the city. Another common abbreviation is 'BCN', which is also the [IATA airport code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code "IATA airport code") of the [Barcelona-El Prat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona-El_Prat_Airport "Barcelona-El Prat Airport").

The city is referred to as the _Ciutat Comtal_ in [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language "Catalan language") and _Ciudad Condal_ in Spanish (i.e., "Comital City" or "City of Counts"), owing to its past as the seat of the [Count of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Barcelona "Count of Barcelona").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-26)

### Legendary founding

The origin of the earliest settlement at the site of present-day Barcelona is unclear. The ruins of an early settlement have been found, including different tombs and dwellings dating to earlier than 5000 BC.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-27)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Monta%C3%B1aCamposFarr%C3%A92008-28) In Greek mythology, the founding of Barcelona had been attributed to the mythological [Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules "Hercules").

### Punic Barcelona

According to tradition, Barcelona was founded by Punic ([Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia")) settlers, who had trading posts along the Catalonian coast.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-britannica.com-9)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-29)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-30) In particular, some historians attribute the foundation of the city directly to the historical [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage") general, [Hamilcar Barca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilcar_Barca "Hamilcar Barca"), father of [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal "Hannibal"), who supposedly named the city _Barcino_ after his family in the 3rd century BC,[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-britannica.com-9)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-31) but this theory has been questioned.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Corporation1957-25) Iberia and Barcelona was controlled by the [Carthaginian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Empire "Carthaginian Empire") and was an important location for trade due to its proximity to Punic [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza "Ibiza"). Archeological evidence in the form of coins from the 3rd century BC have been found on the hills at the foot of Montjuïc with the name Bárkeno written in the Iberian script in the [Iberian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_language "Iberian language").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] Thus, some have argued that the [Laietani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laietani "Laietani"), an ancient [Iberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberians "Iberians") (pre-Roman) people of the Iberian peninsula, who inhabited the area occupied by the city of Barcelona around 3–2 BC after the Punic withdrawal from Iberia, called the area Bàrkeno, which means "The Place of the Plains" (Barrke = plains/terrace).[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-32)

### Roman Barcelona

See also: [Roman Sepulchral way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Sepulchral_way "Roman Sepulchral way") and [Roman walls of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_walls_of_Barcelona "Roman walls of Barcelona")

[![Image 25](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Barcino_marble_barcelona.jpg/250px-Barcino_marble_barcelona.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Barcino_marble_barcelona.jpg)

A marble plaque in the Museu d'Història de la Ciutat de Barcelona, dated from around 110–130 AD and dedicated to the Roman colony of Barcino

In about 15 BC, the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") redrew the town as a _[castrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra "Castra")_ (Roman military camp) centred on the "_Mons Taber_", a little hill near the [Generalitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalitat "Generalitat") (Catalan Government) and city hall buildings. The Roman Forum, at the crossing of the [Cardo Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardo_Maximus "Cardo Maximus") and [Decumanus Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decumanus_Maximus "Decumanus Maximus"), was approximately placed where current [Plaça de Sant Jaume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pla%C3%A7a_de_Sant_Jaume "Plaça de Sant Jaume") is. Thus, the political centre of the city, [Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalonia "Catalonia"), and its domains has remained in the same place for over 2,000 years.

Under the Romans, it was a colony with the surname of _Faventia_,[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-33) or, in full, _Colonia Faventia Julia Augusta Pia Barcino_[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-34) or _Colonia Julia Augusta Faventia Paterna Barcino_. [Pomponius Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomponius_Mela "Pomponius Mela")[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-35) mentions it among the small towns of the district, probably as it was eclipsed by its neighbour _[Tarraco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarragona "Tarragona")_ (modern [Tarragona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarragona "Tarragona")), but it may be gathered from later writers that it gradually grew in wealth and consequence, favoured as it was with a beautiful situation and an excellent harbour.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-36) It enjoyed immunity from imperial burdens.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-37) The city minted its own coins; some from the era of [Galba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galba "Galba") survive.

Important Roman vestiges are displayed in [Plaça del Rei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pla%C3%A7a_del_Rei "Plaça del Rei") underground, as a part of the [Barcelona City History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_City_History_Museum "Barcelona City History Museum") (MUHBA); the typically Roman grid plan is still visible today in the layout of the historical centre, the _[Barri Gòtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El\_G%C3%B2tic "El Gòtic")_ (Gothic Quarter). Some remaining fragments of the [Roman walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_walls_of_Barcelona "Roman walls of Barcelona") have been incorporated into the cathedral.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-38) The cathedral, Catedral Basílica Metropolitana de Barcelona, is also sometimes called _La Seu_, which simply means cathedral (and see, among other things) in Catalan.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-39)[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-40) It is said to have been founded in 343.

### Medieval Barcelona

The city was conquered by the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths "Visigoths") in the early 5th century, becoming for a few years the capital of all [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania "Hispania"). After being conquered by the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate "Umayyad Caliphate") in the early 8th century, it was conquered after a [siege in 801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Barcelona_(801) "Siege of Barcelona (801)") by [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne")'s son [Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_the_Pious "Louis the Pious"), who made Barcelona the seat of the [Carolingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire "Carolingian Empire") "[Hispanic March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marca_Hispanica "Marca Hispanica")" (_Marca Hispanica_), a [buffer zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_zone "Buffer zone") ruled by the [Count of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Barcelona "Counts of Barcelona").[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-41)

[![Image 26](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Muralla_Raval.JPG/250px-Muralla_Raval.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Muralla_Raval.JPG)

The remaining section of the medieval walls

The Counts of Barcelona became increasingly independent and expanded their territory to include much of modern [Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalonia "Catalonia"), although in 985, Barcelona was [sacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looting "Looting") by the army of [Almanzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almanzor "Almanzor").[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-42) The sack was so traumatic that most of Barcelona's population was either killed or enslaved.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-43) In 1137, [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aragon "Kingdom of Aragon") and the [County of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Barcelona "Counts of Barcelona") merged in [dynastic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynastic_union "Dynastic union")[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-bisson_31-44)[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-45) by the marriage of [Ramon Berenguer IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Berenguer_IV,_Count_of_Barcelona "Ramon Berenguer IV, Count of Barcelona") and [Petronilla of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronilla_of_Aragon "Petronilla of Aragon"), their titles finally borne by only one person when their son [Alfonso II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_II_of_Aragon "Alfonso II of Aragon") ascended to the throne in 1162. His territories were later to be known as the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon"), which conquered many overseas possessions and ruled the western [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea") with outlying territories [in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples") and Sicily and as far as [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Athens "Duchy of Athens") in the 13th century.

Barcelona also had a substantial [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Catalonia "History of the Jews in Catalonia") at the time, then the largest Jewish community in the Crown of Aragon. Called "[the Call](https://www.barcelona.cat/museuhistoria/en/itineraris/el-call-de-barcelona)," for the many small streets that defined the area, it later became enclosed. [Montjuïc or Montjuich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc "Montjuïc"), in medieval Latin and Catalan, meaning "Jewish Mountain" and the birthplace of the city, is the site of a medieval Jewish cemetery, Jews continued to live in Barcelona until the [Massacre of 1391](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_1391 "Massacre of 1391") diminished their numbers. The [Spanish Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Inquisition "Spanish Inquisition") forced the remaining Jews who refused to convert to Christianity to be burned at the stake, or sell their property and leave.

Barcelona was the leading [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery "History of slavery") centre of the Crown of Aragon up until the 15th century, when it was eclipsed by [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia "Valencia").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-46) It initially fed from eastern and Balkan slave stock later drawing from a [Maghribian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb "Maghreb") and, ultimately, [Subsaharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsaharan "Subsaharan") pool of slaves.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGonz%C3%A1lez_Ar%C3%A9valo201917-47)

The Bank or [Taula de canvi de Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taula_de_canvi_de_Barcelona "Taula de canvi de Barcelona"), often viewed as the oldest public bank in Europe, was established by the city [magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate "Magistrate") in 1401. It originated from necessities of the state, as did the [Bank of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici_Bank#Rise "Medici Bank") (1402) and the [Bank of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Saint_George "Bank of Saint George") (1407).[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-48)

### Barcelona under the Spanish monarchy

[![Image 27](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/Wyngaerde_Barcelona_1563.jpg/500px-Wyngaerde_Barcelona_1563.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wyngaerde_Barcelona_1563.jpg)

Barcelona in 1563 by [Anton van den Wyngaerde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_van_den_Wyngaerde "Anton van den Wyngaerde")

In the beginning of the early modern period, Barcelona lost political primacy, but the economy managed to achieve a balance between production capacity and imports.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-49) In the context of the wider early recovery of Catalonia from the [17th-century crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_General_Crisis "The General Crisis") in the second half of the century, increasing maritime activity since 1675 doubled traffic in the port of Barcelona compared to figures from the beginning of the 17th century.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-50) In the late 17th and early 18th century, Barcelona repeatedly endured the effects of war, including the 1691 bombing, the sieges of 1697, 1704, 1705, 1706, and the 1713 blockade and ensuing 1714 siege and assault.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArranzGrau1991135%E2%80%93136-51) In the 18th century, the population grew from 30,000 to about 100,000 inhabitants, as the city became one of the key mercantile centres in the Western Mediterranean, with inland influence up to [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza "Zaragoza"), and to the south up to [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante "Alicante").[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-52) A fortress was built at [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc "Montjuïc") that overlooked the harbour. Much of Barcelona was negatively affected by the [Napoleonic wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War "Peninsular War"), but the start of industrialization saw the fortunes of the province improve.

During the 19th century, Catalan business interests, many of them based in Barcelona, became increasingly involved in the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade"), taking advantage of the void left by the [British abolition of the trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_Trade_Act_1807 "Slave Trade Act 1807") in 1807. Catalan immigrants in Spanish America also became heavily involved in owning and running [slave plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_plantation "Slave plantation") in Cuba and Puerto Rico. Between 1817 and 1867, Catalans were involved in the transportation of 700,000 slaves from West Africa to the Caribbean, which "financed much of the industrialisation of Catalonia and the 19th-century building boom in Barcelona." Although the Spanish government had abolished slave trading in 1817, it turned a blind eye to illicit slave trading. When slavery was abolished in the Spanish Empire in 1886, many Catalans returned to Barcelona and invested their newfound fortunes in constructing opulent mansions in areas such as [La Rambla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rambla,_Barcelona "La Rambla, Barcelona").[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Burgen2023-53)[_[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable\_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_]

### Transforming the city

Main article: [Cerdà Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerd%C3%A0_Plan "Cerdà Plan")

[![Image 28](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Barcelona_a_vista_de_p%C3%A1jaro.jpg/250px-Barcelona_a_vista_de_p%C3%A1jaro.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Barcelona_a_vista_de_p%C3%A1jaro.jpg)

Barcelona before the Cerdà Plan (1857)

In the mid-1850s, Barcelona was struggling with population density as it became an industrial, [port city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_city "Port city") and European capital. The city's density was at 856 people per hectare, more than double that of Paris. Mortality rates were on the rise and any outbreaks of disease would devastate the population. To solve the issue, a civil engineer named [Ildefons Cerdà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ildefons_Cerd%C3%A0 "Ildefons Cerdà") proposed a [plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerd%C3%A0_Plan "Cerdà Plan") for a new district known as the [Eixample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eixample "Eixample"). The citizens of Barcelona had begun to demolish the medieval wall surrounding and constricting the city. Cerdà thought it best to transform the land outside the walls into an area characterized by a scientific approach to [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization "Urbanization"). His proposal consisted of a grid of streets to unite the old city and surrounding villages. There would also be wide streets to allow people to breathe clean air, gardens in the centre of each [street block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_block "Street block"), integration of rich and poor giving both groups access to the same services, and smooth-flowing traffic.

Urban quality, egalitarianism, hygiene, sunlight, and efficiency were all major keys for Cerdà's vision. Not everything he imagined would be realized within the Eixample district, but the iconic octagonal superblocks with chamfered corners for better visibility are his direct brainchild and remain immensely helpful even 170 years later. The district and its ideals were not appreciated at the time. The city council awarded the design of the extension plan to another architect. The Spanish government was the one to step in and impose Cerdà's plan, laying the groundwork for many more tensions between the Spanish and Catalan administrations. Regardless, some of the upper class citizens of Barcelona were excited by the new plan and began a race to build "the biggest, tallest, most attractive house" in the district. Their interest and money fueled the rich diversity that we now see in the district's architecture. In the end, Cerdà's ideas would have a lasting impact on Barcelona's development, earning it international recognition for its highly efficient approach to urban planning and design.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-54)[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-55)

### Spanish Civil War and Franco period

[![Image 29](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/RepublicanWoman1936GTaro.jpg/250px-RepublicanWoman1936GTaro.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:RepublicanWoman1936GTaro.jpg)

_[Woman training for a Republican militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman\_training\_for\_a\_Republican\_militia "Woman training for a Republican militia")_ by [Gerda Taro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerda_Taro "Gerda Taro"), [Somorrostro beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somorrostro "Somorrostro") (1936)

Main article: [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War")

Shortly after the establishment of the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic "Second Spanish Republic"), in 1931, it became the capital of the [autonomous region of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_of_Catalonia_(1931%E2%80%931939) "Autonomous Region of Catalonia (1931–1939)"). During the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War"), the city, and Catalonia in general, were resolutely Republican. Many enterprises and public services were [collectivized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_ownership "Collective ownership") by the [CNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederaci%C3%B3n_Nacional_del_Trabajo "Confederación Nacional del Trabajo") and UGT unions. As the power of the Republican government and the Generalitat diminished, much of the city was under the effective control of anarchist groups. The anarchists lost control of the city to their own allies, the Communists and official government troops, after the street fighting of the Barcelona [May Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Days "May Days").

Barcelona was one of the last places in Spain to fall under [Francoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain "Francoist Spain") control, and became the capital of the Second Spanish Republic in November 1937.

[![Image 30](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Barcelona_bombing_%281938%29.jpg/250px-Barcelona_bombing_%281938%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Barcelona_bombing_(1938).jpg)

Aerial photograph of the [bombing of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Barcelona "Bombing of Barcelona") (1938)

From 16 to 18 March 1938, [Barcelona was bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Barcelona "Bombing of Barcelona") several times by [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy") and [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"), in support of the Francoist-led army. It left at least 1,300 people killed and 2,000 wounded. The airstrikes intended to destroy industrial areas and demoralize the republicans, however civil buildings were also destroyed.

The [fall of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Barcelona_(1939) "Fall of Barcelona (1939)") on 26 January 1939, caused a mass exodus of civilians who fled to the French border. The resistance of Barcelona to [Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco "Francisco Franco")'s _coup d'état_ was to have lasting effects after the defeat of the Republican government. The autonomous institutions of [Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalonia "Catalonia") were abolished,[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-56) and the use of the [Catalan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language "Catalan language") in public life was suppressed. Barcelona remained the second largest city in Spain, at the heart of a region which was relatively industrialized and prosperous, despite the devastation of the civil war. The result was a large-scale immigration from poorer regions of Spain (particularly [Andalusia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusia "Andalusia"), [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia "Murcia") and [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_(Spain) "Galicia (Spain)")), which in turn led to rapid urbanization.

During the [francoist period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain "Francoist Spain"), municipal elections were suspended through all Spain and the mayor was appointed by the central government. Pressure for change was particularly strong in Barcelona, which considered that it had been punished during nearly forty years of Francoism for its support of the Republican government.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Mock2011-57) After Franco's death in 1975, massive peaceful demonstrations on 11 September 1977 assembled over a million people in the streets of Barcelona to call for the restoration of Catalan autonomy. It was granted less than a month later.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Friend2012-58) After the adoption of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Spain "Constitution of Spain"), municipal elections were held again ([1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Barcelona_municipal_election "1979 Barcelona municipal election")).

### Recent history

The development of Barcelona was promoted by two events in 1986: [Spanish accession to the European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_European_Communities_(1973-1993) "History of the European Communities (1973-1993)"), and particularly Barcelona's designation as host city of the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics "1992 Summer Olympics").[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-ParkinSharma1999-59)[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-AbebeBolton2013-60) The process of [urban regeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_regeneration "Urban regeneration") has been rapid, and accompanied by a greatly increased international reputation of the city as a tourist destination. The increased cost of housing has led to a slight decline (−16.6%) in the population over the last two decades of the 20th century as many families move out into the suburbs. This decline has been reversed since 2001, as a new wave of immigration (particularly from Latin America and from [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco")) has gathered pace.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-61)

In 1987, an [ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_(separatist_group) "ETA (separatist group)")[car bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_bomb "Car bomb") at Hipercor [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipercor_bombing "Hipercor bombing") 21 people.

In 1992, Barcelona hosted the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics "1992 Summer Olympics"). The after-effects of this are credited with driving major changes in what had, up until then, been a largely industrial city. As part of the preparation for the games, industrial buildings along the sea-front were demolished and 3 km (2 mi) of beach were created. New construction increased the road capacity of the city by 17%, the sewage handling capacity by 27% and the amount of new green areas and beaches by 78%. Between 1990 and 2004, the number of hotel rooms in the city doubled. Perhaps more importantly, the outside perception of the city was changed making, by 2012, Barcelona the 12th most popular city destination in the world and the 5th amongst European cities.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-62)[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-63)[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-64)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-65)[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-66)

The year 2017 was an intense year in Barcelona. On August 17, Barcelona suffered [a terrorist attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Barcelona_attacks "2017 Barcelona attacks") in the iconic [Rambla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rambla,_Barcelona "La Rambla, Barcelona"), where a van drove into pedestrians, killing 13 people and injuring at least 130 others. The perpetrator fled on foot, killing another person to steal his car, and a [large police operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronos_Operation?action=edit&redlink=1 "Cronos Operation (page does not exist)")[[ca](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispositiu%20Cronos "ca:Dispositiu Cronos")] was started. Massive demonstrations in the city center took place the days after the attack, with the motto "_No tinc por_" ("I am not afraid").[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-67)

Other attacks took place elsewhere in Catalonia. The Prime Minister of Spain, [Mariano Rajoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Rajoy "Mariano Rajoy"), called the attack in Barcelona a [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist "Jihadist") attack. [Amaq News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaq_News_Agency "Amaq News Agency") attributed indirect responsibility for the attack to the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_of_Iraq_and_the_Levant "Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant") (ISIL).[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-68)[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-69)[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-70)

In October 2017, after several years of growing of the [Catalan independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_independence_movement "Catalan independence movement"), with massive demonstrations along the decade, an [independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Catalan_independence_referendum "2017 Catalan independence referendum") took place. It was declared unconstitutional by the [Spanish Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Spain "Constitutional Court of Spain"), however the [Catalan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalitat_de_Catalunya "Generalitat de Catalunya") ignored it and celebrated it. This led to another court ordering the Spanish [Civil Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Guard_(Spain) "Civil Guard (Spain)") and [National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Corps_(Spain) "National Police Corps (Spain)") to dismantle voting centers, however their intervention was not pacific. Barcelona was the scenario of several police oppression and chaos, with sources that reported at least 1,066 injured requiring medical attention.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-71)[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-72) Several [NGOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organization "Non-governmental organization") condemned the police's violence and reported an excessive use of force.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-73)[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-74)

The referendum sparked the [2017–2018 Spanish constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%932018_Spanish_constitutional_crisis "2017–2018 Spanish constitutional crisis"), and [later protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Catalan_protests "2019 Catalan protests") took place.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-75)

In July 2023, Barcelona was announced as the UNESCO-UIA World Capital of Architecture for the 2024–2026 term. This means it will be the hub for discussion around global challenges including culture, heritage, urban planning and architecture. In addition to being the capital through 2026, it will also host the UIA World Congress of Architects for that year. The honour is befitting of Barcelona, as its history is peppered with architectural achievement and various iconic styles and influences. From its ancient Roman roots, to the Gothic and Modernisme movements, Barcelona has thrived through the way it ties together architecture and culture.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-76)

Barcelona is located on the northeast coast of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula"), facing the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea"), on a [plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_of_Barcelona "Plain of Barcelona") approximately 5 km (3 mi) wide limited by the mountain range of [Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Collserola "Serra de Collserola"), the [Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llobregat "Llobregat") river to the southwest and the [Besòs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bes%C3%B2s_(river) "Besòs (river)") river to the north.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-gec_3-77) This plain covers an area of 170 km 2 (66 sq mi),[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-gec_3-77) of which 101 km 2 (39.0 sq mi)[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-guies_estadistiques-78) are occupied by the city itself. It is 120 km (75 mi) south of the [Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenees "Pyrenees") and the [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalonia "Catalonia") border with France.

[Tibidabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibidabo "Tibidabo"), 512 m (1,680 ft) high, offers striking views over the city[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-79) and is topped by the 288.4 m (946.2 ft)[Torre de Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_de_Collserola "Torre de Collserola"), a [telecommunications tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_masts_and_towers "Radio masts and towers") that is visible from most of the city. Barcelona is peppered with small hills, most of them urbanized, that gave their name to the neighbourhoods built upon them, such as [Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Carmel "El Carmel") (267 m or 876 ft), [Putxet](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardines%20del%20Tur%C3%B3%20del%20Putxet "es:Jardines del Turó del Putxet") (es) (181 m or 594 ft) and [Rovira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tur%C3%B3_de_la_Rovira "Turó de la Rovira") (261 m or 856 ft). The escarpment of [Montjuïc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc "Montjuïc") (173 m or 568 ft), situated to the southeast, overlooks the harbour and is topped by [Montjuïc Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc_Castle_(Barcelona) "Montjuïc Castle (Barcelona)"), a fortress built in the 17–18th centuries to control the city as a replacement for the Ciutadella. Today, the fortress is a museum and Montjuïc is home to several sporting and cultural venues, as well as Barcelona's biggest [park and gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_gardens_of_Barcelona "Parks and gardens of Barcelona").

The city borders on the municipalities of [Santa Coloma de Gramenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Coloma_de_Gramenet "Santa Coloma de Gramenet") and [Sant Adrià de Besòs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Adri%C3%A0_de_Bes%C3%B2s "Sant Adrià de Besòs") to the north; the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea") to the east; [El Prat de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Prat_de_Llobregat "El Prat de Llobregat") and [L'Hospitalet de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Hospitalet_de_Llobregat "L'Hospitalet de Llobregat") to the south; and [Sant Feliu de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Feliu_de_Llobregat "Sant Feliu de Llobregat"), [Sant Just Desvern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Just_Desvern "Sant Just Desvern"), [Esplugues de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esplugues_de_Llobregat "Esplugues de Llobregat"), [Sant Cugat del Vallès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Cugat_del_Vall%C3%A8s "Sant Cugat del Vallès"), and [Montcada i Reixac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montcada_i_Reixac "Montcada i Reixac") to the west. The municipality includes two small sparsely-inhabited [exclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave "Exclave") to the north-west.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Barcelona "Climate of Barcelona")

According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification"), Barcelona has a hot summer [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate "Mediterranean climate") (_Csa_), with mild winters and warm to hot summers,[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-80) while the rainiest seasons are autumn and spring. The rainfall pattern is characterized by a short (3 months) [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season "Dry season") in summer, as well as less winter rainfall than in a typical Mediterranean climate. However, both June and August are wetter than February, which is unusual for the Mediterranean climate. This subtype, labelled as "Portuguese" by the French geographer George Viers after the climate classification of [Emmanuel de Martonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_de_Martonne "Emmanuel de Martonne")[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-81) and found in the NW Mediterranean area (e.g. [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille#Climate "Marseille")), can be seen as transitional to the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate#Europe "Humid subtropical climate") (_Cfa_) found in inland areas.

Barcelona is densely populated, thus heavily influenced by the [urban heat island effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island_effect "Urban heat island effect"). Areas outside of the urbanized districts can have as much as 2°C of difference in temperatures throughout the year.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-82) Its average annual temperature is 21.2°C (70.2°F) during the day and 15.1°C (59.2°F) at night. The average annual temperature of the sea is about 20°C (68°F). In the coldest month, January, the temperature typically ranges from 12 to 18°C (54 to 64°F) during the day, 6 to 12°C (43 to 54°F) at night and the average sea temperature is 13°C (55°F).[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-weather2travel-83) In the warmest month, August, the typical temperature ranges from 27 to 31°C (81 to 88°F) during the day, about 23°C (73°F) at night and the average sea temperature is 26°C (79°F).[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-weather2travel-83) Generally, the summer or "holiday" season lasts about six months, from May to October. Two months – April and November – are transitional; sometimes the temperature exceeds 20°C (68°F), with an average temperature of 18–19°C (64–66°F) during the day and 11–13°C (52–55°F) at night. December, January and February are the coldest months, with average temperatures around 15°C (59°F) during the day and 9°C (48°F) at night. Large fluctuations in temperature are rare, particularly in the summer months. Because of the proximity to the warm sea plus the [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island "Urban heat island"), frosts are very rare in the city of Barcelona. Snow is also very infrequent in the city of Barcelona, but light snowfalls can occur yearly in the nearby [Collserola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collserola "Collserola") mountains, such as in the [Fabra Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabra_Observatory "Fabra Observatory") located in a nearby mountain.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-84)

Barcelona averages 78 rainy days per year (≥1 mm), and annual average relative humidity is 72%, ranging from 69% in July to 75% in October. Rainfall totals are highest in late summer and autumn (September–November) and lowest in early and mid-summer (June–August), with a secondary winter minimum (February–March). Sunshine duration is 2,524 hours per year, from 138 (average 4.5 hours of sunshine a day) in December to 310 (average 10 hours of sunshine a day) in July.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-Meteorolog%C3%ADa-85)

As of 2025, the population is 1,713,247, of which 47.9% are males, and 52.1% are females. Minors make up 13.8% of the population, and seniors make up 21.0%.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-population-3)

The city is the main component of an [administrative area of Greater Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%80rea_Metropolitana_de_Barcelona "Àrea Metropolitana de Barcelona"), with a population of 3,218,071 in an area of 636 km 2 (246 sq mi) (density 5,060 inhabitants/km 2). The population of the urban area was 4,840,000.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-demographia-4) It is the central nucleus of the [Barcelona metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_metropolitan_area "Barcelona metropolitan area"), which relies on a population of 5,474,482.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-eurostat-5)

In 1900, Barcelona had a population of 533,000,[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-gec_3-77) which grew steadily but slowly until 1950, when it started absorbing a high number of people from other less-industrialized parts of Spain. Barcelona's population peaked in 1979 at 1,906,998, and fell throughout the 1980s and 1990s as more people sought a higher [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life "Quality of life") in outlying cities in the Barcelona Metropolitan Area. After bottoming out in 2000 with 1,496,266 residents, the city's population began to rise again as younger people started to return, causing a great increase in housing prices.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-96)

### Languages spoken

Catalan is the "[own language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language "National language")"[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-97)[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-98) of Catalonia and Barcelona, and therefore its the main language used by the City Council, in schools and it dominates the [linguistic landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_landscape "Linguistic landscape"). All [toponymy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy "Toponymy") of Barcelona, including the stops of the [metro system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Metro "Barcelona Metro"), are exclusively in the [Catalan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language "Catalan language"). Despite this, Spanish is the most spoken language in Barcelona (according to the linguistic census held by the Government of Catalonia in 2013) and it is understood almost universally. [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language "Catalan language") is also very commonly spoken in the city: it is understood by 95% of the population, while 72.3% can speak it, 79% can read it, and 53% can write it.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-99) Knowledge of Catalan has increased significantly in recent decades thanks to a [language immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_immersion "Language immersion") educational system.

After Catalan and Spanish, the most spoken languages in Barcelona are those from North Africa, such as [Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages "Berber languages") and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic"), followed by [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu"), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language "Punjabi language"), [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese "Mandarin Chinese"), [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language "Romanian language"), English, Russian and [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechuan_languages "Quechuan languages"), according to data collected by the University of Barcelona.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona#cite_note-100)
