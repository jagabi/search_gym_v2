_**The Fifth Estate**_ is a 2013 [biographical](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Biographical_film "Biographical film")[thriller film](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Thriller_film "Thriller film") directed by [Bill Condon](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Bill_Condon "Bill Condon"), about the news-leaking website WikiLeaks. The film stars [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch") as its editor-in-chief and founder Julian Assange, and Daniel Brühl as its former spokesperson Daniel Domscheit-Berg.[[4]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-bcjafirstlook-4)Anthony Mackie, David Thewlis, Alicia Vikander, Stanley Tucci, and Laura Linney are featured in supporting roles.[[5]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-5) The film's screenplay was written by Josh Singer based in-part on Domscheit-Berg's book _Inside WikiLeaks: My Time with Julian Assange at the World's Most Dangerous Website_ (2011), as well as _WikiLeaks: Inside Julian Assange's War on Secrecy_ (2011) by British journalists David Leigh and Luke Harding. The film's name is a reference to people who operate in the manner of journalists outside the normal constraints imposed on the mainstream media.[[6]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-6)

Co-produced by [DreamWorks Pictures](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/DreamWorks_Pictures "DreamWorks Pictures") and [Participant Media](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Participant_Media "Participant Media"), _The Fifth Estate_ premiered at the 2013 Toronto International Film Festival,[[7]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-OpeningFilm-7)[[8]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-TIFF-8) and was released in theaters by [Touchstone Pictures](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Touchstone_Pictures "Touchstone Pictures") in the United States on October 18, 2013,[[9]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-9) with international distribution divided among [Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Motion_Pictures "Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures"), [Reliance Entertainment](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Reliance_Entertainment "Reliance Entertainment"), and independent arrangements by Mister Smith Entertainment.[[10]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-THRJan-10)[[11]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-DisneyPress-11) The film performed poorly at the box office[[12]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-12) and garnered mixed critical reaction, receiving criticism for its screenplay and direction, although the acting was praised, particularly Cumberbatch's performance.[[13]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-RadioT-13)

## Plot[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthe-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Fifth_Estate_%28film%29%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D1&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

The story opens in 2010, with the release of the Afghan War Logs. It then flashes back to 2007, where journalist Daniel Domscheit-Berg meets [Australian](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Australia "Australia") computer hacker Julian Assange for the first time, at the Chaos Communication Congress in [Berlin](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"). Daniel's interest in online activism has led him to Assange, with whom he has corresponded by email. They begin working together on WikiLeaks, a website devoted to releasing information being withheld from the public while retaining anonymity for its sources. Their first major target is a private Swiss bank, Julius Baer, whose Cayman Islands branch has been engaged in illegal activities. Despite Baer's filing of a lawsuit and obtaining an injunction, the judge dissolves the injunction, allowing Julian and Daniel to reclaim the domain name. As their confidence increases, the two push forward in publishing information over the next three years, including secrets on Scientology, revealing Sarah Palin's email account, and the membership list of the British National Party.

At first Daniel enjoys changing the world, viewing WikiLeaks as a noble enterprise and Assange as a mentor. However, the relationship between the two becomes strained over time. Daniel loses his job and problems arise in his relationship, particularly concerning the BNP membership leak, which also revealed the addresses of the people involved, and caused several to lose their jobs. Assange openly mocks Daniel's concerns about these issues, implying his own life has been more troubling. Assange's abrasive manner and actions, such as abandoning Daniel at his parents' house after having accepted their dinner invitation, only deepen the strain further. Interspersed throughout the film are flashbacks hinting at Assange's troubled childhood and involvement in a suspicious cult, and that Assange's obsession with WikiLeaks has more to do with childhood trauma than wanting to improve the world. Daniel begins to fear that Assange may be closer to a con-man than a mentor. He also notices that Assange constantly gives different stories about why his hair is white. Assange at first tells Daniel that WikiLeaks has hundreds of workers, but Daniel later finds out that Daniel and Assange are the only members. Most importantly to Daniel, Assange frequently claims that protecting sources is the website's number one goal. However, Daniel begins to suspect that Assange only cares about protecting sources so people will come forward and that Assange does not actually care who gets hurt by the website, though Assange claims that the harm the website may cause is outweighed by good the leaks create. Daniel's girlfriend tells him that she believes in his cause, but that it's his job to prevent Assange from going too far.

The tensions come to a head when Bradley Manning (later known as Chelsea Manning) leaks hundreds of thousands of documents to WikiLeaks, including the "Collateral Murder" video of an airstrike in Baghdad, the Afghan and Iraq War Logs, and 250,000 US Diplomatic Cables. Assange wants to leak the documents immediately, but Daniel insists that they review the documents first. Later, several major newspapers agree to cooperate with WikiLeaks in releasing the documents while spinning WikiLeaks positively. However, both Daniel and the newspapers require the names in the documents be redacted both to protect sources and to assist in the media spin, to which Assange reluctantly agrees. Daniel realizes that Assange has no intention of following through on this promise and is grooming a right-hand man to replace Daniel. The newspapers release the redacted documents. The resulting media and public uproar forces informants to flee from their countries of residence and many U.S. diplomats to resign. Before Assange can go further, however, Daniel and the other members of the original WikiLeaks team delete the site and block Assange's access to the server.

Daniel later talks with a reporter from _[The Guardian](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The\_Guardian "The Guardian")_, and the two fear that giving Assange such a large platform was a mistake. The reporter tells Daniel that while Assange may be untrustworthy, he had done a good thing by uncovering secret dealing in the government and business world and attempting to protect sources. Daniel also reveals the real reason for Assange's hair colour—that it had been a custom of the cult he had been part of in Australia—and reports that he once accidentally discovered Assange dyeing it that colour.

As the film ends, it is revealed that WikiLeaks is continuing to leak information (with Assange implied to have either regained the site or rebuilt it), and the Manning documents were released with no redactions. Daniel has written a book on his involvement with the organization on which this film was based, and Assange has threatened to sue in retaliation. Assange is shown to be living in the Ecuadorian embassy in [London](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/London "London") to avoid arrest on an outstanding warrant for alleged sex crimes. In an interview, he denounces the two upcoming WikiLeaks films, stating that they will be factually inaccurate (having been partly based on Daniel's book). He informs the viewer that individuals are what the government is afraid of and claims that hiring Daniel was the one mistake he made.

## Production[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthe-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Fifth_Estate_%28film%29%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D3&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

It was reported in March 2011 that [Steven Spielberg](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Steven_Spielberg "Steven Spielberg")'s [DreamWorks Studios](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/DreamWorks_Studios "DreamWorks Studios") had acquired the rights of Domscheit-Berg's book _Inside WikiLeaks: My Time with Julian Assange and the World's Most Dangerous Website_, as well as _WikiLeaks: Inside Julian Assange's War on Secrecy_ by British journalists David Leigh and Luke Harding.[[14]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-14) Spielberg was quick to clarify that he is not involved in any way in the adaptation even though his DreamWorks company would produce the film. In July 2012, reports surfaced that [Jeremy Renner](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Jeremy_Renner "Jeremy Renner") was in talks of playing Julian Assange, and [Bill Condon](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Bill_Condon "Bill Condon") was in negotiations to direct. It was also announced that Josh Singer penned the screenplay. Later that year, _Deadline Hollywood_ broke the news that Renner was out of the running and the studio was seeking [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch") instead.[[15]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-15)[Joel Kinnaman](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Joel_Kinnaman "Joel Kinnaman") was also attached at some point, but reports were proven to be premature. The confirmation of Cumberbatch as the lead and Condon as the director also brought the news that [James McAvoy](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/James_McAvoy "James McAvoy") was in talks to play Daniel Domscheit-Berg.[[16]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-16) McAvoy later dropped out because of scheduling conflicts and Daniel Brühl was eventually cast.

In December 2012, the film's title was reported as _The Man Who Sold the World_[[17]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-17) but with the official press release, it was confirmed that the film's title was actually _The Fifth Estate_.

[Principal photography](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began on January 23, 2013, with Michael Sugar and Steve Golin of [Anonymous Content](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Anonymous_Content "Anonymous Content") serving as producers.[[11]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-DisneyPress-11)[[18]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-18) During filming, Cumberbatch had to wear three different wigs, false teeth and blue contact lenses, in order to reflect Julian Assange's physical characteristics.[[19]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-19)

The film's title sequence, which depicts the history of news communication, took over a year to create.[[20]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-20)

The film was partially shot in Belgium, as can be seen in the movie poster, which contains the Liège-Guillemins railway station.

## Criticism by Assange and Wikileaks[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthe-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Fifth_Estate_%28film%29%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D4&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

Template:Quote box

On January 24, 2013, Assange claimed during a presentation of the Sam Adams Award for Integrity in Intelligence—held at Oxford University—that he had read the screenplay of the film, describing it as a[[21]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-21) "serious propaganda attack on WikiLeaks and the integrity of its staff", as a "lie built upon a lie", and as "fanning the flames for war on Iran".[[22]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-22) The opening scene was inside a putative military complex in Iran and nuclear symbols could be seen. Birgitta Jónsdóttir told the WikiLeaks official [Twitter](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") account, "the Iran scene has been written out, plus the name has been changed. Come with constructive ideas how to improve it".[[23]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-23) Birgitta also tweeted that Assange does not possess the latest version of the script.[[24]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-24)

Although Julian Assange has described the film as a "massive propaganda attack",[[25]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-TG-25) he did discuss the film with [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch"), with Cumberbatch claiming that he's "personally supportive" of the organization.[[26]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-26) Cumberbatch stated that, "No matter how you cut it, he's done us a massive service, to wake us up to the zombielike way we absorb our news."[[27]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-27)[[28]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-28) Since Assange had been living inside the Ecuadorian embassy in London under diplomatic asylum, he and Cumberbatch reportedly communicated via email during filming.[[29]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-29)

Template:Quote box

On September 21, 2013, a script, allegedly the film's screenplay, was released by WikiLeaks, along with commentary labeling the film as "fiction masquerading as fact".[[30]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-THRscript-30) Both Assange and WikiLeaks have stated that neither DreamWorks nor Disney approached them for any consultation on the film.[[25]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-TG-25)[[30]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-THRscript-30)[[31]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-31) Assange elaborated on the matter, "I don't think we are in a situation anymore where an organization like DreamWorks or Disney can succinctly decide that it is going to produce a movie about living people, and living political refugees, and people who are embroiled in a grand jury proceeding in the United States, and just smear, without the cost."[[32]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-32)

Template:Quote box

In October 2013, WikiLeaks published a personal letter that Assange wrote to Cumberbatch in January of that year, in which he commended the actor's talent and good intentions, but requested him to reconsider his involvement with the film, which Assange negatively labeled as "a project that vilifies and marginalises a living political refugee to the benefit of an entrenched, corrupt and dangerous state."[[33]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-THRLetter-33) WikiLeaks described Cumberbatch's reply to Assange as "courteous and considered".[[33]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-THRLetter-33) Cumberbatch also admitted his reservations with the early drafts of the film's script, believing that it portrayed Assange as an antagonistic person.[[34]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-34) In regard to his continued involvement with the film, Cumberbatch stated "I wanted to create a three dimensional portrait of a man far more maligned in the tabloid press than he is in our film to remind people that he is not just the weird, white haired Australian dude wanted in Sweden, hiding in an embassy behind Harrods. But a true force to be reckoned with, achieved the realization of the great ideal."[[35]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-35) In an interview with George Stephanopoulos' show _This Week_, Assange stated that "Cumberbatch tried to ameliorate the script but unfortunately with limited success... though I'm pleased he tried."[[36]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-36)

In February 2014, Andrew O'Hagan, the ghostwriter of Assange's autobiography by Canongate, recalled the activist's reaction to the film in a lecture for the London Review of Books. He stated that Assange wanted him to be a consultant of the film and even suggested splitting his fee between the two of them.[[37]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-37)

[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Motion_Pictures "Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures") distributed _The Fifth Estate_ globally through its [Touchstone Pictures](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Touchstone_Pictures "Touchstone Pictures") label,[[39]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-39) except for territories in Europe, Africa and the Middle East, where the film's rights were sold by Mister Smith Entertainment to independent distributors, including [Entertainment One Films](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Entertainment_One_Films "Entertainment One Films") in the [United Kingdom](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom").[[10]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-THRJan-10)[[40]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-DLSept-40)[[41]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-41) DreamWorks' financial partner, [Reliance Entertainment](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Reliance_Entertainment "Reliance Entertainment"), released the film in [India](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/India "India").[[40]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-DLSept-40)

The film opened the Toronto International Film Festival on September 5, 2013.[[7]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-OpeningFilm-7)[[42]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-42)

## Reception[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthe-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Fifth_Estate_%28film%29%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D8&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

### Box office[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthe-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Fifth_Estate_%28film%29%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D9&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

_The Fifth Estate_ grossed $3.3 million in North America and $5.3 million in other territories for a worldwide total of $8.6 million, against a budget of $28 million.

In its opening weekend, the film came in eighth place with $1.7 million, one of the lowest openings for a DreamWorks release and the worst 2013 debut for a wide release in the [United States](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/United_States "United States").[[44]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-44)[[45]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-45)[[46]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-46)[[47]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-47) The film was a box office bomb in the United States,[[48]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-48)[[49]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-49)[[50]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-50) though according to Disney distribution chief Dave Hollis, the film performed best in major North American cities.[[51]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-51)

### Critical response[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthe-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Fifth_Estate_%28film%29%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D10&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

_The Fifth Estate_ received mixed reviews from film critics, although Cumberbatch's performance as Assange received praise.[[52]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-52) On the review aggregate website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") the film has an approval rating of 37%, based on 173 reviews, with an average rating of 5.4/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "Heavy on detail and melodrama but missing the spark from its remarkable real-life inspiration, _The Fifth Estate_ mostly serves as a middling showcase for Benedict Cumberbatch's remarkable talent."[[53]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-rt-53) On [Metacritic](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which assigns a normalized rating based on reviews, the film has a score of 49 out of 100, based on 42 critics, indicating "mixed to average reviews".[[54]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-54) It received an average grade of "B" from market-research firm [CinemaScore](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore").[[55]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-55)

_[Variety](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Variety\_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")_ wrote "that it primarily hobbles itself by trying to cram in more context-needy material than any single drama should have to bear."[[56]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-VarReview-56)_[The Hollywood Reporter](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The\_Hollywood\_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ compared the film unfavorably to _The Social Network_, adding that "Though it will attract attention at the box office, it is unlikely to appeal broadly to moviegoers who, one suspects, have never been as worked up about WikiLeaks as journalists and governments are."[[57]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-THReview-57) Tim Robey of _The Daily Telegraph_ reciprocated the comparison, elaborating that, "At times, this debt is so obvious that the movie's style feels second-hand: an overeager, slightly shop-worn bombardment of finger-on-the-pulse pop-out graphics, representing the giddy proliferation of voices in the misinformation age by simply filling the screen with text."[[58]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-TelegraphReview-58) Alan Scherstuhl of _The Village Voice_ criticized the film for hewing "so closely to [template](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/template "wikt:template") that it's easy to imagine that paperclip from Microsoft Word popping up on Condon's desktop one day to say, 'It looks like you're directing a techno-thriller. Would you like help?'"[[59]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-The_Village_Voice-59)

Owen Gleiberman of _[Entertainment Weekly](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Entertainment\_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ praised the film, describing it as "a vintage journalism thriller, a nihilistic newspaper drama for the dark digital age."[[60]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-EWReview-60)Peter Bradshaw of _[The Guardian](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The\_Guardian "The Guardian")_ gave a positive review, noting that the film "sticks to the ancient movie tradition of depicting journalists as untamed, quasi-bohemian wild men, showing up late, gruff and unshaven in the office."[[61]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-GuardianReview-61)Mark Kermode's reaction was mixed, praising the film's cast and cinematography, but disliking the direction, writing that "_The Fifth Estate_ feels strangely unfocused, uncertain of how to deal with its slippery enigma."[[62]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-ObserverReview-62)_[Rolling Stone](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/Rolling\_Stone "Rolling Stone")'_ s Peter Travers enjoyed the dynamic between Cumberbatch and Brühl, but disliked the focus of the film's subplot.[[63]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-63)Alan Rusbridger, who worked closely with Assange and is portrayed by Peter Capaldi in the film, describes Cumberbatch as "stunning as Assange. The voice and the slightly jerky, stiff, awkward demeanour are just right."[[64]](https://the-jh-movie-collection-official.fandom.com/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_(film)#cite_note-64)
