 #### [1. Steven Spielberg](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000229/?ref_=ls_t_1) 

    *   Producer
    *   Writer
    *   Director

[Schindler's List (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0108052/?ref_=ls_knf_1)

One of the most influential personalities in the history of cinema, Steven Spielberg is Hollywood's best known director and one of the wealthiest filmmakers in the world. He has an extraordinary number of commercially successful and critically acclaimed credits to his name, either as a director, producer or writer since launching the summer blockbuster with [Jaws (1975)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0073195/?ls), and he has done more to define popular film-making since the mid-1970s than anyone else.  
  
Steven Allan Spielberg was born in 1946 in Cincinnati, Ohio, to Leah Frances (Posner), a concert pianist and restaurateur, and [Arnold Spielberg](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0818577/?ls), an electrical engineer who worked in computer development. His parents were both born to Russian Jewish immigrant families. Steven spent his younger years in Haddon Township, New Jersey, Phoenix, Arizona, and later Saratoga, California. He went to California State University Long Beach, but dropped out to pursue his entertainment career. Among his early directing efforts were Battle Squad (1961), which combined World War II footage with footage of an airplane on the ground that he makes you believe is moving. He also directed [Escape to Nowhere (1961)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0054857/?ls), which featured children as World War Two soldiers, including his sister [Anne Spielberg](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0818576/?ls), and [The Last Gun (1959)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0052991/?ls), a western. All of these were short films. The next couple of years, Spielberg directed a couple of movies that would portend his future career in movies. In 1964, he directed [Firelight (1964)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0059181/?ls), a movie about aliens invading a small town. In 1967, he directed [Slipstream (1967)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0249968/?ls), which was unfinished. However, in 1968, he directed [Amblin' (1968)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0064010/?ls), which featured the desert prominently, and not the first of his movies in which the desert would feature. Amblin' also became the name of his production company, which turned out such classics as [E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0083866/?ls). Spielberg had a unique and classic early directing project, [Duel (1971)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0067023/?ls), with [Dennis Weaver](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0915840/?ls). In the early 1970s, Spielberg was working on TV, directing among others such series as [Rod Serling](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0785245/?ls)'s [Night Gallery (1969)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0065327/?ls), [Marcus Welby, M.D. (1969)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0063927/?ls) and [Murder by the Book (1971)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0066932/?ls). All of his work in television and short films, as well as his directing projects, were just a hint of the wellspring of talent that would dazzle audiences all over the world.  
  
Spielberg's first major directorial effort was [The Sugarland Express (1974)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0072226/?ls), with [Goldie Hawn](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000443/?ls), a film that marked him as a rising star. It was his next effort, however, that made him an international superstar among directors: [Jaws (1975)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0073195/?ls). This classic shark attack tale started the tradition of the summer blockbuster or, at least, he was credited with starting the tradition. His next film was the classic [Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0075860/?ls), a unique and original UFO story that remains a classic. In 1978, Spielberg produced his first film, the forgettable [I Wanna Hold Your Hand (1978)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0077714/?ls), and followed that effort with [Used Cars (1980)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0081698/?ls), a critically acclaimed, but mostly forgotten, [Kurt Russell](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000621/?ls)/[Jack Warden](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0912001/?ls) comedy about devious used-car dealers. Spielberg hit gold yet one more time with [Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0082971/?ls), with [Harrison Ford](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000148/?ls) taking the part of Indiana Jones. Spielberg produced and directed two films in 1982. The first was [Poltergeist (1982)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0084516/?ls), but the highest-grossing movie of all time up to that point was the alien story [E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0083866/?ls). Spielberg also helped pioneer the practice of product placement. The concept, while not uncommon, was still relatively low-key when Spielberg raised the practice to almost an art form with his famous (or infamous) placement of Reese's Pieces in "E.T." Spielberg was also one of the pioneers of the big-grossing special-effects movies, like "E.T." and "Close Encounters", where a very strong emphasis on special effects was placed for the first time on such a huge scale. In 1984, Spielberg followed up "Raiders" with [Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom (1984)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0087469/?ls), which was a commercial success but did not receive the critical acclaim of its predecessor. As a producer, Spielberg took on many projects in the 1980s, such as [The Goonies (1985)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0089218/?ls), and was the brains behind the little monsters in [Gremlins (1984)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0087363/?ls). He also produced the cartoon [An American Tail (1986)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0090633/?ls), a quaint little animated classic. His biggest effort as producer in 1985, however, was the blockbuster [Back to the Future (1985)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0088763/?ls), which made [Michael J. Fox](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000150/?ls) an instant superstar. As director, Spielberg took on the book [The Color Purple (1985)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0088939/?ls), with [Whoopi Goldberg](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000155/?ls) and [Oprah Winfrey](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001856/?ls), with great success. In the latter half of the 1980s, he also directed [Empire of the Sun (1987)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0092965/?ls), a mixed success for the occasionally erratic Spielberg. Success would not escape him for long, though.  
  
The late 1980s found Spielberg's projects at the center of pop-culture yet again. In 1988, he produced the landmark animation/live-action film [Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0096438/?ls). The next year proved to be another big one for Spielberg, as he produced and directed [Always (1989)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0096794/?ls) as well as [Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade (1989)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0097576/?ls), and [Back to the Future Part II (1989)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0096874/?ls). All three of the films were box-office and critical successes. Also, in 1989, he produced the little known comedy-drama [Dad (1989)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0097142/?ls), with [Jack Lemmon](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000493/?ls) and [Ted Danson](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001101/?ls), which got mostly mixed results. Spielberg has also had an affinity for animation and has been a strong voice in animation in the 1990s. Aside from producing the landmark "Who Framed Roger Rabbit", he produced the animated series [Tiny Toon Adventures (1990)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0098929/?ls), [Animaniacs (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0105941/?ls), [Pinky and the Brain (1995)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0112123/?ls), [Freakazoid! (1995)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0111970/?ls), [Pinky, Elmyra & the Brain (1998)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0170996/?ls), [Family Dog (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0105996/?ls) and [Toonsylvania (1998)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0143068/?ls). Spielberg also produced other cartoons such as [The Land Before Time (1988)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0095489/?ls), [We're Back! A Dinosaur's Story (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0108526/?ls), [Casper (1995)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0112642/?ls) (the live action version) as well as the live-action version of [The Flintstones (1994)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0109813/?ls), where he was credited as "Steven Spielrock". Spielberg also produced many Roger Rabbit short cartoons, and many Pinky and the Brain, Animaniacs and Tiny Toons specials. Spielberg was very active in the early 1990s, as he directed [Hook (1991)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0102057/?ls) and produced such films as the cute fantasy [Joe Versus the Volcano (1990)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0099892/?ls) and [An American Tail: Fievel Goes West (1991)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0101329/?ls). He also produced the unusual comedy thriller [Arachnophobia (1990)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0099052/?ls), [Back to the Future Part III (1990)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0099088/?ls) and [Gremlins 2: The New Batch (1990)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0099700/?ls). While these movies were big successes in their own right, they did not quite bring in the kind of box office or critical acclaim as previous efforts. In 1993, Spielberg directed [Jurassic Park (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0107290/?ls), which for a short time held the record as the highest grossing movie of all time, but did not have the universal appeal of his previous efforts. Big box-office spectacles were not his only concern, though. He produced and directed [Schindler's List (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0108052/?ls), a stirring film about the Holocaust. He won best director at the Oscars, and also got Best Picture. In the mid-90s, he helped found the production company DreamWorks, which was responsible for many box-office successes.  
  
As a producer, he was very active in the late 90s, responsible for such films as [The Mask of Zorro (1998)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120746/?ls), [Men in Black (1997)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0119654/?ls) and [Deep Impact (1998)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120647/?ls). However, it was on the directing front that Spielberg was in top form. He directed and produced the epic [Amistad (1997)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0118607/?ls), a spectacular film that was shorted at the Oscars and in release due to the fact that its release date was moved around so much in late 1997. The next year, however, produced what many believe was one of the best films of his career: [Saving Private Ryan (1998)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120815/?ls), a film about World War Two that is spectacular in almost every respect. It was stiffed at the Oscars, losing best picture to [Shakespeare in Love (1998)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0138097/?ls).  
  
Spielberg produced a series of films, including [Evolution (2001)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0251075/?ls), [The Haunting (1999)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0171363/?ls) and [Shrek (2001)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0126029/?ls). he also produced two sequels to [Jurassic Park (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0107290/?ls), which were financially but not particularly critical successes. In 2001, he produced a mini-series about World War Two that definitely *was* a financial and critical success: [Band of Brothers (2001)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0185906/?ls), a tale of an infantry company from its parachuting into France during the invasion to the Battle of the Bulge. Also in that year, Spielberg was back in the director's chair for [A.I. Artificial Intelligence (2001)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0212720/?ls), a movie with a message and a huge budget. It did reasonably at the box office and garnered varied reviews from critics.  
  
Spielberg has been extremely active in films there are many other things he has done as well. He produced the short-lived TV series [SeaQuest 2032 (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0106126/?ls), an anthology series entitled [Amazing Stories (1985)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0088478/?ls), created the video-game series "Medal of Honor" set during World War Two, and was a starting producer of [ER (1994)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0108757/?ls). Spielberg, if you haven't noticed, has a great interest in World War Two. He and [Tom Hanks](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000158/?ls) collaborated on [Shooting War: World War II Combat Cameramen (2000)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0250730/?ls), a documentary about World War II combat photographers, and he produced a documentary about the Holocaust called [Eyes of the Holocaust (2000)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0262468/?ls). With all of this to Spielberg's credit, it's no wonder that he's looked at as one of the greatest ever figures in entertainment.

 #### [2. Christopher Nolan](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0634240/?ref_=ls_t_2) 

    *   Producer
    *   Writer
    *   Director

[Tenet (2020)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt6723592/?ref_=ls_knf_2)

Best known for his cerebral, often nonlinear, storytelling, acclaimed Academy Award winner writer/director/producer Sir Christopher Nolan CBE was born in London, England. Over the course of more than 25 years of filmmaking, Nolan has gone from low-budget independent films to working on some of the biggest blockbusters ever made and became one of the most celebrated filmmakers of modern cinema.  
  
At 7 years old, Nolan began making short films with his father's Super-8 camera. While studying English Literature at University College London, he shot 16-millimeter films at U.C.L.'s film society, where he learned the guerrilla techniques he would later use to make his first feature, [Following (1998)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0154506/?ls), on a budget of around $6,000. The noir thriller was recognized at a number of international film festivals prior to its theatrical release and gained Nolan enough credibility that he was able to gather substantial financing for his next film.  
  
Nolan's second film was [Memento (2000)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0209144/?ls), which he directed from his own screenplay based on a short story by his brother [Jonathan Nolan](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0634300/?ls). Starring [Guy Pearce](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001602/?ls), the film brought Nolan numerous honors, including Academy Award and Golden Globe Award nominations for Best Original Screenplay. Nolan went on to direct the critically acclaimed psychological thriller, [Insomnia (2002)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0278504/?ls), starring [Al Pacino](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000199/?ls), [Robin Williams](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000245/?ls) and [Hilary Swank](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0005476/?ls).  
  
The turning point in Nolan's career occurred when he was awarded the chance to revive the Batman franchise in 2005. In [Batman Begins (2005)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0372784/?ls), Nolan brought a level of gravitas back to the iconic hero, and his gritty, modern interpretation was greeted with praise from fans and critics alike. Before moving on to a Batman sequel, Nolan directed, co-wrote, and produced the mystery thriller [The Prestige (2006)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0482571/?ls), starring [Christian Bale](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000288/?ls) and [Hugh Jackman](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0413168/?ls) as magicians whose obsessive rivalry leads to tragedy and murder.  
  
In 2008, Nolan directed, co-wrote, and produced [The Dark Knight (2008)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0468569/?ls). Co-written with by his brother Jonathan, the film went on to gross more than a billion dollars at the worldwide box office. Nolan was nominated for a Directors Guild of America (D.G.A.) Award, Writers Guild of America (W.G.A.) Award and Producers Guild of America (P.G.A.) Award, and the film also received eight Academy Award nominations. The film is widely considered one of the best comic book adaptations of all times, with [Heath Ledger](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0005132/?ls)'s performance as the Joker receiving an extremely high acclaim. Ledger posthumously became the first Academy Award winning performance in a Nolan film.  
  
In 2010, Nolan captivated audiences with the Sci-Fi thriller [Inception (2010)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1375666/?ls), starring [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000138/?ls) in the lead role, which he directed and produced from his own original screenplay that he worked on for almost a decade. The thought-provoking drama was a worldwide blockbuster, earning more than $800,000,000 and becoming one of the most discussed and debated films of the year, and of all times. Among its many honors, Inception received four Academy Awards and eight nominations, including Best Picture and Best Screenplay. Nolan was recognized by his peers with a W.G.A. Award accolade, as well as D.G.A. and P.G.A. Awards nominations for his work on the film.  
  
As one of the best-reviewed and highest-grossing movies of 2012, [The Dark Knight Rises (2012)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1345836/?ls) concluded Nolan's Batman trilogy. Due to his success rebooting the Batman character, Warner Bros. enlisted Nolan to produce their revamped Superman movie [Man of Steel (2013)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0770828/?ls), which opened in the summer of 2013. In 2014, Nolan directed, wrote, and produced the Science-Fiction epic [Interstellar (2014)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0816692/?ls), starring [Matthew McConaughey](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000190/?ls), [Anne Hathaway](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0004266/?ls) and [Jessica Chastain](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm1567113/?ls). Paramount Pictures and Warner Bros. released the film on November 5, 2014, to positive reviews and strong box-office results, grossing over $670 million dollars worldwide.  
  
In July 2017, Nolan released his acclaimed War epic [Dunkirk (2017)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt5013056/?ls), that earned him his first Best Director nomination at the Academy Awards, as well as winning an additional 3 Oscars. In 2020 he released his mind-bending Sci-Fi espionage thriller [Tenet (2020)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt6723592/?ls) starring [John David Washington](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0913475/?ls) in the lead role. Released during the COVID-19 pandemic, the movie grossed relatively less than Nolan's previous blockbusters, though it did do good numbers compared to other movies in that period of time. Hailed as Nolan's most complex film yet, the film was one of Nolan's less-acclaimed films at the time, yet slowly built a fan-base following in later years.  
  
In July 2023, Nolan released his highly acclaimed biographic drama [Oppenheimer (2023)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt15398776/?ls) starring Nolan's frequent collaborator [Cillian Murphy](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0614165/?ls)- in the lead role for the first time in a Nolan film. The movie was a cultural phenomenon that on top of grossing almost 1 billion dollars at the Worldwide Box office, also swept the 2023/2024 award-season and gave Nolan his first Oscars, BAFTAs, Golden Globes, D.G.A. and P.G.A. Awards, as well as a handful of regional critics-circles awards and a W.G.A. nomination. Cillian's performance as quantum physicist J.  #### [4. Ridley Scott](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000631/?ref_=ls_t_4)

[Blade Runner (1982)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0083658/?ref_=ls_knf_4)

Described by film producer [Michael Deeley](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0214303/?ls) as "the very best eye in the business", director Ridley Scott was born on November 30, 1937 in South Shields, Tyne and Wear. His father was an officer in the Royal Engineers and the family followed him as his career posted him throughout the United Kingdom and Europe before they eventually returned to Teesside. Scott wanted to join the British Army (his elder brother Frank had already joined the Merchant Navy) but his father encouraged him to develop his artistic talents instead and so he went to West Hartlepool College of Art and then London's Royal College of Art where he helped found the film department.  
  
In 1962, he joined the BBC as a trainee set designer working on several high profile series. He attended a trainee director's course while he was there and his first directing job was on an episode of the popular BBC police series [Z Cars (1962)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0129723/?ls), [Error of Judgement (1965)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0753147/?ls). More TV work followed until, frustrated by the poor financial rewards at the BBC, he went into advertising. With his younger brother, [Tony Scott](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001716/?ls), he formed the advertising production company RSA (Ridley Scott Associates) in 1967 and spent the next 10 years making some of the best known and best loved TV adverts ever shown on British television, including a series of ads for Hovis bread set to the music of Dvorak's New World Symphony which are still talked about today ("'e were a great baker were our dad.")  
  
He began working with producer [David Puttnam](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0701298/?ls) in the 1970s developing ideas for feature films. Their first joint endeavor, [The Duellists (1977)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0075968/?ls) won the Jury Prize for Best First Work at Cannes in 1977 and was nominated for the Palm d'Or, more than successfully launching Scott's feature film career. The success of [Star Wars: Episode IV - A New Hope (1977)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0076759/?ls) inspired Scott's interest in making science fiction and he accepted the offer to direct [Dan O'Bannon](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0639321/?ls)'s low budget science fiction horror movie [Alien (1979)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0078748/?ls), a critical and commercial success that firmly established his worldwide reputation as a movie director.  
  
[Blade Runner (1982)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0083658/?ls) followed in 1982 to, at best, a lukewarm reception from public and critics but in the years that followed, its reputation grew - and Scott's with it - as one of the most important sci-fi movies ever made. Scott's next major project was back in the advertising world where he created another of the most talked-about advertising spots in broadcast history when his "1984"-inspired ad for the new Apple Macintosh computer was aired during the Super Bowl on January 22, 1984. Scott's movie career has seen a few flops (notably [Legend (1985)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0089469/?ls) and [1492: Conquest of Paradise (1992)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0103594/?ls)), but with successes like [Thelma & Louise (1991)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0103074/?ls), [Gladiator (2000)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0172495/?ls) and [Black Hawk Down (2001)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0265086/?ls) to offset them, his reputation remains solidly intact.  
  
Ridley Scott was awarded Knight Bachelor of the Order of the British Empire at the 2003 Queen's New Year Honours for his "substantial contribution to the British film industry". On July 3, 2015, he was awarded an honorary doctorate by the Royal College of Art in a ceremony at the Royal Albert Hall in London. He was awarded the BAFTA Fellowship in 2018. BAFTA described him as "a visionary director, one of the great British film-makers whose work has made an indelible mark on the history of cinema. Forty years since his directorial debut, his films continue to cross the boundaries of style and genre, engaging audiences and inspiring the next generation of film talent."

[Star Wars: Episode IV - A New Hope (1977)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0076759/?ref_=ls_knf_7)

George Walton Lucas, Jr. was raised on a walnut ranch in Modesto, California. His father was a stationery store owner and he had three siblings. During his late teen years, he went to Thomas Downey High School and was very much interested in drag racing. He planned to become a professional racecar driver. However, a terrible car accident just after his high school graduation ended that dream permanently. The accident changed his views on life.  
  
He decided to attend Modesto Junior College before enrolling in the University of Southern California film school. As a film student, he made several short films including [Electronic Labyrinth THX 1138 4EB (1967)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0062331/?ls) which won first prize at the 1967-68 National Student Film Festival. In 1967, he was awarded a scholarship by Warner Brothers to observe the making of [Finian's Rainbow (1968)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0062974/?ls) which was being directed by [Francis Ford Coppola](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000338/?ls). Lucas and Coppola became good friends and formed American Zoetrope in 1969. The company's first project was Lucas' full-length version of [THX 1138 (1971)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0066434/?ls). In 1971, Coppola went into production for [The Godfather (1972)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0068646/?ls), and Lucas formed his own company, Lucasfilm Ltd.  
  
In 1973, he wrote and directed the semiautobiographical [American Graffiti (1973)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0069704/?ls) which won the Golden Globe and garnered five Academy Award nominations. This gave him the clout he needed for his next daring venture. From 1973 to 1974, he began writing the screenplay which became [Star Wars: Episode IV - A New Hope (1977)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0076759/?ls). He was inspired to make this movie from Flash Gordon and the Planet of the Apes films. In 1975, he established ILM. (Industrial Light & Magic) to produce the visual effects needed for the movie. Another company called Sprocket Systems was established to edit and mix Star Wars and later becomes known as Skywalker Sound. His movie was turned down by several studios until 20th Century Fox gave him a chance. Lucas agreed to forego his directing salary in exchange for 40% of the film's box-office take and all merchandising rights. The movie went on to break all box office records and earned seven Academy Awards. It redefined the term "blockbuster" and the rest is history.  
  
Lucas made the other Star Wars films and along with [Steven Spielberg](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000229/?ls) created the Indiana Jones series which made box office records of their own. From 1980 to 1985, Lucas was busy with the construction of Skywalker Ranch, built to accommodate the creative, technical, and administrative needs of Lucasfilm. Lucas also revolutionized movie theaters with the THX system which was created to maintain the highest quality standards in motion picture viewing.  
  
He went on to produce several more movies that have introduced major innovations in filmmaking technology. He is chairman of the board of the George Lucas Educational Foundation. In 1992, George Lucas was honored with the Irving G. Thalberg Award by the Board of Governors of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences for his lifetime achievement.  
  
He reentered the directing chair with the production of the highly-anticipated Star Wars prequel trilogy beginning with [Star Wars: Episode I - The Phantom Menace (1999)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120915/?ls) . The films have been polarizing for fans and critics alike, but were commercially successful and have become a part of culture. The animated spin-off series [Star Wars: The Clone Wars (2008)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0458290/?ls) was supervised by Lucas. He sold Lucasfilm to Disney in 2012, making co-chair Kathleen Kennedy president. He has attended the premieres of new Star Wars films and been generally supportive of them.

Jonathan Liebesman was born in Johannesburg, South Africa on September 15, 1976. He would remain in his home country, studying filmmaking at the South African School of Motion Picture Medium and Live Performance, until 1996; that year, he traveled to New York City to visit his cousin who was attending New York University. Soon after the visit, Jonathan enrolled at NYU's Tisch School of the Arts.  
  
For his student first film, Jonathan turned [Roald Dahl](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001094/?ls)'s short story [Genesis and Catastrophe (2000)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0259323/?ls) into a 8-minute, award-winning short that help him land Hollywood representation; accolades included the 2000 Hollywood Film Festival's Young Filmmaker Award and the Austin Film Festival's Student Short Film Award.  
  
Jonathan would make his feature-film debut in 2002 with [Darkness Falls (2003)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0282209/?ls), a dark twist on the Tooth Fairy tale that opened at #1 at the box office despite being critically panned. It also served as the big-screen debut for [Emma Caulfield Ford](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0146536/?ls), long a fan-favorite as Anya Jenkins on [Buffy the Vampire Slayer (1997)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0118276/?ls).  
  
[Rings (2005)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0449092/?ls), his short film that connected [The Ring (2002)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0298130/?ls) and its sequel, was first released as a bonus disc with the re-release of The Ring on DVD. The short caught the attention of [Michael Bay](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000881/?ls) and his production company, Platinum Dunes, and soon Jonathan was installed as the director of the company's project [The Texas Chainsaw Massacre: The Beginning (2006)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0420294/?ls), a prequel to [The Texas Chainsaw Massacre (2003)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0324216/?ls), the widely successful remake of the original.  
  
Initially announced as the director of the Friday the 13th reboot, Jonathan's next feature would instead be [The Killing Room (2009)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1119191/?ls) with [Peter Stormare](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001780/?ls), [Chloë Sevigny](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001721/?ls), [Clea DuVall](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0245112/?ls), [Timothy Hutton](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000459/?ls), [Nick Cannon](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0134244/?ls) and [Shea Whigham](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0924154/?ls). The psychological thriller played the festival circuit before being released on DVD in the United States.

The sci-fi/action film was a worldwide hit and put Jonathan in the running for a handful of big-studio pictures.  
  
Before Battle LA's release, he had handed the job of directing [error] as Warner Bros., though that project remains in development at this time. [Kinsey (2004)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0362269/?ref_=ls_knf_21)

Liam Neeson was born on June 7, 1952 in Ballymena, Northern Ireland, to Katherine (Brown), a cook, and Bernard Neeson, a school caretaker. He was raised in a Catholic household. During his early years, Liam worked as a forklift operator for Guinness, a truck driver, an assistant architect and an amateur boxer. He had originally sought a career as a teacher by attending St. Mary's Teaching College, Newcastle. However, in 1976, Neeson joined the Belfast Lyric Players' Theater and made his professional acting debut in the play "The Risen People". After two years, Neeson moved to Dublin's Abbey Theater where he performed the classics. It was here that he was spotted by director [John Boorman](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000958/?ls) and was cast in the film [Excalibur (1981)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0082348/?ls) as Sir Gawain, his first high-profile film role.  
  
Through the 1980s Neeson appeared in a handful of films and British TV series - including [The Bounty (1984)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0086993/?ls), [A Woman of Substance (1984)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0085114/?ls), [The Mission (1986)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0091530/?ls), and [Duet for One (1986)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0092934/?ls) - but it was not until he moved to Hollywood to pursue larger roles that he began to get noticed. His turn as a mute homeless man in [Suspect (1987)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0094082/?ls) garnered good reviews, as did supporting roles in [The Good Mother (1988)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0095238/?ls) and [High Spirits (1988)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0095304/?ls) - though he also starred in the best-to-be-forgotten [Satisfaction (1988)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0096037/?ls), which also featured a then-unknown [Julia Roberts](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000210/?ls) - but leading man status eluded him until the cult favorite [Darkman (1990)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0099365/?ls), directed by [Sam Raimi](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000600/?ls). From there, Neeson starred in [Under Suspicion (1991)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0105691/?ls) and [Ethan Frome (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0106833/?ls), was hailed for his performance in [Woody Allen](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000095/?ls)'s [Husbands and Wives (1992)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0104466/?ls), and ultimately was picked by [Steven Spielberg](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000229/?ls) to play Oskar Schindler in [Schindler's List (1993)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0108052/?ls). The starring role in the Oscar-winning Holocaust film brought Neeson Academy Award, BAFTA and Golden Globe nominations for Best Actor.  
  
Also in 1993, he made his Broadway debut with a Tony-nominated performance in "Anna Christie", in which he co-starred with his future wife [Natasha Richardson](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001670/?ls). The next year, the two also starred opposite [Jodie Foster](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000149/?ls) in the movie [Nell (1994)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0110638/?ls), and were married in July of that year. Leading roles as the 18th century Scottish Highlander [Rob Roy (1995)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0114287/?ls) and the Irish revolutionary leader [Michael Collins (1996)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0117039/?ls) followed, and soon Neeson was solidified as one of Hollywood's top leading men. He starred in the highly-anticipated [Star Wars: Episode I - The Phantom Menace (1999)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120915/?ls) as Qui-Gon Jinn, received a Golden Globe nomination for [Kinsey (2004)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0362269/?ls), played the mysterious Ducard in [Christopher Nolan](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0634240/?ls)'s [Batman Begins (2005)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0372784/?ls), and provided the voice for Aslan in [The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe (2005)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0363771/?ls).  
  
Neeson found a second surprise career as an action leading man with the release of [Taken (2008)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0936501/?ls) in early 2009, an unexpected box office hit about a retired CIA agent attempting to rescue his daughter from being sold into prostitution. However, less than two months after the release of the film, tragedy struck when his wife Natasha Richardson suffered a fatal head injury while skiing and passed away days afterward. Neeson returned to high-profile roles in 2010 with two back-to-back big-budget films, [Clash of the Titans (2010)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0800320/?ls) and [The A-Team (2010)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0429493/?ls), and returned to the action genre with [Unknown (2011)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1401152/?ls), [The Grey (2011)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1601913/?ls), [Battleship (2012)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1440129/?ls) and [Taken 2 (2012)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1397280/?ls), as well as the sequel [Wrath of the Titans (2012)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1646987/?ls).  
  
Neeson was awarded Officer of the Order of the British Empire in the 1999 Queen's New Year's Honours List for his services to drama. He has two sons from his marriage to Richardson: Micheal Richard Antonio Neeson (born June 22, 1995) and Daniel Jack Neeson (born August 27, 1996).
