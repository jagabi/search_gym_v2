John Francis (Frank) Wheaton achieved two historic firsts. He was the first Black student to graduate from the University of Minnesota Law School and the first Black member of the Minnesota Legislature.

Wheaton moved to Minneapolis in 1893 to attend the University of Minnesota Law School. He paid for law school by working as a hotel waiter and a railroad porter. Despite working at the same time, he finished the two-year law program in a single year, graduating in 1894.

In 1898, Wheaton was elected to the Minnesota House of Representatives. He represented the 42nd District in Minneapolis. During his time in the State Legislature, Wheaton proposed an amendment to the Equal Accommodations Act of 1885 which guaranteed African Americans equal access to all businesses. The bill was signed into law on March 6, 1899.

Frank Wheaton’s story was documented as part of the Minneapolis African American Historic and Cultural Context study.
