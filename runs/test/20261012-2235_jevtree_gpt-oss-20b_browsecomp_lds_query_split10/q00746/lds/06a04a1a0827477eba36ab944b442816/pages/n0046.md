**Saint Paul** (often abbreviated to **St. Paul**) is the [capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States "List of capitals in the United States") of the [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") of [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota") and the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat "County seat") of [Ramsey County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_County,_Minnesota "Ramsey County, Minnesota").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-StP_RC_MMSP-6) As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census "2020 United States census"), the city had a population of 311,527, making it Minnesota's [second-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Minnesota "List of cities in Minnesota") and the [69th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population "List of United States cities by population") in the United States. Saint Paul and neighboring [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") form the core of the [Twin Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities "Twin Cities") metropolitan area, the third most populous in the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States") with around 3.7 million residents.

The [Minnesota State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Capitol "Minnesota State Capitol") and the state government offices sit on a hill next to downtown Saint Paul overlooking a bend in the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River"). Local cultural offerings include the [Science Museum of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Museum_of_Minnesota "Science Museum of Minnesota"), the [Ordway Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordway_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "Ordway Center for the Performing Arts"), and the [Minnesota History Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_History_Center "Minnesota History Center"). Three of the region's professional sports teams play in Saint Paul: the [Minnesota Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Wild "Minnesota Wild") and [Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Frost "Minnesota Frost") (at the [Grand Casino Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Casino_Arena "Grand Casino Arena")) and [Minnesota United FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_United_FC "Minnesota United FC") (at [Allianz Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_Field "Allianz Field")). The minor-league baseball team the [St. Paul Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Saints "St. Paul Saints") play at [CHS Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHS_Field "CHS Field"), 10 miles (16 km) from their major-league affiliate the [Minnesota Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Twins "Minnesota Twins") in downtown Minneapolis.

The Legislative Assembly of the [Minnesota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Territory "Minnesota Territory") established the Town of Saint Paul as its capital near existing [Dakota Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people "Dakota people") settlements in November 1849. Named after a [log chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Cathedral_of_Saint_Paul_(Minnesota) "First Cathedral of Saint Paul (Minnesota)") established by [Lucien Galtier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Galtier "Lucien Galtier"), it remained a town until 1854. The city rose to prominence as the headquarters of 19th-century industrialist [James J. Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J._Hill "James J. Hill")'s railroad empire, with his transcontinental [Great Northern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Railway_(U.S.) "Great Northern Railway (U.S.)") then one of the nation's most dominant. Saint Paul has a [mayor–council government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government "Mayor–council government"). The mayor is [Kaohly Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaohly_Her "Kaohly Her"), who took office in 2026.

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Main article: [History of Saint Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "History of Saint Paul, Minnesota")

[![Image 24](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Indian_Mounds_park.jpg/250px-Indian_Mounds_park.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Indian_Mounds_park.jpg)

A burial mound at [Indian Mounds Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mounds_Park_(Saint_Paul,_Minnesota) "Indian Mounds Park (Saint Paul, Minnesota)")

Burial mounds in present-day [Indian Mounds Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mounds_Park_(Saint_Paul,_Minnesota) "Indian Mounds Park (Saint Paul, Minnesota)") suggest the area was inhabited by the [Hopewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_tradition "Hopewell tradition") Native Americans about 2,000 years ago.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Trimble-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-8) From the early 17th century to 1837, the [Mdewakanton Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mdewakanton "Mdewakanton"), a band of the [Dakota people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people "Dakota people"), lived near the mounds at the village of [Kaposia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaposia "Kaposia") and consider the area encompassing present-day Saint Paul [Bdóte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bd%C3%B3te "Bdóte"), the site of creation for their people.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Trimble-7)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-DakotaLife-9) The Dakota called the area _Imniza-Ska_ ('white cliffs') for its exposed white sandstone cliffs on the river's eastern side.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-NPS-11) The Imniza-Ska were full of caves that were useful to the Dakota. The explorer [Jonathan Carver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Carver "Jonathan Carver") documented the historic Wakan Tipi in the bluff below the burial mounds in 1767. In the [Menominee language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee_language "Menominee language") Saint Paul was called _Sāēnepān-Menīkān_, which means 'ribbon, silk or satin village', suggesting its role in trade throughout the region after the introduction of European goods.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-12)

After the 1803 [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase"), U.S. Army Lieutenant [Zebulon Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebulon_Pike "Zebulon Pike") negotiated approximately 100,000 acres (40,000 ha; 160 sq mi) of land from the indigenous Dakota in 1805 to establish a fort. A military reservation was intended for the confluence of the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River") and [Minnesota rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_River "Minnesota River") on both sides of the Mississippi up to [Saint Anthony Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Anthony_Falls "Saint Anthony Falls"). All of what is now the Highland Park neighborhood was included in this. Pike planned a second military reservation at the confluence of the [St. Croix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Croix_River_(Wisconsin%E2%80%93Minnesota) "St. Croix River (Wisconsin–Minnesota)") and Mississippi rivers.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-13) In 1819, [Fort Snelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Snelling "Fort Snelling") was built at the Minnesota and Mississippi confluence. The 1837 Treaty with the Sioux ceded all tribal lands east of the Mississippi to the U.S. government.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-14)[Chief Little Crow III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoyateduta "Taoyateduta") moved his village, [Kaposia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaposia "Kaposia"), from south of Mounds Park across the river a few miles onto Dakota land.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-15)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-16) Fur traders, explorers, and settlers came to the area for the fort's security. Many were [French-Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Canadians "French-Canadians") who predated American pioneers by some time. A whiskey trade flourished among the squatters and the fort's commander evicted them all from the fort's reservation. Fur trader turned bootlegger ["Pig's Eye" Parrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Parrant "Pierre Parrant"), who set up business just outside the reservation, particularly irritated the commander.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-StP_PE_Schaper-17)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-NPS-11) By the early 1840s, a community had developed nearby that locals called "Pig's Eye" ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"): _L'Œil du Cochon_) or "Pig's Eye Landing" after Parrant's popular tavern.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-StP_PE_Schaper-17) In 1842, a raiding party of Ojibwe attacked the Kaposia encampment south of Saint Paul. A battle ensued where a creek drained into wetlands two miles south of Wakan Tipi.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-18) The creek was thereafter called Battle Creek and is today parkland. In the 1840s-70s the [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis "Métis") brought their oxen and [Red River Carts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Carts "Red River Carts") down Kellogg Street to Lambert's landing to send [buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bison "American Bison") hides to market from the [Red River of the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North "Red River of the North"). Saint Paul was the southern terminus of the [Red River Trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Trails "Red River Trails").

In 1841, Catholic missionary [Lucien Galtier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Galtier "Lucien Galtier") was sent to minister to the French Canadians at Mendota. He had [a chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Cathedral_of_Saint_Paul_(Minnesota) "First Cathedral of Saint Paul (Minnesota)") he named for [St. Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle "Paul the Apostle") built on the bluff above the riverboat landing downriver from Fort Snelling.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-19)[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-20) Galtier informed the settlers that they were to adopt the chapel's name for the settlement and cease the use of "Pig's Eye".[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-StP_PE_Schaper-17) In 1847, New York educator [Harriet Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Bishop "Harriet Bishop") moved to the settlement and opened the city's first school.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Gilman-21) The [Minnesota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Territory "Minnesota Territory") was created in 1849 with Saint Paul as the capital. The U.S. Army made the territory's first improved road, Point Douglas Fort Ripley Military Road, in 1850. It passed through what became Saint Paul neighborhoods.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-22) In 1857, the territorial legislature voted to move the capital to [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter,_Minnesota "St. Peter, Minnesota"), but [Joe Rolette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Rolette "Joe Rolette"), a territorial legislator, stole the text of the bill and went into hiding, preventing the move.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-23)

[![Image 25](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Red_river_ox_cart_and_driver_in_St._Paul.jpg/250px-Red_river_ox_cart_and_driver_in_St._Paul.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Red_river_ox_cart_and_driver_in_St._Paul.jpg)

Red river ox cart and driver in St. Paul

The year 1858 saw more than 1,000 steamboats service Saint Paul,[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Gilman-21) making it a gateway for settlers to the Minnesota frontier or [Dakota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Territory "Dakota Territory"). Geography was a primary reason the city became a transportation hub. The location was the last good point to land riverboats coming upriver due to the river valley's topography. For a time, Saint Paul was called "The Last City of the East".[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-OAH-24) Fort Snelling was important to Saint Paul from the start. Direct access from Saint Paul did not happen until the 7th bridge was built in 1880. Before that, there was a cable ferry crossing dating to at latest the 1840s. Once streetcars appeared, a new bridge to Saint Paul was built in 1904. Until the town built its first jail the fort's brig served Saint Paul. Industrialist [James J. Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J._Hill "James J. Hill") founded his railroad empire in Saint Paul. The [Great Northern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Railway_(U.S.) "Great Northern Railway (U.S.)") and the [Northern Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pacific_Railway "Northern Pacific Railway") were both headquartered in Saint Paul until they merged with the [Burlington Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Northern "Burlington Northern"). Today they are part of the BNSF Railway.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-OAH-24)

On August 20, 1904, severe [thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorm "Thunderstorm") and [tornadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado "Tornado") damaged hundreds of downtown buildings, causing $1.78 million ($63.78 million today) in damages and ripping spans from the [High Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Bridge_(St._Paul) "High Bridge (St. Paul)").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-gendisasters-25) During the 1960s, in conjunction with [urban renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal "Urban renewal"), Saint Paul razed neighborhoods west of downtown for the creation of the interstate freeway system.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-26) From 1959 to 1961, the [Rondo neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondo_neighborhood "Rondo neighborhood") was demolished for the construction of [Interstate 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_94_in_Minnesota "Interstate 94 in Minnesota"). The loss of that [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American "African American") enclave brought attention to [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation "Racial segregation") and unequal housing in northern cities.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-27) The annual [Rondo Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondo_Days "Rondo Days") celebration commemorates the African American community.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-28)

Downtown Saint Paul had skyscraper-building booms beginning in the 1970s. Because the city center is directly beneath the flight path into the airport across the river there is a height restriction for all construction. The tallest buildings, such as [Galtier Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galtier_Plaza "Galtier Plaza") (Jackson and Sibley Towers), The Pointe of Saint Paul condominiums, and the city's tallest building, [Wells Fargo Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo_Place "Wells Fargo Place") (formerly Minnesota World Trade Center), were constructed in the late 1980s.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-29) In the 1990s and 2000s, the tradition of bringing new immigrant groups to the city continued. As of 2004, nearly 10% of the city's population were recent [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people "Hmong people") immigrants from [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), and [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar").[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-30) Saint Paul is the location of the [Hmong Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_Archives "Hmong Archives").[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-ht-31)

[Operation Metro Surge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Metro_Surge "Operation Metro Surge"), a [Department of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Homeland_Security "Department of Homeland Security") operation involving [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement") (ICE) and [United States Customs and Border Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Customs_and_Border_Protection "United States Customs and Border Protection") (CBP), began in December 2025, focused on detaining and deporting [undocumented immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigration "Illegal immigration").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Martin-32) Initially targeting Minneapolis and Saint Paul,[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Martin-32) between 2,000 and 3,000 agents deployed in the state.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-33) Activists took trainings to observe and record ICE actions.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-34) In January 2026, federal officers [killed Renée Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good "Killing of Renée Good") and [Alex Pretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Alex_Pretti "Killing of Alex Pretti") in Minneapolis.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-:1-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-36) Tens of thousands engaged in mostly peaceful protests.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-37) Legal challenges were filed to stop the large-scale deployment of ICE agents.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-38)

## Geography

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Geography")]

[![Image 26](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Meeker_Island_Lock_and_Dam.jpg/250px-Meeker_Island_Lock_and_Dam.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Meeker_Island_Lock_and_Dam.jpg)

The [Meeker Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meeker_Island "Meeker Island") lock and dam was the first lock and dam on the Mississippi River in 1902.

Saint Paul's history and growth as a landing port are tied to water. The city's defining physical characteristic, the confluence of the Mississippi and Minnesota Rivers, was carved into the region during the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_glaciation "Wisconsin glaciation"), as were the steep river bluffs and dramatic palisades on which the city is built. Receding [glaciers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier "Glacier") and [Lake Agassiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Agassiz "Lake Agassiz") forced torrents of water from a [glacial river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_River_Warren "Glacial River Warren") that served the river valleys.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-39) The city is situated in east-central Minnesota.

The Mississippi River forms a municipal boundary on part of the city's west, southwest, and southeast sides. [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"), the state's largest city, lies to the west. [Falcon Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_Heights,_Minnesota "Falcon Heights, Minnesota"), [Lauderdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauderdale,_Minnesota "Lauderdale, Minnesota"), [Roseville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roseville,_Minnesota "Roseville, Minnesota"), and [Maplewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maplewood,_Minnesota "Maplewood, Minnesota") are north, with Maplewood lying to the east. The cities of [West Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_St._Paul,_Minnesota "West St. Paul, Minnesota") and [South Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Saint_Paul "South Saint Paul") are to the south, as are [Lilydale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilydale,_Minnesota "Lilydale, Minnesota"), [Mendota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendota,_Minnesota "Mendota, Minnesota"), and [Mendota Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendota_Heights "Mendota Heights"), across the river from the city. The city's largest lakes are Pig's Eye Lake, which is part of the Mississippi, [Lake Phalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Phalen "Lake Phalen"), and [Lake Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Como_(Minnesota) "Lake Como (Minnesota)"). According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau "United States Census Bureau"), the city has an area of 56.18 square miles (145.51 km 2), of which 51.98 square miles (134.63 km 2) is land and 4.20 square miles (10.88 km 2) is water.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Gazetteer_files-40)

The Parks and Recreation department is responsible for 160 parks and 41 recreation centers.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-41) The city ranked #2 in park access and quality, after only [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"), in the 2018 ParkScore ranking of the top 100 park systems across the United States according to the nonprofit [Trust for Public Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_for_Public_Land "Trust for Public Land").[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-42)

### Climate

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Climate")]

See also: [Climate of the Twin Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_Twin_Cities "Climate of the Twin Cities") and [Climate of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Minnesota "Climate of Minnesota")

[![Image 27](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Saint_paul_mn.jpg/250px-Saint_paul_mn.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_paul_mn.jpg)

The city skyline from the southwest in the winter

Saint Paul has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate "Humid continental climate") typical of the [Upper Midwestern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest "Upper Midwest"). Winters are frigid and snowy, while summers are warm to hot and humid. On the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification"), Saint Paul falls in the hot summer [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate "Humid continental climate") zone (_Dfa_ bordering on _Dwa_). The city experiences a full range of precipitation and related weather events, including snow, [sleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_and_snow_mixed "Rain and snow mixed"), ice, rain, [thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorm "Thunderstorm"), [tornadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado "Tornado"), and [fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fog "Fog").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-45)

Due to its northerly location and lack of large bodies of water to moderate the air, Saint Paul is sometimes subjected to cold Arctic [air masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_mass "Air mass"), especially during late December, January, and February. The average annual temperature of 46.5°F (8.1°C) gives the Minneapolis−Saint Paul metropolitan area the coldest annual mean temperature of any major metropolitan area in the [continental U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_United_States#Continental_and_mainland_United_States "Contiguous United States")[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-46)

Saint Paul is expected to be affected by climate change. More extreme heat waves are expected, as is increased precipitation in the spring and summer, which could cause river and flash flooding. Vector-borne transmission of such diseases as [West Nile virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Nile_virus "West Nile virus"), [Lyme disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyme_disease "Lyme disease"), and human [anaplasmosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaplasmosis "Anaplasmosis") may increase due to changes in temperature and precipitation patterns.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-47)

### 2020 census

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 2020 census")]

As of the [census of 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census "2020 United States census"),[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-2020-census-2758000-57) the population was 311,527. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density "Population density") was 5,994.0 inhabitants per square mile (2,314.3/km 2). There were 127,392 housing units at an average density of 2,451.1 per square mile (946.4/km 2). In terms of [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States_census "Race and ethnicity in the United States census"), the city's population was 50.5% White (21.1% [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans "German Americans")), 19.2% Asian (10.9% [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_Americans "Hmong Americans"), 2.53% [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_Americans "Burmese Americans"), 0.85% [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Americans "Vietnamese Americans"), 0.69% [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Americans "Chinese Americans"), 0.51% [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Americans "Indian Americans")), 16.8% Black or African American (1.7% [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Americans "Somali Americans"), 1.5% [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans "Ethiopian Americans")), 1.0% Native American, 4.8% from other races and 7.6% from two or more races. Residents of Hispanic or Latino ancestry, of any race, made up 9.7% of the population (6.58% [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans "Mexican Americans"), 0.68% [Salvadoran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Americans "Salvadoran Americans")).[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-statisticalatlas.com-58)

The 2020 census of the city included 291 people incarcerated in adult correctional facilities and 5,640 people in student housing.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-2020-P5-2758000-59)

According to the [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey "American Community Survey") estimates for 2016–2020, the median income for a household in the city was $59,717, and the median income for a family was $74,852. Male full-time workers had a median income of $50,186 versus $45,541 for female workers. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income "Per capita income") was $32,779. About 13.2% of families and 17.9% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_threshold "Poverty threshold"), including 27.0% of those under age 18 and 10.1% of those age 65 or over.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-2020-EconChar-2758000-60) Of the population age 25 and over, 87.6% were high school graduates or higher and 41.3% had a bachelor's degree or higher.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-2020-SocChar-2758000-61)

### 2010 census

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: 2010 census")]

As of the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census "2010 United States census"),[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-wwwcensusgov-62) there were 285,068 people, 111,001 households, and 59,689 families residing in the city. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density "Population density") was 5,484.2 inhabitants per square mile (2,117.5/km 2). There were 120,795 housing units at an average density of 2,323.9 per square mile (897.3/km 2). The [racial makeup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States_census "Race and ethnicity in the United States census") of the city was 60.1% White, 15.7% African American, 1.1% Native American, 15.0% Asian, 0.1% Pacific Islander, 3.9% from other races, and 4.2% from two or more races. Hispanic or Latino people of any race were 9.6% of the population.

There were 111,001 households, of which 30.4% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 34.1% were [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage "Marriage") living together, 14.8% had a female householder with no husband present, 4.9% had a male householder with no wife present, and 46.2% were non-families. 35.8% of all households were made up of individuals, and 8.5% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.47 and the average family size was 3.33.

The median age in the city was 30.9 years. 25.1% of residents were under the age of 18; 13.9% were between the ages of 18 and 24; 29.6% were from 25 to 44; 22.6% were from 45 to 64; and 9% were 65 years of age or older. The gender makeup of the city was 48.9% male and 51.1% female.

### Ethnic history

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Ethnic history")]

The earliest known inhabitants of the Saint Paul area, from about 400 AD, were members of the [Hopewell tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_tradition "Hopewell tradition"), who buried their dead in mounds on the river bluffs (now [Indian Mounds Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mounds_Park_(Saint_Paul,_Minnesota) "Indian Mounds Park (Saint Paul, Minnesota)")). The next known inhabitants were the [Mdewakanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mdewakanton "Mdewakanton")[Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux "Sioux") in the 17th century, who fled their ancestral home of [Mille Lacs Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mille_Lacs_Lake "Mille Lacs Lake") in central Minnesota in response to westward expansion of the [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe "Ojibwe") nation.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-DakotaLife-9) The Ojibwe later occupied the north (east) bank of the Mississippi River.

By 1800, [French-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Canadian "French-Canadian") explorers came through the region and attracted fur traders. [Fort Snelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Snelling "Fort Snelling") and Pig's Eye Tavern also brought the first Yankees from [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England "New England") and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people "English people"), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_people "Irish people"), and [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people "Scottish people")[immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant "Immigrant"), who had enlisted in the army and settled nearby after discharge. These early settlers and entrepreneurs built houses on the heights north of the river. The first wave of immigration came with the Irish, who settled at [Connemara Patch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connemara_Patch "Connemara Patch") along the Mississippi, named for their home, [Connemara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connemara "Connemara"), Ireland. The Irish [became prolific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Irish_in_Saint_Paul "History of the Irish in Saint Paul") in politics, city governance, and public safety, much to the chagrin of the Germans and French, who had grown into the majority. In 1850, the first of many groups of [Swedish immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_people "Swedish people") passed through Saint Paul on their way to farming communities in northern and western regions of the [territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Territory "Minnesota Territory"). A large group settled in [Swede Hollow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swede_Hollow "Swede Hollow"), which later became home to Poles, Italians, and Mexicans. The last Swedish presence moved up St. Paul's East Side along [Payne Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payne_Avenue_State_Bank "Payne Avenue State Bank") in the 1950s.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-63)

Of people who specified [European ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_American "European American") in the 2005–07 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey "American Community Survey") of St. Paul, 26.4% were [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American "German American"), 13.8% [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American "Irish American"), 8.4% [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_American "Norwegian American"), 7.0% [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_American "Swedish American"), and 6.2% [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American "English American"). There is also a visible community of people of [Sub-Saharan African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa") ancestry, representing 4.2% of the population.

By the 1980s, the Thomas-Dale area, once an Austro-Hungarian enclave known as [Frogtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogtown,_Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "Frogtown, Saint Paul, Minnesota") (German: _Froschburg_), became home to Vietnamese and other Southeast Asian people who had left their war-torn countries. A settlement program for the [Hmong diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_diaspora "Hmong diaspora") came soon after, and by 2000, St. Paul had the largest urban Hmong contingent in the nation.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-64)[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-65)[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-66)[Hmong Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_Americans "Hmong Americans") make up 11% of Saint Paul's population as of 2021. Saint Paul, as well as the Twin Cities area in general, is considered the center of Hmong culture in the U.S. Hmongs are most concentrated in the neighborhoods of Frogtown, [Payne-Phalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payne-Phalen,_Saint_Paul "Payne-Phalen, Saint Paul"), [Dayton's Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton's_Bluff,_Saint_Paul "Dayton's Bluff, Saint Paul"), the [North End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_End,_Saint_Paul "North End, Saint Paul"), and the [Greater East Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_East_Side,_Saint_Paul "Greater East Side, Saint Paul"),[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-statisticalatlas.com-58) which are considered ethnic enclaves for [Hmong Minnesotans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Hmong_in_Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul "History of the Hmong in Minneapolis–Saint Paul"), with a large number of businesses, organizations, and events catering to the Hmong population, such as the [Hmongtown Marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmongtown_Marketplace "Hmongtown Marketplace") in Frogtown.

Other large Southeast Asian populations live in Saint Paul, particularly [Burmese Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_Americans "Burmese Americans") of the [Karen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_people "Karen people") and [Karenni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenni_people "Karenni people") ethnic group, who immigrated to the U.S. as refugees in the 2000s and 2010s due to [internal conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_conflict "Myanmar conflict") and discrimination in [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"). Minnesota is believed to have the largest population of [Karen Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Americans "Karen Americans"), with a population of 12,000 in 2017,[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-67) who are mostly concentrated in Saint Paul. Burmese and Karen residents of Saint Paul make up 5.2% of the population in 2021, and are most concentrated in the neighborhoods of the [North End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_End,_Saint_Paul "North End, Saint Paul"), [Payne-Phalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payne-Phalen,_Saint_Paul "Payne-Phalen, Saint Paul"), and [Frogtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogtown,_Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "Frogtown, Saint Paul, Minnesota").[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-statisticalatlas.com-58)

Mexican immigrants have settled in Saint Paul since the 1930s; although Mexican populations exist throughout Saint Paul, by far the largest concentration of [Mexican Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans "Mexican Americans") is on Saint Paul's [West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side,_Saint_Paul "West Side, Saint Paul"), where Mexicans form a plurality of the population; Mexico opened a foreign consulate there in 2005.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-68)[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-69) Saint Paul also has a large population of Central Americans, particularly [Salvadorans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Americans "Salvadoran Americans"), throughout eastern St. Paul and the West Side.

St. Paul has become home to a large number of [Somalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Americans "Somali Americans") and [Ethiopians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans "Ethiopian Americans") since the 1990s, largely as refugees fleeing conflict in their home regions. Somali and Ethiopian populations are largest in the neighborhoods of [Summit-University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit-University,_Saint_Paul "Summit-University, Saint Paul") and Frogtown, where there are many businesses and organizations for Somali and Ethiopian populations.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-statisticalatlas.com-58)

[African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") in Saint Paul initially entered through servitude to officers at Fort Snelling, marking a crucial point in their history. Despite the absence of legal slavery in Minnesota, Army officers were permitted to bring their enslaved individuals into the region.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-70) Today, African Americans are one of the largest groups among Saint Paul's population; African Americans make up approximately 14% of Saint Paul's population, the second-largest background group, before Hmongs and after German Americans. The city's African American residents are concentrated in its central and eastern neighborhoods.

Most Saint Paul residents claiming religious affiliation are [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), split between the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church "Roman Catholic Church") and various [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant") denominations. The Roman Catholic presence comes from Irish, German, Scottish, and French Canadian settlers, later bolstered by Hispanic immigrants. There are [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism")[synagogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue "Synagogue") such as [Mount Zion Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Zion_Temple "Mount Zion Temple") and significant populations of [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), and [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism").[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-71) Among [neopagans in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neopaganism_in_Minnesota "Neopaganism in Minnesota"), the city has been dubbed "paganistan" due to its large [Wiccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicca "Wicca") population.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-72)

## Economy

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Economy")]

[![Image 31](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/MN_Ford_plant.jpg/250px-MN_Ford_plant.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MN_Ford_plant.jpg)

The [Ford Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company")'s [Twin Cities Assembly Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_Assembly_Plant "Twin Cities Assembly Plant") in 2006

The Minneapolis–Saint Paul–Bloomington area employs 1,570,700 people in the private sector as of July 2008, 82.43% of whom work in private service providing-related jobs.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-73)

Major corporations headquartered in Saint Paul include [Ecolab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecolab "Ecolab"), a chemical and cleaning product company[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-74) that the _Minneapolis/St. Paul Business Journal_ named in 2008 as the eighth-best place to work in the Twin Cites for companies with 1,000 full-time Minnesota employees,[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-75) and [Securian Financial Group Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securian_Financial_Group_Inc. "Securian Financial Group Inc.")[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-76)

The [3M Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M_Company "3M Company") moved to St. Paul in 1910. It built a [headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M_Administration_Building "3M Administration Building") in 1940 that still stands and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Headquarters operations moved to the suburban Maplewood campus in 1962; manufacturing continued until 2009, when the last St. Paul operations ceased.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-77)

The city was home to the [Ford Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company")'s [Twin Cities Assembly Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_Assembly_Plant "Twin Cities Assembly Plant"), which opened in 1924 and closed at the end of 2011. The plant was in [Highland Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Saint_Paul "Highland Park, Saint Paul") on the Mississippi River, adjacent to [Lock and Dam No. 1, Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_and_Dam_No._1,_Mississippi_River "Lock and Dam No. 1, Mississippi River"), which generates hydroelectric power.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-78) The site is being redeveloped into a mixed-used area called [Highland Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Bridge_(development)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Highland Bridge (development) (page does not exist)") which, when complete, will include 3,800 housing units, most opening in 2023.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-79)

Saint Paul has financed city development with [tax increment financing (TIF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_increment_financing "Tax increment financing"). In 2018, it had 55 TIF districts. Projects that have benefited from TIF funding include the [St. Paul Saints stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHS_Field "CHS Field"), and the [affordable housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing "Affordable housing") along the Twin Cities [Metro Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Green_Line_(Minnesota) "Metro Green Line (Minnesota)").[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-80)

### Housing

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Housing")]

In November 2021, Saint Paul became the only Midwestern city to regulate rent increases when voters passed a [rent control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_control "Rent control") ordinance as part of a larger effort to curb rising housing costs.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-passed_law-81)[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-reformer_rent-82) The law limited annual rent increases to 3% and prohibited higher increases after a tenant vacated a unit.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-reformer_rent-82) This resulted in an 80% reduction in requests for new housing permits, while in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"), permits were up 70%.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Reason_2022-03-22-83)[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-MinnPost_2022-03-16-84) Saint Paul's rent control initiative has since been significantly rolled back.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-85) In September 2022, the Saint Paul City Council amended the law to allow higher vacancy increases and exempt units built in the preceding or following 20 years from the increase cap.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Star_Tribune,_September_20th,_2022-86)[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-Pioneer_Press,_September_21,_2022-87) Since then, according to [Zillow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zillow "Zillow") estimates, St. Paul's rents have grown at a pace similar to Minneapolis's, since the ordinance gives landlords several options to raise rents by more than 3%.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-88)

As of the 2020 U.S. census, Saint Paul had about 134,080 housing units and 126,409 households, with an average of 2.3 persons per household. The median value of owner-occupied homes was approximately $293,500, and tenure is nearly evenly split between owner- and renter-occupied units.[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-89)

## Culture

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paul,_Minnesota&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Culture")]

[![Image 32](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Comoplant.jpg/250px-Comoplant.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Comoplant.jpg)

[Como Park Zoo and Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como_Park_Zoo_and_Conservatory "Como Park Zoo and Conservatory") is a free public greenhouse and urban zoo open year-round.

Every January, Saint Paul hosts the [Saint Paul Winter Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul_Winter_Carnival "Saint Paul Winter Carnival"), a tradition that began in 1886 when a New York reporter called Saint Paul "another [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia")".[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-RCHS2023-90) The organizers had a model in the [Montreal Winter Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Winter_Carnivals "Montreal Winter Carnivals") the year before. Architect A. C. Hutchinson designed the Montreal ice castle and was hired to design St. Paul's first.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-91) The event has now been held 135 times with an attendance of 350,000. It includes an [ice sculpting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_sculpture "Ice sculpture") competition, a snow sculpting competition, a medallion [treasure hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul_Winter_Carnival#Medallion_Hunt "Saint Paul Winter Carnival"), food, activities, and an ice palace when it can be arranged.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-92) The [Como Zoo and Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como_Zoo_and_Conservatory "Como Zoo and Conservatory") and adjoining Japanese Garden are popular year-round. The historic [Landmark Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmark_Center_(St._Paul) "Landmark Center (St. Paul)") in downtown Saint Paul hosts cultural and arts organizations. The city's recreation sites include [Indian Mounds Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mounds_Park_(Saint_Paul,_Minnesota) "Indian Mounds Park (Saint Paul, Minnesota)"), Battle Creek Regional Park, [Harriet Island Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Island_Regional_Park "Harriet Island Regional Park"), [Highland Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Saint_Paul "Highland Park, Saint Paul"), the [Wabasha Street Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabasha_Street_Caves "Wabasha Street Caves"), [Lake Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Como_(Minnesota) "Lake Como (Minnesota)"), [Lake Phalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Phalen "Lake Phalen"), and [Rice Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_Park "Rice Park"), as well as several areas abutting the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River"). The [Irish Fair of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Irish_in_Saint_Paul "History of the Irish in Saint Paul") is held annually at the [Harriet Island Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Island_Pavilion "Harriet Island Pavilion") area. The country's largest Hmong American sports festival, the Freedom Festival, is held the first weekend of July at McMurray Field near Como Park.

The city is associated with the [Minnesota State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Fair "Minnesota State Fair") just west of Como Park; the Fairgrounds are in [Falcon Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_Heights,_Minnesota "Falcon Heights, Minnesota"), not St. Paul proper, but have a St. Paul address.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-93)

[![Image 33](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Minnesota_Centennial_Showboat_at_night%2C_October_2013.jpg/250px-Minnesota_Centennial_Showboat_at_night%2C_October_2013.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Minnesota_Centennial_Showboat_at_night,_October_2013.jpg)

The [Minnesota Centennial Showboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Centennial_Showboat "Minnesota Centennial Showboat") was anchored in the Mississippi River along Harriet Island.

Saint Paul is the birthplace of [cartoonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoonist "Cartoonist")[Charles M. Schulz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_M._Schulz "Charles M. Schulz"), who lived in Merriam Park from infancy until 1960.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-94) Schulz's _[Peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanuts "Peanuts")_ inspired giant, decorated sculptures around the city, a Chamber of Commerce promotion in the late 1990s.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-95) Other notable residents include writer [F. Scott Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Scott_Fitzgerald "F. Scott Fitzgerald") and playwright [August Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Wilson "August Wilson"), who premiered many of the ten plays in his [Pittsburgh Cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Wilson#The_Pittsburgh_Cycle "August Wilson") at the local Penumbra Theater.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-96)

The [Ordway Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordway_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "Ordway Center for the Performing Arts") hosts theater productions and the [Minnesota Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Opera "Minnesota Opera") is a founding tenant.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-97)[RiverCentre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RiverCentre "RiverCentre"), attached to [Grand Casino Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Casino_Arena "Grand Casino Arena"), serves as the city's convention center. The city has contributed to the [music of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Minnesota "Music of Minnesota") and the Twin Cities music scene through various venues. Great jazz musicians have passed through the influential [Artists' Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists'_Quarter "Artists' Quarter"), first established in the 1970s in [Whittier, Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittier,_Minneapolis "Whittier, Minneapolis"), and moved to downtown Saint Paul in 1994.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-98) Artists' Quarter also hosts the Soapboxing Poetry Slam, home of the 2009 [National Poetry Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Poetry_Slam "National Poetry Slam") Champions. At The Black Dog, in Lowertown, many French or European jazz musicians (Evan Parker, Tony Hymas, Benoît Delbecq, François Corneloup) have met Twin Cities musicians and started new groups touring in Europe. Groups and performers such as Fantastic Merlins, Dean Magraw/Davu Seru, Merciless Ghosts, and [Willie Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Murphy_(musician) "Willie Murphy (musician)") are regulars. The [Turf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turf_Club_(Saint_Paul) "Turf Club (Saint Paul)") in Midway has been a music scene landmark since the 1940s.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-99) Saint Paul is also the home base of the internationally acclaimed [Rose Ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Ensemble "Rose Ensemble").[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-100) As an Irish stronghold, the city boasts popular Irish pubs with live music, such as Shamrocks, The Dubliner, and until its closure in 2019, O'Gara's.[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-101) The internationally acclaimed [Saint Paul Chamber Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul_Chamber_Orchestra "Saint Paul Chamber Orchestra") is the nation's only full-time professional chamber orchestra.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-102) The [Minnesota Centennial Showboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Centennial_Showboat "Minnesota Centennial Showboat") on the Mississippi River began in 1958 with Minnesota's first centennial celebration.[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-103)

Saint Paul has a number of museums, including the University of Minnesota's Goldstein Museum of Design,[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-104) the [Minnesota Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Children's_Museum "Minnesota Children's Museum"),[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-105) the Schubert Club Museum of Musical Instruments,[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-106)[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-107) the [Minnesota Museum of American Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Museum_of_American_Art "Minnesota Museum of American Art"),[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-108)[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-109) the Traces Center for History and Culture,[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-110) the [Minnesota History Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_History_Center "Minnesota History Center"), the [Alexander Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Ramsey "Alexander Ramsey") House, the [James J. Hill House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J._Hill_House "James J. Hill House"), the [Minnesota Transportation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Transportation_Museum "Minnesota Transportation Museum"), the [Science Museum of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Museum_of_Minnesota "Science Museum of Minnesota"), and the Twin City Model Railroad Museum.

Saint Paul hosts a number of professional, semi-professional, and amateur sports teams. The [Minnesota Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Wild "Minnesota Wild") play their home games at downtown Saint Paul's [Grand Casino Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Casino_Arena "Grand Casino Arena"), which opened in 2000. The Wild brought the NHL back to Minnesota for the first time since 1993, when the [Minnesota North Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_North_Stars "Minnesota North Stars") left the state for [Dallas, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas,_Texas "Dallas, Texas").[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-StP_Hockey_returns-112) The [World Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Association "World Hockey Association")'s [Minnesota Fighting Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Fighting_Saints "Minnesota Fighting Saints") played in Saint Paul from 1972 to 1977. Citing the history of hockey in the Twin Cities and teams at all levels, _[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports\_Illustrated "Sports Illustrated")_ called Saint Paul the new [Hockeytown U.S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockeytown "Hockeytown") in 2007.[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-113)

The Grand Casino Arena, a multipurpose entertainment and sports venue, can host concerts and accommodate nearly all sporting events. The Grand Casino Arena hosts the [Minnesota high school boys hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_high_school_boys_hockey "Minnesota high school boys hockey") tournament, the Minnesota high school girls' volleyball tournament, and concerts throughout the year. In 2004, it was named the best overall sports venue in the US by [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN "ESPN").[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota#cite_note-114)
