**Hubert Horatio Humphrey Jr.** (May 27, 1911 – January 13, 1978) was an American politician who served from 1965 to 1969 as the 38th [vice president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president_of_the_United_States "Vice president of the United States"). He twice served in the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate"), representing [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota") from 1949 to 1964 and from 1971 to 1978. As a senator, he was a major leader of [modern liberalism in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States "Modern liberalism in the United States"). As President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson")'s vice president, he supported the controversial [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). An intensely divided [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Democratic_Party_(United_States) "History of the Democratic Party (United States)")[nominated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries "1968 Democratic Party presidential primaries") him in the [1968 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_United_States_presidential_election "1968 United States presidential election"), which he narrowly lost to [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Republican_Party_(United_States) "History of the Republican Party (United States)") nominee [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon").

Born in [Wallace, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace,_South_Dakota "Wallace, South Dakota"), Humphrey attended the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota"). In 1943, he became a professor of political science at [Macalester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College "Macalester College") and ran a failed campaign for [mayor of Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Minneapolis "List of mayors of Minneapolis"). He helped found the [Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party "Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party") (DFL) in 1944; the next year he was elected mayor of Minneapolis, serving until 1948 and co-founding the [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States "Progressivism in the United States")[non-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Stalinist_left "Anti-Stalinist left") group [Americans for Democratic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_for_Democratic_Action "Americans for Democratic Action") in 1947. In 1948, he was elected to the U.S. Senate and successfully advocated for the inclusion of a proposal to end [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation "Racial segregation") in the [1948 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Democratic_National_Convention "1948 Democratic National Convention")'s party platform.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Smithsonian_1948_DNC-1)

Humphrey served three terms in the Senate from 1949 to 1964, and was the [Senate Majority Whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Majority_Whip "Senate Majority Whip") for the last four years of his tenure. During this time, he was the lead author of the [Civil Rights Act of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964 "Civil Rights Act of 1964"), introduced the first initiative to create the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps "Peace Corps"), and chaired the [Select Committee on Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Select_Committee_on_Disarmament "United States Senate Select Committee on Disarmament"). He unsuccessfully sought his party's presidential nomination in [1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries,_1952 "Democratic Party presidential primaries, 1952") and [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries,_1960 "Democratic Party presidential primaries, 1960"). After Lyndon B. Johnson acceded to the presidency, he chose Humphrey as his running mate, and the Democratic ticket won a landslide victory in the [1964 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election "1964 United States presidential election").

In March 1968, Johnson made his surprise announcement that he [would not seek reelection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_Lyndon_B._Johnson_from_the_1968_United_States_presidential_election "Withdrawal of Lyndon B. Johnson from the 1968 United States presidential election"), and Humphrey launched [his campaign for the presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey_1968_presidential_campaign "Hubert Humphrey 1968 presidential campaign"). Loyal to the Johnson administration's policies on the Vietnam War, he was opposed by many within his party and avoided the [primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries,_1968 "Democratic Party presidential primaries, 1968") to focus on winning the delegates of non-primary states at the [Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Democratic_National_Convention "1968 Democratic National Convention"). His delegate strategy succeeded in clinching the nomination, and he chose Senator [Edmund Muskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Muskie "Edmund Muskie") as his running mate. In the general election, he nearly matched Nixon's tally in the popular vote but lost the [electoral vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(United_States) "Electoral College (United States)") by a wide margin. After the defeat, he [returned to the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "1970 United States Senate election in Minnesota") and served from 1971 until his death in 1978. He ran again in the [1972 Democratic primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries,_1972 "Democratic Party presidential primaries, 1972") but lost to [George McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern "George McGovern") and declined to be McGovern's running mate.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Nixonland-2) From 1977 to 1978, he served as the [deputy president pro tempore of the United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_President_pro_tempore_of_the_United_States_Senate "Deputy President pro tempore of the United States Senate").

## Early life and education

Hubert Horatio Humphrey Jr. was born in a room over his father's drugstore in [Wallace, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace,_South_Dakota "Wallace, South Dakota").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198435-3) He was the son of Ragnild Kristine Sannes (1883–1973), a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_American "Norwegian American") immigrant,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-4) and Hubert Horatio Humphrey Sr. (1882–1949).[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Family_Tree-5) His paternal ancestors migrated from England to the Massachusetts Bay Colony around 1648.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-6) Humphrey spent most of his youth in [Doland, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doland,_South_Dakota "Doland, South Dakota"), on the Dakota prairie; the town's population was about 600. His father was a licensed pharmacist and merchant who served as mayor and a town council member. The father also served briefly in the South Dakota state legislature and was a South Dakota delegate to the [1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Democratic_National_Convention "1944 Democratic National Convention") and [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Democratic_National_Convention "1948 Democratic National Convention") Democratic National Conventions.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198441-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198453-8) In the late 1920s, a severe economic downturn hit Doland; both banks in the town closed and Humphrey's father struggled to keep his store open.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198444-9)

[![Image 7](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Humphrey-pharmacy.jpg/250px-Humphrey-pharmacy.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Humphrey-pharmacy.jpg)

Humphrey practicing pharmacy in his father's drugstore

After his son graduated from Doland's high school, Hubert Sr. left Doland and opened a new drugstore in the larger town of [Huron, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huron,_South_Dakota "Huron, South Dakota") (population 11,000), where he hoped to improve his fortunes.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-10) Because of the family's financial struggles, Humphrey had to leave the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota") after just one year.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_UTexas_Profile-11) He earned a pharmacist's license from the Capitol College of Pharmacy in [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver "Denver"), Colorado (completing a two-year licensure program in just six months),[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Luzer,_2012-12) and helped his father run his store from 1931 to 1937.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198448-13) Both father and son were innovative in finding ways to attract customers: "to supplement their business, the Humphreys had become manufacturers ... of patent medicines for both hogs and humans. A sign featuring a wooden pig was hung over the drugstore to tell the public about this unusual service. Farmers got the message, and it was Humphrey's that became known as the farmer's drugstore."[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-14) One biographer noted, "while Hubert Jr. minded the store and stirred the concoctions in the basement, Hubert Sr. went on the road selling 'Humphrey's BTV' (Body Tone Veterinary), a mineral supplement and dewormer for hogs, and 'Humphrey's Chest Oil' and 'Humphrey's Sniffles' for two-legged sufferers."[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-15) Humphrey later wrote, "we made 'Humphrey's Sniffles', a substitute for [Vick's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicks "Vicks") Nose Drops. I felt ours were better. Vick's used mineral oil, which is not absorbent, and we used a vegetable-oil base, which was. I added [benzocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzocaine "Benzocaine"), a local anesthetic, so that even if the sniffles didn't get better, you felt it less."[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-16) The various "Humphrey cures ... worked well enough and constituted an important part of the family income ... the farmers that bought the medicines were good customers."[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-17) Over time Humphrey's Drug Store became a profitable enterprise and the family again prospered.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Cohen,_p._54-18) While living in Huron, Humphrey regularly attended Huron's largest Methodist church and became scoutmaster of the church's Boy Scout Troop 6.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198448-13) He "started basketball games in the church basement ... although his scouts had no money for camp in 1931, Hubert found a way in the worst of that summer's dust-storm grit, grasshoppers, and depression to lead an overnight [outing]."[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198448%E2%80%9349-19)

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Hubert_H._Humphrey_1939.png/250px-Hubert_H._Humphrey_1939.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hubert_H._Humphrey_1939.png)

Humphrey in the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota") yearbook, 1939

Humphrey did not enjoy working as a pharmacist, and continued to desire a doctorate in political science and become a college professor.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Luzer,_2012-12) His unhappiness was manifested in "stomach pains and fainting spells", though doctors could find nothing wrong with him.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198450-20) In August 1937, he told his father that he wanted to return to the University of Minnesota.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Cohen,_p._54-18) Hubert Sr. tried to convince his son not to leave by offering him a full partnership in the store, but Hubert Jr. refused and told his father "how depressed I was, almost physically ill from the work, the [dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_Bowl "Dust Bowl"), the conflict between my desire to do something and be somebody and my loyalty to him ... he replied 'Hubert, if you aren't happy, then you ought to do something about it'."[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-21) Humphrey returned to the University of Minnesota to complete his degree, graduating _[magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna\_cum\_laude "Magna cum laude")_ with a Bachelor of Arts in political science and earning [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") distinction in 1939.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Offner2018-22) During his time at the University of Minnesota, Humphrey—who grew up in a predominantly white, rural environment—became aware of the antisemitism and racial discrimination prevalent in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"), which biographers note influenced his subsequent focus on civil rights advocacy.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Offner2018-22)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Wilson_Center-23) He was a member of [Phi Delta Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Delta_Chi "Phi Delta Chi"), a pharmacy fraternity. He also earned a master's degree from [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University "Louisiana State University") in 1940, serving as an assistant instructor of political science there.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Debolt_and_Baugess,_2011-24)

He then became an instructor and [doctoral student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate_of_philosophy "Doctorate of philosophy") at the University of Minnesota from 1940 to 1941 (joining the [American Federation of Teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Teachers "American Federation of Teachers")), and was a supervisor for the [Works Progress Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_Progress_Administration "Works Progress Administration") (WPA).[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Mayor_Humphrey-25) Humphrey was a star on the university's debate team; one of his teammates was future Minnesota Governor and US Secretary of Agriculture [Orville Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orville_Freeman "Orville Freeman").[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-26) In the [1940 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_United_States_presidential_election "1940 United States presidential election") Humphrey and future University of Minnesota president [Malcolm Moos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Moos "Malcolm Moos") debated the merits of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt"), the Democratic nominee, and [Wendell Willkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Willkie "Wendell Willkie"), the Republican nominee, on a Minneapolis radio station. Humphrey supported Roosevelt.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-27) Humphrey soon became active in Minneapolis politics, and as a result never finished his PhD.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-28)

## Marriage and early career

In 1934, Humphrey began dating [Muriel Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_Buck "Muriel Buck"), a [bookkeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookkeeper "Bookkeeper") and graduate of local Huron College.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-29) They were married from 1936 until Humphrey's death nearly 42 years later.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198452-30) They had four children: Nancy Faye, [Skip Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skip_Humphrey "Skip Humphrey"), Robert Andrew, and Douglas Sannes.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Mooar,_1998-31) Money was an issue that plagued the Humphreys consistently. One biographer noted, "For much of his life he was short of money to live on, and his relentless drive to attain the White House seemed at times like one long, losing struggle to raise enough campaign funds to get there."[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984437-32) To help boost his salary, Humphrey frequently took paid outside speaking engagements. Through most of his years as a U.S. senator and vice president, he lived in a middle-class suburban housing development in [Chevy Chase, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevy_Chase,_Maryland "Chevy Chase, Maryland"). In 1958, the Humphreys used their savings and his [speaking fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaking_fee "Speaking fee") to build a lakefront home in [Waverly, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waverly,_Minnesota "Waverly, Minnesota"), about 40 miles west of Minneapolis.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984197-33)

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Humphrey tried three times to join the armed forces but failed.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-34) His first two attempts were to join the Navy, first as a commissioned officer and then as an enlisted man. He was rejected both times for color blindness.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Cohen,_p._105-35) He then tried to enlist in the Army in December 1944 but failed the physical exam because of a double hernia, color blindness, and calcification of the lungs.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Cohen,_p._105-35) Despite his attempts to join the military, one biographer would note that "all through his political life, Humphrey was dogged by the charge that he was a draft dodger" during the war.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-36)

Humphrey led various wartime government agencies and worked as a college instructor. In 1942, he was the state director of new production training and reemployment and chief of the Minnesota war service program.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Encyclopedia_of_the_U.S._Congress-37) In 1943 he was the assistant director of the [War Manpower Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Manpower_Commission "War Manpower Commission").[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Wilson_Center-23) From 1943 to 1944, Humphrey was a professor of political science at [Macalester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College "Macalester College") in [Saint Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "Saint Paul, Minnesota"), where he headed the university's recently created international debate department, which focused on the international politics of World War II and the creation of the United Nations.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-38) After leaving Macalester in the spring of 1944, Humphrey worked as a news commentator for a Minneapolis radio station until 1945.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Wilson_Center-23)

In 1943, Humphrey made his first run for elective office, for [mayor of Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Minneapolis "List of mayors of Minneapolis"). He lost, but his poorly funded campaign still captured over 47% of the vote.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Mayor_Humphrey-25) In 1944, Humphrey was one of the key players in the merger of the [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Democratic_Party "United States Democratic Party") and [Farmer-Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Farmer-Labor_Party "Minnesota Farmer-Labor Party") parties of Minnesota to form the [Minnesota Democratic-Farmer-Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic-Farmer-Labor_Party "Minnesota Democratic-Farmer-Labor Party") (DFL).[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-American_Midwest_Encyclopedia-39) He also worked on President [Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt "Franklin Roosevelt")'s 1944 reelection campaign.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-40) When Minnesota [Communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist "Communist") tried to seize control of the new party in 1945, Humphrey became an engaged [anticommunist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommunist "Anticommunist") and led the successful fight to oust the Communists from the DFL.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-41)

## Mayor of Minneapolis (1945–1948)

After the war, Humphrey again ran for mayor of Minneapolis; this time, he won the June 1945 general election with 61% of the vote.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Mayor_Humphrey-25) As mayor, he helped ensure the appointment of a friend and previous neighbor, Edwin Ryan, as head of the police department, as he needed a "police chief whose integrity and loyalty would be above reproach."[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Reichard,_1998-42) Though they had differing views of labor unions, Ryan and Humphrey worked together to crack down on crime in Minneapolis. Humphrey told Ryan, "I want this town cleaned up and I mean I want it cleaned up now, not a year from now or a month from now, right now", and "You take care of the law enforcement. I'll take care of the politics."[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Romskog-43) Humphrey served as mayor from 1945 to 1948,[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Bioguide-44) winning reelection in 1947 by the largest margin in the city's history to that time. Humphrey gained national fame by becoming one of the founders of the liberal anticommunist [Americans for Democratic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_for_Democratic_Action "Americans for Democratic Action") (ADA), and he served as chairman from 1949 to 1950.[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-45) He also reformed the Minneapolis police force.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-46) The city had been named the "[antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism") capital" of the country,[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-47) and its small African-American population also faced discrimination. Humphrey's mayoralty is noted for his efforts to fight all forms of bigotry.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTECaro2002440-48) He formed the Council on Human Relations and established a municipal version of the [Fair Employment Practice Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Employment_Practice_Committee "Fair Employment Practice Committee"), making Minneapolis one of only a few cities in the United States to prohibit racial discrimination in the workforce.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-49) Humphrey and his publicists were proud that the Council on Human Relations brought together individuals of varying ideologies.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-50) In 1960, Humphrey told journalist [Theodore H. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_H._White "Theodore H. White"), "I was mayor once, in Minneapolis ... a mayor is a fine job, it's the best job there is between being a governor and being the President."[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-51)

A 1993 survey of historians, political scientists and urban experts by Melvin G. Holli of the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago "University of Illinois at Chicago") ranked Humphrey as the 28th-best American big-city mayor to have served between 1820 and 1993.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-52)

## 1948 DNC involvement

The Democratic Party of 1948 was split between those, mainly Northerners, who thought the federal government should actively protect [civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights "Civil rights") for racial minorities, and those, mainly Southerners, who believed that states should be able to enforce [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States "Racial segregation in the United States") within their borders.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198413-53)

At the [1948 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Democratic_National_Convention "1948 Democratic National Convention"), the [party platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_platform "Political party platform") reflected the division by containing only platitudes supporting civil rights.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-54) The incumbent president, [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman"), had shelved most of his 1946 Commission on Civil Rights's recommendations to avoid angering Southern Democrats.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-55) But Humphrey had written in _The Progressive_ magazine, "The Democratic Party must lead the fight for every principle in the report. It is all or nothing."[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg198413-53)

[![Image 9](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Hubert_H._Humphrey--1948_Democratic_National_Convention--.jpg/250px-Hubert_H._Humphrey--1948_Democratic_National_Convention--.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hubert_H._Humphrey--1948_Democratic_National_Convention--.jpg)

Humphrey at the [1948 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Democratic_National_Convention "1948 Democratic National Convention") in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia")

A diverse coalition opposed the convention's tepid civil rights platform, including anticommunist liberals like Humphrey, [Paul Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Douglas_(Illinois_politician) "Paul Douglas (Illinois politician)") and [John F. Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Shelley "John F. Shelley"), all of whom would later become known as leading progressives in the Democratic Party. They proposed adding a "minority plank" to the party platform that would commit the Democratic Party to more aggressive opposition to [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation "Racial segregation").[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-56) The minority plank called for federal legislation against [lynching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynching "Lynching"), an end to legalized school segregation in the South, and ending job discrimination based on skin color.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Debolt_and_Baugess,_2011-24) Also strongly backing the minority plank were Democratic urban bosses like Ed Flynn of the [Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx "Bronx"), who promised the votes of northeastern delegates to Humphrey's platform, [Jacob Arvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Arvey "Jacob Arvey") of Chicago, and [David Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_L._Lawrence "David L. Lawrence") of [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh"). Although seen as conservatives, the urban bosses believed that Northern Democrats could gain many black votes by supporting civil rights, with only comparatively small losses from Southern Democrats.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-57) Although many scholars[_[who?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual\_of\_Style/Words\_to\_watch#Unsupported\_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")_] have suggested that labor unions were leading figures in this coalition, no significant labor leaders attended the convention, except for the heads of the Congress of Industrial Organizations Political Action Committee ([CIO-PAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIO-PAC "CIO-PAC")), [Jack Kroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kroll_(labor_leader) "Jack Kroll (labor leader)") and A.F. Whitney.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-58)

Despite Truman's aides' aggressive pressure to avoid forcing the issue on the Convention floor, Humphrey spoke for the minority plank.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Debolt_and_Baugess,_2011-24) In a renowned speech,[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-59) Humphrey passionately told the convention, "To those who say, my friends, to those who say that we are rushing this issue of civil rights, I say to them we are 172 years (too) late! To those who say this civil rights program is an infringement on states' rights, I say this: the time has arrived in America for the Democratic Party to get out of the shadow of states' rights and walk forthrightly into the bright sunshine of human rights!"[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-60) Humphrey and his allies succeeded: the convention adopted the pro-civil-rights plank by a vote of 651+1⁄2 to 582+1⁄2.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-61)

After the convention's vote, the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi "Mississippi") delegation and half of the [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama "Alabama") delegation walked out of the hall.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Smithsonian_1948_DNC-1) Many Southern Democrats were so enraged at this affront to their "way of life" that they formed the [Dixiecrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixiecrat "Dixiecrat") party[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-62) and nominated their own presidential candidate, Governor [Strom Thurmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strom_Thurmond "Strom Thurmond") of [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina "South Carolina").[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-63) The Dixiecrats' goal was to take Southern states away from Truman and thus cause his defeat.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-64) They reasoned that after such a defeat, the national Democratic Party would never again aggressively pursue a pro-civil rights agenda. The move backfired: although the civil rights plank cost Truman the Dixiecrats' support, a significant number of black voters switched their support from [Henry A. Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_A._Wallace "Henry A. Wallace") to him.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-65) As a result, Truman defeated Thurmond and the [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") nominee, [Thomas E. Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_E._Dewey "Thomas E. Dewey").[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-66) The result demonstrated that the Democratic Party could win presidential elections without the "Solid South" and weakened Southern Democrats. [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize")-winning historian [David McCullough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_McCullough "David McCullough") has written that Humphrey probably did more to get Truman elected in 1948 than anyone other than Truman himself.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-McCullough-67)

Humphrey was elected to the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") in [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_U.S._Senate_election "1948 U.S. Senate election") on the [DFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic-Farmer-Labor_Party "Minnesota Democratic-Farmer-Labor Party") ticket, defeating James M. Shields in the DFL primary with 89% of the vote,[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-68) and unseating incumbent Republican [Joseph H. Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_H._Ball "Joseph H. Ball") in the general election with 60% of the vote.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-69) He took office on January 3, 1949, becoming the first Democrat elected senator from Minnesota since before the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War").[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-70) Humphrey wrote that the victory heightened his sense of self, as he had beaten the odds of defeating a Republican with statewide support.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-71) Humphrey's father died that year, and Humphrey stopped using the "Jr." suffix on his name. He was reelected in [1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_U.S._Senate_election "1954 U.S. Senate election") and [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_U.S._Senate_election "1960 U.S. Senate election").[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Bioguide-44) His colleagues selected him as [majority whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_Whip_of_the_United_States_Senate "Majority Whip of the United States Senate") in 1961, a position he held until he left the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") on December 29, 1964, to assume the vice presidency.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-72) Humphrey served from the [81st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81st_United_States_Congress "81st United States Congress") to the [87th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/87th_United_States_Congress "87th United States Congress") sessions of Congress, and in a portion of the [88th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/88th_United_States_Congress "88th United States Congress").

[![Image 13](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4d/Senator_Humphrey.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Senator_Humphrey.jpg)

Senator Humphrey

Initially, Humphrey's support of civil rights led to his being ostracized by Southern Democrats, who dominated Senate leadership positions and wanted to punish him for proposing the civil rights platform at the 1948 Convention. Senator [Richard Russell Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Russell_Jr. "Richard Russell Jr.") of Georgia, a leader of Southern Democrats, once remarked to other senators as Humphrey walked by, "Can you imagine the people of Minnesota sending that damn fool down here to represent them?"[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTECaro2002448-73) Humphrey was reportedly "crushed",[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-74) so hurt by the remark that he broke into tears while driving home.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-75) But he refused to be intimidated and stood his ground; his integrity, passion and eloquence eventually earned him the respect of even most of the Southerners.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984180-76) The Southerners were also more inclined to accept Humphrey after he became a protégé of Senate [Majority Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_Leader "Majority Leader") Lyndon B. Johnson of [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Debolt_and_Baugess,_2011-24) Humphrey became known for his advocacy of liberal causes (such as [civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights "Civil rights"), [arms control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_control "Arms control"), a [nuclear test ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_testing "Nuclear testing"), [food stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_Stamp_Program "Food Stamp Program"), and humanitarian [foreign aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_aid "Foreign aid")), and for his long and witty speeches.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-77)

Humphrey was a liberal leader who fought to uphold Truman's veto of the [McCarran Act of 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarran_Internal_Security_Act "McCarran Internal Security Act"). The bill was designed to suppress the [American Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA "Communist Party USA"). With a small group of liberals he supported the [Kilgore substitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley_M._Kilgore "Harley M. Kilgore") that would allow the president to lock up subversives, without trial, in a time of national emergency. The model was the [internment of West Coast Japanese in 1942](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Americans "Internment of Japanese Americans"). The goal was to split the McCarran coalition. For years critics charged that Humphrey supported concentration camps. The ploy failed to stop the new law; the Senate voted 57 to 10 to overturn Truman's veto.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-78)[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-79)[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTEYbarra2004517%E2%80%93533-80) In 1954 he proposed to make membership in the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPUSA "CPUSA") a felony. It was another ploy to derail a bill that would hurt labor unions. Humphrey's proposal did not pass.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTEYbarra2004743%E2%80%93744-81)

Humphrey was the author of the first humane slaughter bill introduced in the U.S. Congress and chief Senate sponsor of the Humane Slaughter Act of 1958.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-82)

Humphrey chaired the [Select Committee on Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senate_Select_Committee_on_Disarmament "U.S. Senate Select Committee on Disarmament") ([84th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/84th_United_States_Congress "84th United States Congress") and [85th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85th_United_States_Congress "85th United States Congress") Congresses).[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-83) In February 1960 he introduced a bill to establish a National Peace Agency.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-84) With another former pharmacist, Representative [Carl Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_T._Durham "Carl T. Durham"), Humphrey cosponsored the [Durham-Humphrey Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham-Humphrey_Amendment "Durham-Humphrey Amendment"), which amended the [Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Food,_Drug,_and_Cosmetic_Act "Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act"), defining two specific categories for medications, legend ([prescription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescription_drug "Prescription drug")) and [over-the-counter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-counter_drug "Over-the-counter drug") (OTC).[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-85)

As Democratic [whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_(politics) "Whip (politics)") in the Senate in 1964, Humphrey was instrumental in the passage of the [Civil Rights Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964 "Civil Rights Act of 1964") that year. He was a lead author of its text, alongside Senate Republican Minority Leader [Everett Dirksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everett_Dirksen "Everett Dirksen") of Illinois.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-86) Humphrey's consistently cheerful and upbeat demeanor, and his forceful advocacy of liberal causes, led him to be nicknamed "The Happy Warrior" by many of his Senate colleagues and political journalists.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-87)

Humphrey introduced the first bill to create the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps "Peace Corps") in 1957—three years before [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy")'s proposal.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-88) In 1969, three journalists wrote of Humphrey that "few men in American politics have achieved so much of lasting significance. It was Humphrey, not Senator [Everett] Dirksen, who played the crucial part in the complex parliamentary games that were needed to pass the Civil Rights Act of 1964. It was Humphrey, not John Kennedy, who first proposed the Peace Corps. The Food for Peace program was Humphrey's idea, and so was Medicare, passed sixteen years after he first proposed it. He worked for Federal aid to education from 1949, and for a nuclear-test ban treaty from 1956. These are the solid monuments of twenty years of effective work for liberal causes in the Senate."[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Chester,_p._147-89) President Johnson once said, "Most Senators are minnows ... Hubert Humphrey is among the whales."[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Chester,_p._147-89) In his autobiography, _The Education of a Public Man_, Humphrey wrote:[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-90)

> There were three bills of particular emotional importance to me: the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps "Peace Corps"), a disarmament agency, and the Nuclear Test Ban Treaty. The President, knowing how I felt, asked me to introduce legislation for all three. I introduced the first Peace Corps bill in 1957. It did not meet with much enthusiasm. Some traditional diplomats quaked at the thought of thousands of young Americans scattered across their world. Many senators, including liberal ones, thought the idea was silly and unworkable. Now, with a young president urging its passage, it became possible and we pushed it rapidly through the Senate. It is fashionable now to suggest that Peace Corps Volunteers gained as much or more, from their experience as the countries they worked. That may be true, but it ought not demean their work. They touched many lives and made them better.

On April 9, 1950, Humphrey predicted that President Truman would sign a $4 billion housing bill and charge Republicans with having removed the bill's main middle-income benefits during Truman's tours of the Midwest and Northwest the following month.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-91)

On January 7, 1951, Humphrey joined Senator [Paul Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Douglas_(Illinois_politician) "Paul Douglas (Illinois politician)") in calling for an $80 billion federal budget to combat Communist aggression along with a stiff tax increase to prevent borrowing.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-92)

In a January 1951 letter to President Truman, Humphrey wrote of the necessity of a commission akin to the [Fair Employment Practices Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Employment_Practices_Commission "Fair Employment Practices Commission") that would be used to end discrimination in defense industries and predicted that establishing such a commission by executive order would be met with high approval by Americans.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-93)

On June 18, 1953, Humphrey introduced a resolution calling for the US to urge free elections in [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany "East Germany") in response to the recent [anti-communist uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_German_uprising_of_1953 "East German uprising of 1953") there.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-94)

In December 1958, after receiving a message from [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev "Nikita Khrushchev") during a visit to the Soviet Union, Humphrey returned insisting that the message was not negative toward America.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-95) In February 1959, Humphrey said American newspapers should have ignored Khrushchev's comments calling him a purveyor of fairy tales.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-96) In a September address to the National Stationery and Office Equipment Association, Humphrey called for further inspection of Khrushchev's "live and let live" doctrine and maintained the Cold War could be won by using American "weapons of peace".[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-97)

In June 1963, Humphrey accompanied his longtime friend labor leader [Walter Reuther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Reuther "Walter Reuther") on a trip to [Harpsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpsund "Harpsund"), the Swedish Prime Minister's summer country retreat, to meet with European socialist leaders for an exchange of ideas.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984219-98) Among the European leaders who met with Humphrey and Reuther were the prime ministers of Britain, Sweden, Denmark, and Norway, as well as future German chancellor [Willy Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Brandt "Willy Brandt").[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984219-98)

## Political ambitions (1952–1964)

[![Image 14](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/1960_Dem_Primaries.svg/330px-1960_Dem_Primaries.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1960_Dem_Primaries.svg)

In the 1960 primaries, Humphrey won South Dakota and Washington, D.C.

Humphrey ran for the Democratic presidential nomination twice before his election to the vice presidency in 1964. The first time was as Minnesota's [favorite son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favorite_son "Favorite son") in 1952; he received only 26 votes on the first ballot.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-99) The second time was in 1960. In between these two bids, Humphrey was part of the free-for-all for the vice-presidential nomination at the [1956 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Democratic_National_Convention "1956 Democratic National Convention"), where he received 134.5 votes on the first ballot and 74.5 on the second.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-100)

In [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries "1960 Democratic Party presidential primaries"), Humphrey ran for the nomination against fellow Senator [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy") in the primaries. Their first meeting was in the Wisconsin primary, where Kennedy's well-organized and well-funded campaign overcame Humphrey's energetic but poorly funded effort.[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-101) Humphrey believed defeating Kennedy in Wisconsin would weaken and slow the momentum of the latter's campaign.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-102)[Kennedy's attractive brothers, sisters, and wife Jacqueline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_family "Kennedy family") combed the state for votes. At one point Humphrey memorably complained that he "felt like an independent merchant competing against a chain store".[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984205-103) Humphrey later wrote in his memoirs that "Muriel and I and our 'plain folks' entourage were no match for the glamour of Jackie Kennedy and the other Kennedy women, for [Peter Lawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Lawford "Peter Lawford") ... and [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra") singing their commercial 'High Hopes'. Jack Kennedy brought family and Hollywood to Wisconsin. The people loved it, and the press ate it up."[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-104) Kennedy won the Wisconsin primary, but by a smaller margin than anticipated. Some commentators argued that Kennedy's victory margin had come almost entirely from areas with large [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic "Roman Catholic") populations,[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984208-105) and that [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants "Protestants") had supported Humphrey. As a result, Humphrey refused to quit the race and decided to run against Kennedy again in the West Virginia primary. According to one biographer, "Humphrey thought his chances were good in West Virginia, one of the few states that had backed him in his losing race for vice-president four years earlier ... West Virginia was more rural than urban, [which] seemed to invite Humphrey's folksy stump style. The state, moreover, was a citadel of labor. It was depressed; unemployment had hit hard; and coal miners' families were hungry. Humphrey felt he could talk to such people, who were 95% Protestant (Humphrey was a [Congregationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregationalist "Congregationalist"))[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-106) and deep-dyed Bible-belters besides."[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984208-105)

Kennedy chose to meet the religion issue head-on. In radio broadcasts, he carefully redefined the issue from Catholic versus [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant") to tolerance versus intolerance. Kennedy's appeal placed Humphrey, who had championed tolerance his entire career, on the defensive, and Kennedy attacked him with a vengeance. [Franklin D. Roosevelt Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt_Jr. "Franklin D. Roosevelt Jr."), the son of the former president, stumped for Kennedy in West Virginia and raised the issue of Humphrey's failure to serve in the armed forces in World War II. Roosevelt told audiences, "I don't know where he [Humphrey] was in World War Two," and handed out flyers charging that Humphrey was a draft dodger.[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984209-107) Historian [Robert Dallek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Dallek "Robert Dallek") has written that [Robert F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy "Robert F. Kennedy"), who was serving as his brother's campaign manager, came into "possession of information that Humphrey may have sought military deferments during World War Two ... he pressed Roosevelt to use this."[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Dallek,_p._256-108) Humphrey believed Roosevelt's draft-dodger claim "had been approved by Bobby [Kennedy], if not Jack".[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Dallek,_p._256-108) The claims that Humphrey was a draft dodger were inaccurate, because during the war Humphrey had "tried and failed to get into the [military] service because of physical disabilities".[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Dallek,_p._256-108) After the West Virginia primary, Roosevelt sent Humphrey a written apology and retraction.[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984209-107) According to historian [Arthur Schlesinger, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Schlesinger,_Jr. "Arthur Schlesinger, Jr."), Roosevelt "felt that he had been used, blaming [the draft-dodger charge] on Robert Kennedy's determination to win at any cost ... Roosevelt said later that it was the biggest political mistake of his career."[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-109)

Short on funds, Humphrey could not match the well-financed Kennedy operation. He traveled around the state in a rented bus while Kennedy and his staff flew in a large, family-owned airplane.[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-110) According to his biographer Carl Solberg, Humphrey spent only $23,000 on the West Virginia primary while Kennedy's campaign privately spent $1.5 million, well over their official estimate of $100,000.[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984210%E2%80%93211-111) Unproven accusations claimed that the Kennedys had bought the West Virginia primary by bribing county sheriffs and other local officials to give Kennedy the vote.[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-112) Humphrey later wrote, "as a professional politician I was able to accept and indeed respect the efficacy of the Kennedy campaign. But underneath the beautiful exterior, there was an element of ruthlessness and toughness that I had trouble either accepting or forgetting."[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-113) Kennedy defeated Humphrey soundly in West Virginia with 60.8% of the vote.[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-114) That evening, Humphrey announced that he was leaving the race.[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-115) By winning West Virginia, Kennedy overcame the belief that Protestant voters would not elect a Catholic to the presidency and thus sewed up the Democratic nomination.[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984209-107)

Humphrey won the South Dakota and District of Columbia primaries, which Kennedy did not enter.[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-116) At the [1960 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Democratic_National_Convention "1960 Democratic National Convention"), he received 41 votes even though he was no longer a candidate.

Vice President-elect Humphrey alongside [Coretta Scott King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coretta_Scott_King "Coretta Scott King") and civil rights leader Dr. [Martin Luther King Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr. "Martin Luther King Jr.") in 1964.

### Vice presidential campaign

Further information: [1964 Democratic Party vice presidential candidate selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Democratic_Party_vice_presidential_candidate_selection "1964 Democratic Party vice presidential candidate selection") and [Lyndon B. Johnson 1964 presidential campaign §Democratic national convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson_1964_presidential_campaign#Democratic_national_convention "Lyndon B. Johnson 1964 presidential campaign")

Humphrey's defeat in 1960 had a profound influence on his thinking; after the primaries he told friends that, as a relatively poor man in politics, he was unlikely to ever become president unless he served as vice president first.[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984240-117) Humphrey believed that only in this way could he attain the funds, nationwide organization, and visibility he would need to win the Democratic nomination. So as the [1964 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election "1964 United States presidential election") began, Humphrey made clear his interest in becoming Lyndon Johnson's running mate. At the [1964 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Democratic_National_Convention "1964 Democratic National Convention"), Johnson kept the three likely vice-presidential candidates, Connecticut Senator [Thomas Dodd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Dodd "Thomas J. Dodd"), fellow Minnesota Senator [Eugene McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_McCarthy "Eugene McCarthy"), and Humphrey,[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-118) as well as the rest of the nation, in suspense before announcing his choice of Humphrey with much fanfare, praising his qualifications at considerable length before announcing his name.[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-119)

The following day Humphrey's acceptance speech overshadowed Johnson's own acceptance address:

> Hubert warmed up with a long tribute to the President, then hit his stride as he began a rhythmic jabbing and chopping at [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater "Barry Goldwater"). "Most Democrats and Republicans in the Senate voted for an $11.5 billion tax cut for American citizens and American business," he cried, "but not Senator Goldwater. Most Democrats and Republicans in the Senate – in fact four-fifths of the members of his own party – voted for the Civil Rights Act, but not Senator Goldwater." Time after time, he capped his indictments with the drumbeat cry: "But not Senator Goldwater!" The delegates caught the cadence and took up the chant. A quizzical smile spread across Humphrey's face, then turned to a laugh of triumph. Hubert was in fine form. He knew it. The delegates knew it. And no one could deny that Hubert Humphrey would be a formidable political antagonist in the weeks ahead.[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-120)

In an address before labor leaders in [Youngstown, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngstown,_Ohio "Youngstown, Ohio"), on September 7, 1964, Humphrey said the labor movement had "more at stake in this election than almost any other segment of society".[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-121) In [Jamesburg, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamesburg,_New_Jersey "Jamesburg, New Jersey"), on September 10, Humphrey remarked that Goldwater had a "record of retreat and reaction" when it came to issues of urban housing.[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-122) During a September 12 [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver "Denver") Democratic rally, Humphrey charged Goldwater with having rejected programs that most Americans and members of his own party supported.[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-123) At a [Santa Fe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe,_New_Mexico "Santa Fe, New Mexico") September 13 rally, Humphrey said the Goldwater-led Republican Party was seeking "to divide America so that they may conquer" and that Goldwater would pinch individuals in his reduction of government.[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-124) On September 16, Humphrey said the [Americans for Democratic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_for_Democratic_Action "Americans for Democratic Action") supported the Johnson administration's economic sanctions against Cuba, and that the organization wanted to see a free Cuban government.[[125]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-125) The following day in [San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio "San Antonio"), Texas, Humphrey said Goldwater opposed programs favored by most Texans and Americans.[[126]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-126) During a September 27 appearance in [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland "Cleveland"), Ohio, Humphrey said the Kennedy administration had led America in a prosperous direction and called for voters to issue a referendum with their vote against "those who seek to replace the Statue of Liberty with an iron-padlocked gate."[[127]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-127) At an October 1 rally in [Tacoma, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma,_Washington "Tacoma, Washington"), Humphrey attacked Goldwater as a radical who opposed the 1960 GOP platform and true conservatism, which in Humphrey's opinion meant a balance of tradition and progress.[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-128)

At Shrine Auditorium in Los Angeles, California, on October 2, Humphrey said the general election would give voters a choice between his running mate and a candidate "who curses the darkness and never lights a candle".[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-129) During an October 9 [Jersey City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City,_New_Jersey "Jersey City, New Jersey"), appearance, Humphrey responded to critics of the administration, who he called "sick and tired Americans", by touting the accomplishments of both Kennedy's and Johnson's presidencies.[[130]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-130) In [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida "Tampa, Florida"), on October 18, a week after the resignation of [Walter Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Jenkins "Walter Jenkins") amid a scandal, Humphrey said he was unaware of any potential security leaks relating to the case.[[131]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-131) In [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") on October 24, Humphrey listed the censure vote toward Senator [Joseph McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McCarthy "Joseph McCarthy"), the civil rights bill, and the nuclear test ban treaty as "three great issues of conscience to come before the United States Senate in the past decade" that Goldwater had voted incorrectly on as a Senator.[[132]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-132) In an October 26 speech in Chicago, Humphrey called Goldwater "neither a Republican nor a Democrat" and "a radical".[[133]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-133)

The Johnson-Humphrey ticket won the election overwhelmingly, with 486 electoral votes out of 538.[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-134) Only five Southern states and Goldwater's home state of Arizona supported the Republican ticket.[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-135) In October Humphrey had predicted that the ticket would win by a large margin but not carry every state.[[136]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-136)

### Vice President-elect of the United States

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/President_Lyndon_B._Johnson_and_Vice-President-Elect_Hubert_Humphrey_%28436-253-wh64%29.jpg/250px-President_Lyndon_B._Johnson_and_Vice-President-Elect_Hubert_Humphrey_%28436-253-wh64%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Lyndon_B._Johnson_and_Vice-President-Elect_Hubert_Humphrey_(436-253-wh64).jpg)

Humphrey (right) horse-riding with President Johnson (left) at the [LBJ ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LBJ_Ranch "LBJ Ranch"), c.November 4, 1964

Soon after winning the election, Humphrey and Johnson went to [LBJ ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LBJ_Ranch "LBJ Ranch") near [Stonewall, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall,_Texas "Stonewall, Texas").[[137]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-137) On November 6, 1964, Humphrey traveled to the [Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Islands "Virgin Islands") for a two-week vacation.[[138]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-138) News stations aired taped remarks in which Humphrey stated that he had not discussed with Johnson what his role would be as vice president and that national campaigns should be reduced by four weeks.[[139]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-139) In a November 20 interview, Humphrey announced he would resign his Senate seat midway through the next month so that [Walter Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mondale "Walter Mondale") could assume the position.[[140]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-140)

On December 10, 1964, Humphrey met with Johnson in the Oval Office, the latter charging the vice president-elect with "developing a publicity machine extraordinaire and of always wanting to get his name in the paper." Johnson showed Humphrey a George Reed memo with the allegation that the president would die within six months from an already acquired fatal heart disease.[[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-141) The same day, during a speech in Washington, Johnson announced Humphrey would have the position of giving assistance to governmental civil rights programs.[[142]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-142)

On January 19, 1965, the day before the inauguration, Humphrey told the [Democratic National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee "Democratic National Committee") that the party had unified because of the national consensus established by the presidential election.[[143]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-143)

## Vice presidency (1965–1969)

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Hubert_Humphrey_1965.jpg/250px-Hubert_Humphrey_1965.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hubert_Humphrey_1965.jpg)

Vice President Humphrey at a meeting in the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office "Oval Office"), c.June 21, 1965

Humphrey took office on January 20, 1965,[[144]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-144) ending the 14-month vacancy of the vice president of the United States, which had remained empty when then-Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson assumed the presidency after the [assassination of John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy "Assassination of John F. Kennedy") in November 1963.[[145]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-145) He was an early skeptic of the then growing [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). Following a successful [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong "Viet Cong") hit-and-run [attack on a U.S. military installation at Pleiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Camp_Holloway "Attack on Camp Holloway") on February 7, 1965 (where 7 Americans were killed and 109 wounded), Humphrey returned from Georgia to Washington D.C., to attempt to prevent further escalation.[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-T._Hoopes,_p31-146) He told President Johnson that bombing [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam") was not a solution to the problems in [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam"), but that bombing would require the injection of US ground forces into South Vietnam to protect the airbases.[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-T._Hoopes,_p31-146) Presciently, he noted that a military solution in Vietnam would take several years, well beyond the next election cycle. In response to this advice, President Johnson punished Humphrey by treating him coldly and restricting him from his inner circle for a number of months, until Humphrey decided to "get back on the team" and fully support the war effort.[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-T._Hoopes,_p31-146)

As vice president, Humphrey was criticized for his complete and vocal loyalty to Johnson and the policies of the Johnson administration, even as many of his liberal admirers opposed the president's policies with increasing fervor regarding the Vietnam War.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Wilson_Center-23) Many of Humphrey's liberal friends and allies abandoned him because of his refusal to publicly criticize Johnson's Vietnam War policies. Humphrey's critics later learned that Johnson had threatened Humphrey – Johnson told Humphrey that if he publicly criticized his policies, he would destroy Humphrey's chances to become president by opposing his nomination at the next Democratic Convention.[[147]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984282%E2%80%93284-147) However, Humphrey's critics were vocal and persistent: even his nickname, "the Happy Warrior", was used against him. The nickname referred not to his military hawkishness, but rather to his crusading for social welfare and civil rights programs.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Humphrey_Wilson_Center-23) After his narrow defeat in the 1968 presidential election, Humphrey wrote that "After four years as Vice-President ... I had lost some of my personal identity and personal forcefulness. ... I ought not to have let a man [Johnson] who was going to be a former President dictate my future."[[148]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolberg1984407-148)

While he was vice president, Hubert Humphrey was the subject of a satirical song by songwriter/musician [Tom Lehrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Lehrer "Tom Lehrer") entitled "Whatever Became of Hubert?" The song addressed how some liberals and [progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism "Progressivism") felt let down by Humphrey, who had become a much more mute figure as vice president than he had been as a senator. The song goes _"Whatever became of Hubert? Has anyone heard a thing? Once he shone on his own, now he sits home alone and waits for the phone to ring. Once a fiery liberal spirit, ah, but now when he speaks he must clear it. ..."_

During these years Humphrey was a repeated and favorite guest of [Johnny Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Carson "Johnny Carson") on _[The Tonight Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Tonight\_Show "The Tonight Show")_.[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-149)[[150]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-150) He also struck up a friendship with [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra"), who supported his campaign for president in 1968 before his conversion to the Republican party in the early 1970s,[[151]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-151) and was perhaps most on notice in the fall of 1977 when Sinatra was the star attraction and host of a tribute to a then-ailing Humphrey. He also appeared on _[The Dean Martin Celebrity Roast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Dean\_Martin\_Celebrity\_Roast "The Dean Martin Celebrity Roast")_ in 1973.

[![Image 18](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Gemini_4_Astronauts_Meet_Yuri_Gagarin.jpg/250px-Gemini_4_Astronauts_Meet_Yuri_Gagarin.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gemini_4_Astronauts_Meet_Yuri_Gagarin.jpg)

Humphrey with Soviet cosmonaut [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin "Yuri Gagarin") and [Gemini 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Gemini "Project Gemini") astronauts at the 1965 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show "Paris Air Show")

On April 15, 1965, Humphrey delivered an address to the American Society of Newspaper Editors, pledging the incumbent session of Congress would "do more for the lasting long-term health of this nation" since the initial session in office at the time of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") assuming the presidency in 1933 and predicting 13 major measures of President Johnson's administration would be passed ahead of the session's conclusion.[[152]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-152) In mid-May 1965, Humphrey traveled to [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas "Dallas"), Texas for an off-the-record discussion with donors of President Johnson's campaign. During the visit, Humphrey was imposed tight security as a result of the JFK assassination a year and a half prior and the mother of [Lee Harvey Oswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Harvey_Oswald "Lee Harvey Oswald") was placed under surveillance by Police Chief Cato Hightower.[[153]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-153)

During a May 31, 1966, appearance at Huron College, Humphrey said the U.S. should not expect "either friendship or gratitude" in helping poorer countries.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-154) At a September 22, 1966 [Jamesburg, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamesburg,_New_Jersey "Jamesburg, New Jersey") Democratic Party fundraiser, Humphrey said the Vietnam War would be shortened if the US stayed firm and hastened the return of troops: "We are making a decision not only to defend Vietnam, we are defending the United States of America."[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-155)

During a May 1967 news conference, Humphrey said American anger toward Vietnam was losing traction and that he could see a growth in popularity for President Johnson since a low point five months prior.[[156]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-156) During an August 2, 1967, appearance in [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit"), Humphrey proposed each state consider forming peacekeeping councils focused on preventing violence, gaining community cooperation, and listening to "the voices of those who have gone unheard."[[157]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-157)

On November 4, 1967, Humphrey cited [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") as an example of what Vietnam could resemble post a Viet Cong defeat while in [Jakarta, Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta,_Indonesia "Jakarta, Indonesia").[[158]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-158) The following day, Vice President Humphrey requested Indonesia attempt mediation in the Vietnam War during a meeting with [Suharto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suharto "Suharto") at Merdeka palace.[[159]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-159) On December 7, Vice President Humphrey said in an interview that the Viet Cong could potentially be the factor in creating a political compromise with the government of Saigon.[[160]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-160)

### Civil rights

In February 1965, President Johnson appointed Humphrey to the chairmanship of the President's Council on Equal Opportunity.[[161]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Dallek224-161) The position and board had been proposed by Humphrey, who told Johnson that the board should consist of members of the Cabinet and federal agency leaders and serve multiple roles: assisting agency cooperation, creating federal program consistency, using advanced planning to avoid potential racial unrest, creating public policy, and meeting with local and state level leaders.[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Thurber171-162) During his tenure, he appointed [Wiley A Branton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiley_A_Branton "Wiley A Branton") as executive director.[[163]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-163) During the first meeting of the group on March 3, Humphrey stated the budget was US$289,000 and pledged to ensure vigorous work by the small staff.[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Thurber171-162) Following the [Watts riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts_riots "Watts riots") in August of that year, Johnson downsized Humphrey's role as the administration's expert on civil rights. Dallek wrote the shift in role was in line with the change in policy the Johnson administration underwent in response to "the changing political mood in the country on aid to African Americans."[[161]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-Dallek224-161) In a private meeting with Joseph Califano on September 18, 1965, President Johnson stated his intent to remove Humphrey from the post of "point man" on civil rights within the administration, believing the vice president was tasked with enough work.[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-164) Days later, Humphrey met with Johnson, Attorney General [Nicholas Katzenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Katzenbach "Nicholas Katzenbach"), and [White House Counsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Counsel "White House Counsel")[Lee C. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_C._White "Lee C. White"). Johnson told Humphrey he would shorten his role within the administration's civil rights policies and pass a portion to Katzenbach, Califano writing that Humphrey agreed to go along with the plan reluctantly.[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-165)

In an August 1967 speech at a county officials national convention in Detroit, Humphrey called for the establishment of a [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan "Marshall Plan") that would curb poverty in the United States as well as address racial violence, and advocated for the creation of civil peace councils that would counter rioting. He said the councils should include representation from all minority groups and religions, state governments, the National Guard, and law enforcement agencies and that the United States would see itself out of trouble only when law and order was reestablished.[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey#cite_note-166)
