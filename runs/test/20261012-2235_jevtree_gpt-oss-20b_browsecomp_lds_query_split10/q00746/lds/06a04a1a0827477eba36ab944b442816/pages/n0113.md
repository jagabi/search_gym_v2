**Michael Joseph Mansfield** (March 16, 1903 – October 5, 2001) was an American [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") politician and diplomat who represented [Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana "Montana") in the [United States House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") from 1943 to 1953 and [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") from 1953 to 1977. As the leader of the [Senate Democratic Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Democratic_Caucus "Senate Democratic Caucus") from 1961 to 1977, Mansfield shepherded [Great Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Society "Great Society") programs through the Senate; his tenure of exactly sixteen years was the longest of any [party leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_leaders_of_the_United_States_Senate "Party leaders of the United States Senate") in Senate history, until the record was broken by [Mitch McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_McConnell "Mitch McConnell") in 2023.

Born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York, Mansfield grew up in [Great Falls, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Falls,_Montana "Great Falls, Montana"). He lied about his age to serve in the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy") during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"). After the war, he became a professor of history and political science at the [University of Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montana "University of Montana"). He won election to the House of Representatives and served on the [House Committee on Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs "United States House Committee on Foreign Affairs") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").

In 1952, he defeated incumbent Republican Senator [Zales Ecton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zales_Ecton "Zales Ecton") to take a seat in the Senate. Mansfield served as [Senate Majority Whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Majority_Whip "Senate Majority Whip") from 1957 to 1961. Mansfield ascended to [Senate Majority Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Majority_Leader "Senate Majority Leader") after [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson") resigned from the Senate to become vice president. In the later years of the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), he opposed escalation of American involvement and supported President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon")'s plans for [Vietnamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamization "Vietnamization").

After retiring from the Senate, Mansfield served as [United States Ambassador to Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Ambassador_to_Japan "United States Ambassador to Japan") from 1977 to 1988. Mansfield is the longest-serving American ambassador to Japan in history.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-wsj-1) Upon his retirement, he was awarded the nation's highest civilian honor, the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom"). Mansfield also worked as a senior adviser on East Asian affairs to [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs "Goldman Sachs").

## Early childhood

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early childhood")]

Michael Joseph (Mike) Mansfield was born on March 16, 1903, in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), to [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans "Irish Americans") immigrant parents, Patrick J. Mansfield and Josephine (née O'Brien) Mansfield.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-charting-2) His father worked various jobs, including as a construction worker, hotel porter, and maintenance man, to support the family.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-3) In 1910, Josephine died from nephritis.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-mansfieldfdn-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Oberdorfer,_p._18-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Oral_History_Number:_391-022_pdf-6) Shortly before her death, Patrick had been injured in a construction accident, which left him unable to care for his three children.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Younggren2023-7) As a result, Patrick sent Mike and his two sisters to live with his great-aunt and uncle, Richard and Margaret, in [Great Falls, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Falls,_Montana "Great Falls, Montana").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-mansfieldfdn-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Oberdorfer,_p._18-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Oral_History_Number:_391-022_pdf-6) In Montana, he attended local public schools, and worked in his relatives' grocery store.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-charting-2) He turned into a habitual runaway, even living at a state orphanage in [Twin Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Bridges,_Montana "Twin Bridges, Montana") for half a year.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-tribune-8)

In 1917, at age 14, Mansfield dropped out of school, left home and made his way to a [logging camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging_camp "Logging camp") in [Leavenworth, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavenworth,_Washington "Leavenworth, Washington"). There, he met members of the [Oregon National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_National_Guard "Oregon National Guard"), who were stationed nearby. They helped him board their troop train as it traveled east to New York, en route to their eventual deployment in [Europe.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_theatre_of_World_War_I "European theatre of World War I")[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Younggren2023-7)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-9) He made it to New York City, where he reunited with his father.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Younggren2023-7)

## Military service

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Military service")]

Wanting to join the war effort during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"),[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Younggren2023-7) the 14 year old Mansfield forged his father's signature in order to enlist in the [US Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy "US Navy").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-senate-11) He went on several overseas convoys on the [USS _Minneapolis_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Minneapolis_(C-13) "USS Minneapolis (C-13)"), but was discharged by the Navy after his real age was discovered.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-senate-11) (He was the last known veteran of the war to die before he reached the age of 100 and the last World War I veteran to sit in the US Senate.) After his Navy discharge, he enlisted in the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army "US Army"), serving as a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_(rank) "Private (rank)") from 1919 to 1920.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-congress-12)

Mansfield was a [Private First Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_First_Class "Private First Class") in the [US Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Marine_Corps "US Marine Corps") from 1920 to 1922.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-congress-12) He served in the Western Recruiting Division at San Francisco until January 1921, when he was transferred to the Marine Barracks at Puget Sound, Washington. The following month, he was detached to the Guard Company, Marine Barracks, Navy Yard, Mare Island, California. In April, he boarded the _USAT Sherman_, bound for the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"). After a brief stopover at the Marine Barracks at [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite "Cavite"), he arrived at his duty station on May 5, 1921, the Marine Barracks, Naval Station, Olongapo, Philippine Islands. One year later, Mansfield was assigned to Company A, Marine Battery, Asiatic Fleet. A short tour of duty with the Asiatic Fleet took him along the coast of China before he returned to Olongapo in late May 1922.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-senate-11) His service with the Marines established a lifelong interest in Asia.

That August, Mansfield returned to Cavite in preparation for his return to the United States and eventual discharge. On November 9, 1922, Private Mansfield was released from the Marine Corps on the completion of his enlistment. He was awarded the Good Conduct Medal, his character being described as "excellent" during his two years as a Marine.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-WhosWho-Bio-13)

## Education

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Education")]

Following his return to Montana in 1922, Mansfield worked as a "mucker" and shoveled ore and other waste in the [copper mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_mine "Copper mine") of [Butte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana "Butte, Montana") for eight years.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-congress-12) Having never attended high school, he gained admission to [Montana School of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Tech_of_the_University_of_Montana "Montana Tech of the University of Montana") (MSM) in Butte by taking their entrance examinations. He attended MSM from 1927 to 1928, studying to become a mining engineer.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-tribune-8)

In 1928, Mansfield met Maureen Hayes, a local schoolteacher who would become his wife. She encouraged him to pursue further education.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-MansfieldCenter-14) In December 1931, Mansfield began his studies at the [University of Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montana "University of Montana") (UM) in [Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula,_Montana "Missoula, Montana"),[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-tribune-8) where he took both high school and college courses.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-MansfieldFDN-Maureen-15) Maureen moved to Missoula, and they married on September 13, 1932. She worked as a social worker, to help support Mansfield through his studies.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-MansfieldFDN-Maureen-15)

At the University of Montana, Mansfield joined the [Alpha Tau Omega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Tau_Omega "Alpha Tau Omega") fraternity. In 1933, he earned both his [high school equivalency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_equivalency_degree "High school equivalency degree") and his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-mansfieldfdn-4) He continued with graduate studies at UM, while also working part-time in the [registrar's office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registrar_(education)#Registrar's_office "Registrar (education)")[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-charting-2) and teaching two courses as a [graduate assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_assistant "Graduate assistant").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-mansfieldfdn-4) He earned a Master of Arts degree from UM in 1934 with a thesis titled "American Diplomatic Relations with Korea, 1866–1910."

After completing his graduate studies, Mansfield accepted a position as administrator and professor at UM, teaching courses in Latin American and Far Eastern History,[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-UM-Library-Bio-16) and occasionally lecturing on Greek and Roman history.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-tribune-8) Additionally, he studied towards a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles") (UCLA) from 1936 to 1937.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-congress-12) Though he left UM in 1942 to enter politics, he remained a [tenured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_tenure "Academic tenure") Professor of History at the University of Montana until his death.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-MansfieldCenter-14)

## U.S. Representative

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: U.S. Representative")]

In 1940, Mansfield ran for the [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") nomination for the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") in [Montana's 1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana's_1st_congressional_district "Montana's 1st congressional district") but was defeated by [Jerry J. O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_J._O'Connell "Jerry J. O'Connell"), a former holder of the seat, in the primary. The general election was won by [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)")[Jeannette Rankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeannette_Rankin "Jeannette Rankin"), who had previously won what was formerly an at-large seat in the House in 1916 and served until her defeat in 1920.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-senate-11) Mansfield decided to run for the seat again in the following election and won it by defeating the businessman Howard K. Hazelbaker after Rankin, who had voted against the entry of the United States into World War II, decided not to run for what would have been her third term.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-wilson-17)

> A new-comer to the House, who is reportedly internationalist-minded, having been professor of history and political science at Montana State University[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-UMnames-18) for ten years. Though a supporter of the Administration's foreign policy, he is likely to be strongly critical of the smallness of China's share of [Lend-Lease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-Lease "Lend-Lease"), and of what he fears is the Administration's tendency to regard the Atlantic as more important than the Pacific, and of its apparent reluctance to regard the Chinese as an ally on equal footing. His strongly pro-Chinese sentiments may tend to make him somewhat anti-British on this score.

Mansfield served five terms in the House, being re-elected in 1944, 1946, 1948, and 1950. His military service and academic experience landed him a seat on the [House Foreign Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs "United States House Committee on Foreign Affairs").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-charting-2) He went to China on a special mission for US President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") in 1944 and served as a delegate to the ninth Inter-American Conference in [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia") in 1948.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-wilson-17) In 1951, he was appointed by President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman") as a delegate to the United Nations' sixth session in Paris. During his House tenure, he also expressed his support for [price controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_controls "Price controls"), a higher [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage_in_the_United_States "Minimum wage in the United States"), the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan "Marshall Plan"), and aid to [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey") and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece"). He opposed the [House Un-American Activities Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Un-American_Activities_Committee "House Un-American Activities Committee"), the [Taft–Hartley Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taft%E2%80%93Hartley_Act "Taft–Hartley Act"), and the [Twenty-second Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twenty-second Amendment to the United States Constitution").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-wilson-17)

## U.S. Senator

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: U.S. Senator")]

In [1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_United_States_Senate_election_in_Montana "1952 United States Senate election in Montana"), Mansfield was elected to the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") after he had narrowly defeated the Republican incumbent, [Zales Ecton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zales_Ecton "Zales Ecton").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-senate-11) He served as [Senate Majority Whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_party_leaders_of_the_United_States_Senate "Assistant party leaders of the United States Senate") under Majority Leader [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson") from 1957 to 1961.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-congress-12) In 1961, after Johnson resigned from the Senate to become Vice President, Mansfield was unanimously elected the Democratic floor leader and thus [Senate Majority Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_leaders_of_the_United_States_Senate "Party leaders of the United States Senate"). Serving sixteen years, from 1961 until his retirement in 1977, Mansfield is the longest-serving Majority Leader in the history of the Senate.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-senate-11)_[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Washington\_Post "The Washington Post")_ compared Mansfield's behavior as Majority Leader to Johnson's by saying, "Instead of Johnson's browbeating tactics, Mansfield led by setting an example of humility and accommodation."[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-tribune-8)

Mansfield was critical of US involvement in [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"). On December 28, 1960, he opined that US aid to Laos had produced nothing but "chaos, discontent, armies on the loose, and a large mission of hundreds of officials in [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane "Vientiane")."[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-19)

An early supporter of [Ngo Dinh Diem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngo_Dinh_Diem "Ngo Dinh Diem"), Mansfield altered his opinion on the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") after a visit to Vietnam in 1962. He reported to [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy") on December 2, 1962, that US money given to Diem's government was being squandered and that the US should avoid further involvement in [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"). He was thus the first American official to comment even mildly negatively on the war's condition.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-20)

On September 25, 1963, Mansfield introduced Kennedy during a joint appearance with him at the Yellowstone County Fairgrounds, Kennedy expressing his appreciation afterward and adding, "I know that those of you who live in Montana know something of his character and his high standard of public service, but I am not sure that you are completely aware of what a significant role he has played in the last three years in passing through the United States Senate measure after measure which strengthens this country at home and abroad."[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-21)

Mansfield delivered a eulogy on November 24, 1963, as President Kennedy's casket [lay in state in the Capitol rotunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funeral_of_John_F._Kennedy "State funeral of John F. Kennedy"), saying, "He gave that we might give of ourselves, that we might give to one another until there would be no room, no room at all, for the bigotry, the hatred, prejudice, and the arrogance which converged in that moment of horror to strike him down."[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Mansfield-22)

During the Johnson administration, Mansfield, convinced that it was a blunder based on just aims, became a skeptic of US involvement in the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). In February 1965, he lobbied against escalating aerial bombardment of [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam") in the aftermath of [Pleiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleiku "Pleiku"), arguing in a letter to the president that [Operation Rolling Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rolling_Thunder "Operation Rolling Thunder") would lead to a need for "vastly strengthened... American forces."[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-23)

In 1964, Mansfield, as Senate Majority Leader, filed a procedural motion to have the [Civil Rights Act of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964 "Civil Rights Act of 1964") discussed by the whole Senate rather than by the [Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Judiciary_Committee "Senate Judiciary Committee"), which had killed similar legislation seven years earlier.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-judiciarysenategov-24) Mansfield voted in favor of the [Civil Rights Acts of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964 "Civil Rights Act of 1964") and [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1968 "Civil Rights Act of 1968"),[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-26) as well as the [24th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twenty-fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution") and the [Voting Rights Act of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act_of_1965 "Voting Rights Act of 1965").[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-27)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-29) Mansfield voted in favor of the initial Senate amendment to the [Civil Rights Act of 1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1957 "Civil Rights Act of 1957") on August 7, 1957,[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-30) but did not vote on the House amendment to the bill on August 29, 1957.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-31) Mansfield did not vote on the [Civil Rights Act of 1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1960 "Civil Rights Act of 1960") or the confirmation of [Thurgood Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurgood_Marshall "Thurgood Marshall") to the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-32)[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-33) Mansfield co-sponsored [Mark Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hatfield "Mark Hatfield")'s Neighborhood Government Act, which would have diverted social service provisions to neighborhood levels, and also supported the [Comprehensive Child Development Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Child_Development_Act "Comprehensive Child Development Act") of 1971 and the [Equal Rights Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Rights_Amendment "Equal Rights Amendment") in 1972.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-36)

He hailed the new [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") administration, especially the "[Nixon Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_Doctrine "Nixon Doctrine")" announced at [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam") in 1969 that the US would honor all treaty commitments, provide a [nuclear umbrella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_umbrella "Nuclear umbrella") for its allies, and supply weapons and technical assistance to countries where warranted without committing American forces to local conflicts.

In turn, Nixon turned to Mansfield for advice and as his liaison with the Senate on Vietnam. Nixon began a steady withdrawal and replacement of US troops shortly after he took office in January 1969, a policy supported by Mansfield. During his first term, Nixon reduced American forces by 95%, leaving only 24,200 in late 1972; the last ones left in March 1973.

During the economic crisis of 1971, Mansfield was not afraid to reach across the aisle to help the economy:

> What we're in is not a Republican recession or a Democratic recession; both parties had much to do with bringing us where we are today. But we're facing a national situation which calls for the best which all of us can produce, because we know the results will be something which we will regret.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-37)

Mansfield attended the November 17, 1976, meeting between President-elect [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter") and Democratic congressional leaders in which Carter sought out support for a proposal to have the president's power to reorganize the government reinstated with potential to be vetoed by Congress.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-38)

### Mansfield Amendments

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Mansfield Amendments")]

Two controversial amendments by Mansfield limiting military funding of research were passed by Congress.

*   The Mansfield Amendment of 1969, passed as part of the fiscal year 1970 Military Authorization Act (Public Law 91-121), prohibited military funding of research that lacked a direct or apparent relationship to specific military function. Through subsequent modification the Mansfield amendment moved the Department of Defense toward the support of more short-term applied research in universities.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-39) The amendment affected the military, such as research funding by the Office of Naval Research (ONR).[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-40)
*   The Mansfield Amendment of 1973 expressly limited appropriations for defense research through the [Advanced Research Projects Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Research_Projects_Agency "Advanced Research Projects Agency"), which is largely independent of the military, to projects with direct military application.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-41) This controversial amendment greatly reduced ARPA funding for many university-based computer projects, thereby forcing many American [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science") experts to move to private sector research facilities such as [Xerox PARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerox_PARC "Xerox PARC"). However, for that very reason, the amendment is also credited with giving birth to the contemporary computer technology industry.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Waks_Page_74-42)

An earlier Mansfield Amendment, offered in 1971, called for the number of US troops stationed in Europe to be halved. On May 19, 1971, however, the Senate defeated the resolution 61–36.

## U.S. ambassador to Japan

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: U.S. ambassador to Japan")]

[![Image 7](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Mike_mansfield.jpg/250px-Mike_mansfield.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mike_mansfield.jpg)

1978 painting of Mansfield

Mansfield did not run for reelection in 1976 and retired in January 1977; in April 1977 President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter") appointed him as ambassador to Japan.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-43) a role that he retained during the [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_administration "Reagan administration") until 1988. While serving in Japan, Mansfield was highly respected and was particularly renowned for describing the US-Japan relationship as the "most important bilateral relationship in the world, bar none."[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-44) Mansfield's successor in Japan, [Michael Armacost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Armacost "Michael Armacost"), noted in his memoirs that for Mansfield, the phrase was a "mantra." While in office, Mansfield also fostered relations between his home state of Montana and Japan. The state capital of [Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena,_Montana "Helena, Montana") is the sister city to [Kumamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumamoto "Kumamoto"), on the island of [Kyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu "Kyushu").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-mansfieldsen-45)

## Honors

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Honors")]

The [Maureen and Mike Mansfield Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_and_Mike_Mansfield_Library "Maureen and Mike Mansfield Library") at the [University of Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montana_%E2%80%93_Missoula "University of Montana – Missoula"), [Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula,_Montana "Missoula, Montana"), is named after him and his wife Maureen,[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-MansfieldLibrary-46) as was his request when informed of the honor. The library also contains the Maureen and Mike Mansfield Center, which is dedicated to [Asian studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_studies "Asian studies"), international relations, and the preservation and promotion of democracy. The [Mike Mansfield Federal Building and United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield_Federal_Building_and_United_States_Courthouse "Mike Mansfield Federal Building and United States Courthouse") in [Butte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana "Butte, Montana") was renamed in his honor in 2002.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-47)

The [Montana Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Democratic_Party "Montana Democratic Party") holds an annual Mansfield-Metcalf Dinner named partially in his honor.

In 1977, Mansfield received the US Senator John Heinz Award for Greatest Public Service by an Elected or Appointed Official, an award given out annually by [Jefferson Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Awards_for_Public_Service "Jefferson Awards for Public Service").[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-48)

In 1977, he was awarded the [Laetare Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laetare_Medal "Laetare Medal") by the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame "University of Notre Dame"), the oldest and most prestigious award for [American Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Catholics "American Catholics").[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-49)

On January 19, 1989, Mansfield and Secretary of State [George P. Shultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_P._Shultz "George P. Shultz") were awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom") by President [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan"). In his speech, Reagan recognized Mansfield as someone who has "distinguished himself as a dedicated public servant and loyal American."[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-50) In 1990, he was given both the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy "United States Military Academy"), [Sylvanus Thayer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvanus_Thayer_Award "Sylvanus Thayer Award") and Japan's [Order of the Rising Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Rising_Sun "Order of the Rising Sun") with Paulownia Flowers, Grand Cordon. This is Japan's highest honor for someone who is not a head of state.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-mongolia2007-51)

In 1999, Missoula's daily newspaper, the _[Missoulian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoulian "Missoulian")_, chose Mansfield as The Most Influential Montanan of the 20th Century.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-52)

## Final years and death

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mike_Mansfield&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Final years and death")]

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Remarks_by_the_President_and_Ambassador_to_Japan_Howard_Baker_During_Swearing-In_Ceremony.jpg/250px-Remarks_by_the_President_and_Ambassador_to_Japan_Howard_Baker_During_Swearing-In_Ceremony.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Remarks_by_the_President_and_Ambassador_to_Japan_Howard_Baker_During_Swearing-In_Ceremony.jpg)

Just a few months before his death, Mansfield appears behind [Howard Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Baker "Howard Baker"), the new U.S. Ambassador to Japan with [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States")[George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush"), June 26, 2001

Throughout his later years, Mansfield lived in Washington, D.C., where he remained active, delivering [speaking engagements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaking_engagement "Speaking engagement") well into his nineties and working from his office at Goldman Sachs until the week before his death.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Rosenbaum-53)

Mansfield's wife, Maureen, died on September 20, 2000.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-54) He died from heart failure at [Walter Reed Army Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Reed_Army_Medical_Center "Walter Reed Army Medical Center") just over one year later, on October 5, 2001, at the age of 98.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-mongolia2007-51)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Rosenbaum-53)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-55) He was survived by his daughter, Anne Fairclough Mansfield (1939–2013),[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-Washington_post_obituary_Anne_F_Mansfield-56) and one granddaughter.

The [burial plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grave_(burial) "Grave (burial)") of Mansfield and his wife can be found in section 2, marker 49-69F of [Arlington National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_National_Cemetery "Arlington National Cemetery"). His gravemarker reads, "Michael Joseph Mansfield - PVT, US Marine Corps."[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#cite_note-MansfieldFellowshipProgram-Bio-57)
