**Joan Mondale** (née **Adams**; August 8, 1930 – February 3, 2014) was the [second lady of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_ladies_and_gentlemen_of_the_United_States "Second ladies and gentlemen of the United States") from 1977 until 1981 as the wife of [Walter Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mondale "Walter Mondale"), the 42nd [vice president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president_of_the_United_States "Vice president of the United States"). She was an artist and author and served on the boards of several organizations.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-NYT_Obit-1) For her promotion of the arts, she was affectionately dubbed **Joan of Art**.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-BrianBakst-2)

## Family and education

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Joan_Mondale&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Family and education")]

Joan Adams was born on August 8, 1930, in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon "Eugene, Oregon"), one of three daughters of the Rev. John Maxwell Adams, a [Presbyterian minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_polity#Offices "Presbyterian polity"), and his wife, the former Eleanor Jane Hall.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-NYT_Obit-1) She attended [Media Friends School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media-Providence_Friends_School "Media-Providence Friends School"), an integrated [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker "Quaker") school in [Media, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media,_Pennsylvania "Media, Pennsylvania"); a public school in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio"); and later [St. Paul Academy and Summit School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School "St. Paul Academy and Summit School") in [St. Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul,_Minnesota "St. Paul, Minnesota"). In 1952, she graduated from [Macalester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College "Macalester College") in St. Paul, where her father served as chaplain, with a bachelor's degree in history. Following graduation from college, she worked at the [Boston Museum of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Fine_Arts,_Boston "Museum of Fine Arts, Boston") and the [Minneapolis Institute of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_Institute_of_Art "Minneapolis Institute of Art").

On December 27, 1955, Joan married [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") lawyer Walter "Fritz" Mondale, whom she had met on a [blind date](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/blind%20date "wiktionary:blind date").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-bloomberg1-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-4)

The couple had three children:

*   [Ted Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Mondale "Ted Mondale") (b. October 12, 1957), Minnesota politician, former [State Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Senate "Minnesota Senate"), and candidate for [Governor of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Minnesota "Governor of Minnesota") in 1998.
*   [Eleanor Jane Mondale Poling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Mondale "Eleanor Mondale") (January 19, 1960 – September 17, 2011), television and radio personality who died of brain cancer at the age of 51.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-bloomberg1-3)
*   William Hall Mondale (b. February 27, 1962), Assistant Attorney General, [Office of the Minnesota Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Minnesota "Attorney General of Minnesota") (1990–2000)

## Political life

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Joan_Mondale&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Political life")]

[![Image 5](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/1984JoanWalterMondale.jpg/250px-1984JoanWalterMondale.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1984JoanWalterMondale.jpg)

Joan and Walter Mondale in 1984

In 1964, Walter Mondale replaced [Hubert Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey "Hubert Humphrey") as a U.S. Senator, serving until 1976, when Democratic presidential candidate [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter") selected him as his running-mate in his successful bid for the Presidency.

Joan Mondale then became Second Lady, succeeding [Happy Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Rockefeller "Happy Rockefeller"), during her husband's term as vice president from 1977 to 1981. She and her husband also became the first Second Lady and vice president to live at [Number One Observatory Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_One_Observatory_Circle "Number One Observatory Circle").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-7) She was succeeded as Second Lady by [Barbara Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Bush "Barbara Bush").

Joan Mondale was a supporter of the [Equal Rights Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Rights_Amendment "Equal Rights Amendment") and often lent her support publicly to the cause.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-NYT_Obit-1)

Out of office during [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan")'s first term as president, Walter Mondale won the Democratic presidential nomination in 1984. As a prospective First Lady, Joan Mondale told [Maureen Dowd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_Dowd "Maureen Dowd") of _The New York Times_ that she would not talk about recipes or clothes during the campaign, but when her husband's political opponents took issue with this, costing him votes, she published _The Mondale Family Cookbook_, with recipes including Fettucine à la Pimiento Mondale, and declared that she was a "traditional wife and mother and supporter".[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-8)

Walter Mondale lost the election, and the Mondales returned to Minnesota, where they lived until his term as [U.S. Ambassador to Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Ambassador_to_Japan "U.S. Ambassador to Japan") from 1993 to 1996, after which he resumed his Minneapolis-based law practice.

## Joan of Art

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Joan_Mondale&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Joan of Art")]

[![Image 6](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Joan_Mondale_Playing_Drums.jpg/250px-Joan_Mondale_Playing_Drums.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Joan_Mondale_Playing_Drums.jpg)

Mondale playing the drums after a press conference at the [National Museum of African Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_African_Art "National Museum of African Art") with, (l–r), Rep. [Lindy Boggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindy_Boggs "Lindy Boggs"), Rep. [Walter E. Fauntroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_E._Fauntroy "Walter E. Fauntroy"), [Warren M. Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_M._Robbins "Warren M. Robbins") (founder of the museum), and Sen. [Wendell Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Anderson "Wendell Anderson"), 1978

Joan Mondale was a lifelong practitioner, patron, and advocate of the arts, and her nickname 'Joan of Art' was a tribute.

An accomplished potter, she studied art at college, and then worked in galleries, before moving to Washington as a Senator's wife in 1964, and led guided tours at the [National Gallery of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Art "National Gallery of Art"). In 1972, she wrote a book _Politics in Art_, examining how political commentary is reflected in artworks. Later she regularly gave tours as a guide for friend Ellen Proxmire (the then wife of Wisconsin Democratic Senator [William Proxmire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Proxmire "William Proxmire"))'s company whirl-around.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-9)

Later, as Second Lady, she turned the [Vice Presidential Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_One_Observatory_Circle "Number One Observatory Circle") into a showcase of American art, with works by artists including [Robert Rauschenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rauschenberg "Robert Rauschenberg"), [Edward Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Hopper "Edward Hopper"), [Louise Nevelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Nevelson "Louise Nevelson"), and [Ansel Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansel_Adams "Ansel Adams"). At this time, she also served as chairperson of the [Federal Council on the Arts and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Council_on_the_Arts_and_Humanities "Federal Council on the Arts and Humanities").

As the U.S. Ambassador's wife in Japan, she enthusiastically promoted inter-cultural understanding through art, redecorating the Embassy with American paintings and organising tours with a bi-lingual guide. She studied [Japanese art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_art "Japanese art"),[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-10) and impressed the Mayor of [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto "Kyoto") by presenting him with a ceramic bowl she had made herself in the traditional Mashiko style.

She was the author of _Letters From Japan_, a collection of essays about life overseas published in 1998.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-NYT_Obit-1)

Back in Minnesota, Mondale continued to make her own pottery and promote the arts. She served on the boards of the [Minnesota Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Orchestra "Minnesota Orchestra"), [Walker Art Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_Art_Center "Walker Art Center"), [Macalester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College "Macalester College") and the [National Portrait Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Portrait_Gallery_(United_States) "National Portrait Gallery (United States)"). In 2004, the Textile Center in Minneapolis endowed an exhibition space in her honor, the Joan Mondale Gallery.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-11)

She also served as a member of the Postmaster General's [Citizens' Stamp Advisory Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Stamp_Advisory_Committee "Citizens' Stamp Advisory Committee") from 2005 to 2010.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-12)

## Death

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Joan_Mondale&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Death")]

On February 2, 2014, Mondale's family announced that she had entered hospice care due to [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-13)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-14) She died at a care facility in Minneapolis the following day, at age 83.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-15) Her remains were cremated,[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-16) and later dispersed along the [St. Croix River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Croix_River_(Wisconsin%E2%80%93Minnesota) "St. Croix River (Wisconsin–Minnesota)").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale#cite_note-17)
