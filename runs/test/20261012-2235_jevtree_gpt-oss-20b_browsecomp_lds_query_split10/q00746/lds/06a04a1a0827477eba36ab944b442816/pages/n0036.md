**St. Paul Academy and Summit School** is a private [college preparatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_preparatory_school "College preparatory school") day school in [Saint Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "Saint Paul, Minnesota"), United States, for students in grades [K–12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%E2%80%9312 "K–12").

The school was established in 1969 through a merger of St. Paul Academy, a school for boys, and Summit School, a school for girls. St. Paul Academy was founded in 1900 and Summit School in 1917.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-2) Accredited by the Independent Schools Association of the Central States, SPA is a member of the [National Association of Independent Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Independent_Schools "National Association of Independent Schools"), the Cum Laude Society, and [The College Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_College_Board "The College Board"), and is a founding member of the Association of College Counselors in Independent Schools.

## Campuses

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Campuses")]

The school has two campuses: the Goodrich Campus and the Randolph Campus.

*   The **Goodrich Campus**, site of the old Summit School for girls, is the home of the Lower School (grades K–5, ~290 students) and contains the Sarah Converse Auditorium, formerly the home of SPA theater productions. It is at 1150 [Goodrich Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Goodrich "Aaron Goodrich").
*   The **Randolph Campus**, site of the old St. Paul Academy for boys, is the home of the Middle School (grades 6–8, ~240 students) and the Upper School (grades 9–12, ~380 students). Drake hockey arena, the Harry M. Drake Gallery, and the Huss Center for the Performing Arts are on this campus, at 1712 Randolph Avenue.

School hours are from 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. (8:45 a.m. to 3 p.m. on Wednesdays), with exceptions for after-school activities.

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")]

The school has two predecessor schools, The Barnard School, set up in or before 1887, and The Freeman School, set up around the same time. The Barnard School became St. Paul Academy for boys in 1900. The Freeman School became Summit School in 1916.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-3) These schools merged in 1969.

## Academics

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Academics")]

In the Upper School (grades 9–12), SPA has a college-preparatory liberal arts curriculum.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-4) SPA teaches an independent curriculum that does not follow either the [AP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Placement "Advanced Placement") or [IB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baccalaureate "International Baccalaureate") curriculum models, though individual students may opt to take AP tests. Courses have been taught using [Harkness tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harkness_table "Harkness table"), distinctive elliptical wooden tables that seat 12–15 students, since 2005.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-5)

## Athletics

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Athletics")]

SPA competes in the Independent Metro Athletic Conference (IMAC), which is part of the Minnesota State High School League. The school mascot is a Spartan, and the school's main rivals are [Breck School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breck_School "Breck School"), [Blake School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_School_(Minneapolis,_Minnesota) "Blake School (Minneapolis, Minnesota)"), and [Minnehaha Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnehaha_Academy "Minnehaha Academy"). SPA offers 15 varsity sports and 34 teams. The school also recognizes club sports, including Ultimate Frisbee, trap shooting, and sailing.

### Tennis

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Tennis")]

Several notable SPA tennis players have won state championships, including Gina Suh, who won the Minnesota State Tennis Single's Championship five years in a row. The girls' program has made over 20 state appearances, claiming the state title in 1976, 1977, 1979, 1980, 1981, 1982, 1983, 1986, and 1988, while the boys' program has made 13 appearances and won the state championship in 1993, 2004, 2007, 2008, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-6)

## Academic teams

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Academic teams")]

SPA has a Quiz Bowl team, which is registered with [NAQT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAQT "NAQT") and participates in the Minnesota High School Quiz Bowl's East Division.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-7) SPA sent its A team to the NAQT High School National Championship Tournament in 2014, and both its A and B team in 2015 and 2016. It also has competitive math, chess, debate and science teams. As of the 2014–15 debate season, the debate team has made the Minnesota State High School Debate Tournament 14 straight years and 17 out of the last 18 years.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-8)

## The Rubicon

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: The Rubicon")]

The Rubicon, the school's newspaper, has received the Pacemaker from the [National Scholastic Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Scholastic_Press_Association "National Scholastic Press Association") for ten years in a row and Crown awards from the [Columbia Scholastic Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Scholastic_Press_Association "Columbia Scholastic Press Association"), the country's top awards for student journalism, consecutively since 2015.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Academy_and_Summit_School#cite_note-9)

*   [Steven Levitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Levitt "Steven Levitt") (1985) is the author of 2005 [New York Times bestselling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times_bestselling "New York Times bestselling") book _[Freakonomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freakonomics "Freakonomics")_. He led the SPA [Quiz Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiz_Bowl "Quiz Bowl") team to nationals two years consecutively.
*   [John C. Lilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Lilly "John C. Lilly") (1933) was a neuroscientist, psychoanalyst, philosopher, and inventor, known for his research on dolphin communication and psychedelic drugs. His family is the namesake of SPA's Lilly Courtyard.
*   [Amos Magee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Magee "Amos Magee") (born 1971), soccer player, coach, and front office
*   [Anil Menon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Menon_(NASA_astronaut) "Anil Menon (NASA astronaut)") (1995) is an American physician and NASA astronaut.

*   [Joan Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale "Joan Mondale") (1948) was an author, advocate for the arts, and the Second Lady of the United States. *   [William Pedersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Pedersen_(architect) "William Pedersen (architect)") (1956), partner in [Kohn Pedersen Fox Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohn_Pedersen_Fox_Associates "Kohn Pedersen Fox Associates"), is the lead architect on the [Shanghai World Financial Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_World_Financial_Center "Shanghai World Financial Center"), one of the [world's tallest buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_tallest_building "World's tallest building").
*   [Tony Sanneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Sanneh "Tony Sanneh") (1990) is a professional [soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer "Soccer") player who has won two [Major League Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Soccer "Major League Soccer") Cups and played every minute for the United States in the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup "2002 FIFA World Cup") in Korea/Japan.
*   [Tim Sheehy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Sheehy "Tim Sheehy") (2004), U.S. Senator from Montana.
*   [John Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tate_(mathematician) "John Tate (mathematician)") (1942) was a number theorist and winner of the 2010 [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize").
*   [Shirley Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Williams "Shirley Williams") (attended 1940–43) was a politician and a [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom "House of Commons of the United Kingdom") cabinet member in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom")
