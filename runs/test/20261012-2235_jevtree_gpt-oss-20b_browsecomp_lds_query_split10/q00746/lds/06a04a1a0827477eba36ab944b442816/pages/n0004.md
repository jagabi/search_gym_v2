We honor the life and legacy of our beloved friend, Former State Representative Karen Joy Clark ![Image 1: 🌻](https://static.xx.fbcdn.net/images/emoji.php/v9/t1a/1/16/1f33b.png)

Karen Clark proudly served South Minneapolis in the Minnesota House of Representatives for nearly four decades, from 1981 to 2019. She represented District 62A, including portions of the Whittier, Phillips, Ventura Village, Seward, and Lyn-Lake neighborhoods.

Over 19 terms representing the Southside in the Legislature, Karen was deeply committed to environmental justice, and a fierce advocate for working people, economic justice, and human rights, with a decades-long legacy as a champion of the people we love, and the places we call home.

Her Worker’s Right-to-Know bill in the early 1980’s made it our right to be notified and protected when exposed to toxic chemicals at a worksite. In 1993, she played a critical role in passing an amendment to the MN Human Rights Act that banned LGBT discrimination in housing, employment, public services, credit, and education.

In 2008, Karen passed first-of-its-kind legislation to require new operations in the Phillips neighborhoods to account for the “cumulative effects” of long-term pollution on residents in the Southside Green Zone. In 2013, Karen was the House sponsor on the bill that legalized same-sex marriage in the State of Minnesota.

She passed the Dislocated Workers Training Fund, created culturally specific shelters for those experiencing domestic violence, passed the BPA-free baby bottle bill to reduce toxins in consumer products, and a bill that converted military industrial jobs to peaceful socially responsible manufacturing.

As a Board member for the East Phillips Neighborhood Institute, for over a decade, she fought tooth and nail to help the neighborhood win control of the Roof Depot site to realize the community’s vision of an indoor urban farm. She lived to see the neighborhood finally purchase it from the City. We will honor her memory by doing everything in our power to see this project through and make it a reality.

Karen Clark dedicated her life to making Minnesota a better place.

She has left a lasting impact on Minnesota for generations to come.

We are deeply grateful and indebted for her service.
