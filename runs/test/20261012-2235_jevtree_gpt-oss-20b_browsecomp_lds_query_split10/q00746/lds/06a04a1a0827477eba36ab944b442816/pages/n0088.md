**Walter Frederick** "**Fritz**" **Mondale** (January 5, 1928–April 19, 2021) was an American politician who was the 42nd [vice president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president_of_the_United_States "Vice president of the United States"), serving from 1977 to 1981 under President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter"). A member of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"), he represented [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota") in the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") from 1964 to 1976, and was the [Democratic nominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries "1984 Democratic Party presidential primaries") in the [1984 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election "1984 United States presidential election").

Mondale was born in the small municipality of [Ceylon, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon,_Minnesota "Ceylon, Minnesota"), and graduated from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota") in 1951 after attending [Macalester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College "Macalester College"). He then served in the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") during the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War") before earning a [law degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_degree "Law degree") in 1956. He married [Joan Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mondale "Joan Mondale") in 1955. Working as a lawyer in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"), Mondale was appointed [Minnesota Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Attorney_General "Minnesota Attorney General") in 1960 by Governor [Orville Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orville_Freeman "Orville Freeman") and was elected to a full term as attorney general in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Minnesota_Attorney_General_election "1962 Minnesota Attorney General election") with 60% of the vote. He was appointed to the U.S. Senate by Governor [Karl Rolvaag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rolvaag "Karl Rolvaag") upon the resignation of U.S. senator [Hubert Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey "Hubert Humphrey") following Humphrey's election as vice president in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election "1964 United States presidential election"). Mondale was elected to a full Senate term in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "1966 United States Senate election in Minnesota") and reelected in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "1972 United States Senate election in Minnesota"), resigning in 1976 as he prepared to succeed to the vice presidency in 1977. While in the Senate, he supported [consumer protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_protection "Consumer protection"), [fair housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_housing "Fair housing"), [tax reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_reform "Tax reform"), and the [desegregation of schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_integration_in_the_United_States "School integration in the United States"); he served on the [Church Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Committee "Church Committee").

In [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election "1976 United States presidential election"), Jimmy Carter, the Democratic presidential nominee, chose Mondale as his vice-presidential running mate. The Carter–Mondale ticket narrowly defeated the Republican ticket of incumbent president [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford "Gerald Ford") and his running mate [Bob Dole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dole "Bob Dole"). The economy worsened during Carter and Mondale's time in office, and they lost the [1980 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_presidential_election "1980 United States presidential election") in a [landslide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_victory "Landslide victory") to Republicans [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") and [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush "George H. W. Bush"). In [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election "1984 United States presidential election"), Mondale won the Democratic presidential nomination and campaigned for a [nuclear freeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Freeze_campaign "Nuclear Freeze campaign"), the ratification of the [Equal Rights Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Rights_Amendment "Equal Rights Amendment"), an increase in taxes, and a reduction of [U.S. public debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_debt_of_the_United_States "National debt of the United States"). His vice presidential nominee, U.S. representative [Geraldine Ferraro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Ferraro "Geraldine Ferraro") from New York, was the first female vice-presidential nominee of any major party in U.S. history. Mondale and Ferraro lost the election to the incumbents Reagan and Bush, with Reagan winning 49 states and Mondale carrying only his home state of Minnesota and [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.")

After his defeat, Mondale joined the Minnesota-based law firm [Dorsey & Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsey_&_Whitney "Dorsey & Whitney") and the [National Democratic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Institute "National Democratic Institute") (1986–1993). President [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") appointed Mondale [United States ambassador to Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_Japan "List of ambassadors of the United States to Japan") in 1993; he retired from that post in 1996.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-1) In [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "2002 United States Senate election in Minnesota"), Mondale became the last-minute choice of the [Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party "Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party") to run for Senate after Democratic U.S. Senate member [Paul Wellstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wellstone "Paul Wellstone") was killed in a plane crash less than two weeks before the election. Mondale narrowly lost the race to the [mayor of Saint Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "List of mayors of Saint Paul, Minnesota"), [Norm Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_Coleman "Norm Coleman"). He then returned to working at Dorsey & Whitney and remained active in the Democratic Party. Mondale later took up a part-time teaching position at the University of Minnesota's [Humphrey School of Public Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_School_of_Public_Affairs "Humphrey School of Public Affairs").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-2) He died in 2021 from [natural causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manner_of_death "Manner of death").

## Early life

Walter Frederick Mondale was born on January 5, 1928, in [Ceylon, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon,_Minnesota "Ceylon, Minnesota"),[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-3) to Theodore Sigvaard Mondale, a [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodism "Methodism") minister, and his second wife Claribel Hope (née Cowan), a part-time music teacher.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-5) Mondale had two full brothers, Clarence, (known as Pete) and William (known as Mort). Their half-brother [Lester Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Mondale "Lester Mondale") became a [Unitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Unitarian_Association "American Unitarian Association") minister.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-6) The brothers' paternal grandparents were [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Americans "Norwegian Americans") immigrants, with some distant [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans "German Americans") ancestry,[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-7) and their paternal grandfather Frederik Mundal had emigrated from [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") with his family in 1856, eventually settling in the southern part of Minnesota in 1864.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-8) The surname "Mondale" was [Americanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanization_(immigration) "Americanization (immigration)") from that of Mundal, a valley and village in the [Fjærland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fj%C3%A6rland "Fjærland") region of Norway.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-nytobit-9) His mother was born in [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa "Iowa"), the daughter of a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian "Canadian") immigrant father, Robert Cowan (1861-1950), who was born a British subject in [Seaforth, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaforth,_Ontario "Seaforth, Ontario") six years before [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation "Canadian Confederation"); she was of [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Americans "Scottish Americans") and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Americans "English Americans") descent.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-10)

In his youth, Mondale's family thought the names "Walter" and "Frederick" were too stilted for a boy, so they called him "[Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz "Fritz")", a common German and [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia "Scandinavia") diminutive form of Friedrich or Frederick.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-11) Due to the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States "Great Depression in the United States"), Mondale grew up in poverty. His family moved from Ceylon to [Heron Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heron_Lake,_Minnesota "Heron Lake, Minnesota") in 1934, and to [Elmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmore,_Minnesota "Elmore, Minnesota") in 1937.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-12) Throughout his youth, Mondale was influenced heavily by his father's religious beliefs, including support for the civil rights movement.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-13) In 1948, his father died of a stroke.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-14) Mondale attended public schools and then [Macalester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College "Macalester College") for two years before transferring to the University of Minnesota, from which he graduated _[cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin\_honors "Latin honors")_ with a Bachelor of Arts degree in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science") in 1951.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-congressbio-15)

Mondale (middle row, second from left) with other staff members of the _[Minnesota Law Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota\_Law\_Review "Minnesota Law Review")_, 1955

As Mondale could not afford to attend law school, he enlisted in the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") in 1951, shortly after graduating.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-Yearbook-16) He served with the [3rd Armored Division Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Armored_Division_Artillery_(United_States) "3rd Armored Division Artillery (United States)") at [Fort Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Knox "Fort Knox"), Kentucky, during the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War"), first as an armored reconnaissance vehicle crewman, and later as an education programs specialist and associate editor of the unit's newsletter, _Tanker's Dust_.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-Yearbook-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-17) He attained the rank of [corporal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporal#United_States "Corporal") and was honorably discharged in 1953.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-Yearbook-16) With money earned from his military service, Mondale enrolled at the [University of Minnesota Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Law_School "University of Minnesota Law School"), aided by the [G.I. Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Bill "G.I. Bill"), and graduated _cum laude_ with a [Bachelor of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws "Bachelor of Laws") in 1956. In law school, he served on the _[Minnesota Law Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota\_Law\_Review "Minnesota Law Review")_ and as a [law clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_clerk "Law clerk") for [Minnesota Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Supreme_Court "Minnesota Supreme Court") justice [Thomas F. Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_F._Gallagher "Thomas F. Gallagher"). In 1955, Mondale married Joan Adams, whom he met on a blind date. He then practiced law in Minneapolis for four years before entering politics.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-18)

## Entry into politics

Mondale became involved in national politics in the 1940s. At age 20, he was visible in Minnesota politics by helping organize Hubert Humphrey's successful Senate campaign in [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "1948 United States Senate election in Minnesota"). Humphrey's campaign assigned Mondale to cover the staunchly Republican [2nd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota's_2nd_congressional_district "Minnesota's 2nd congressional district"). Mondale, who had grown up in the region, was able to win the district for Humphrey by a comfortable margin.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-19) After working with Humphrey, Mondale went on to work on several campaigns for Orville Freeman. Mondale worked on Freeman's unsuccessful [1952 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Minnesota_gubernatorial_election "1952 Minnesota gubernatorial election") for the governorship as well as his successful campaign in [1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Minnesota_gubernatorial_election "1954 Minnesota gubernatorial election") and his [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Minnesota_gubernatorial_election "1958 Minnesota gubernatorial election") reelection campaign.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-thegoodfight-20) In 1960, Freeman appointed Mondale [Minnesota Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Minnesota "Attorney General of Minnesota") following the resignation of [Miles Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Lord "Miles Lord"). At the time he was appointed, Mondale was 32 years old and had been practicing law for four years. He was elected to the post in his own right in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Minnesota_Attorney_General_election "1962 Minnesota Attorney General election").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-21)

## U.S. Senate (1964–1976)

[![Image 9](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/93/Mondale_as_Senator.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mondale_as_Senator.jpg)

Senator Walter F. Mondale

On December 30, 1964, [Governor of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Minnesota "Governor of Minnesota") Karl Rolvaag appointed Mondale to the United States Senate to fill the vacancy created by Hubert Humphrey's resignation; Humphrey had stepped down after being elected [Vice President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States "Vice President of the United States"). Mondale was elected to the Senate for the first time in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "1966 United States Senate election in Minnesota"), defeating [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") candidate Robert A. Forsythe by a margin of 53.9% to 45.2%.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-25)

In [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_United_States_presidential_election "1972 United States presidential election"), Democratic presidential candidate [George McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern "George McGovern") from [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota "South Dakota") offered Mondale an opportunity to be his vice-presidential running mate; he declined.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-26) That year, Mondale was [re-elected to the U.S. Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "1972 United States Senate election in Minnesota") with over 57% of the vote,[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-27) even as Republican president [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") carried Minnesota.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-28) He served in the [88th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/88th_United_States_Congress "88th United States Congress"), the [89th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89th_United_States_Congress "89th United States Congress"), the [90th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90th_United_States_Congress "90th United States Congress"), the [91st United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/91st_United_States_Congress "91st United States Congress"), the [92nd United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/92nd_United_States_Congress "92nd United States Congress"), the [93rd United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/93rd_United_States_Congress "93rd United States Congress"), and the [94th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_United_States_Congress "94th United States Congress").[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-29)

### Policies

Mondale worked hard to build up the center of the party on economic and social issues. Unlike his father, a fervent liberal, he was not a crusader for the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal "New Deal"). Instead, he realized that the Democratic base (especially ethnic blue-collar workers) was gradually moving to the right, and he worked to keep their support.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-30) Mondale showed little or no interest in foreign policy until about 1974, when he realized that some foreign policy knowledge was necessary if he had loftier aspirations than the Senate. He developed a centrist position, avoiding alignment with either the party's hawks (such as [Henry M. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_M._Jackson "Henry M. Jackson")) or its doves (such as McGovern).[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-31)

Mondale took a liberal position on civil rights, which proved acceptable in Minnesota, a state with "a minuscule black population".[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-32) Mondale was a chief sponsor of the federal Fair Housing Act as part of the [Civil Rights Act of 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1968 "Civil Rights Act of 1968"), which prohibits discrimination in housing and created the [United States Department of Housing and Urban Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development "United States Department of Housing and Urban Development")'s [Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Fair_Housing_and_Equal_Opportunity "Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity") as the primary enforcer of the law.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-33) During [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson")'s presidency, Mondale supported the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). After Nixon became president in 1969, Mondale began to oppose the war and participated in legislation to restrict Nixon's ability to prolong it. Mondale supported abortion rights.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-35)

### Committees

Mondale rotated on and off numerous committees, including the [United States Senate Committee on Aeronautical and Space Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Aeronautical_and_Space_Sciences "United States Senate Committee on Aeronautical and Space Sciences"); the [United States Senate Committee on Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Finance "United States Senate Committee on Finance"); the [United States Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Health,_Education,_Labor_and_Pensions "United States Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions"); the [United States Senate Committee on the Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_the_Budget "United States Senate Committee on the Budget"); and the [United States Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Banking,_Housing,_and_Urban_Affairs "United States Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs"). He also served as chairman of the Select Committee on Equal Education Opportunity and the [United States Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence "United States Senate Select Committee on Intelligence")'s Domestic Task Force. He additionally served as chairman of the Labor and Public Welfare Committee's subcommittee on Children and Youth and the Senate subcommittee on social security financing.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-36)

In 1975, Mondale served on the [Committee to Study Governmental Operations with Respect to Intelligence Activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Committee "Church Committee"), chaired by U.S. Senate member [Frank Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Church "Frank Church") of [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho"), that investigated alleged abuses by the [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") and the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation").[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-UK_Hosts_Historical_Reunion_of_Members_of_Church_Committee-37) Documents declassified in 2017 show that the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency "National Security Agency") had created a file on Mondale as part of its monitoring of prominent U.S. citizens whose names appeared in [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence "Signals intelligence").[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-38)

### Apollo 1 accident (1967)

In 1967, Mondale served on the Aeronautical and Space Sciences Committee, then chaired by [Clinton Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Anderson_(New_Mexico_politician) "Clinton Anderson (New Mexico politician)"), when astronauts [Gus Grissom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Grissom "Gus Grissom"), [Ed White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_White_(astronaut) "Ed White (astronaut)"), and [Roger B. Chaffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_B._Chaffee "Roger B. Chaffee") were killed in a fire on January 27 while testing the Apollo 204 (later renumbered [Apollo 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_1 "Apollo 1")) spacecraft. [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") administrator [James E. Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Webb "James E. Webb") secured President Lyndon Johnson's approval for NASA to internally investigate the cause of the accident according to its established procedures, subject to Congressional oversight. NASA's procedure called for Deputy Administrator (and de facto general manager) [Robert Seamans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Seamans "Robert Seamans") to appoint and oversee an investigative panel.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-A&A1967-39)

In February, a reporter leaked to Mondale the existence of a [Phillips report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Report "Phillips Report") issued in 1965 by Apollo program director [Samuel C. Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_C._Phillips "Samuel C. Phillips"), detailing management, cost, delivery, and quality problems of the Apollo prime contractor [North American Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation "North American Aviation"). In the February 27 hearing, Mondale asked Webb if he knew of such a report. Webb had not yet seen the December 1965 written report, so he responded in the negative. Seamans had passed along to Webb neither the written report nor the briefing presentation made to him in January 1966 by Phillips and Phillips's boss, Manned Space Flight Administrator [George Mueller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mueller_(engineer) "George Mueller (engineer)").[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-wamu-40) Both Seamans and Mueller had also been called to testify at this session. Mueller denied the report's existence, though he must have been aware of it, as he had appended his own strongly worded letter to the copy sent to North American Aviation president [John Leland Atwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Leland_Atwood "John Leland Atwood").[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-phillips_report-41)

Seamans was afraid Mondale might be in possession of a copy (he was not), so he admitted that NASA often reviewed its contractors' performance, with both positive and negative results, but claimed that was nothing extraordinary. Under repeated questioning from Mondale, Webb promised that he would investigate whether the "Phillips Report" existed, and if so, whether a controlled release could be made to Congress. Immediately after the hearing, Webb saw the Phillips report for the first time.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-wamu-40) The controversy spread to both houses of Congress and grew (through the efforts of Mondale's fellow committee member, Republican U.S. Senate member [Margaret Chase Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Chase_Smith "Margaret Chase Smith") from [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine "Maine") to include the second-guessing of NASA's original selection in 1961 of North American as the prime Apollo spacecraft contractor, which Webb became forced to defend). The House NASA oversight committee, which was conducting its own hearings and had picked up on the controversy, was ultimately given a copy of the Phillips report.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-A&A1967-39)

While the committee, as a whole, believed that NASA should have informed Congress of the Phillips review results in 1966, its final report issued on January 30, 1968, concluded (as had NASA's own accident investigation completed on April 5, 1967) that "the findings of the [Phillips] task force had no effect on the accident, did not lead to the accident, and were not related to the accident". Yet Mondale wrote a minority opinion accusing NASA of "evasiveness,... lack of candor, ... patronizing attitude exhibited toward Congress, ... refusal to respond fully and forthrightly to legitimate congressional inquiries, and ... solicitous concern for corporate sensitivities at a time of national tragedy".[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-as204_senate-42) Mondale explained his actions in a 2001 interview: "I think that by forcing a public confrontation about these heretofore secret and deep concerns about the safety and the management of the program, it forced NASA to restructure and reorganize the program in a way that was much safer."[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-wamu-40)

## Vice presidency (1977–1981)

Further information: [1976 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election "1976 United States presidential election") and [Presidency of Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Jimmy_Carter "Presidency of Jimmy Carter")

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/1976-07-15CarterMondaleDNC.jpg/250px-1976-07-15CarterMondaleDNC.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1976-07-15CarterMondaleDNC.jpg)

Carter (left) and Mondale at the [1976 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Democratic_National_Convention "1976 Democratic National Convention")

When Jimmy Carter won the Democratic nomination for [President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election "1976 United States presidential election"), he chose Mondale as his running mate. Mondale [campaigned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter_1976_presidential_campaign "Jimmy Carter 1976 presidential campaign") for the ticket in various states. While campaigning in [Toledo, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Ohio "Toledo, Ohio") he said that the country needed a strong president to stop inflation and added that President Gerald Ford did not have the guts to stand up to big businesses.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-43) The ticket was narrowly elected on November 2, 1976, and Mondale was inaugurated as Vice President of the United States on January 20, 1977. He also became the first vice president to live at [Number One Observatory Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_One_Observatory_Circle "Number One Observatory Circle").[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-44)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-46)

Under Carter, Mondale traveled extensively throughout the nation and the world advocating the administration's foreign policy. His travels also included a visit to the [USS _Midway_(CV-41)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Midway_(CV-41) "USS Midway (CV-41)"), which was on station at the time in the Indian Ocean during the [Iran hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis "Iran hostage crisis"). Mondale was the first vice president to have an office in the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") and established the concept of an "activist Vice President". He had weekly lunches with the president and expanded the vice president's role from [figurehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurehead "Figurehead") to presidential advisor, full-time participant, and troubleshooter for the administration. Subsequent vice presidents have followed this model.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-47) In 1979, [Twin Cities Public Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_PBS "Twin Cities PBS") produced a documentary about his trip to Norway, titled _Walter Mondale: There's a [Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord "Fjord") in Your Past_, a play on the well-known advertising slogan "There's a [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company") in Your Future".[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-48)[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-49)

[![Image 11](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/1979-01-04MondaleCarter.jpg/250px-1979-01-04MondaleCarter.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1979-01-04MondaleCarter.jpg)

Mondale and Carter in January 1979

Mondale cast one [tie-breaking vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tie-breaking_votes_cast_by_the_vice_president_of_the_United_States "List of tie-breaking votes cast by the vice president of the United States") in the U.S. Senate on November 4, 1977, allowing the Social Security financing bill to be passed.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-50)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-51)

In 1980, Mondale opened the [Olympic Winter Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Winter_Olympics "1980 Winter Olympics") in [Lake Placid, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Placid,_New_York "Lake Placid, New York"); as of 2026, he was the most recent non-head of state of a republic to open any Olympic Games.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-52)

### 1980 election

Further information: [1980 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_presidential_election "1980 United States presidential election")

Carter and Mondale were renominated at the [1980 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Democratic_National_Convention "1980 Democratic National Convention"), but soundly lost to the Republican ticket of [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") and [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush "George H. W. Bush").

Carter and Mondale were the longest-living post-presidential team in American history. On May 23, 2006, they had been out of office for 9,254 days (25 years, 4 months and 3 days), surpassing the former record established by President [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") and Vice President [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson"), both of whom died on July 4, 1826. On September 8, 2012, Carter surpassed [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover "Herbert Hoover") as the president with the longest retirement from office. On April 23, 2014, Mondale surpassed [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") as the vice president with the longest retirement from office at 12,146 days (33 years, 3 months and 3 days). At the time of his death, Mondale was the [oldest living U.S. vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vice_presidents_of_the_United_States_by_age#Timeline_of_oldest_living_vice_presidents "List of vice presidents of the United States by age") and Carter was the [oldest living U.S. president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_United_States_by_age#Oldest_living "List of presidents of the United States by age").[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-53)

### 1984 presidential campaign

Main article: [Walter Mondale 1984 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mondale_1984_presidential_campaign "Walter Mondale 1984 presidential campaign")

Further information: [1984 Democratic Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries "1984 Democratic Party presidential primaries") and [1984 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election "1984 United States presidential election")

After losing the 1980 election, Mondale returned briefly to the practice of law at [Winston & Strawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_&_Strawn "Winston & Strawn"), a large Chicago-based law firm, but he intended to return to politics before long.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-54)

Mondale ran for the Democratic Party presidential nomination in the [Democratic presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries,_1984 "Democratic Party presidential primaries, 1984") preceding the 1984 election, and was soon the front-runner. His opposition included Reverend [Jesse Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Jackson "Jesse Jackson") and Senator [Gary Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Hart "Gary Hart") from Colorado. Hart won the [New Hampshire primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_primary "New Hampshire primary") in March, but Mondale had much of the party leadership behind him. To great effect, Mondale used the [Wendy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy's "Wendy's") slogan "[Where's the beef?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where's_the_beef%3F "Where's the beef?")" to describe Hart's policies as lacking depth. Jackson, widely regarded as the first serious African-American candidate for president, held on longer, but Mondale gained the nomination with the majority of delegates.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-55)

[![Image 15](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/49/Mondale_Ferraro_bumper_sticker_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mondale_Ferraro_bumper_sticker_1.jpg)

A Mondale—Ferraro bumper sticker

At the [Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Democratic_National_Convention "1984 Democratic National Convention"), Mondale chose [United States House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") member [Geraldine Ferraro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Ferraro "Geraldine Ferraro") from New York as his running mate, making her the first woman nominated for that position by a major party. Aides later said that Mondale was determined to make a historic choice with his vice presidential candidate, having considered San Francisco Mayor [Dianne Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Feinstein "Dianne Feinstein") (female and Jewish); Los Angeles Mayor [Tom Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Bradley_(mayor) "Tom Bradley (mayor)"), an African American; and [San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio "San Antonio") Mayor [Henry Cisneros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cisneros "Henry Cisneros"), a Mexican American, as finalists.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-56) Others preferred U.S. Senator [Lloyd Bentsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Bentsen "Lloyd Bentsen") of [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas") because he would appeal to voters in the Deep South which would [balance the ticket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_the_ticket "Balance the ticket"), or even nomination rival [Gary Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Hart "Gary Hart") that could have unified the party. Ferraro, as a Catholic, was harshly criticized by some Catholic Church leaders for being [pro-choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-choice "Pro-choice"). Much more controversy erupted over her changing positions about the release of her husband's tax returns, and her own ethics record in the House. Ferraro was on the defensive throughout much of the campaign, largely negating her breakthrough as the first woman on a major national ticket. She was also the first Italian American to reach that level in American politics.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-57)

When Mondale made his acceptance speech at the Democratic Convention, he said: "By the end of my first term, I will reduce the Reagan budget deficit by two‑thirds. Let's tell the truth. It must be done, it must be done. Mr. Reagan will raise taxes, and so will I. He won't tell you. I just did."[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-:0-58) While this was meant to show that Mondale would be honest with voters, it was instead largely interpreted as a campaign pledge to raise taxes to spend on domestic programs, which was unappealing to many voters.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-59)

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Candidates_Walter_Mondale_and_Geraldine_Ferraro_campaigning_at_Ft._Lauderdale%2C_4-27-84..jpg/250px-Candidates_Walter_Mondale_and_Geraldine_Ferraro_campaigning_at_Ft._Lauderdale%2C_4-27-84..jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Candidates_Walter_Mondale_and_Geraldine_Ferraro_campaigning_at_Ft._Lauderdale,_4-27-84..jpg)

Mondale and Ferraro campaigning in 1984

Mondale ran a [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States "Modern liberalism in the United States") campaign, supporting a [Nuclear Freeze campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Freeze_campaign "Nuclear Freeze campaign") and the [Equal Rights Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Rights_Amendment "Equal Rights Amendment") (ERA). He spoke against [Reaganonomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaganomics "Reaganomics") and in support of reducing federal [deficit spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficit_spending "Deficit spending"). However, the incumbent was popular, and Mondale's campaign was widely considered ineffective. Mondale was also perceived as supporting the poor at the expense of the middle class. In the first televised debate Mondale performed unexpectedly well, which led many to question Reagan's age and capacity to endure the grueling demands of the presidency (Reagan was the oldest person to serve as president—73 at the time—while Mondale was 56). In the next debate on October 21, 1984, Reagan deflected the issue by quipping, "I will not make age an issue of this campaign. I am not going to exploit, for political purposes, my opponent's youth and inexperience."[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-newshourdod-60)

Mondale was defeated in one of the biggest landslides in American history, receiving 37,577,352 votes (40.6% of the popular vote), and winning only [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") and his home state of Minnesota (even there his margin of victory was fewer than 3,800 votes),[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-61) securing only 13 electoral votes to Reagan's 525. The result was the worst electoral college defeat for any Democratic Party candidate in history, and the worst for any major-party candidate since [Alf Landon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Landon "Alf Landon")'s loss to [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") in [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election "1936 United States presidential election").[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-62)

### Private citizen and ambassador

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Waltermondaleasdiplomat_%283x4%29.jpg/250px-Waltermondaleasdiplomat_%283x4%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Waltermondaleasdiplomat_(3x4).jpg)

Official portrait as Ambassador, 1993

Mondale returned to private law practice with [Dorsey & Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsey_&_Whitney "Dorsey & Whitney") in Minneapolis in 1987. From 1986 to 1993, he chaired the [National Democratic Institute for International Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Institute_for_International_Affairs "National Democratic Institute for International Affairs").

During Bill Clinton's presidency, Mondale was United States Ambassador to Japan from 1993 to 1996. During his tenure, he oversaw the U.S. response to the [1995 Okinawa rape incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Okinawa_rape_incident "1995 Okinawa rape incident"), when U.S. servicemen raped a 12-year old girl, with Mondale apologizing to Okinawa Governor [Masahide Ōta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahide_%C5%8Cta "Masahide Ōta").[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-The_Asahi_Shimbun-63) Mondale also chaired a bipartisan group to study [campaign finance reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_finance_reform_in_the_United_States "Campaign finance reform in the United States") and served as Clinton's [special envoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_rank "Diplomatic rank") to Indonesia in 1998.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-nytobit-9)

Until his appointment as Ambassador to Japan, Mondale was a Distinguished University Fellow in Law and Public Affairs at the [Humphrey School of Public Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_School_of_Public_Affairs "Humphrey School of Public Affairs") at the University of Minnesota. In 1990, he established the Mondale Policy Forum at the Humphrey Institute. The forum has brought together leading scholars and policymakers for annual conferences on domestic and international issues.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-64)

Mondale spoke before the U.S. Senate on September 4, 2002, delivering a lecture on his service, with commentary on the transformation of the office of the vice president during the Carter administration, the Senate [cloture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloture "Cloture") rule for ending debate, and his view of the future of the Senate. The lecture was a part of a continuing Senate "Leaders Lecture Series" that ran from 1998 to 2002.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-65)

### 2002 U.S. Senate election and beyond

Main articles: [2002 United States Senate elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_United_States_Senate_elections "2002 United States Senate elections") and [2002 United States Senate election in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota "2002 United States Senate election in Minnesota")

In the 2002 midterm elections, two term incumbent Democratic Senator [Paul Wellstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wellstone "Paul Wellstone") from Minnesota, who was campaigning for reelection to a third term, tragically died in a [plane crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_accidents_and_incidents "Aviation accidents and incidents") 11 days before the November 5 election. Mondale quickly replaced Wellstone on the ballot at the urging of Wellstone's relatives. The Senate seat was the same one Mondale had held before resigning to become vice president in 1977.

[![Image 18](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/2015WalterMondaleJoeBiden.jpg/250px-2015WalterMondaleJoeBiden.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2015WalterMondaleJoeBiden.jpg)

Mondale with Vice President and future 46th President Joe Biden in 2015

During his debate with the Republican nominee, former mayor of [Saint Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "Saint Paul, Minnesota")[Norm Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_Coleman "Norm Coleman"), Mondale emphasized his experience, while painting Coleman as right-wing partisan in-line with then-president Bush.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-66) Mondale despite his popularity within Minnesota unexpectedly lost the election, receiving 1,067,246 votes (47.34%) to Coleman's 1,116,697 (49.53%). Upon conceding defeat, Mondale said, "At the end of what will be my last campaign, I want to say to Minnesota, you always treated me well, you always listened to me."[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-67) Mondale's loss in this election made him the first major party candidate to lose an election in every state. Had Mondale won the election, he would have been the first former vice president since [Hubert Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey "Hubert Humphrey") in 1971 to serve in the Senate after leaving office.

In 2004, Mondale became co-chairman of the [Constitution Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Project "Constitution Project")'s bipartisan Right to Counsel Committee.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-68) He endorsed U.S. Senate member [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton") of New York for president in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Rodham_Clinton_presidential_campaign,_2008 "Hillary Rodham Clinton presidential campaign, 2008").[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-69) On June 3, 2008, following the final primary contests, Mondale endorsed U.S. Senate member [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") of [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois"), who had clinched the nomination the previous evening, and ultimately won the presidency defeating Republican Party nominee [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain "John McCain").[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-70)

Following the [presidential election of 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election "2004 United States presidential election") and the midterm elections of 2006, Mondale is seen in the documentary _[Al Franken: God Spoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al\_Franken:\_God\_Spoke "Al Franken: God Spoke")_ talking with [Al Franken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Franken "Al Franken") about the possibility of the latter running against Coleman for U.S. Senate in 2008.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-71) In the film, Mondale encourages Franken to run, but cautions him, saying that Coleman's allies and the Republican Party would look for anything they could use against him. Franken ultimately ran and won the [2008 Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota,_2008 "United States Senate election in Minnesota, 2008") by 312 votes, with Coleman contesting the election results until June 30, 2009.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-72) Mondale and U.S. Senate member [Amy Klobuchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Klobuchar "Amy Klobuchar") stood with Franken in the [United States Senate chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_chamber "United States Senate chamber") when Franken was sworn in on July 7, 2009.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-73)

Mondale then stood again with U.S. senator Klobuchar when [Tina Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Smith "Tina Smith") was sworn in on January 3, 2018. He endorsed [Klobuchar's unsuccessful campaign for president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Klobuchar_2020_presidential_campaign "Amy Klobuchar 2020 presidential campaign") in February 2019.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-74)

## Family and personal life

[![Image 19](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/1984JoanWalterMondale.jpg/250px-1984JoanWalterMondale.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1984JoanWalterMondale.jpg)

Joan and Walter Mondale in 1984

Mondale's wife, Joan Mondale, was a national advocate for the arts and was the Honorary Chairman of the Federal Council on the Arts and Humanities during the Carter Administration. On February 3, 2014, she died at a hospice in Minneapolis surrounded by family members.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-75)

The Mondales' eldest son, [Ted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Mondale "Ted Mondale"), is an entrepreneur and the CEO of Nazca Solutions, a technology fulfillment venture. He is also a former Minnesota state senator. In 1998, Ted Mondale unsuccessfully sought the Democratic nomination for Minnesota governor, running as a fiscal moderate who had distanced himself from labor.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-76) The Mondales' daughter, [Eleanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Mondale "Eleanor Mondale"), was a television personality. She also had radio talk shows in Chicago and a long-running program on [WCCO (AM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCCO_(AM) "WCCO (AM)") in Minneapolis. She died of brain cancer at her home in Minnesota on September 17, 2011, at the age of 51.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-77) Their younger son, William Hall Mondale, is a former assistant [Attorney General of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Minnesota "Attorney General of Minnesota").[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-78)

Mondale had a residence near [Lake of the Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Isles "Lake of the Isles") in Minneapolis. He was a [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian "Presbyterian"). He enjoyed fishing, reading [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare") and historical accounts, barbecuing, skiing, watching _[Monty Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty\_Python "Monty Python")_, and playing tennis.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-79) Mondale was the recipient of numerous distinctions. He was inducted into [Omicron Delta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omicron_Delta_Kappa "Omicron Delta Kappa") as an honoris causa initiate at the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina "University of South Carolina") in 1981. Mondale also maintained strong ties to the [University of Minnesota Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Law_School "University of Minnesota Law School"). In 2002 the school renamed its building Walter F. Mondale Hall. Mondale contributed cameo appearances to the law school's annual T.O.R.T. ("Theater of the Relatively Talentless") productions and allowed his name to be used as the nickname of the school's hockey team: the "Fighting Mondales".[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-80)

Mondale had deep connections to his ancestral Norway. Upon entering the Senate in 1964, he took over the seat of vice president Hubert Humphrey, another [Norwegian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Americans "Norwegian Americans"). In later years, Mondale served on the executive committee of the Peace Prize Forum, an annual conference co-sponsored by the [Norwegian Nobel Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Institute "Norwegian Nobel Institute").[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-81) On December 5, 2007, Norwegian Minister of Foreign Affairs [Jonas Gahr Støre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Gahr_St%C3%B8re "Jonas Gahr Støre") announced that Mondale would be named [Honorary Consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Consul "Honorary Consul")-General of Norway, representing the Norwegian state in Minnesota.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-82) In 2015, Mondale was awarded the Public Leadership in Neurology Award from the [American Academy of Neurology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Neurology "American Academy of Neurology") for raising awareness for brain health, having lost both his wife and daughter to brain diseases.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-83)

## Death

> Dear Team,  
> Well my time has come. I am eager to rejoin Joan and Eleanor. Before I Go I wanted to let you know how much you mean to me. Never has a public servant had a better group of people working at their side!  
> Together we have accomplished so much and I know you will keep up the good fight.  
> [Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") in the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") certainly helps.  
> I always knew it would be okay if I arrived some place and was greeted by one of you!  
> My best to all of you!  
> Fritz

—Mondale's final message to his staff[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-84)

Mondale died of [natural causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manner_of_death "Manner of death") in his sleep at his home in Minneapolis, Minnesota, on April 19, 2021, at the age of 93.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-85)[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-86)[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-87) At the time of his death, Mondale was the [oldest living former U.S. vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vice_presidents_of_the_United_States_by_age "List of vice presidents of the United States by age"). On the day before his death, he had several phone conversations with Jimmy Carter, Bill Clinton, [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden"), [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris"), and Minnesota governor [Tim Walz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Walz "Tim Walz"). Mondale also emailed a final message to his staff, as he and his family had come to the conclusion that "his death was imminent".[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-88)[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-89)

Carter said in a statement: "Today I mourn the passing of my dear friend Walter Mondale, who I consider the best vice president in our country's history ... [Rosalynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalynn_Carter "Rosalynn Carter") and I join all Americans in giving thanks for his exemplary life, and we extend our deepest condolences to his family."[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-90)[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-91) Carter had last seen Mondale in person at the [Carter Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Center "Carter Center") in June 2019.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-92)

President Biden paid tribute to Mondale in a public statement, calling him a "dear friend and mentor" who had "defined the vice presidency as a full partnership, and helped provide a model for my service".[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-93) On April 20, 2021, Biden ordered all flags at government properties, office buildings and public grounds to be flown at [half-staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-mast "Half-mast") until that Tuesday evening in honor of Mondale.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-94)[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-95)

## Legacy

In the "Walter F. Mondale Papers" at the [Minnesota Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Historical_Society "Minnesota Historical Society"), digital content is available for research use. Contents include speech files, handwritten notes, memoranda, annotated briefings, schedules, correspondence, and visual materials. The collection includes senatorial, vice presidential, ambassadorial, political papers and campaign files, and personal papers documenting most aspects of Mondale's 60‑year-long career, including all of his public offices, campaigns, and Democratic Party and other non-official activities.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-98) The University of Minnesota Law Library's Walter F. Mondale website is devoted to Mondale's senatorial career. Mondale's work is documented in full text access to selected proceedings and debates on the floor of the Senate as recorded in the _[Congressional Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional\_Record "Congressional Record")_.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-99)

The [Joan and Walter Mondale Award for Public Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey-Mondale_Awards "Humphrey-Mondale Awards") is named for him and his wife.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter%20Mondale#cite_note-100)

## In popular culture

In the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") series _[Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession\_(TV\_series) "Succession (TV series)")_, Tom Wambsgans' and Shiv Roy's dog is called "Mondale."

In the 2017 biopic of serial killer [Jeffery Dahmer’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Dahmer "Jeffrey Dahmer") adolescence, [My Friend Dahmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Friend_Dahmer_(film) "My Friend Dahmer (film)") which was based on the [2012 graphic novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Friend_Dahmer "My Friend Dahmer"), Mondale is portrayed by Tom Luce in a scene where the future serial killer portrayed by [Ross Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Lynch "Ross Lynch") unexpectedly meets Mondale on a school field trip to Washington D.C.
