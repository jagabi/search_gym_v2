The **governor of Maine** is the [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government") of the U.S. state of [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine "Maine"). Before Maine was [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union "Admission to the Union") in 1820, Maine was part of [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts") and the [governor of Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Massachusetts "Governor of Massachusetts") was chief executive.

The current governor of Maine is [Janet Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Mills "Janet Mills"), a [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"), who took office January 2, 2019.

The governor of Maine receives a salary of $70,000, which is the lowest salary out of all 50 state governors, as of 2022.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-2) This will be raised to $125,000 upon a new governor taking office in early 2027, per a bill passed in 2025, as the Maine Constitution prohibits changing the salary of the sitting governor.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-3)

## Eligibility

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Governor_of_Maine&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Eligibility")]

Under Article V, Section 4, a person must as of the commencement of the term in office, be 30 years old, for 15 years a citizen of the United States, and for five years a resident of Maine. A governor must retain residency in Maine while in office. Section 5 provides that a person shall not assume the office of Governor[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-Art5-4) while holding any other office under the United States, Maine, or "any other power".

## Elections and terms of office

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Governor_of_Maine&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Elections and terms of office")]

Further information: [Elections in Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Maine "Elections in Maine")

Governors are elected directly for four-year terms. They may be elected any number of times, but with a [limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limit "Term limit") of two consecutive elected terms.(Article V, Section 2).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-Art5-4) Elections are by popular vote, but if two people tie for first place, the Legislature meets in [joint session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_session "Joint session") to choose between them (Article V, Section 3).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-Art5-4)

The [Maine Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Maine "Constitution of Maine") of 1820 originally established a gubernatorial term of one year,[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-5) to begin on the first Wednesday of January; constitutional amendments expanded this to two years in 1879[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-6) and to four years in 1957.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-amend84-7) The 1957 amendment also prohibited governors from succeeding themselves after serving two terms.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-amend84-7)

## Executive powers

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Governor_of_Maine&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Executive powers")]

The governor is [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of "the army and navy of the State, and of the militia" (the [Maine National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Department_of_Defense,_Veterans,_and_Emergency_Management "Maine Department of Defense, Veterans, and Emergency Management")), except when under [federal control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Guard "United States National Guard") (Article V, Section 7).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-Art5-4) The governor generally has the power to appoint civil, military, and judicial officers (aside from probate judges and justices of the peace), subject to [confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent "Advice and consent") by the Legislature, unless the Maine Constitution or a statute has provided another means of appointment (Article V, Section 8).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-Art5-4) The governor also has the power to grant [pardons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardon "Pardon"), reprieves, and [commutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutation_(law) "Commutation (law)"), except in cases of [impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_in_the_United_States "Impeachment in the United States"). This clemency power also includes juvenile offenses (Article V, Section 11).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-Art5-4)

## Cabinet

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Governor_of_Maine&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Cabinet")]

The governor oversees the executive branch, which includes Maine's state agencies. Their cabinet is often considered to be the state's commissioners, which are generally nominated by the governor but legally chosen by the [Maine Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Legislature "Maine Legislature").

## Succession

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Governor_of_Maine&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Succession")]

See also: [Gubernatorial lines of succession in the United States §Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gubernatorial_lines_of_succession_in_the_United_States#Maine "Gubernatorial lines of succession in the United States")

Maine is one of five states that does not have an office of [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_(United_States)#Maine "Lieutenant governor (United States)").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-21) Under current law, if there is a vacancy in the office of governor, the [president of the Maine Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Maine_Senate "List of presidents of the Maine Senate") becomes governor. As of December 4,2024[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Governor_of_Maine&action=edit), the Senate president is Democrat [Mattie Daughtry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattie_Daughtry "Mattie Daughtry").

## Official residence

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Governor_of_Maine&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Official residence")]

[The Blaine House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blaine_House "The Blaine House") in [Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta,_Maine "Augusta, Maine") is the official governor's mansion, and is located across the street from the [Maine State House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_State_House "Maine State House"). It became the official residence in 1919, and is named for [James G. Blaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_G._Blaine "James G. Blaine"), who once owned the mansion. The house was built by Captain James Hall in 1833 and declared a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark") in 1964.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine#cite_note-22)
