FAQ

### Who was Abraham's Lincoln's vice president?

Hannibal Hamlin, a Republican senator from Maine was Abraham Lincoln's vice president. The Republican Party believed that Hamlin, a former Democrat from the Northeast would balance the ticket with Lincoln, a former Whig from the Midwest.

### Why did Lincoln choose Johnson?

After the Civil War, the Republican Party believed that Andrew Johnson, a southern Democrat from the border state of Tennessee, would provide a better balance to the presidential ticket.

## [Who Was Hannibal Hamlin?](https://study.com/academy/lesson/hannibal-hamlin-vice-president-biography-facts.html)

**Hannibal Hamlin**was born in Maine in 1809. He practiced law before entering politics in 1836 as a **Jacksonian Democrat** in the Maine State Legislature. He next served in the US House of Representatives before being elected a US Senator.

Hamlin left the Democratic Party in 1856 over the party's support of the **Kansas-Nebraska Act**. He joined the **Republican Party** where he again served as a US Senator before being nominated as **Abraham Lincoln**'s vice presidential running mate in 1860.

He served as the Vice President of the United States from 1861-1865. When Hamlin was not renominated for the vice presidency, he returned to the US Senate where he supported Reconstruction.

## [Hannibal Hamlin's Early Life](https://study.com/academy/lesson/hannibal-hamlin-vice-president-biography-facts.html)

Hannibal Hamlin was born on August 27, 1809, in Paris Hill, Maine. Hamlin's father was a doctor and a farmer, but the family still struggled financially. After his father died, Hamlin had to take over the family farm and had to work to support his family.

Hamlin worked as a surveyor, compositor, and teacher before he began studying law. He was admitted to the Maine bar in 1833.

### Education and Legal Training

In his youth, Hamlin attended local schools before matriculating into Hebron Academy, a college preparatory school. Hamlin later began studying law at a law firm led by Samuel Fessenden. Fessenden, an abolitionist, became his mentor.

From 1827-1833, Hamlin published a political newspaper, _The Jeffersonian_, in Paris, Maine. In 1833, Hamlin moved the paper to Portland, Maine, where the paper became the _Oxford Democrat_. The paper acted as the mouthpiece for Maine's Democratic Party, and the paper endorsed Hamlin's political campaigns long after he had left the paper.

### The Personal Life of Hannibal Hamlin

In 1833 Hamlin opened a law firm in Hampden, Maine. He also married Sarah Jane Emery in 1833. The couple had four children: George (1835-1844), Charles (1837-1911), Cyrus (1839-1867), and Sarah (1842-1878).

Sarah died in 1855. In 1856 Hamlin married Sarah's half-sister Ellen Vesta Emery. They had two children: Hannibal (1858-1938) and Frank (1862-1922).

## [Hannibal Hamlin's Political Career](https://study.com/academy/lesson/hannibal-hamlin-vice-president-biography-facts.html)

Hamlin had a distinguished career in both state and national politics.

### Maine State Politics

Hamlin won the election to the Maine state House of Representatives in 1835. Hamlin ran as a Jacksonian Democrat who opposed slavery. He served from 1836-1841 and as Speaker of the House in 1837, 1839, and 1840.

During Hamlin's time in state office, a national debate about slavery was growing. At stake in this debate was whether states seeking admission to the Union should allow or prohibit slavery. In 1820, when Hamlin was just a boy, the US Congress passed the **Missouri Compromise**. The Compromise accepted Missouri into the Union as a slave state and Maine into the Union as a free state. The Compromise also drew an imaginary line across the 36th parallel and prohibited slavery above that line.

For the next two decades, the debate over slavery was relatively quiet. In 1836 Arkansas entered the Union as a slave state, and in 1837 Michigan entered the Union as a free state. However, by the time Hamlin entered national politics in the late 1840s, the country was again grappling with the issue of slavery.

### Congressional Career Before the Civil War

After an unsuccessful run for a seat in the US House of Representatives in 1840, Hannibal was elected as a Democratic Representative in 1842. He served as Maine's Representative from 1843-1847. He was elected as a Jacksonian Democrat, putting him in direct opposition to Henry Clay. Clay, who'd long been a political opponent of Andrew Jackson. Clay would later be crucial in orchestrating the Compromise of 1850 which Hamlin opposed.

In 1846 Hamlin lost his bid for a US Senate seat. He briefly returned to the Maine Senate in 1847 before being elected to the US Senate in 1848. The same year that Hamlin was elected to the US Senate, the Free Soil Party, a new political party, formed. The [Free Soilers](https://study.com/academy/lesson/what-was-the-free-soil-party-definition-beliefs.html) opposed any westward expansion of slavery and would later become the Republican Party.

Though Hamlin would later join the Republican Party, in 1848 he was elected as an anti-slavery Democrat. He held the Senate seat until 1857. During Hamlin's time in office, slavery continued to impact national politics.

#### The Wilmot Proviso

By the time Hamlin took national office, the debate about slavery was growing in national importance. From 1846-1848 US troops battled Mexican troops in the Mexican-American War. The war, initially fought over the US annexation of Texas, resulted in the US acquisition of over 500,000 square miles of new land.

This acquisition renewed the question of slavery expansion in new US territories. In 1846 President James K. Polk submitted an appropriations bill to Congress, requesting $2,000,000 to negotiate a border settlement with Mexico. Pennsylvania Democrat David Wilmot attempted to add to the bill an amendment that would prohibit slavery in any new territory. Hamlin supported the [Wilmot Proviso](https://study.com/academy/lesson/wilmot-proviso-of-1846-definition-summary-significance.html), but the amendment was not passed and the debate continued.

During this time, Hamlin also received criticism from President James K. Polk. Hamlin was among a group of congressman pushing legislation that would prohibit slavery in any new territory acquired in the Mexican-American War.

#### The Compromise of 1850

In 1849 California petitioned to be allowed into the Union as a free state, potentially upsetting the balance between slave and free states. Additionally, the question of whether slavery would be allowed in the newly acquired territories from Mexico had not been settled. These disagreements about slavery threatened to divide the Union. In an attempt to appease both pro-slavery and anti-slavery factions, Senator Henry Clay of Kentucky proposed several measures that would become known as **The Compromise of 1850**.

Senator Henry Clay, fearing that slavery would split the Union, proposed measures to appease pro-and-anti-slavery groups.

![Image 5: Oil portrait of Henry Clay by Theodore Sydney Moise](https://study.com/cimages/multimages/16/henry_clay_met_dp3643197605033299637255076.jpg)

The Compromise of 1850 included the following measures that were enacted in September of 1850:

*   California was admitted to the Union as a free state.
*   The current border of the state of Texas was established and Texas gave up much of the land it had claimed after the Mexican-American War.
*   These lands ceded by Texas became the territories of New Mexico and Utah. Slavery was left an open question in these territories.
*   The slave trade, though not slavery itself, was banned in the District of Columbia.
*   Congress passed a more powerful Fugitive Slave Act that gave the responsibility of returning runaway slaves to the federal government.

Hamlin did not support the Compromise of 1850.

#### The Kansas-Nebraska Act

In 1854 Senator Stephen Douglas brought a bill to Congress that would split the land directly west of Missouri into the Kansas and Nebraska territories. His bill permitted these territories to decide for themselves whether slavery would be allowed. Douglas's bill was passed on May 30, 1854, effectively ending the Missouri Compromise.

The Kansas-Nebraska Act repealed the Missouri Compromise and opened western territories to slavery.

![Image 6: A map of US states and territories, reflecting areas open to slavery after the Kansas-Nebraska Act.](https://study.com/cimages/multimages/16/672px-map_of_free_and_slave_states7599001935647388798.jpg)

#### The Effects of the Kansas-Nebraska Act

The **Kansas-Nebraska Act**had huge political implications. The Whig Party split over the Act. Whigs in southern states were subsumed into the Democratic Party, while Whigs in the north eventually reorganized as the Republican Party. In 1856 Hamlin left the Democratic Party because it had supported the Act, and he soon joined the Republican Party. His membership in the newly formed party also had huge implications on his political career.

The Republican Party urged Hamlin to resign his Senate seat in order to run for governor of Maine. Hamlin reluctantly agreed and easily won the gubernatorial race. He took office in January 1857, however, he resigned in February 1857 and returned to the US Senate as a Republican.

### Hannibal Hamlin: Vice President of the United States

In 1860 the Republican National Convention nominated Hannibal Hamlin to be the party's vice-presidential candidate. Their hope was that Hamlin, a former Democrat from the Northeast, would politically and regionally balance out Abraham Lincoln, a former Whig from the Midwest. Lincoln and Hamlin won the election, and in January 1861, Hamlin resigned from the Senate to serve as vice president.

In 1860 Hannibal Hamlin ran for vice president.

![Image 7: An 1860 presidential campaign medal with a portrait of Hannibal Hamlin](https://study.com/cimages/multimages/16/presidential_campaign_medal8156127264576888999.jpg)

Hamlin did not enjoy his role as vice president, and he wasn't particularly close to Lincoln. While Lincoln did seek Hamlin's advice on filling cabinet positions at the start of their term, he did not have much responsibility in the administration. At the start of the Civil War, Hamlin encouraged Lincoln to work for the complete emancipation of slaves and to allow black men to enlist in the Union army.

While he was vice president, Hamlin enlisted as a private in the Maine state militia. When his company was called to war, Hamlin served with them, performing guard duty and cooking for his company. He was promoted to corporal.

In 1864 the Republican Party dropped Hamlin from the presidential ticket, instead choosing Andrew Johnson for Lincoln's running mate. The Party believed that Johnson, a southern Democrat from the border state of Tennessee, would better balance the ticket. Johnson appointed Hamlin Collector of the Port of Boston in 1866, but he soon resigned in protest of Johnson's policies.

### Later Senate Career

Hamlin returned to the Senate in 1869, where he would serve until 1880. Early on, Hamlin continued to butt heads with Andrew Johnson. Hamlin supported **Radical Reconstruction**, a process by which Congress took control of Reconstruction from Johnson. Led by Radical Republicans like Hamlin, Congress sent federal troops to the South to help ensure democratic state governments and pushed for the protection of freedmen's civil rights. Radical Reconstruction lasted until 1877.

### Minister to Spain

At the end of his career, Hamlin served as minister to Spain from December 20, 1881 to October 17, 1882. His official title was Envoy Extraordinary and Minister Plenipotentiary.

## [Hannibal Hamlin's Final Years and Legacy](https://study.com/academy/lesson/hannibal-hamlin-vice-president-biography-facts.html)

Hamlin retired from public service in 1882. He returned home to Bangor, Maine, where he would live until his death. Hamlin died on July 4, 1891, after collapsing while playing cards at the Tarrantine Club in Bangor. He was placed on one of the club's couches, where he died a few hours later. The couch now sits in the rotunda of the Bangor Public Library.

Hamlin left behind a legacy of staunch abolitionism, even when his beliefs conflicted with those of his party. In Congress, Hamlin supported policies that would end slavery. He carried this stance into his tenure as Abraham Lincoln's vice president, encouraging the president to emancipate all of the slaves and to allow black men to enlist in the Union army.

## [Lesson Summary](https://study.com/academy/lesson/hannibal-hamlin-vice-president-biography-facts.html)

Born in 1809, **Hannibal Hamlin** practiced law before being elected to the Maine State House of Representatives. He ran as an anti-slavery **Jacksonian Democrat**. Hamlin entered national politics in 1842 when he was elected to the US House of Representatives. In 1848 he became a US Senator.

The slavery question was at the forefront of the national conversation throughout Hamlin's career. The **Missouri Compromise**, passed in 1820 when Hamlin was still a boy, admitted Missouri into the Union as a slave state and Maine into the Union as a free state.

In 1846 Hamlin supported the **Wilmot Proviso** that would have prevented slavery in any new territory. Hamlin opposed the **Compromise of 1850** that left the issue of slavery up to the decision of new territories. He also opposed the **Kansas-Nebraska Act** in 1854, which effectively repealed the Missouri Compromise. Hamlin left the Democratic party because of its support of the Kansas-Nebraska Act, and he soon joined the **Republican Party**.

In 1860, the Republican Party nominated Hamlin to be **Abraham Lincoln**'s running mate. Hamlin had few responsibilities as vice president, but he did encourage Lincoln to pass the Emancipation Proclamation and to allow black men to enlist in the Union Army. The Party chose **Andrew Johnson** as Lincoln's running mate for the 1864 election.

Hamlin later returned to the US Senate where he supported **Radical Reconstruction**. He died in 1891.

## Additional Info

## Hannibal Hamlin

Hannibal Hamlin was born on August 27, 1809, in Paris Hill, Maine to Dr. Cyrus Hamlin. Young Hamlin grew up in a prosperous family where his love of reading, outgoing personality, and physique made him a leader among his peers. He attended public schools in Maine before eventually practicing law, also in Maine. Hamlin suspended his legal career to take care of the farm and his ailing mother after his father's death. When he returned to his legal career, he worked for abolitionist Samuel Fessenden. Fessenden's influence resulted in Hamlin setting up his own law office in Hampden, Maine.

Hamlin became a member of the Democratic Party and was elected to the state House in 1835. Due to his likable personality, he became Speaker; his most important piece of legislation abolished the death penalty in Maine. Hamlin set his eyes of the U.S. House of Representatives but lost his bid in 1840, only to win a seat later in 1843. In Congress, he set himself up as a Jacksonian Democrat in opposition to the **American System** set up by Henry Clay that established high tariffs, a public works program, and a highly debatable centralized banking system.

### Hamlin the Abolitionist Congressman

As new territories were added to the U.S., the issue of slavery was at the heart of the argument between the free states of the North and the slave holding states of the expanding South. Hamlin's home state of Maine was added under the Missouri Compromise where one slave state (Missouri) was added to the Union along with one free-state (Maine). This process of adding one free-state to one slave state would continue well into Hamlin's entry into national politics, and color his efforts and successes as a Congressman. In 1846 when the U.S. went to war against Mexico, Hamlin was among the Congressmen to spearhead legislation that would prevent any new territory added from the spoils of war to allow slavery. This angered then Democratic President Polk.

Polk would go on to question Hamlin's loyalty to the Democratic Party due to his abolitionist views, but Hamlin would stand resolute in his stance against slavery. While serving in the House Hamlin would also make a political enemy, Jefferson Davis. Conflicts in ideology with Davis would devolve to the level of Hamlin carrying a pistol with him around the Capital due to death threats. When a Senate seat opened up in Maine, Hamlin was elected in 1848 which was the same year that northern Democrats and Whigs formed the anti-slavery **Free Soil Party**.

Hamlin was loyal to the Democratic Party despite the fact that his antislavery ideas put him on the fringes. He was elected to the Senate as a Democrat and refused to leave his party for the comfort of the Free Soil Party. He was also, an early believer in **temperance**, due to the heavy use of alcohol by members of Congress around the Capital and the subsequent violence.

When the Republican Party formed in the 1850's they courted Hamlin. Republicans used extreme measures and essentially gave him the choice of losing his Senate seat or joining their party and running for governor. Reluctantly, he agreed upon the condition that he be allowed to return to the Senate as soon as an opportunity presented itself. Hamlin took office in January of 1857, but by February resigned to start his third term as Senator. Back in the Senate, he would meet and befriend Andrew Johnson, work against policies of President Buchanan, and consider running for president in 1860. It would be Abraham Lincoln who would receive the Republican nomination that year, and to the shock of Hamlin, he was picked as the running mate.

### Vice President Hamlin

When the Civil War began, Hamlin formed a regiment for Maine and kept track of troop movements around New York for Lincoln. Hamlin found the Vice Presidency frustrating as he saw his once influential role in the Senate reduced to presiding over a body where he was no more than an observer. Hamlin became more radical, and by the time Lincoln's first term in office was over, Hamlin was firmly among the growing faction of **Radical Republicans**. Hamlin thought Lincoln wanted to keep him on the ticket for the Election of 1864, but he assumed wrong. To shore up support from the border-states not in succession, Lincoln turned to Tennessean Andrew Johnson as a running mate. Upon being dumped from the ticket, Hamlin served in the Union Army until the war ended.

### Post-Vice Presidency

There was no animosity between Johnson and Hamlin after the election. Hamlin attended the inauguration and then left for Maine. First, Hamlin tried and failed to return to the Senate. He then was nominated by President Johnson to serve as a collector for the port of Boston, but Johnson's Reconstruction obstruction was too much for Hamlin to take as a Radical Republican.

He was considered as a running mate again for U.S. Grant in 1868 and made a triumphant return to the Senate in 1869. Heart disease would plague his last years in the Senate. His last role as a public servant was his time as minister to Spain. He retired from public service in 1882, ending a long and storied career. On July 4, 1891, Hamlin died from a heart attack at age 81.

## Lesson Summary

**Hannibal Hamlin** was a leader in the fight to end slavery, and worked as a member of both the Democratic and the Republican parties to end the heinous institution. His duality as Democrat and abolitionist was unusually balanced where neither affiliation took precedence over the other. Without his presence in Congress, slavery measures would have passed more frequently. Without his presence on the ticket in 1860, the Great Emancipator may not have been elected, the Civil War never fought, and slavery never ended.
