The **vice president of the United States** (**VPOTUS**, **VP**, or informally **veep**)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Veep-7) is the second-highest office in the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch_of_the_United_States_government "Executive branch of the United States government")[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-9) of the [U.S. federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._federal_government "U.S. federal government"), after the [president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States"), and ranks first in the [presidential line of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_line_of_succession "United States presidential line of succession"). The vice president is also an officer in the [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_branch_of_the_United_States_federal_government "Legislative branch of the United States federal government"), as the **president of the Senate**. In this capacity, the vice president is empowered to [preside over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presiding_officer_of_the_United_States_Senate "Presiding officer of the United States Senate") the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate"), but may not vote except to [cast a tie-breaking vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tie-breaking_votes_cast_by_the_vice_president_of_the_United_States "List of tie-breaking votes cast by the vice president of the United States").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-USlegal-VP-10) The vice president is elected at the same time as the president to a four-year term of office by the people of the United States through the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(United_States) "Electoral College (United States)"), but the electoral votes are cast separately for these two offices.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-USlegal-VP-10) Following the passage in 1967 of the [Twenty-fifth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twenty-fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution") to the U.S. Constitution, a vacancy in the office of vice president may be filled by presidential nomination and confirmation by a majority vote in both houses of Congress. This was based on the [Tyler Precedent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Precedent "Tyler Precedent") set in 1841 when [John Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyler "John Tyler") became the first vice president to take over for a deceased president following the death of [William Henry Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison "William Henry Harrison").

The modern vice presidency is a position of significant power and is widely seen as an integral part of a president's administration. The presidential candidate selects the candidate for the vice presidency as their [running mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_mate "Running mate") in the lead-up to the presidential election. While the exact nature of the role varies in each administration, since the vice president's service in office is by election, the president cannot dismiss the vice president, and the personal working-relationship with the president varies, most modern vice presidents serve as a key presidential advisor, governing partner, and representative of the president. The vice president is also a statutory member of the [United States Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cabinet "United States Cabinet") and [United States National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Security_Council "United States National Security Council")[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-USlegal-VP-10) and thus plays a significant role in executive government and national security matters. As the vice president's role within the executive branch has expanded, the legislative branch role has contracted; for example, vice presidents now preside over the Senate only infrequently.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Garvey-11)

The role of the vice presidency has changed dramatically since the office was created during the [1787 Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_(United_States) "Constitutional Convention (United States)"). Originally something of an afterthought, the vice presidency was considered an insignificant office for much of the nation's history, especially after the [Twelfth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twelfth Amendment to the United States Constitution") meant that vice presidents were no longer the runners-up in the presidential election. The vice president's role began steadily growing in importance during the 1930s, with the [Office of the Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Vice_President_of_the_United_States "Office of the Vice President of the United States") being created in the executive branch in 1939, and has since grown much further. Due to its increase in power and prestige, the vice presidency is now often considered to be a stepping stone to the presidency. Since the 1970s, the vice president has been afforded an official residence at [Number One Observatory Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_One_Observatory_Circle "Number One Observatory Circle").[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-12)

The Constitution does not expressly assign the vice presidency to a branch of the government, causing a dispute among scholars about which branch the office belongs to (the executive, the legislative, both, or neither).[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Garvey-11)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-24KJLPP1-13) The modern view of the vice president as an officer of the executive branch—one isolated almost entirely from the legislative branch—is due in large part to the assignment of executive authority to the vice president by either the president or Congress.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Garvey-11)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Goldstein-14) Nevertheless, many vice presidents have previously served in Congress, and are often tasked with helping to advance an administration's legislative priorities. [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") was the country's first vice president. [JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Vance "JD Vance") is the 50th and current vice president since January 20, 2025.

## History and development

### Constitutional Convention

No mention of an office of vice president was made at the 1787 [Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_(United_States) "Constitutional Convention (United States)") until near the end, when an eleven-member committee on "Leftover Business" proposed a method of electing the chief executive (president).[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-15) Delegates had previously considered the selection of the Senate's presiding officer, deciding that "the Senate shall choose its own President", and had agreed that this official would be designated the executive's immediate successor. They had also considered the mode of election of the executive but had not reached consensus. This all changed on September 4, when the committee recommended that the nation's chief executive be elected by an [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(United_States) "Electoral College (United States)"), with each [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") having a number of presidential electors equal to the sum of that state's allocation of [representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") and [senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Garvey-11)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-RA2005TLR-16)

Recognizing that loyalty to one's individual state outweighed loyalty to the new federation, the Constitution's framers assumed individual electors would be inclined to choose a candidate from their own state (a so-called "[favorite son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favorite_son "Favorite son")" candidate) over one from another state. So they created the office of vice president and required the electors to vote for two candidates, at least one of whom must be from outside the elector's state, believing that the second vote would be cast for a candidate of national character.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-RA2005TLR-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-17) Additionally, to guard against the possibility that electors might [strategically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamesmanship#Usage_outside_of_games "Gamesmanship") waste their second votes, it was specified that the first runner-up would become vice president.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-RA2005TLR-16)

The resultant method of electing the president and vice president, spelled out in [Article II, Section 1, Clause 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_Two_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Clause_3:_Electoral_College "Article Two of the United States Constitution"), allocated to each [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") a number of electors equal to the combined total of its Senate and House of Representatives membership. Each elector was allowed to vote for two people for president (rather than for both president and vice president), but could not [differentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_voting "Ranked voting") between their first and second choice for the presidency. The person receiving the greatest number of votes (provided it was an [absolute majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_majority "Absolute majority") of the whole number of electors) would be president, while the individual who received the next largest number of votes became vice president. If there were a tie for first or for second place, or if no one won a majority of votes, the president and vice president would be selected by means of [contingent elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contingent_election "Contingent election") protocols stated in the clause.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-A2TKec-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-CRS2017THN-19)

### Early vice presidents and Twelfth Amendment

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/John_Adams_1800_to_1815_Portrait_%284x5_cropped%29.jpg/250px-John_Adams_1800_to_1815_Portrait_%284x5_cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_Adams_1800_to_1815_Portrait_(4x5_cropped).jpg)

[John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"), the first vice president of the United States

The first two vice presidents, [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") and [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson"), both of whom gained the office by virtue of being runners-up in presidential contests, presided regularly over Senate proceedings and did much to shape the role of Senate president.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-VP-PS-20)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-21) Several 19th-century vice presidents—such as [George Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Dallas "George M. Dallas"), [Levi Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Morton "Levi Morton"), and [Garret Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garret_Hobart "Garret Hobart")—followed their example and led effectively, while others were rarely present.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-VP-PS-20)

The emergence of [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party "Political party") and nationally coordinated election campaigns during the 1790s (which the Constitution's framers had not contemplated) quickly frustrated the election plan in the original Constitution. In the [election of 1796](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1796_United_States_presidential_election "1796 United States presidential election"), [Federalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_Party "Federalist Party") candidate John Adams won the presidency, but his bitter rival, [Democratic-Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-Republican_Party "Democratic-Republican Party") candidate Thomas Jefferson, came second and thus won the vice presidency. As a result, the president and vice president were from opposing parties; and Jefferson used the vice presidency to frustrate the president's policies. Then, four years later, in the [election of 1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_United_States_presidential_election "1800 United States presidential election"), Jefferson and fellow Democratic-Republican [Aaron Burr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Burr "Aaron Burr") each received 73 electoral votes. In the contingent election that followed, Jefferson finally won the presidency on the 36th ballot, leaving Burr the vice presidency. Afterward, the system was overhauled through the [Twelfth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twelfth Amendment to the United States Constitution") in time to be used in the [1804 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1804_United_States_presidential_election "1804 United States presidential election").[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-HF-XII-22)

### 19th and early 20th centuries

For much of its existence, the office of vice president was seen as little more than a minor position. John Adams, the first vice president, was the first of many frustrated by the "complete insignificance" of the office. To his wife [Abigail Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abigail_Adams "Abigail Adams") he wrote, "My country has in its wisdom contrived for me the most insignificant office that ever the invention of man... or his imagination contrived or his imagination conceived; and as I can do neither good nor evil, I must be borne away by others and met the common fate."[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-23)[Thomas R. Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_R._Marshall "Thomas R. Marshall"), who served as vice president from 1913 to 1921 under President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson "Woodrow Wilson"), lamented: "Once there were two brothers. One ran away to sea; the other was elected Vice President of the United States. And nothing was heard of either of them again."[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-casevpquote-24) His successor, [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge "Calvin Coolidge"), was so obscure that [Major League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball "Major League Baseball") sent him free passes that misspelled his name, and a fire marshal failed to recognize him when Coolidge's Washington residence was evacuated.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-greenberg2007-25)[John Nance Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nance_Garner "John Nance Garner"), who served as vice president from 1933 to 1941 under President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt"), claimed that the vice presidency "isn't worth a pitcher of warm piss".[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-26)[Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman"), who also served as vice president under Franklin D. Roosevelt, said the office was as "useful as a cow's fifth teat".[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-27)[Walter Bagehot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Bagehot "Walter Bagehot") remarked in _[The English Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_English\_Constitution "The English Constitution")_ that "[t]he framers of the Constitution expected that the _vice_-president would be elected by the Electoral College as the second wisest man in the country. The vice-presidentship being a [sinecure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinecure "Sinecure"), a second-rate man agreeable to the wire-pullers is always smuggled in. The chance of succession to the presidentship is too distant to be thought of."[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-28)

When the [Whig Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_Party_(United_States) "Whig Party (United States)") asked [Daniel Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Webster "Daniel Webster") to run for the vice presidency on [Zachary Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachary_Taylor "Zachary Taylor")'s ticket, he replied "I do not propose to be buried until I am really dead and in my coffin."[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-webster-novp-29) This was the second time Webster declined the office, which [William Henry Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison "William Henry Harrison") had first offered to him. Ironically, both the presidents making the offer to Webster died in office, meaning the three-time candidate would have become president had he accepted either. Since presidents rarely die in office, however, the better preparation for the presidency was considered to be the office of [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State "United States Secretary of State"), in which Webster served under Harrison, Tyler, and later, Taylor's successor, Fillmore.

In the first hundred years of the United States' existence no fewer than seven proposals to abolish the office of vice president were advanced.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-ames-30) The first such constitutional amendment was presented by [Samuel W. Dana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_W._Dana "Samuel W. Dana") in 1800; it was defeated by a vote of 27 to 85 in the [United States House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives").[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-ames-30) The second, introduced by United States Senator [James Hillhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hillhouse "James Hillhouse") in 1808, was also defeated.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-ames-30) During the late 1860s and 1870s, five additional amendments were proposed.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-ames-30) One advocate, [James Mitchell Ashley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mitchell_Ashley "James Mitchell Ashley"), opined that the office of vice president was "superfluous" and dangerous.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-ames-30)

[Garret Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garret_Hobart "Garret Hobart"), the first vice president under [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McKinley "William McKinley"), was one of the very few vice presidents at this time who played an important role in the administration. A close confidant and adviser of the president, Hobart was called "Assistant President".[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-31) However, until 1919, vice presidents were not included in meetings of the [President's Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cabinet "United States Cabinet"). This precedent was broken by Woodrow Wilson when he asked Thomas R. Marshall to preside over Cabinet meetings while Wilson was in France negotiating the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles "Treaty of Versailles").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-32) President [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding "Warren G. Harding") also invited Calvin Coolidge to meetings. The next vice president, [Charles G. Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_G._Dawes "Charles G. Dawes"), did not seek to attend Cabinet meetings under President Coolidge, declaring that "the precedent might prove injurious to the country."[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-33)

### Emergence of the modern vice presidency

[![Image 9](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Harry_S._Truman.jpg/250px-Harry_S._Truman.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Harry_S._Truman.jpg)

Though prominent as a Missouri Senator, [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman") had been vice president only three months when he became president; he was never informed of [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt")'s war or postwar policies while serving as vice president. This led to several statutory reforms concerning the office.

In 1929, Herbert Hoover raised the status of the office by renewing the practice of inviting the vice president to cabinet meetings, which every president since has maintained.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-34)

Unlike his predecessor U.S. vice-presidents, [John Nance Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nance_Garner "John Nance Garner") had a highly prominent role in shaping the president's policies, with Roosevelt using Garner's knowledge and experience to pilot [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal "New Deal") legislation through Congress.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-garnerpowerandinfluencevicepresident-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-garnerandfdr-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-vicepresidentprominence-37) However, Garner broke with Roosevelt over the "[court-packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Reorganization_Bill_of_1937 "Judiciary Reorganization Bill of 1937")" issue early in his second term, and became Roosevelt's leading critic. At the start of that term, on [January 20, 1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Second inauguration of Franklin D. Roosevelt"), Garner had been the first vice president to be sworn into office on the Capitol steps in the same ceremony with the president, a tradition that continues. Prior to that time, vice presidents were traditionally inaugurated at a separate ceremony in the Senate chamber. [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford "Gerald Ford") and [Nelson Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Rockefeller "Nelson Rockefeller"), who were each appointed to the office under the terms of the 25th Amendment, were inaugurated in the House and Senate chambers respectively.

At the [1940 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_Democratic_National_Convention "1940 Democratic National Convention"), Roosevelt selected his own running mate, [Henry Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_A._Wallace "Henry A. Wallace"), instead of leaving the nomination to the convention, when he wanted Garner replaced.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-VPrising-38) He then gave Wallace major responsibilities during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). However, after numerous policy disputes between Wallace and other [Roosevelt Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Presidency of Franklin D. Roosevelt") and Democratic Party officials, he was denied re-nomination at the [1944 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Democratic_National_Convention "1944 Democratic National Convention"). [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman") was selected instead. During his 82-day vice presidency, Truman was never informed about any war or post-war plans, including the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project").[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-JSTOR_daily-39) Truman had no visible role in the Roosevelt administration outside of his congressional responsibilities and met with the president only a few times during his tenure as vice president.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-40) Roosevelt died on April 12, 1945, and Truman succeeded to the presidency (the state of Roosevelt's health had also been kept from Truman). At the time he said, "I felt like the moon, the stars and all the planets fell on me."[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-41) Determined that no future vice president should be so uninformed upon unexpectedly becoming president, Truman made the vice president a member of the [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Security_Council "United States National Security Council"), a participant in Cabinet meetings and a recipient of regular security briefings in 1949.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-JSTOR_daily-39)

The stature of the vice presidency grew again while [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") was in office (1953–1961). He attracted the attention of the media and the Republican Party, when [Dwight Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Eisenhower "Dwight Eisenhower") authorized him to preside at [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States "Cabinet of the United States") meetings in his absence and to assume temporary control of the executive branch, which he did after Eisenhower suffered a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction "Myocardial infarction") on September 24, 1955, [ileitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ileitis "Ileitis") in June 1956, and a [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke "Stroke") in November 1957. Nixon was also visible on the world stage during his time in office.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-JSTOR_daily-39)

Until 1961, vice presidents had their offices on [Capitol Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Hill "Capitol Hill"), a formal office in the Capitol itself and a working office in the [Russell Senate Office Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Senate_Office_Building "Russell Senate Office Building"). [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson") was the first vice president to also be given an office in the White House complex, in the [Old Executive Office Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Executive_Office_Building "Old Executive Office Building"). The former Navy Secretary's office in the OEOB has since been designated the "Ceremonial Office of the Vice President" and is today used for formal events and press interviews. President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter") was the first president to give his vice president, [Walter Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mondale "Walter Mondale"), an office in the [West Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Wing "West Wing") of the White House, which all vice presidents have since retained. Because of their function as president of the Senate, vice presidents still maintain offices and staff members on Capitol Hill. This change came about because Carter held the view that the office of the vice presidency had historically been a wasted asset and wished to have his vice president involved in the decision-making process. Carter pointedly considered, according to Joel Goldstein, the way Roosevelt treated Truman as "immoral".[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-WPO_41921-42)

Another factor behind the rise in prestige of the vice presidency was the expanded use of presidential preference primaries for choosing party nominees during the 20th century. By adopting primary voting, the field of candidates for vice president was expanded by both the increased quantity and quality of presidential candidates successful in some primaries, yet who ultimately failed to capture the presidential nomination at the convention.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-VPrising-38)

At the start of the 21st century, [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney "Dick Cheney") (2001–2009) held much power within the administration, and frequently made policy decisions on his own, without the knowledge of the president.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Kenneth_T._Walsh-43) This rapid growth led to [Matthew Yglesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Yglesias "Matthew Yglesias") and [Bruce Ackerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Ackerman "Bruce Ackerman") calling for the abolition of the vice presidency[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-44)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-45) while both of [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election")'s vice presidential candidates, [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin "Sarah Palin") and [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden"), said they would reduce the role to simply being an adviser to the president.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-46)

Although delegates to the constitutional convention approved establishing the office, with both its executive and senatorial functions, not many understood the office, and so they gave the vice president few duties and little power.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-VP-PS-20) Only a few states had an analogous position. Among those that did, [New York's constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Constitution "New York Constitution") provided that "the lieutenant-governor shall, by virtue of his office, be president of the Senate, and, upon an equal division, have a casting voice in their decisions, but not vote on any other occasion".[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-47) As a result, the vice presidency originally had authority in only a few areas, although constitutional amendments have added or clarified some matters.

### President of the Senate

[Article I, Section 3, Clause 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_One_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Clause_4:_Vice_President_as_President_of_Senate "Article One of the United States Constitution") confers upon the vice president the title "President of the Senate", authorizing the vice president to [preside over Senate meetings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presiding_Officer_of_the_United_States_Senate "Presiding Officer of the United States Senate"). In this capacity, the vice president is responsible for maintaining order and decorum, recognizing members to speak, and interpreting the Senate's rules, practices, and precedent. With this position also comes the authority to [cast a tie-breaking vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tie-breaking_votes_cast_by_the_vice_president_of_the_United_States "List of tie-breaking votes cast by the vice president of the United States").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-VP-PS-20) In practice, the number of times vice presidents have exercised this right has varied greatly. [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") holds the record at 33 votes, followed by [John C. Calhoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Calhoun "John C. Calhoun") who had previously held the record at 31 votes; [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") ranks third with 29.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-48)[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-49) Twelve vice presidents did not cast any tie-breaking votes.

As the framers of the Constitution anticipated that the vice president would not always be available to fulfill this responsibility, the Constitution provides that the Senate may elect a [president pro tempore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_pro_tempore_of_the_United_States_Senate "President pro tempore of the United States Senate") (or "[president for a time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_tempore "Pro tempore")") in order to maintain the proper ordering of the legislative process. In practice, since the early 20th century, neither the president of the Senate nor the pro tempore regularly presides; instead, the president pro tempore usually delegates the task to other Senate members.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-50)[Rule XIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Rules_of_the_Senate_Rule_XIX "Standing Rules of the Senate Rule XIX"), which governs debate, does not authorize the vice president to participate in debate, and grants only to members of the Senate (and, upon appropriate notice, former presidents of the United States) the privilege of addressing the Senate, without granting a similar privilege to the sitting vice president. Thus, _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time\_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ magazine wrote in 1925, during the tenure of [Charles G. Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_G._Dawes "Charles G. Dawes"), "once in four years the vice president can make a little speech, and then he is done. For four years he then has to sit in the seat of the silent, attending to speeches ponderous or otherwise, of deliberation or humor."[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-51)

#### Presiding over impeachment trials

In their capacity as president of the Senate, the vice president may preside over most [impeachment trials of federal officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_impeachment_trial_in_the_United_States "Federal impeachment trial in the United States"), although the Constitution does not specifically require it. However, whenever the president of the United States is on trial, the Constitution requires that the [chief justice of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_justice_of_the_United_States "Chief justice of the United States") must preside. This stipulation was designed to avoid the possible conflict of interest in having the vice president preside over the trial for the removal of the one official standing between them and the presidency.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-A1trial-52) In contrast, the Constitution is silent about which federal official would preside were the vice president on trial by the Senate.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-24KJLPP1-13)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-53) No vice president has ever been impeached, thus leaving it unclear whether an impeached vice president could, as president of the Senate, preside at their own impeachment trial.

#### Presiding over electoral vote counts

The Twelfth Amendment provides that the vice president, in their capacity as the president of the Senate, receives the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(United_States) "Electoral College (United States)") votes, and then, in the presence of the Senate and House of Representatives, opens the sealed votes.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-A2TKec-18) The votes are then counted by Congress during a [joint session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_session_of_the_United_States_Congress "Joint session of the United States Congress"), in accordance with the additional procedures prescribed by the [Electoral Count Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Count_Act "Electoral Count Act") and amended by [Electoral Count Reform and Presidential Transition Improvement Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Count_Reform_and_Presidential_Transition_Improvement_Act "Electoral Count Reform and Presidential Transition Improvement Act").[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-54) The former specifies that the president of the Senate presides over the joint session,[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-55) and the latter clarifies the solely ministerial role the president of the Senate serves in the process.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-56) The next such joint session is expected to take place following the [2028 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_United_States_presidential_election "2028 United States presidential election"), on January 6, 2029 (unless Congress sets a different date by law).[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-CRS2017THN-19)

In this capacity, four vice presidents have been able to announce their own election to the presidency: [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") in 1797, [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson") in 1801, [Martin Van Buren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren "Martin Van Buren") in 1837 and [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush "George H. W. Bush") in 1989.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-VP-PS-20) Conversely, four vice presidents have needed to announce their opponent's election to the presidency: [John C. Breckinridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Breckinridge "John C. Breckinridge") in 1861,[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-57)[Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") in 1961,[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-EV1969count-58)[Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore "Al Gore") in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_States_Electoral_College_vote_count "2001 United States Electoral College vote count"),[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-59) and [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_Electoral_College_vote_count "2025 United States Electoral College vote count").[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-60) In 1969, Vice President [Hubert Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey "Hubert Humphrey") would have done so as well, following his 1968 loss to Richard Nixon; however, on the date of the congressional joint session, Humphrey was in [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") attending the funeral of [Trygve Lie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Lie "Trygve Lie"), the first elected [Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General "Secretary-General") of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"). The president pro tempore, [Richard Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Russell_Jr. "Richard Russell Jr."), presided in his absence.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-EV1969count-58) On February 8, 1933, Vice President [Charles Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Curtis "Charles Curtis") announced the election victory of his successor, House Speaker [John Nance Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nance_Garner "John Nance Garner"), while Garner was seated next to him on the House [dais](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/dais "wikt:dais").[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-61) Similarly, [Walter Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mondale "Walter Mondale"), in 1981, [Dan Quayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Quayle "Dan Quayle"), in 1993, and [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence "Mike Pence"), in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_States_Electoral_College_vote_count "2021 United States Electoral College vote count"), each had to announce their successor's election victory, following their re-election losses.

### Successor to the U.S. president

[![Image 15: An illustration:Tyler stands on his porch in Virginia, approached by a man with an envelope. Caption reads "Tyler receiving the news of Harrison's death."](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Tyler_receives_news.jpg/330px-Tyler_receives_news.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tyler_receives_news.jpg)

1888 illustration of [John Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyler "John Tyler") receiving the news of President [William Henry Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison "William Henry Harrison")'s death from Chief Clerk of the State Department [Fletcher Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fletcher_Webster "Fletcher Webster")

[Article II, Section 1, Clause 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_Two_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Clause_6:_Vacancy_and_disability "Article Two of the United States Constitution") stipulates that the vice president takes over the "powers and duties" of the presidency in the event of a president's removal, death, resignation, or inability.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-FordhamLaw2011-62) Even so, it did not clearly state whether the vice president became president or simply [acted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting_(law) "Acting (law)") as president in a case of succession. Debate records from the 1787 Constitutional Convention, along with various participants' later writings on the subject, show that the framers of the Constitution intended that the vice president would temporarily exercise the powers and duties of the office in the event of a president's death, disability or removal, but not actually become the president of the United States in their own right.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-PVPS2004OCL-63)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-64)

This understanding was first tested in 1841, following the death of President [William Henry Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison "William Henry Harrison"), only 31 days into his term. Harrison's vice president, [John Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyler "John Tyler"), asserted that under the Constitution, he had succeeded to the presidency, not just to its powers and duties. He had himself [sworn in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oath_of_office_of_the_President_of_the_United_States "Oath of office of the President of the United States") as president and assumed full presidential powers, refusing to acknowledge documents referring to him as "Acting President".[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-JTDA-65) Although some in Congress denounced Tyler's claim as a violation of the Constitution,[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-FordhamLaw2011-62) he adhered to his position. His view ultimately prevailed as both the Senate and House voted to acknowledge him as president.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-66) The [Tyler Precedent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Precedent "Tyler Precedent") that a vice president assumes the full title and role of president upon the death, resignation, or removal from office (via impeachment conviction) of their predecessor was codified through the [Twenty-fifth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twenty-fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution") in 1967.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-67)[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-68) Altogether, nine vice presidents have succeeded to the presidency intra-term. In addition to Tyler, they are [Millard Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millard_Fillmore "Millard Fillmore"), [Andrew Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Johnson "Andrew Johnson"), [Chester A. Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_A._Arthur "Chester A. Arthur"), [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt"), [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge "Calvin Coolidge"), [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman"), [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson"), and [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford "Gerald Ford"). Four of them—Roosevelt, Coolidge, Truman, and Lyndon B. Johnson—were later elected to full terms of their own.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-PVPS2004OCL-63)

Four sitting vice presidents have been elected president: [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") in [1796](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1796_United_States_presidential_election "1796 United States presidential election"), [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson") in [1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_United_States_presidential_election "1800 United States presidential election"), [Martin Van Buren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren "Martin Van Buren") in [1836](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1836_United_States_presidential_election "1836 United States presidential election"), and [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush "George H. W. Bush") in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_United_States_presidential_election "1988 United States presidential election"). Likewise, two former vice presidents have won the presidency, [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_United_States_presidential_election "1968 United States presidential election") and [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election"). Five incumbent vice presidents lost a presidential election: Breckinridge in [1860](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_United_States_presidential_election "1860 United States presidential election"), Nixon in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_United_States_presidential_election "1960 United States presidential election"), Humphrey in 1968, Gore in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election "2000 United States presidential election"), and Harris in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election"). Likewise, former vice president Walter Mondale lost in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election "1984 United States presidential election").[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-69) In total, 15 vice presidents have become president.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-70)

### Acting president

Further information: [Acting President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting_President_of_the_United_States "Acting President of the United States")

Sections 3 and 4 of the Twenty-fifth Amendment provide for situations where the president is temporarily or permanently unable to lead, such as if the president has a surgical procedure, becomes seriously ill or injured, or is otherwise unable to discharge the powers or duties of the presidency. Section 3 deals with self-declared incapacity, and Section 4 addresses incapacity declared by the joint action of the vice president and of a majority of the Cabinet.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-NCC25th-71) While Section 4 has never been invoked, Section 3 has been invoked on four occasions by three presidents, first in 1985. When invoked on November 19, 2021, [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") became the first woman in U.S. history to have presidential powers and duties.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-72)

Sections 3 and 4 were added because there was ambiguity in the Article II succession clause regarding a disabled president, including what constituted an "[inability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incapacity "Incapacity")", who determined the existence of an inability, and if a vice president became president for the rest of the presidential term in the case of an inability or became merely "acting president". During the 19th century and first half of the 20th century, several presidents experienced periods of severe illness, physical disability or injury, some lasting for weeks or months. During these times, even though the nation needed effective presidential leadership, no vice president wanted to seem like a usurper, and so power was never transferred. After President [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower "Dwight D. Eisenhower") openly addressed his health issues and made it a point to enter into an agreement with Vice President Richard Nixon that provided for Nixon to act on his behalf if Eisenhower became unable to provide effective presidential leadership (Nixon did informally assume some of the president's duties for several weeks on each of three occasions when Eisenhower was ill), discussions began in Congress about clearing up the Constitution's ambiguity on the subject.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-FordhamLaw2011-62)[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-NCC25th-71)

## Modern roles

The present-day power of the office flows primarily from formal and informal delegations of authority from the president and Congress.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-24KJLPP1-13) These delegations can vary in significance; for example, the vice president is a statutory member of both the National Security Council and the board of regents of the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution "Smithsonian Institution").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-USlegal-VP-10) The extent of the roles and functions of the vice president depend on the specific relationship between the president and the vice president, but often include tasks such as drafter and spokesperson for the administration's policies, adviser to the president, and being a symbol of American concern or support. The influence of the vice president in these roles depends almost entirely on the characteristics of the particular administration.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-73)

### Presidential advisor

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/President_Donald_Trump_and_Vice_President_JD_Vance_walk_along_the_Colonnade_towards_the_Oval_Office_%2854508186298%29.jpg/250px-President_Donald_Trump_and_Vice_President_JD_Vance_walk_along_the_Colonnade_towards_the_Oval_Office_%2854508186298%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Donald_Trump_and_Vice_President_JD_Vance_walk_along_the_Colonnade_towards_the_Oval_Office_(54508186298).jpg)

Vice President [JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Vance "JD Vance") and President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"), 2025.

Most recent vice presidents have been viewed as important presidential advisors. Walter Mondale, unlike his immediate predecessors, did not want specific responsibilities to be delegated to him. Mondale believed, as he wrote in a memo to President-elect Jimmy Carter following the 1976 election, that his most important role would be as a "general adviser" to the president.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-WPO_41921-42)[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-74) Al Gore was an important adviser to President [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") on matters of [foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy "Foreign policy") and the [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy "Environmental policy"). Dick Cheney was widely regarded as one of President George W. Bush's closest confidants. Joe Biden asked President Barack Obama to let him always be the "last person in the room" when a big decision was made; later, as president himself, Biden adopted this model with his own vice president, Kamala Harris.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Politico-75)[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-BusinessInsider-76)

### Governing partner

Recent vice presidents have been delegated authority by presidents to handle significant issue areas independently. Joe Biden (who has held the office of President and Vice President of the United States) has observed that the presidency is "too big anymore for any one man or woman".[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-77) Dick Cheney was considered to hold a tremendous amount of power and frequently made policy decisions on his own, without the knowledge of the president.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Kenneth_T._Walsh-43) Biden was assigned by Barack Obama to oversee Iraq policy; Obama was said to have said, "Joe, you do Iraq."[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-78) In February 2020, [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") appointed Mike Pence to lead his response to COVID-19.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-79) In March 2021, President Biden put Kamala Harris in charge of addressing the influx of migrants at the [U.S.–Mexico border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Mexico_border "U.S.–Mexico border").[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-80)

### Congressional liaison

The vice president is often an important liaison between the administration and Congress, especially in situations where the president has not previously served in Congress or served only briefly. Vice presidents are often selected as running mates in part due to their legislative relationships, notably including Richard Nixon, Lyndon Johnson, Walter Mondale, Dick Cheney, Joe Biden, and Mike Pence among others. In recent years, Dick Cheney held weekly meetings in the Vice President's Room at the [United States Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Capitol "United States Capitol"), Joe Biden played a key role in bipartisan budget negotiations, and Mike Pence often met with House and Senate Republicans. Kamala Harris presided over a 50–50 split Senate during the [117th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/117th_United_States_Congress "117th United States Congress"), which provided her with a key role in passing legislation.

### Representative at events

Under the American [system of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system") the president is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") and [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government"),[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-81) and the ceremonial duties of the former position are often delegated to the vice president. The vice president will on occasion represent the president and the U.S. government at state funerals abroad, or at various events in the United States. This often is the most visible role of the vice president. The vice president may also meet with other heads of state at times when the administration wishes to demonstrate concern or support but cannot send the president personally.

### National Security Council member

Since 1949, the vice president has legally been a member of the [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Security_Council "United States National Security Council"). [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman"), having not been told about any war or post-war plans during his vice presidency (notably the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project")), recognized that upon assuming the presidency a vice president needed to be already informed on such issues. Modern vice presidents have also been included in the president's [daily intelligence briefings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Daily_Brief "President's Daily Brief")[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Politico-75) and frequently participate in meetings in the [Situation Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situation_Room "Situation Room") with the president.

## Selection process

### Eligibility

To be constitutionally eligible to serve as the nation's vice president, a person must, according to the Twelfth Amendment, meet the eligibility requirements to become president (which are stated in [Article II, Section 1, Clause 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_Two_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Clause_5:_Qualifications_for_office "Article Two of the United States Constitution")). Thus, to serve as vice president, an individual must:

*   be a [natural-born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural-born-citizen_clause_(United_States) "Natural-born-citizen clause (United States)")[U.S. citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_nationality_law "United States nationality law");
*   be at least 35 years old;
*   be a [resident in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_(domicile)#United_States "Residency (domicile)") for at least 14 years.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-AC-XII-82)

A person who meets the above qualifications is still disqualified from holding the office of vice president under the following conditions:

*   Under [Article I, Section 3, Clause 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_One_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Clause_7:_Judgment_in_cases_of_impeachment;_Punishment_on_conviction "Article One of the United States Constitution"), upon conviction in impeachment cases, the Senate has the option of disqualifying convicted individuals from holding federal office, including that of vice president;
*   Under the [Twelfth Amendment to the United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twelfth Amendment to the United States Constitution"), "no person constitutionally ineligible to the office of President shall be eligible to that of Vice President of the United States".[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-AC-XII-82)
*   Under [Section 3 of the Fourteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution#Participants_in_rebellion "Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution"), no person who has sworn an oath to support the Constitution, who has later "engaged in insurrection or rebellion" against the United States, or given aid and comfort to the nation's enemies can serve in a state or federal office—including as vice president. This disqualification, originally aimed at former supporters of the [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America "Confederate States of America"), may be removed by a two-thirds vote of each house of the Congress.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-83)

### Nomination

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/GERALDINEFERRARO.jpg/250px-GERALDINEFERRARO.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:GERALDINEFERRARO.jpg)

[Geraldine Ferraro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Ferraro "Geraldine Ferraro") speaks following her selection as the party's vice presidential nominee.

The vice presidential candidates of the major national political parties are formally selected by each party's quadrennial nominating convention, following the selection of the party's presidential candidate. The official process is identical to the one by which the presidential candidates are chosen, with delegates placing the names of candidates into nomination, followed by a ballot in which candidates must receive a majority to secure the party's nomination.

In modern practice, the presidential nominee has considerable influence on the decision, and since the mid-20th century it became customary for that person to select a preferred running mate, who is then nominated and accepted by the convention. Prior to Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1940, only two presidents—Andrew Jackson in [1832](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1832_Democratic_National_Convention "1832 Democratic National Convention") and Abraham Lincoln in [1864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1864_National_Union_National_Convention "1864 National Union National Convention")—had done so.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States#cite_note-Smithsonian112014-84) In recent years, with the presidential nomination usually being a foregone conclusion as the result of the primary process, the selection of a vice presidential candidate is often announced prior to the actual balloting for the presidential candidate, and sometimes before the beginning of the convention itself. The most recent presidential nominee not to name a vice presidential choice, leaving the matter up to the convention, was Democrat [Adlai Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlai_Stevenson_II "Adlai Stevenson II") in 1956. The convention chose [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee") Senator [Estes Kefauver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estes_Kefauver "Estes Kefauver") over [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts") Senator (and later president) [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy"). At the tumultuous 1972 Democratic convention, presidential nominee [George McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern "George McGovern") selected [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri "Missouri") Senator [Thomas Eagleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Eagleton "Thomas Eagleton") as his running mate, but numerous other candidates were either nominated from the floor or received votes during the balloting. Eagleton nevertheless received a majority of the votes and the nomination, though he later resigned from the ticket, resulting in [Sargent Shriver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargent_Shriver "Sargent Shriver") from [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland") becoming McGovern's final running mate; both lost to the Nixon–Agnew ticket by a wide margin, carrying only Massachusetts and the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.").

During times in a presidential election cycle before the identity of the presidential nominee is clear, including cases where the presidential nomination is still in doubt as the convention approaches, campaigns for the two positions may become intertwined. In 1976, [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan"), who was trailing President [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford "Gerald Ford") in the presidential delegate count, announced prior to the Republican National Convention that, if nominated, he would select [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania") Senator [Richard Schweiker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schweiker "Richard Schweiker") as his running mate. Reagan was the first presidential aspirant to announce his selection for vice president before the beginning of the convention.
