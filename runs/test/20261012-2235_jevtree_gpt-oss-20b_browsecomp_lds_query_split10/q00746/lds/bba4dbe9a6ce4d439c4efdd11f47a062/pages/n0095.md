## Proposal to add a talk page guideline for Meta

[[edit](https://meta.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=Meta:Babel&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Proposal to add a talk page guideline for Meta")]

Latest comment: [12 hours ago](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Meta:Babel#c-Pine-20261010030700-~2026-33630-51-20260831031500)42 comments 14 people in discussion

Hello, currently Meta does not have a talk page guideline or policy listed under [Meta:Policies and guidelines](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Meta:Policies_and_guidelines "Meta:Policies and guidelines").

Reason for this proposal: [a UCoC case](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Universal_Code_of_Conduct/Coordinating_Committee/Cases/2026/Chilling_effect_on_Metawiki "Universal Code of Conduct/Coordinating Committee/Cases/2026/Chilling effect on Metawiki") (which should not be litigated in this Babel discussion) leads me to believe that Meta should have such a policy or guideline at least for _user_ talk pages, so that informal consensus or unwritten norms are not used as a basis for enforcement actions related to user talk page conduct on Meta. A written policy or guideline may be used by everyone to understand the expectations on Meta for talk page activities, and also by administrators and others as a point of reference for possible enforcement actions related to talk page conduct. Administrators or others who may be hesitant to intervene in a problematic situation on talk pages hopefully will become less hesitant if they can refer to this policy or guideline page as a point of reference.

[en:Wikipedia:Talk page guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Talk_page_guidelines "en:Wikipedia:Talk page guidelines") is much longer than I hope that the Meta policy or guideline will be.

I have not yet drafted a page but I would like to get feedback regarding elements to include.

Suggested elements of the Meta page:

*   The purpose of Meta
*   General civility expectations
*   The general purpose and format of talk pages
*   Recommended practices on talk pages, such as the use of a talk page header, ping, and subscribing to discussions.
*   Generally prohibited activities on talk pages, such as misusing another person's signature to misrepresent who made a statement, or removing a warning. See [en:Wikipedia:Talk page guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Talk_page_guidelines "en:Wikipedia:Talk page guidelines") for more.
*   Similarities and differences between general talk pages and user talk pages.
*   User talk pages are not "owned".
*   How to use talk pages in specific use cases

*   When recruiting for new participants for an activity such as Wiki Loves Monuments, joining an affiliate, or voting in steward elections.
*   When contacting people who may be interested and eligible for an activity (same examples as above) and have participated in it previously but did not sign up for a newsletter or for another round of participation.
*   When contacting people who may be interested and eligible for for an activity (same examples as above), but did not sign up for a newsletter or for a round of participation and have not participated in it within the past few years.
*   When petitioning for people who have signed up for official roles to take certain actions, such as a request to engage in a discussion or to take a specific action.
*   When advocating for or against candidates for a role or an award. This is typically prohibited as "canvassing".
*   When contacting someone on their user talk page related to a discussion that is already happening on a more centralized location such as a project talk page, to discuss a specific point related to them or related to their comments in that more centralized location.

*   Escalation pathway for conduct concerns, starting with a courtesy notice and then escalating to a formal warning, and finally to administrator involvement. Remember that some users on Meta may act on talk pages in ways that are consistent with norms on other wikis but need to be informed of Meta norms.

What do others think about having the above as a starting framework for a draft?

## La adaptación española de «Don't be a jerk» se aleja de su original

[[edit](https://meta.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=Meta:Babel&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: La adaptación española de «Don't be a jerk» se aleja de su original")]

Comunidad (principalmente pero no limitada a la de habla hispana),

En la página de discusión del ensayo [No seas estúpido](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/No_seas_est%C3%BApido "No seas estúpido") estoy cuestionando su estado actual, ya que considero que se ha alejado considerablemente de su versión original y de lo que pretende transmitir como adaptación de su contraparte en inglés, [Don't be a jerk](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_be_a_jerk "Don't be a jerk").

Me parece que existen diferencias importantes tanto en el contenido como en el enfoque del ensayo, particularmente en la forma en que se aborda la conducta de los usuarios y el uso de etiquetas dirigidas a las personas.

Me gustaría conocer la opinión de la comunidad sobre si la versión en español debería revisarse para reflejar mejor el sentido del ensayo original.
