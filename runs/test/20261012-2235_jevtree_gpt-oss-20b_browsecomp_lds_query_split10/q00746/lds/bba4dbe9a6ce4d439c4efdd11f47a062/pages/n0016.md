Where your donation goes

 Accountability and transparency are core values at the Wikimedia Foundation. We manage funds and resources to ensure that every contribution supports our mission. We have earned the Platinum Seal of Transparency from Candid (formerly GuideStar), and Charity Navigator awarded us its highest rating. You can [read our most recent annual report](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/) for more information about our financial health. 

45% Investment in Technology 
Nearly half of our budget goes toward supporting the technology that powers Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects. We are constantly working to enhance the user experience for both contributors and readers, improve site security, and ensure reliable access to our websites globally. This infrastructure and product support sustain one of the top ten most visited websites in the world, all at a fraction of the cost of popular for-profit websites.

32% Support for Volunteers 
The global reach of Wikimedia projects is made possible by the hard work of volunteers from across the globe. We provide grants, legal support, and other resources to help build vibrant volunteer communities. Additionally, we promote community engagement through outreach initiatives and advocate for the growth and protection of free knowledge.

12% Allocation to Fundraising Efforts 
Donor support is crucial to sustaining Wikipedia and our other free knowledge endeavors. Our team is committed to efficient and effective fundraising throughout the year, ensuring that every contribution helps advance our mission.

11% General and Administrative Expenses 
Operational costs are essential for the smooth management and governance of the Wikimedia Foundation. These expenses help us recruit top talent and support staff around the world, empowering them to carry out the mission of the Wikimedia Foundation.

[![Image 13: Poecilia vandepolli](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Poecilia_vandepolli_male.jpg/250px-Poecilia_vandepolli_male.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Poecilia_vandepolli_male.jpg "Poecilia vandepolli")

_**[Poecilia vandepolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poecilia\_vandepolli "Poecilia vandepolli")**_ is a [poeciliid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poeciliidae "Poeciliidae") fish native to [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba "Aruba"), [Bonaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaire "Bonaire"), and [Curaçao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cura%C3%A7ao "Curaçao"). It is one of the most common fish in its range, inhabiting fresh, [brackish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackish_water "Brackish water"), salt, and [hypersaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersaline_lake "Hypersaline lake") waters. It is variable in size and color, with males ranging from 2.6 to 5.3 cm (1.0 to 2.1 in) and females from 3.2 to 9.6 cm (1.3 to 3.8 in), and coloration varying from grayish-brown to yellow or orange. Marine fish are generally larger, stronger, and more colorful than freshwater specimens. Males' ability to develop an [intense orange coloration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthochromism "Xanthochromism") makes the species attractive to aquarists. Forming [shoals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoaling_and_schooling "Shoaling and schooling") near the surface, it feeds on [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae "Algae"), [detritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detritus "Detritus"), and [aquatic invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_animal "Aquatic animal"), and is preyed on by other fish and birds. Females [produce live fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovoviviparity "Ovoviviparity"); successful reproduction depends on shelter availability for fry. The species is drawn to freshwater habitats, which disappear each [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season "Dry season"), killing freshwater populations; these habitats are [recolonized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonisation_(biology) "Colonisation (biology)") from the sea when the rains return. _(**[Full article...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poecilia\_vandepolli "Poecilia vandepolli")**)_

**[There have been 16 governors of Imo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Imo_State "List of governors of Imo State")**, located in the [South East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_(Nigeria) "South East (Nigeria)") geopolitical zone of Nigeria. [Imo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imo_State "Imo State") has been governed by a succession of [military governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_government "Military government"), [administrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_the_government "Administrator of the government"), and elected governors since its formation on 3 February 1976, when it was carved out of the former [East Central State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Central_State "East Central State"), which covered much of the [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_language "Igbo language")-speaking area until its division in 1976. During that period, [Ukpabi Asika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukpabi_Asika "Ukpabi Asika") served as administrator until 1975, followed by [Anthony Ochefu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ochefu "Anthony Ochefu") until the state's dissolution. From its creation, Imo State was governed by military appointees. With the advent of the [Second Nigerian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nigerian_Republic "Second Nigerian Republic"), [Sam Mbakwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mbakwe "Sam Mbakwe")_(statue pictured)_ of the [Nigerian People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_People's_Party "Nigerian People's Party") served as the first civilian governor from October 1979 to December 1983. Since the return of democracy under the [Fourth Nigerian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Nigerian_Republic "Fourth Nigerian Republic") in 1999, all of Imo's governors have been elected. The incumbent governor is [Hope Uzodimma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Uzodimma "Hope Uzodimma") of the [All Progressives Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Progressives_Congress "All Progressives Congress"), who has been in office since [his election in March 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Imo_State_gubernatorial_election "2019 Imo State gubernatorial election"). [![Image 18: Assassination of Inejirō Asanuma](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/9f/Tokyo_Stabbing.jpg/500px-Tokyo_Stabbing.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tokyo_Stabbing.jpg "Assassination of Inejirō Asanuma")The **[assassination of Inejirō Asanuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Inejir%C5%8D_Asanuma "Assassination of Inejirō Asanuma")** took place at [Hibiya Public Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiya_Public_Hall "Hibiya Public Hall") in Tokyo, Japan, on 12 October 1960. Asanuma, the chairman of the [Japan Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Socialist_Party "Japan Socialist Party"), was delivering a televised election speech when the 17-year-old ultranationalist [Otoya Yamaguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otoya_Yamaguchi "Otoya Yamaguchi") rushed onto the stage and stabbed him with a _[wakizashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakizashi "Wakizashi")_, a traditional Japanese short sword. Asanuma suffered a punctured [aorta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aorta "Aorta") and died en route to hospital. Yamaguchi was arrested at the scene, but hanged himself in detention three weeks later. The assassination sparked widespread protests and weakened the Socialist Party. This photograph, capturing Yamaguchi attempting to make a second stab of Asanuma, was taken by [Yasushi Nagao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasushi_Nagao "Yasushi Nagao") of the _[Mainichi Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainichi\_Shimbun "Mainichi Shimbun")_ and won the 1961 [Pulitzer Prize for Photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Photography "Pulitzer Prize for Photography").
