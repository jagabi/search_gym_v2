## What is Minnesota?

Minnesota, the 32nd state, joined the United States on May 11, 1858. It is the northernmost of the 48 conterminous U.S. states due to a small extension of its northern boundary.

Minnesota is known as the "[Land of 10,000 Lakes](https://www.britannica.com/place/Minnesota/Introduction#ref1)" because it has over 10,000 lakes larger than 10 acres. Its name comes from the Dakota word for the Mississippi River's major tributary, meaning "sky-tinted water".

The Twin Cities region (Minneapolis–St. Paul) is Minnesota's major administrative, economic, and cultural hub. St. Paul is the state capital. The estimated population of Minnesota as of 2024 is 5,793,151.

Minnesota's economy initially relied on natural resources like soil, iron ore, and timber. Today, services are the dominant economic activity, surpassing farming, mining, and manufacturing.

## Where is Minnesota located in the United States?

Minnesota is a constituent state of the United States, becoming the 32nd state on May 11, 1858. It is one of the north-central states, with a small extension of its northern boundary making it the most northerly of the 48 conterminous U.S. states.

Minnesota is bordered by the Canadian provinces of Manitoba and Ontario to the north, Lake Superior and the state of Wisconsin to the east, Iowa to the south, and South Dakota and North Dakota to the west. Population densities are greatest in the eastern and southern parts of the state, declining towards the north and west.

## What are some major cities in Minnesota?

Minnesota has many cities, the most prominent being the Twin Cities region, which includes [Minneapolis](https://www.britannica.com/place/Minneapolis/Introduction#ref1) and [St. Paul](https://www.britannica.com/place/Saint-Paul-Minnesota/Introduction#ref1). Minneapolis is the state's most populous city and lies on the Mississippi River. With St. Paul, it forms the largest metropolitan area in the state and the north-central U.S. St. Paul, the state capital, is the second most populous city in Minnesota and is known for its bluffs along the Mississippi River.

Other cities in Minnesota include Bloomington, Brooklyn Park, Plymouth, St. Louis Park, and Columbia Heights. Each year, Mankato attracts many spectators to its annual powwow.

## What is the climate like in Minnesota?

Minnesota has a continental climate, characterized by cold winters and warm summers. Temperature variations occur seasonally and across the state.

Summer: Southern Minnesota experiences hot temperatures, with average daily maximums in July reaching the mid-80s °F (about 29 °C). The northern regions can experience frost in any month, with temperatures in the low 70s °F (about 21 °C) along Lake Superior.

Winter: Average daily January highs range from the mid-20s °F (about -4 °C) in the south to around 15 °F (-9 °C) in the north; minimums range from about 5 °F (-15 °C) to roughly -5 °F (-21 °C). The state has recorded lows of -60 °F (-51 °C). Continuous snow cover lasts for 90 days in many areas, from mid-December to mid-March.

Precipitation: Average annual precipitation ranges from less than 20 inches (500 mm) in the northwest to more than 30 inches (750 mm) in the southeast. Snowfall varies from less than 40 inches (1,000 mm) in the west to over 70 inches (1,800 mm) in the northeast.

## What natural features is Minnesota known for?

Minnesota is known for its extensive woodlands, fertile prairies, and numerous lakes, which has earned it the nickname "Land of 10,000 Lakes". The state has more than 10,000 lakes that are larger than 10 acres. The nearly 5,000 square miles of inland fresh water is a dominant feature. Most of Minnesota was covered by glaciers several times, and the land’s surface was shaped by the alternate freezing, thawing, and movement of those glaciers.

The majority of Minnesota’s lakes are located in the areas of glacial moraine, where glaciers deposited hills of sand and gravel. The shoreline of [Lake Superior](https://www.britannica.com/place/Lake-Superior), one of the largest freshwater lakes in the world, forms the state’s northeastern border for some 160 miles. Minnesota’s elevations range from 602 feet above sea level at Lake Superior to 2,301 feet above sea level at Eagle Mountain.

Minnesota’s thousands of rivers flow northward via the Red and Rainy rivers to Hudson Bay, eastward through the Great Lakes to the Atlantic Ocean, and southward through the [Mississippi River](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mississippi-River) to the Gulf of Mexico.

## How has Minnesota's history shaped its culture today?

Minnesota's history has significantly shaped its culture through waves of diverse settlers. The earliest were Canadians and New Englanders of English, Scottish, and Scotch-Irish descent, who established important institutions and traditions such as open forums for debate.

Germans, Swedes, and Norwegians arrived in the latter half of the 19th century, engaging in logging, railroad construction, farming, and trade. Each group brought their own religious traditions, with central and southern Minnesota becoming heavily Roman Catholic due to German, Polish, and Bohemian heritage, while others of German and Scandinavian descent are largely Lutheran.

Minnesota's ethnic diversity is showcased through various cultural events such as Heritage Fest in New Ulm, celebrating German culture; a Czech harvest festival in New Prague; the Minnesota Scottish Fair and Highland Games in Farmington; Native American powwows; and the Festival of Nations in St. Paul.
