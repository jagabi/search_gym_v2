The **Minnesota House of Representatives** is the [lower chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_chamber "Lower chamber") of the [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") of [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota")'s [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Legislature "Minnesota Legislature"). It operates in conjunction with the [Minnesota Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Senate "Minnesota Senate"), the state's upper chamber, to write and pass legislation, which is then subject to approval by the [governor of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Minnesota "Governor of Minnesota").

Established in 1858, the Minnesota House of Representatives has 134 members elected from [single-member districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_districts "Single-member districts") across the state. Representatives serve two-year terms without [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits "Term limits"), with all seats up for election every two years. The House is led by the [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speakers_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives "Speakers of the Minnesota House of Representatives"), who is elected by members of the House, while political party leadership is governed by the [Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_majority_leaders_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives "List of majority leaders of the Minnesota House of Representatives") and [Minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minority_leaders_of_the_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives "List of minority leaders of the Minnesota House of Representatives") Leaders.

The Minnesota House of Representatives meets in the north wing of the [State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Capitol "Minnesota State Capitol") in [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota "Saint Paul, Minnesota"). Member and staff offices, as well as most committee hearings, are in the nearby State Office Building.

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Minnesota_House_of_Representatives&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

The Minnesota House of Representatives was officially established on May 11, 1858, when Minnesota was admitted as the 32nd state in the Union. It replaced the [Minnesota Territorial Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Territorial_Legislature "Minnesota Territorial Legislature"). It was formed alongside the [Minnesota Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Senate "Minnesota Senate") to create the [Minnesota State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Legislature "Minnesota State Legislature"), the bicameral legislative body of the state.

In 1913, Minnesota legislators began to be elected on nonpartisan ballots. While campaigning and caucusing, legislators identified themselves as "Liberals" or "Conservatives." In 1973, a law change brought party designations back, beginning with the [1974 Minnesota House of Representatives election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives_election "1974 Minnesota House of Representatives election").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-3)

After the [Nineteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Nineteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution") was ratified in 1920, women were eligible for election to the legislature. In 1922, [Mabeth Hurd Paige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabeth_Hurd_Paige "Mabeth Hurd Paige"), [Hannah Kempfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Kempfer "Hannah Kempfer"), [Sue Metzger Dickey Hough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sue_Metzger_Dickey_Hough "Sue Metzger Dickey Hough"), and [Myrtle Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtle_Cain "Myrtle Cain") were elected to the House of Representatives.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-4) Until Representative [Melissa Hortman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Hortman "Melissa Hortman") was assassinated, a record-high 55 women served in the House in 2025.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-5)

## Elections

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Minnesota_House_of_Representatives&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Elections")]

Each Minnesota Senate district is divided in half and given the suffix _A_ or _B_ to form a House district (for example, House district 32B is in Senate district 32). Members are elected to two-year terms.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-6) Districts are redrawn after the decennial [United States Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census "United States Census") in time for the primary and general elections in years ending in 2. The [most recent election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives_election "2024 Minnesota House of Representatives election") was on November 5, 2024.

The 94th Minnesota Legislature began on January 14, 2025. For each major party, 67 representatives were elected, the second ever tie in the Minnesota House.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-17) After the election of Curtis Johnson (District 40B) was nullified in court,[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-roseville-ruling-7) the session began with 67 Republican members, while the 66 elected DFL members sat out in an effort to deny quorum.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-l772-18) On March 11, 2025, [David Gottfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gottfried_(politician) "David Gottfried (politician)") (DFL) was elected to the seat, restoring the 67–67 tie.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-AP-40Bwin-19) After Gottfried was seated, [Melissa Hortman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Hortman "Melissa Hortman") was granted the title "DFL Leader" instead of "Minority Leader", and the two parties entered into a power-sharing agreement.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-leaders-1) After Hortman's assassination, [Zack Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Stephenson "Zack Stephenson") became the House DFL Leader.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives#cite_note-20)
