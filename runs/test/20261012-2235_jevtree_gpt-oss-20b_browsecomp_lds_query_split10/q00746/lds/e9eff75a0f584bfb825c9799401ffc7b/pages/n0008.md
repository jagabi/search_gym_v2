**Ilhan Abdullahi Omar** (born October 4, 1982) is an  American politician serving as the [U.S. representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._representative "U.S. representative") for [Minnesota's 5th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota's_5th_congressional_district "Minnesota's 5th congressional district") since 2019. The district includes all of [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") and some of its [first-ring suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_suburb "Inner suburb"). From 2017 to 2019, Omar served in the [Minnesota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives "Minnesota House of Representatives"), representing part of Minneapolis. She is a member of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)").

Omar is the first [Somali American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_American "Somali American") in the [United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") and the first [woman of color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person_of_color "Person of color") to represent Minnesota.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Golden_Nov_2018-4) She is also one of the first two [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "Muslim") women (along with [Rashida Tlaib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Tlaib "Rashida Tlaib")) to serve in Congress.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-O'Grady-5)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-6) Her political opponents, including [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"), have made derogatory comments related to her background. She has also received several death threats.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-7)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-BBCThreat-8)

A [progressive Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States "Progressivism in the United States"), Omar serves as deputy chair of the [Congressional Progressive Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Progressive_Caucus "Congressional Progressive Caucus") and has advocated for a [$15 minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_for_$15 "Fight for $15"), [universal healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_healthcare "Universal healthcare"), [student loan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_loans_in_the_United_States "Student loans in the United States") debt forgiveness, the protection of [Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deferred_Action_for_Childhood_Arrivals "Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals"), and [abolishing U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_ICE "Abolish ICE") (ICE). A frequent critic of Israel, Omar supports the [Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions "Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions") (BDS) movement and has denounced Israel's [settlement policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_settlement "Israeli settlement") and [military campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza%E2%80%93Israel_conflict "Gaza–Israel conflict") in the occupied [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories "Palestinian territories"), as well as the influence of [pro-Israel lobbies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_lobby_in_the_United_States "Israel lobby in the United States") in American politics.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-9)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-10)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-11) In February 2023, the Republican-controlled House voted to remove Omar from her seat on the [Foreign Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs "United States House Committee on Foreign Affairs"), citing past comments she had made about Israel and concerns over her objectivity.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-12)

## Early life and education

Omar was born Ilham Abdullahi Omar[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Ilham-13) in [Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu "Mogadishu"), Somalia, on October 4, 1982,[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-14)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Montemayor-15) and spent her early years in [Baidoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidoa "Baidoa"), in southern Somalia.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Reinl-16)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-17) She was the youngest of seven siblings. Her father is Nur Omar Mohamed, an ethnic [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalis "Somalis") from the [Osman Mohamud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Mohamoud_(clan) "Osman Mohamoud (clan)") sub-clan of [Majeerteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majeerteen "Majeerteen"),[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-18) a clan in [northeastern Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puntland "Puntland").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Marlowe-19) He was a colonel in the [Somali Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Army "Somali Army") under [Siad Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre "Siad Barre"), and served in the [Ogaden War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden_War "Ogaden War") (1977–78). He also worked as a teacher trainer.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-20)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Zurowski-21)

Omar's mother, Fadhuma Abukar Haji Hussein, an ethnic [Benadiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benadiri_people "Benadiri people"), died when Omar was two.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-22)[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-VONY-23)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Adam-25) Omar was raised by her father and grandfather, who were moderate [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni "Sunni")[Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims") opposed to the rigid [Wahhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabi "Wahhabi") interpretation of Islam.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-26)[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Holpuch-27) Her grandfather Abukar was the director of Somalia's National Marine Transport, and some of Omar's uncles and aunts also worked as [civil servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_servant "Civil servant") and educators.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Zurowski-21) She and her family fled Somalia to escape the [Somali Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Civil_War "Somali Civil War") and spent four years in a [Dadaab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadaab "Dadaab")[refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_camp "Refugee camp") in [Garissa County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garissa_County "Garissa County"), [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-28)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-29)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-30)

Omar's family secured [asylum in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_in_the_United_States "Asylum in the United States") and arrived in [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") in 1995,[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-31)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Luckhurst-32) then lived for a time in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Virginia "Arlington, Virginia"),[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24) before moving to and settling in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"),[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24) where her father worked first as a taxi driver and later for the post office.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24) Her father and grandfather emphasized the importance of [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy") during her upbringing, and at age 14 she accompanied her grandfather to [caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus "Caucus") meetings, serving as his [interpreter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_interpretation "Language interpretation").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Holpuch-27)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Omar_M-33) She has spoken about [school bullying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_bullying "School bullying") she endured during her time in Virginia, stimulated by her distinctive Somali appearance and wearing of the [hijab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijab "Hijab"). She recalls gum being pressed into her hijab, being pushed down stairs, and physical taunts while she was changing for gym class.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24) Omar remembers her father's reaction to these incidents: "They are doing something to you because they feel threatened in some way by your existence."[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24) Omar became a [U.S. citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._citizen "U.S. citizen") in 2000 when she was 17 years old.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Forliti-34)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24)

Omar attended [Thomas Edison High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_High_School_(Minnesota) "Edison High School (Minnesota)"), from which she graduated in 2001, and volunteered as a student organizer.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Duarte-35) She graduated from [North Dakota State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_State_University "North Dakota State University") in 2011 with a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree"), majoring in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science") and [international studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_studies "International studies").[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-36)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Omar_M-33) Omar was a policy fellow at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota")'s [Humphrey School of Public Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_School_of_Public_Affairs "Humphrey School of Public Affairs").[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Ilhan's_Story-37)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-legstatereference-38)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-39)

## Early career

[![Image 7](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Ilhan_Omar%2C_Brent_Hardt%2C_Mark_Ritchie_and_Jim_Core_in_Paris_-_2017_%2837746187644%29.jpg/250px-Ilhan_Omar%2C_Brent_Hardt%2C_Mark_Ritchie_and_Jim_Core_in_Paris_-_2017_%2837746187644%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ilhan_Omar,_Brent_Hardt,_Mark_Ritchie_and_Jim_Core_in_Paris_-_2017_(37746187644).jpg)

Omar with [John Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Sullivan_(diplomat) "John J. Sullivan (diplomat)") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") as part of Minnesota's [World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_fair "World's fair") Bid Committee, 2017

Omar began her professional career as a community nutrition educator at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota"), working in that capacity from 2006 to 2009 in the greater [Minneapolis–Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul "Minneapolis–Saint Paul") area. In 2012, she served as [campaign manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_manager "Campaign manager") for [Kari Dziedzic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kari_Dziedzic "Kari Dziedzic")'s reelection campaign for the [Minnesota State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Senate "Minnesota State Senate"). Between 2012 and 2013, she was a child nutrition outreach coordinator at the [Minnesota Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Department_of_Education "Minnesota Department of Education").[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-40)

In 2013, Omar managed [Andrew Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Johnson_(Minnesota_politician) "Andrew Johnson (Minnesota politician)")'s campaign for [Minneapolis City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_City_Council "Minneapolis City Council"). After Johnson was elected, she served as his senior policy aide from 2013 to 2015.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Ilhan's_Story-37) In February 2014, during a contentious precinct caucus that turned violent, Omar was attacked by five people and injured.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Zurowski-21) According to _[MinnPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MinnPost "MinnPost")_, the day before the caucus, Minneapolis city council member [Abdi Warsame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdi_Warsame "Abdi Warsame") had told Johnson to warn Omar not to attend the meeting.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-NordBierschbach-41)

As of September 2015, Omar was the Director of Policy Initiatives of the Women Organizing Women Network, advocating for women from [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa") to take on civic and political leadership roles.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Ilhan's_Story-37) In September 2018, Jeff Cirillo of _[Roll Call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roll\_Call "Roll Call")_ called her a "progressive rising star".[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Cirillo-42)

In 2016, Omar ran on the [Democratic–Farmer–Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party "Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party") (DFL) ticket for the [Minnesota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives_election,_2016 "Minnesota House of Representatives election, 2016") in District 60B, which includes part of northeast Minneapolis. On August 9, Omar defeated [Mohamud Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamud_Noor "Mohamud Noor") and incumbent [Phyllis Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn "Phyllis Kahn") in the DFL primary.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-43) Her chief opponent in the general election was Republican nominee Abdimalik Askar, another activist in the Somali-American community. In late August, Askar announced his withdrawal from the campaign.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Sawyer-44) In November, Omar won the general election, becoming the first [Somali-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali-American "Somali-American") legislator in the United States.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Blair-45) Her term began on January 3, 2017.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-46)

### Tenure and activity

During her tenure as state representative for District 60B, Omar was an assistant minority leader for the DFL caucus.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-47)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-48) She authored 38 bills during the 2017–2018 legislative session.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-49)[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-50)

### Campaign finance investigations

In 2018, Republican state representative [Steve Drazkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Drazkowski "Steve Drazkowski") publicly accused Omar of campaign finance violations,[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Montemayor-15) claiming that she used campaign funds to pay a divorce lawyer, and that her acceptance of speaking fees from public colleges violated Minnesota House rules. Omar responded that the attorney's fees were not personal but campaign-related; she offered to return the speaking fees.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Bierschbach-52)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Van_Berkel-53) Drazkowski later accused Omar of improperly using campaign funds for personal travel to Estonia and locations in the U.S.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Montemayor-15)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-AP_Questions-54)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Forliti-34)

Omar's campaign dismissed the accusations as politically motivated and accused Drazkowski of using public funds to harass a Muslim candidate.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Forliti-34)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Luckhurst-32) In response to an editorial in the Minneapolis _[Star Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star\_Tribune "Star Tribune")_ arguing that Omar should be more transparent about her use of campaign funds, she said: "these people are part of systems that have historically been disturbingly motivated to silence, discredit and dehumanize influencers who threaten the establishment."[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Forliti-34)

In June 2019, Minnesota campaign finance officials ruled that Omar had to pay back $3,500 that she had spent on out-of-state travel and tax filing in violation of state law, plus a $500 fine.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-55) The Campaign Finance Board's investigation also found that, in 2014 and 2015, Omar had jointly filed taxes with a man to whom she was not legally married. Unlike some states, Minnesota does not recognize [common-law marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common-law_marriage_in_the_United_States "Common-law marriage in the United States"), and so such a joint filing is not legally permitted. But experts have said that if the taxpayer files a correction within three years, as Omar's attorney and accountants did in 2016, then there are normally no further consequences, and the Internal Revenue Service is unlikely to pursue punitive measures unless there is a large discrepancy or fraudulent intent. In response to the [AP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press")'s request for comment, her campaign sent a statement saying, "all of Rep. Omar's tax filings are fully compliant with all applicable tax law."[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-56)[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-57)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Forliti2019-58)

### Elections

#### 2018

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Welcoming_several_of_the_new_female_CBC_members_49798884_862840530725748_4594476672882835456_o.jpg/250px-Welcoming_several_of_the_new_female_CBC_members_49798884_862840530725748_4594476672882835456_o.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Welcoming_several_of_the_new_female_CBC_members_49798884_862840530725748_4594476672882835456_o.jpg)

Omar and other female members of the [Congressional Black Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Black_Caucus "Congressional Black Caucus") in January 2019

On June 5, 2018, Omar filed to run for the [United States House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") from [Minnesota's 5th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota's_5th_congressional_district "Minnesota's 5th congressional district") after six-term incumbent [Keith Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Ellison "Keith Ellison") announced he would not seek reelection.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Potter-59) On June 17, she was endorsed by the Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party after two rounds of voting.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-60) Omar won the August 14 primary with 48.2% of the vote.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-61)

The 5th district is the most Democratic district in Minnesota and the Upper Midwest (it has a [Cook Partisan Voting Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Partisan_Voting_Index "Cook Partisan Voting Index") of D+26) and the DFL has held it since 1963. Omar faced health care worker and conservative activist Jennifer Zielinski in the November 6 general election[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-62) and won with 78.0% of the vote, becoming the first Somali American elected to the [U.S. Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Congress "U.S. Congress"), the first woman of color to serve as a U.S. representative from Minnesota,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-O'Grady-5) and (alongside former Michigan state representative [Rashida Tlaib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Tlaib "Rashida Tlaib")) one of the first Muslim women elected to the Congress.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Magane-63)[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Newburger-64)[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-65)

Omar received the largest percentage of the vote of any female candidate for U.S. House in state history,[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Ostermeier-66) as well as the largest percentage for a non-incumbent U.S. House candidate (excluding those running against only minor-party candidates).[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Ostermeier-66) She was sworn in on a copy of the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran") owned by her grandfather.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Herrera-67)[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Karas-68)

Omar won the Democratic nomination in the August 11 Democratic primary, in which she faced four opponents. The strongest was mediation lawyer Antone Melton-Meaux, who raised $3.2 million in April–June 2020, compared to about $500,000 by Omar; much of Melton-Meaux's funding came from pro-Israel groups.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-69)[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-70) Melton-Meaux was also endorsed by Minnesota's largest newspaper, The _[Star Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star\_Tribune "Star Tribune")_.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-71) This led some analysts to predict a close race,[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-72) but Omar received 57.4% of the vote to Melton-Meaux's 39.2%.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-73)[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-74)

She defeated Republican Lacy Johnson and [Legal Marijuana Now Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_Marijuana_Now_Party "Legal Marijuana Now Party") candidate Michael Moore in the November 3 general election, with 64.3% of the vote to Johnson's 25.8% and Moore's 9.5%.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-MNgenr-75) Omar's margin of victory was 24 points less than Biden's in the district, the highest underperformance of any Democrat in the nation, which Nathaniel Rakich of _[FiveThirtyEight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FiveThirtyEight "FiveThirtyEight")_ attributed to increased Republican spending and Moore's progressive pro-marijuana campaign.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-76)

### Tenure

Following Omar's election, the [ban on head coverings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives_ban_on_head_covering "United States House of Representatives ban on head covering") in the U.S. House was modified, and Omar became the first woman to wear a [hijab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijab "Hijab") on the House floor.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24) She is a member of the informal group known as "[The Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Squad_(United_States_Congress) "The Squad (United States Congress)")", whose members form a unified front to push for progressive changes such as the [Green New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_New_Deal "Green New Deal") and [Medicare for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_for_All "Medicare for All"). The other members of the Squad are [Ayanna Pressley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayanna_Pressley "Ayanna Pressley"), [Rashida Tlaib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Tlaib "Rashida Tlaib"), and [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez "Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez").[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-83)

Brian Stelter of [CNN Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_Business "CNN Business") found that from January to July 2019 Omar was mentioned about twice as often on [Fox News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News "Fox News") as on CNN and MSNBC, and about six times as often as [James Clyburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clyburn "James Clyburn"), a Democratic House leader.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-84) A [CBS News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News "CBS News") and [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov "YouGov") poll of almost 2,100 U.S. adults conducted from July 17 to 19 found that Republican respondents were more aware of Omar than Democratic respondents. Omar has very unfavorable ratings among Republican respondents and favorable ratings among Democratic respondents. The same is true of the other members of the Squad.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-85)

On September 10, 2025, Omar condemned the [assassination of Charlie Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Charlie_Kirk "Assassination of Charlie Kirk"), saying, "political violence is completely unacceptable and indefensible."[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-86) She called conservatives who blamed the left for the shooting "full of shit"[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-87) and reposted a video calling Kirk a "stochastic terrorist" who "with his last dying words ... was spewing racist dogwhistles". This led to two Republican efforts to strip Omar of her committee assignments.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-88) One of them, led by Representative [Nancy Mace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Mace "Nancy Mace"), failed on September 17, 2025, with four Republicans joining all Democrats to kill the measure.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-89)

#### Legislation

In July 2019, Omar introduced a resolution co-sponsored by Rashida Tlaib and Georgia representative [John Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis "John Lewis") stating that "all Americans have the right to participate in boycotts in pursuit of civil and human rights at home and abroad, as protected by the [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution") to the Constitution". The resolution "opposes unconstitutional [legislative efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Anti-Boycott_Act "Israel Anti-Boycott Act") to limit the use of boycotts to further civil rights at home and abroad", and "urges Congress, States, and civil rights leaders from all communities to endeavor to preserve the freedom of advocacy for all by opposing anti-boycott resolutions and legislation".[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-90) In the same month, Omar was one of 17 members of Congress to vote against a House resolution condemning the BDS movement.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-91)

On January 7, 2021, Omar led a group of 13 House members introducing [articles of impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_impeachment "Articles of impeachment") against Trump on charges of [high crimes and misdemeanors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_crimes_and_misdemeanors "High crimes and misdemeanors").[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-92) The charges are related to [Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s alleged [interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump%E2%80%93Raffensperger_scandal "Trump–Raffensperger scandal") in the [2020 presidential election in Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election_in_Georgia "2020 United States presidential election in Georgia") and incitement of the [attack at the U.S. Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_States_Capitol_attack "2021 United States Capitol attack") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), by his [supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstrations_in_support_of_Donald_Trump "Demonstrations in support of Donald Trump"), which occurred during the certification of electoral votes in the 2020 presidential election that affirmed [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden")'s victory.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-93)[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-94)

In 2022, Omar urged the renewal of the MEALS Act, which provided free meals to 20-30 million children amid rising prices due to the [Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_(2022%E2%80%93present) "Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)").[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-95) Additionally, her [sponsored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponsor_(legislative) "Sponsor (legislative)") piece of legislation, a bill to rename the [central Minneapolis post office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Olav_Sabo_Post_Office "Martin Olav Sabo Post Office") after former congressman [Martin Olav Sabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Olav_Sabo "Martin Olav Sabo"), became law.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-96)

### Education

Omar supports broader access to student loan forgiveness programs, as well as [free tuition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_education "Free education") for college students whose family income is below $125,000.[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-110) Omar supports [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders")'s plan to eliminate all $1.6 trillion in outstanding [student debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_debt "Student debt"), funded by an 0.5% tax on stock transactions and a 0.1% tax on bond transactions;[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-WaPoPlan-111) she introduced a companion bill in the House of Representatives.[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-112) In June 2019, Omar and Senator [Tina Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Smith "Tina Smith") introduced the No Shame at School Act, which would end the marking of—and punishment for—students with [school meal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_meal_programs_in_the_United_States "School meal programs in the United States") debt.[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-113)

### Health care

Omar supports [Medicare for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_for_All "Medicare for All") as proposed in the [Expanded and Improved Medicare for All Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_and_Improved_Medicare_for_All_Act "Expanded and Improved Medicare for All Act").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Dec_30-24)[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Healthcare-114)

On July 19, 2022, after the Supreme Court overruled _[Roe v. Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe\_v.\_Wade "Roe v. Wade")_ in _[Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbs\_v.\_Jackson\_Women's\_Health\_Organization "Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization")_, Omar and 17 other members of Congress were arrested in an act of [civil disobedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_disobedience "Civil disobedience") for refusing to clear a street during a protest for reproductive rights outside the [Supreme Court Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Supreme_Court_Building "United States Supreme Court Building").[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-115)[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-NBC-116)

### Foreign affairs

Omar has criticized [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia")'s [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Saudi_Arabia "Human rights in Saudi Arabia") and the [Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi-led_intervention_in_the_Yemeni_civil_war "Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war").[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Salem-117)[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Brodey-118) In October 2018, she tweeted: "The Saudi government might have been strategic at covering up the daily atrocities carried out against minorities, women, activists and even the #YemenGenocide, but the [murder of Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Jamal_Khashoggi "Assassination of Jamal Khashoggi") should be the last evil act they are allowed to commit."[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Brodey-118) She also called for a [boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott "Boycott") of Saudi Arabia's regime, tweeting: "#BDSSaudi."[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Beinart-119) The Saudi Arabian government responded by having dozens of anonymous Twitter [troll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troll_(slang) "Troll (slang)") accounts it controlled post tweets critical of Omar.[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Salem-117)

Omar condemned [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China")'s treatment of its ethnic [Uyghur people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_people "Uyghur people").[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Bayoumi-120) In a _Washington Post_ op-ed, Omar wrote, "Our criticisms of oppression and regional instability caused by [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") are not legitimate if we do not hold [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") and [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain "Bahrain") to the same standards. And we cannot continue to turn a blind eye to repression in Saudi Arabia—a country that is consistently ranked among the worst of the worst human rights offenders."[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Values-121) She also condemned the Assad regime in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria").[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-122) Omar criticized Trump's decision to impose further [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Iran "Sanctions against Iran") on Iran, saying the sanctions devastated the "country's middle class and increased hostility toward the United States, with tensions between the two countries rising to dangerous levels."[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-wpost-omar-123)

Omar opposed the October [2019 Turkish offensive into north-eastern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Turkish_offensive_into_north-eastern_Syria "2019 Turkish offensive into north-eastern Syria"), writing, "What has happened after Turkey's invasion of northeastern Syria is a disaster—tens of thousands of civilians have been forced to flee, hundreds of Islamic State fighters have escaped, and Turkish-backed rebels have been credibly accused of atrocities against the [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds "Kurds")."[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-wpost-omar-123)

In October 2019, Omar voted "present" on H.Res. 296, to [recognize the Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide_recognition "Armenian genocide recognition"),[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-124) causing a backlash.[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-125)[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-126) She said in a statement that "accountability and recognition of genocide should not be used as cudgel in a political fight" and argued that such a step should include both the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") and the [Native American genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_genocide "Native American genocide").[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-127) In November, after her controversial vote, Omar publicly condemned the Armenian genocide at a rally for presidential candidate [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders").[[125]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-128)[[126]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-129)

In September 2025, Omar introduced a [war powers resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Powers_Resolution "War Powers Resolution") to prevent the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Trump_Administration "2nd Trump Administration") from conducting future strikes in the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") following a [US attack on a Venezuelan boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_strikes_on_alleged_drug_traffickers_during_Operation_Southern_Spear "United States strikes on alleged drug traffickers during Operation Southern Spear").[[127]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-130)

### Immigration

In a March 2019 _[Politico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politico "Politico")_ interview, Omar criticized [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama")'s "caging of kids" along the Mexican border.[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Le_Miere-131)[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Pesce-132) Omar accused _Politico_ of distorting her comments and said that she had been "saying how [President] Trump is different from Obama, and why we should focus on policy not politics," adding, "One is human[_[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_], the other is really not."[[130]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-133)

In June 2019, Omar was one of four Democratic representatives to vote against the Emergency Supplemental Appropriations for Humanitarian Assistance and Security at the Southern Border Act, a $4.5 billion border funding bill that required Customs and Border Protection to enact health standards for individuals in custody such as standards for "medical emergencies; nutrition, hygiene, and facilities; and personnel training." "Throwing more money at the very organizations committing human rights abuses—and the very Administration directing these human rights abuses—is not a solution. This is a humanitarian crisis ... inflicted by our own leadership," she said.[[131]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-134)[[132]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-135)

### Infrastructure spending

On November 5, 2021, Omar was one of six House Democrats to break with their party and vote against the [Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure_Investment_and_Jobs_Act "Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act") because it was decoupled from the social safety net provisions in the [Build Back Better Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Build_Back_Better_Act "Build Back Better Act").[[133]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-136)[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-137)

building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_settlement "Israeli settlement") in the Israeli-occupied [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli-occupied_territories In 2018, Omar came under criticism for statements she made about Israel before she was in the Minnesota legislature.[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-ToI-138)[[137]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Kampeas-140) In a 2012 tweet, she wrote, "Israel has hypnotized the world, may [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah "Allah") awaken the people and help them see the evil doings of Israel."[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-ToI-138)[[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Crowe-144) The comment, particularly that Israel had "hypnotized the world", was criticized as drawing on [antisemitic tropes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitic_trope "Antisemitic trope").[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-ToI-138) Then-_[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ columnist [Bari Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari_Weiss "Bari Weiss") wrote that Omar's statement tied into a millennia-old "conspiracy theory of the Jew as the hypnotic conspirator".[[142]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-145) When asked in an interview how she would respond to American Jews who found the remark offensive, Omar replied: "I don't know how my comments would be offensive to Jewish Americans. My comments precisely are addressing what was happening during the [Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_(2012) "Gaza War (2012)") and I'm clearly speaking about the way the Israeli regime was conducting itself in that war."[[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Crowe-144) After reading Weiss's commentary, Omar apologized for not "disavowing the anti-Semitic trope I unknowingly used".[[143]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Flynn-146)

In September 2019, Omar condemned [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu")'s plans to [annex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_the_Jordan_Valley "Annexation of the Jordan Valley") the eastern portion of the occupied West Bank known as the [Jordan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Valley "Jordan Valley").[[144]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-147) Omar said Israelis should not vote for Netanyahu in the [September 2019 Israeli legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2019_Israeli_legislative_election "September 2019 Israeli legislative election").[[145]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-148)

#### Remarks on AIPAC and American support for Israel

In February 2019, Republican [House Minority Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Minority_Leader "House Minority Leader")[Kevin McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_McCarthy "Kevin McCarthy") threatened to "take action" against Omar and Rashida Tlaib for their support of the [BDS movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions "Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions"). When journalist [Glenn Greenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Greenwald "Glenn Greenwald") responded that it was remarkable "how much time U.S. political leaders spend defending a foreign nation even if it means attacking free speech rights of Americans", and tagged Omar for a comment, she replied with a quote from a hip hop song, "[It's All About the Benjamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_All_About_the_Benjamins "It's All About the Benjamins")", alluding to a slang term for U.S. [$100 bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_one-hundred-dollar_bill "United States one-hundred-dollar bill"). Both Democratic and Republican politicians accused her of using an antisemitic trope regarding Jews and money, although some Democratic politicians defended Omar's comment. Omar later said that she was referring to the influence of [pro-Israel lobbyists in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_lobby_in_the_United_States "Israel lobby in the United States"), especially [AIPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Israel_Public_Affairs_Committee "American Israel Public Affairs Committee").[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Ehrenreich-149)[[147]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Yglesias-150)

A number of Democratic leaders—including House Speaker [Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi "Nancy Pelosi"), Majority Leader [Steny Hoyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steny_Hoyer "Steny Hoyer"), and Majority Whip [Jim Clyburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Clyburn "Jim Clyburn")—condemned the tweet, which was interpreted as implying that money was fueling American politicians' support of Israel.[[148]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-DeBonis_&_Bade-151) The Democratic House leadership released a statement accusing Omar of "engaging in deeply offensive anti-Semitic tropes".[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Feb_11-152) The [Jewish Democratic Council of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Democratic_Council_of_America "Jewish Democratic Council of America") (JDCA) also denounced her statements.[[150]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Wilner_&_Beeri-153) Omar issued an apology the next day, saying, "I am grateful for Jewish allies and colleagues who are educating me on the painful history of anti-Semitic tropes", and adding, "I reaffirm the problematic role of lobbyists in our politics, whether it be AIPAC, the NRA or the fossil fuel industry."[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Stolberg_Feb_11-152) The [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League "Anti-Defamation League") accused her of promoting an "ugly conspiracy theory" about Jewish influence in politics.[[151]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Gambino-154) Journalist [Peter Beinart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Beinart "Peter Beinart"), after tweeting that the controversy was about "policing the American debate over Israel",[[152]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-155) thought Omar's statement inaccurate, wrong and irresponsible, but argued that her congressional critics were more "bigoted" on Israeli-Palestinian issues than Omar.[[153]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-156)

On February 27, 2019, Omar said of her critics: "I want to talk about the political influence in this country that says it is OK for people to push for allegiance to a foreign country." The statements were quickly criticized as allegedly drawing on [antisemitic tropes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitic_trope "Antisemitic trope"). House Foreign Affairs Committee chairman [Eliot Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliot_Engel "Eliot Engel") said it was "deeply offensive to call into question the loyalty of fellow American citizens" and asked Omar to retract her statement.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-:0-157) House Appropriations Committee chairwoman [Nita Lowey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nita_Lowey "Nita Lowey") also called for an apology and criticized the statements in a March 3 tweet, which led to an online exchange between the two. In response, Omar reaffirmed her position, insisting that she "should not be expected to have allegiance/pledge support to a foreign country in order to serve my country in Congress or serve on committee."[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Cummings-158)[[156]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-159) Omar said she was simply criticizing Israel, drawing a distinction between criticism of [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu") and being antisemitic.[[157]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-160)[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Cummings-158) Omar's spokesman, Jeremy Slevin, said Omar was speaking out about "the undue influence of lobbying groups for foreign interests."[[158]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-161)

Reaction among 2020 Democratic presidential candidates was mixed. Senators [Elizabeth Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Warren "Elizabeth Warren"), [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris"), and [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders") defended Omar.[[159]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-162) While Senator [Cory Booker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Booker "Cory Booker") found her comments "disturbing", he recognized that some of the attacks against her had "anti-Islamic sentiment". [Kirsten Gillibrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Gillibrand "Kirsten Gillibrand") said, "those with critical views of Israel should be able to express their views without employing anti-Semitic tropes about money or influence", but also criticized the Republican Party for censuring Omar while saying "little or nothing" when President Trump "defended white supremacists at Charlottesville." New York City Mayor [Bill de Blasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_de_Blasio "Bill de Blasio") called Omar's remarks "unacceptable".[[160]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Golshan-163)[[161]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-164) According to _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Guardian "The Guardian")_, election records archived by [OpenSecrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenSecrets "OpenSecrets") "suggest a correlation between pro-Israel lobby campaign contributions and Democratic presidential candidates' position on the controversy."[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Perkins-165) Some members of the [Congressional Black Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Black_Caucus "Congressional Black Caucus") believed Omar was unfairly targeted because she is a black Muslim, saying that "the Democratic leadership did not draft a resolution condemning Donald Trump or other white male Republicans over their antisemitic remarks."[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Perkins-165) The second round of remarks prompted the Democratic leadership to introduce a resolution condemning antisemitism that did not specifically refer to Omar. After objections by a number of congressional progressive Democrats, the resolution was amended to include Islamophobia, racism, and homophobia.[[163]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-166) On March 7, the House passed the amended resolution. Omar called the resolution "historic on many fronts" and said, "We are tremendously proud to be part of a body that has put forth a condemnation of all forms of bigotry including [anti-Semitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism"), [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism "Racism"), and [white supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacy "White supremacy")."[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-167) Some Minnesota Jewish and Muslim community leaders later expressed continuing concern about Omar's statements and indicated that the issue remained divisive in Omar's district.[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-168)

On March 7, 2019, the U.S. House of Representatives voted 407–23 to condemn "anti-Semitism, Islamophobia, racism and other forms of bigotry" in response to Omar's remarks concerning Israel.[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-169) On February 2, 2023, the Republican-led House of Representatives passed a resolution, on a party-line vote, to remove Omar from the [House Foreign Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Foreign_Affairs_Committee "House Foreign Affairs Committee") for what Speaker [Kevin McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_McCarthy "Kevin McCarthy") called "repeated antisemitic and anti-American remarks."[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Removal-170)[[168]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-171) Many prominent House Democrats stood by Omar.[[169]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-172) On July 18, 2023, she voted against a [congressional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress")[non-binding resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binding_resolution "Non-binding resolution") proposed by [August Pfluger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Pfluger "August Pfluger"), which states that "the State of Israel is not a [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism_as_settler_colonialism#Historiography "Zionism as settler colonialism") or [apartheid state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_apartheid "Israeli apartheid")", that Congress rejects "all forms of antisemitism and xenophobia", and that "the United States will always be a staunch partner and supporter of Israel".[[170]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-173) On October 16, 2023, Omar signed a resolution calling for a ceasefire in the [Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war"). She criticized the United States' support for [Israeli bombing of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_bombing_of_the_Gaza_Strip "Israeli bombing of the Gaza Strip").[[171]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-174) In May 2024, Omar voiced support for the [International Criminal Court investigation in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Palestine "International Criminal Court investigation in Palestine"), saying that the ICC "must be allowed to conduct its work independently and without interference."[[172]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-175)[[173]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-176) In August 2024, she criticized the Biden administration's [arms shipments to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_support_for_Israel_in_the_Gaza_war "United States support for Israel in the Gaza war"), saying that "if you really want a ceasefire, you just stop sending the weapons."[[174]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-177) In June 2026, Omar accused Israel of acting with "near-total impunity", saying it had repeatedly been able to "commit genocide and other atrocities" without meaningful accountability. In a post on X, she wrote that Israel was "exporting the Gaza playbook to Lebanon" and that the conflict in Lebanon was killing thousands and displacing more than a million people. Omar also called for an end to U.S. military aid to Israel.[[175]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-178)[[176]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-179)

#### Ban from entering Israel

In August 2019, Omar and Representative [Rashida Tlaib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Tlaib "Rashida Tlaib") were banned from entering Israel, a reversal from the July 2019 statement by [Israeli Ambassador to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Ambassador_to_the_United_States "Israeli Ambassador to the United States")[Ron Dermer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Dermer "Ron Dermer") that "any member of Congress" would be allowed in. Israeli [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Israel "Prime Minister of Israel")[Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu") attributed the ban to [Israeli law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendment_No._28_to_the_Entry_Into_Israel_Law "Amendment No. 28 to the Entry Into Israel Law") preventing the entry of people who call for a boycott of Israel (as Omar and Tlaib had done with their support for [BDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions "Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions")). Netanyahu also cited Omar and Tlaib listing their destination as Palestine instead of Israel, claiming he thus viewed their visit as an attempt to "hurt Israel and increase its unrest". Netanyahu also said that Omar and Tlaib did not plan on visiting or meeting with any Israeli officials from the government or the opposition, and additionally accused Miftah, the sponsor of Omar's trip, of having members who support terrorism against Israel (in 2016, Israel approved a visit by five U.S. representatives to Israel that Miftah co-sponsored, but that was before Israel enacted its anti-BDS law).[[177]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-miftah-180)[[178]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-181) Less than two hours before the ban, President Trump tweeted that Israel allowing the visit would "show great weakness" when Omar and Tlaib "hate Israel & all Jewish people".[[179]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-182)[[180]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-183)[[181]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-184)[[177]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-miftah-180) Omar said that Netanyahu had caved to Trump's demand and that "Trump's Muslim ban is what Israel is implementing". She responded to Netanyahu that she had intended to meet members of Israel's legislative [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset") and Israeli security officials. Both Democratic and Republican legislators criticized the ban and requested that Israel rescind it.[[182]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-185)[[183]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-186) AIPAC released a statement saying that it disagreed with Israel's move and that Omar and Tlaib should have been allowed to "experience Israel firsthand", while the head of the [American Jewish Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_Committee "American Jewish Committee") put out a statement agreeing with AIPAC on the matter.[[184]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-187) U.S. Representative [Max Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Rose "Max Rose") also criticized the move to ban Omar, saying that Omar and Tlaib did not speak for the Democratic Party.[[185]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-188)

### LGBT rights

In March 2019, Omar addressed a rally in support of a Minnesota bill that would ban [gay conversion therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_therapy "Conversion therapy") in the state. She co-sponsored a similar bill when she was a member of the Minnesota House.[[186]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-AP_2019-189) In May 2019, Omar introduced legislation that would sanction [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei") over a recently introduced law that would make homosexual sex and adultery punishable by death.[[187]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-190) In June 2019, she participated in [Twin Cities Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_Pride "Twin Cities Pride") in [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota").[[188]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-191) In August 2019, Omar wrote on Twitter in support of the [Palestinian LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_the_State_of_Palestine "LGBTQ rights in the State of Palestine") group [Al Qaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qaws "Al Qaws") after the [Palestinian Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Authority "Palestinian Authority") banned Al Qaws's activities in the [West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank "West Bank").[[189]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-192)[[190]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-193)

### Military policy

Omar has been critical of [U.S. foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_United_States "Foreign policy of the United States"), and has called for reduced [funding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_the_United_States "Military budget of the United States") for "perpetual war and military aggression",[[191]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Witt-194) saying, "knowing my tax dollars pay for bombs killing children in [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen "Yemen") makes my heart break," with "everyone in Washington saying we don't have enough money in the budget for universal health care, we don't have enough money in the budget to guarantee college education for everyone."[[191]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Witt-194) Omar has criticized the U.S. government's [drone assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposition_Matrix "Disposition Matrix") program, citing the Obama administration's policy of "droning of countries around the world".[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Le_Miere-131)[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Pesce-132) She has said, "we don't need nearly [800 military bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_military_bases "List of United States military bases") outside the United States to keep our country safe."[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Values-121)

In 2019, Omar signed a letter led by Representative [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna "Ro Khanna") and Senator [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul "Rand Paul") to President Trump asserting that it is "long past time to rein in the use of force that goes beyond congressional authorization" and that they hoped this would "serve as a model for ending hostilities in the future—in particular, as you and your administration seek a political solution to our involvement in Afghanistan."[[192]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-195)[[193]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-196)

In May 2020, Omar signed a letter backed by AIPAC calling for the continuation of the UN embargo against Iran,[[194]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-197) with her office noting that it was a "narrow ask that we couldn't find anything wrong with." Her office said that she has opposed human rights abuse "for a long time" and that signing onto it should be not be seen as a sign she supports the Trump administration's policy on Iran.[[195]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-198)

On July 6, 2023, President Biden authorized the provision of [cluster munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_munition "Cluster munition") to [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine") in support of a [Ukrainian counter-offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Ukrainian_counteroffensive "2023 Ukrainian counteroffensive") against Russian forces in Russian-[occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts "Russian annexation of Donetsk, Kherson, Luhansk and Zaporizhzhia oblasts") southeastern Ukraine.[[196]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-199) Omar opposed the decision, saying, "We can support the people of Ukraine in their freedom struggle while also opposing violations of international law."[[197]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-200)

On June 4, 2026, Omar was the only Democrat to vote against the H.R. 2913 Ukraine Support Act, which authorized funding for military aid to Ukraine and [sanctions on Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "International sanctions during the Russian invasion of Ukraine").[[198]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-hill_ukraine_aid-201)

### Minneapolis Police Department

In June 2020, the "[defund the police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defund_the_police "Defund the police")" slogan gained widespread popularity following the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd "Murder of George Floyd"). [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter "Black Lives Matter") and other activists used the phrase to call for police budget reductions and a plan to delegate certain police responsibilities to other organizations. Reacting to the murder of Floyd, the majority of the [Minneapolis City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_City_Council "Minneapolis City Council") voted to dismantle the city's police department. In a statement, the Minneapolis mayor said they planned to work to address "systemic racism in police culture".[[201]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-204)[[202]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-kesslen-205) Following the murder of Floyd, Omar supported the [police abolition movement in Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_abolition_movement_in_Minneapolis "Police abolition movement in Minneapolis") that sought to dismantle the [Minneapolis Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_Police_Department "Minneapolis Police Department"), saying that the department had "proven themselves beyond reform."[[202]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-kesslen-205) Omar hoped to see a new police department that would be modeled after the [Camden County Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden_County_Police_Department "Camden County Police Department") in New Jersey.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

### TikTok

Omar has opposed a [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") ban.[[203]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-206) In March 2024, she raised [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution") concerns in opposing [a bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protecting_Americans_from_Foreign_Adversary_Controlled_Applications_Act "Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act") that would ban the app if its Chinese owner did not sell, saying: "We should create actual standards & regulations around privacy violations across social media companies—not target platforms we don't like."[[204]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-207)

On March 12, Omar was asked about TikTok-related national security concerns, such as China using the app to ramp up divisions in the U.S., and replied, "We had an intel briefing, and none of the information that was provided to us really was persuasive in the fact that there is anything to be really concerned", adding, "for the first time in our nation's history, Americans have access to real images [through TikTok] of the horrors that are experienced by Palestinians daily."[[205]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-208)[[206]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-209)

### Venezuela crisis

In January 2019, amid the [Venezuelan presidential crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_presidential_crisis "Venezuelan presidential crisis"), Omar joined Democrats [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna "Ro Khanna") and [Tulsi Gabbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsi_Gabbard "Tulsi Gabbard") in denouncing the Trump administration's decision to recognize [Juan Guaidó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Guaid%C3%B3 "Juan Guaidó"), the president of the [Venezuelan National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_National_Assembly "Venezuelan National Assembly"), as Venezuela's interim president.[[207]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Daugherty-210) She described Trump's action as a "U.S. backed coup" and said that the U.S. should not "hand pick" foreign leaders[[208]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-211) and should support "Mexico, Uruguay & the Vatican's efforts to facilitate a peaceful dialogue."[[207]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Daugherty-210) In response to criticisms of her comments, Omar wrote that "No one is defending Maduro" and that opposing US intervention is not the equivalent of supporting the existing leadership of a country.[[209]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-212)

In February 2019, Omar questioned [Elliott Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Abrams "Elliott Abrams"), Trump's envoy to Venezuela, noting that he had pleaded guilty to two counts of withholding information from Congress regarding the [Iran–Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair "Iran–Contra affair").[[210]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-213) Omar also quoted Abrams's claim that U.S. involvement in the [Salvadoran civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_civil_war "Salvadoran civil war") had been a "fabulous achievement" and contrasted it with reports of the [El Mozote massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mozote_massacre "El Mozote massacre").[[211]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-214)[[212]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-Hansler-215)

In May 2019, Omar said in an interview on _[Democracy Now!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy\_Now! "Democracy Now!")_ that she believed [U.S. foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_United_States "Foreign policy of the United States") and [economic sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_during_the_Venezuelan_crisis "Sanctions during the Venezuelan crisis") are aimed at regime change and have contributed to the "devastation in Venezuela".[[213]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-216)

## Death threats and harassment

### DFL caucus attack

On February 4, 2014, Omar was attacked and injured by multiple attendees during a [DFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party "Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party") caucus for [Minnesota's House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives "Minnesota House of Representatives") District 60B.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-NordBierschbach-41) She was organizing the event and was a policy aide to Minneapolis City Councilman [Andrew Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Johnson_(Minnesota_politician) "Andrew Johnson (Minnesota politician)") at the time. She sustained a concussion and was sent to the hospital.[[214]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-217)

### Death threats

In February 2019, the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") arrested [United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard "United States Coast Guard") Lieutenant Christopher Paul Hasson, who was allegedly plotting to assassinate various journalists and political figures in the United States, including Omar. According to prosecutors, Hasson is a self-described "long time White Nationalist" and former [skinhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_power_skinhead "White power skinhead") who wanted to use violence to "establish a white homeland." Prosecutors also alleged that Hasson was in contact with an American neo-Nazi leader, stockpiled weapons, and compiled a hit list.[[215]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-218)

On April 7, 2019, Patrick Carlineo Jr., was arrested for threatening to assault and murder Omar in a phone call to her office. He reportedly told investigators that he did not want Muslims in the government.[[216]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-219)[[217]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-220) In May 2019, Carlineo was released from custody and placed on [house arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_arrest "House arrest").[[218]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-221) He pleaded guilty to the offense on November 19.[[219]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-guardian-28nov2019-222) Omar asked the court to be lenient with him.[[220]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-223)

In April 2019, Omar said that she had received more death threats after Trump made comments about her and 9/11, "many directly referencing or replying to the president's video".[[221]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-McCarthy-224) In August 2019, she published an anonymous threat she had received of being shot at the [Minnesota State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Fair "Minnesota State Fair"), saying that such threats were why she now had security protection.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-BBCThreat-8) In September 2019, she asserted Trump was putting her life in danger by retweeting a tweet falsely claiming she had "partied on the anniversary of 9/11".[[222]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-225)

Two Republican candidates for congressional office have called for Omar's execution.[[223]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-226) In November 2019, Danielle Stella, Omar's Republican opponent for Congress, was banned from Twitter for suggesting that Omar be hanged for treason if found guilty of passing information to Iran.[[219]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-guardian-28nov2019-222) In December 2019, George Buck, another Republican running for Congress, also suggested that Omar be hanged for treason. In response, Buck was removed from the [National Republican Congressional Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Republican_Congressional_Committee "National Republican Congressional Committee")'s Young Guns program.[[224]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-guardian-4dec2019-227) Neither candidate won their primary election.[[225]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-MNsosr-228)[[226]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-229)

In January 2026, 60-year-old Adam Lee Osborn of [Wichita, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas "Wichita, Kansas"), was arrested following threats he had made against Omar and [New York City Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City "Mayor of New York City")[Zohran Mamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohran_Mamdani "Zohran Mamdani") on social media. In [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") posts, Osborn had threatened to kill Omar, writing: "If illegals can come here unimpeded, I can kill them unimpeded. How the fuck do I end up a minority in my own country? This shit comes to an end, NOW!" According to an affidavit, during his interrogation, Osborn continued to threaten Omar and Mamdani and said he "wouldn't mind if they were killed".[[227]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-230)

### "Go back" to their countries Trump tweet

On July 14, 2019, Trump tweeted that [The Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squad_(U.S._Congress) "Squad (U.S. Congress)")—a group that consists of Omar and three other young congresswomen of color, most of whom were born and raised in the U.S.—should ["go back" to the "places from which they came"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_back_to_where_you_came_from "Go back to where you came from").[[228]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-APgoback-231)[[229]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-NYTgoback-232)[[230]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-BBCgoback-233) In response, Omar said Trump was "stoking [white nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism "White nationalism")" because he was "angry that people like us are serving in Congress and fighting against your hate-filled agenda."[[230]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-BBCgoback-233) Two days later, the House of Representatives voted 240–187 to condemn Trump's "racist comments".[[231]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-234) On July 17, it was reported that the [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Equal_Employment_Opportunity_Commission "U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission") lists the phrase "Go back to where you came from" as an example of "harassment based on national origin".[[232]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-235)

At a July 17 campaign rally in North Carolina, Trump made additional comments about The Squad: "They never have anything good to say. That's why I say, 'Hey if you don't like it, let 'em leave, let 'em leave'", and "I think in some cases they hate our country".[[233]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-236) He made a series of false and misleading claims about Omar, including allegations that she had praised [al-Qaeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda "Al-Qaeda") and "smeared" American soldiers who had fought in the [Battle of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993) "Battle of Mogadishu (1993)") by bringing up the numerous Somali civilian casualties.[[234]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-237)[[235]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-238)[[236]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-239) The crowd reacted by chanting, "Send her back, Send her back."[[237]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-240)[[238]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-241) Trump later called the crowd "incredible people, incredible patriots" and accused Omar of racism and antisemitism.[[239]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-242) On July 19, he falsely claimed that Omar and the rest of The Squad had used the term "evil Jews".[[240]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar#cite_note-243)
