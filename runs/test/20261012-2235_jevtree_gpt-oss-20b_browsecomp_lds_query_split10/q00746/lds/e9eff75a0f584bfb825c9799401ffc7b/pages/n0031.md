Where your donation goes

 Accountability and transparency are core values at the Wikimedia Foundation. We manage funds and resources to ensure that every contribution supports our mission. We have earned the Platinum Seal of Transparency from Candid (formerly GuideStar), and Charity Navigator awarded us its highest rating. You can [read our most recent annual report](https://wikimediafoundation.org/annualreports/current/) for more information about our financial health. 

45% Investment in Technology 
Nearly half of our budget goes toward supporting the technology that powers Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects. We are constantly working to enhance the user experience for both contributors and readers, improve site security, and ensure reliable access to our websites globally. This infrastructure and product support sustain one of the top ten most visited websites in the world, all at a fraction of the cost of popular for-profit websites.

32% Support for Volunteers 
The global reach of Wikimedia projects is made possible by the hard work of volunteers from across the globe. We provide grants, legal support, and other resources to help build vibrant volunteer communities. Additionally, we promote community engagement through outreach initiatives and advocate for the growth and protection of free knowledge.

12% Allocation to Fundraising Efforts 
Donor support is crucial to sustaining Wikipedia and our other free knowledge endeavors. Our team is committed to efficient and effective fundraising throughout the year, ensuring that every contribution helps advance our mission.

11% General and Administrative Expenses 
Operational costs are essential for the smooth management and governance of the Wikimedia Foundation. These expenses help us recruit top talent and support staff around the world, empowering them to carry out the mission of the Wikimedia Foundation.

**Phyllis Lorberblatt Kahn** (born March 23, 1937) is an American politician and former member of the [Minnesota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_House_of_Representatives "Minnesota House of Representatives"). A member of the [Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party "Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party") (DFL), she represented portions of the city of [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis,_Minnesota "Minneapolis, Minnesota") for 44 years.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-leg.state.mn.us-1) On August 9, 2016, [Ilhan Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilhan_Omar "Ilhan Omar") defeated Kahn in the DFL primary for District 60B.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-2)

## Education and career

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Phyllis_Kahn&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Education and career")]

Kahn received a B.A. in physics from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") and a Ph.D. in biophysics from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). She later received an [M.P.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Public_Administration "Master of Public Administration") from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). She is a retired professor of [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics").

## Political career

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Phyllis_Kahn&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Political career")]

She was the chief author and advocate of the 1975 Minnesota Clean Indoor Air Act, one of the first laws enacted anywhere in the world banning the smoking of tobacco in public places. This law has been used as a template for later legislation to protect the public from "secondhand smoke," and was updated and expanded in 2005.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-3) In 1989, Kahn proposed reducing the [voting age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_age "Voting age") in Minnesota to 12. She said "history has shown us that when a segment of society is denied the right to vote, all the rights of that segment of society are then inferior."

Kahn has also repeatedly advocated for repeal of so-called "[Blue Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_laws_in_the_United_States#Minnesota "Blue laws in the United States")," which forbid liquor stores from operating on Sundays and some holidays.

In 2003 she introduced a bill to repeal the ban on first-cousin marriage in Minnesota, but it died in committee. Republican Minority Leader [Marty Seifert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Seifert "Marty Seifert") criticized the bill in response, saying it would "turn us into a cold Arkansas."[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-4) According to the University of Minnesota's _The Wake_, Kahn was aware the bill had little chance of passing but introduced it anyway to draw attention to the issue. She reportedly got the idea after learning that [cousin marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin_marriage "Cousin marriage") is an acceptable form of marriage among some cultural groups that have a strong presence in Minnesota, namely the [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people "Hmong people") and [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people "Somali people").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-5)

She is a longtime advocate for [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education "Education"), [health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care "Health care") and [medical technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_technology "Medical technology"), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture") and the [natural environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment"), and [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights "Human rights"), particularly for [senior citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_citizens "Senior citizens") and [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women "Women").

### Minnesota House of Representatives

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Phyllis_Kahn&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Minnesota House of Representatives")]

Kahn was first elected to represent her area of [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") in 1972 and served continuously from then until January 2017.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-6) In the August 2016 DFL primary election for District 60B, Kahn finished third of three candidates, so her name was not on the November 2016 ballot.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-8)

## Personal life

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Phyllis_Kahn&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Personal life")]

Kahn is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-10)

Kahn was married to [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota") mathematics professor Donald Kahn for 58 years, until his death in January 2015. Her father-in-law was investor [Irving Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kahn "Irving Kahn"), who lived to the age of 109.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-12)

In 2004, Kahn was charged with theft for removal of [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Minnesota "Republican Party of Minnesota") campaign literature from doorsteps of several houses.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-13) She pleaded guilty and paid a $200 fine.

Kahn lives on [Nicollet Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicollet_Island "Nicollet Island")[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-15) and was a leading opponent of efforts by [DeLaSalle High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeLaSalle_High_School_(Minneapolis) "DeLaSalle High School (Minneapolis)") to construct an athletic field on its campus on Nicollet Island. Ultimately, the school was allowed to build the field.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-16)

Kahn once ran a marathon in 3 hours and 17 minutes, and signed up for the 2002 [Twin Cities Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_Marathon "Twin Cities Marathon").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Kahn#cite_note-17)
