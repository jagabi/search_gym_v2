**Helen Adams Keller** (June 27, 1880– June 1, 1968) was an American author, [disability rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability_rights "Disability rights") advocate, political activist and lecturer. Born in West [Tuscumbia, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscumbia,_Alabama "Tuscumbia, Alabama"), she [lost her sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_impairment "Visual impairment") and [her hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deafness "Deafness") after a bout of illness when she was 19 months old. She then communicated primarily using [home signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_sign "Home sign") until the age of seven, when she met her first teacher and life-long companion [Anne Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Sullivan "Anne Sullivan"). Sullivan taught Keller language, including reading and writing. After an education at both specialist and mainstream schools, Keller attended [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College "Radcliffe College") of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") and became the first [deafblind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deafblind "Deafblind") person in the United States to earn a college diploma.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-1)

Keller was also a prolific author, writing 14 books and hundreds of speeches and essays on topics ranging from animals to [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-2) Keller campaigned for those with [disabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability "Disability") and for [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage "Women's suffrage"), [labor rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_rights "Labor rights"), and [world peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace "World peace"). In 1909, she joined the [Socialist Party of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_America "Socialist Party of America") (SPA). She was a founding member of the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union "American Civil Liberties Union") (ACLU).[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-3)

Keller's autobiography, _[The Story of My Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Story\_of\_My\_Life\_(biography) "The Story of My Life (biography)")_ (1903), publicized her education and life with Sullivan. The playwright [William Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gibson_(playwright) "William Gibson (playwright)") wrote a theatrical adaptation, _[The Miracle Worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Miracle\_Worker "The Miracle Worker")_, in 1959, which he adapted as [a film under the same title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(1962_film) "The Miracle Worker (1962 film)") in 1962. Her birthplace has been designated and preserved as a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark"). Since 1954, it has been operated as a house museum[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-birthplace-4) and sponsors an annual "[Helen Keller Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller_Day "Helen Keller Day")".[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-5)

## Early childhood and illness

[![Image 7](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Helen_Keller_Birthplace_House_in_Tuscumbia%2C_Alabama.jpg/330px-Helen_Keller_Birthplace_House_in_Tuscumbia%2C_Alabama.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_Keller_Birthplace_House_in_Tuscumbia,_Alabama.jpg)

Keller's birthplace in [Tuscumbia, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscumbia,_Alabama "Tuscumbia, Alabama")

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Helen_Keller_with_Anne_Sullivan_in_July_1888.jpg/250px-Helen_Keller_with_Anne_Sullivan_in_July_1888.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_Keller_with_Anne_Sullivan_in_July_1888.jpg)

Keller (left) with [Anne Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Sullivan_Macy "Anne Sullivan Macy") vacationing on [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod "Cape Cod") in July 1888

Keller was born Helen Adams Keller on June 27, 1880, in Tuscumbia, Alabama, the daughter of [Arthur Henley Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_H._Keller "Arthur H. Keller") (1836–1896),[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-6) and Catherine Everett (née Adams) Keller (1856–1921), known as "Kate".[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Kate-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-perkinsFAQ-8) The Keller family lived on a [homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_(building) "Homestead (building)"), [Ivy Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Green "Ivy Green"),[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-birthplace-4) which her paternal grandfather had built decades earlier.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen2007-9) She had four siblings: two full siblings, Mildred Campbell (née Keller) Tyson and Phillip Brooks Keller; and two older half-brothers from her father's first marriage, James McDonald Keller and William Simpson Keller.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Ask1006-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-11)

Keller's father worked for many years as an editor of the Tuscumbia _North Alabamian_. He had served as a captain in the [Confederate Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_Army "Confederate Army").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-perkinsFAQ-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen2007-9) The family was part of the [slaveholding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States "Slavery in the United States") elite before [the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"), but lost status later.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen2007-9) Her mother was the daughter of [Charles W. Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_W._Adams_(Confederate_general) "Charles W. Adams (Confederate general)"), a Confederate general.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Eicher587-12) Keller's paternal lineage was traced to Casper Keller, a native of Switzerland.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-StoryofmyLife-13)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Ask1105-14) One of Helen's Swiss ancestors was the first teacher for the deaf in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich "Zürich"). Keller reflected on this fact in her first autobiography, asserting that "there is no king who has not had a slave among his ancestors, and no slave who has not had a king among his".[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-StoryofmyLife-13)

At 19 months old, Keller contracted an unknown illness described by doctors as "an acute congestion of the stomach and the brain".[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-askKellerIllness-15) Contemporary doctors believe it may have been [meningitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningitis "Meningitis"), caused by the bacterium _[Neisseria meningitidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neisseria\_meningitidis "Neisseria meningitidis")_ (meningococcus),[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-16) by [rubella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubella "Rubella"), [scarlet fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_fever "Scarlet fever"),[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-perkinsFAQ-8)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-17) or by _[Haemophilus influenzae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemophilus\_influenzae "Haemophilus influenzae")_, which can cause the same symptoms but is less likely because of its 97% juvenile mortality rate at that time.

Keller was able to recover from her illness, but was left permanently blind and deaf, as she recalled in her autobiography, "at sea in a dense fog".[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Helen_Keller's_Moment-18) At that time, Keller was able to communicate somewhat with Martha Washington, who was two years older and the daughter of the family cook, and understood the girl's signs;[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-mylife-19):11 by the age of seven, Keller had more than 60 [home signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_sign "Home sign") to communicate with her family, and could distinguish people by the vibration of their footsteps.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-20) In 1886, Keller's mother, inspired by an account in [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens "Charles Dickens")' _[American Notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American\_Notes "American Notes")_ of the successful education of [Laura Bridgman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bridgman "Laura Bridgman"), a deaf and blind woman, dispatched the young Keller and her father to consult physician J. Julian Chisholm, an eye, ear, nose and throat specialist in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"), for advice.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Worthington-21)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen2007-9) Chisholm referred the Kellers to [Alexander Graham Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Graham_Bell "Alexander Graham Bell"), who was working with deaf children at the time. Bell advised them to contact the [Perkins Institute for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perkins_Institute_for_the_Blind "Perkins Institute for the Blind"), the school where Bridgman had been educated. It was then located in [South Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Boston,_Boston,_Massachusetts "South Boston, Boston, Massachusetts"). Michael Anagnos, the school's director, asked [Anne Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Sullivan_Macy "Anne Sullivan Macy"), a 20-year-old alumna of the school who was visually impaired, to become Keller's instructor. It was the beginning of a nearly 50-year-long relationship Sullivan developed with Keller as her [governess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governess "Governess") and later her [companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady's_companion "Lady's companion").[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-mylife-19)

Sullivan arrived at Keller's house on March 5, 1887, a day Keller would forever remember as "my soul's birthday".[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Helen_Keller's_Moment-18) Sullivan immediately began to teach Helen to communicate by spelling words into her hand, beginning with "d-o-l-l" for the doll that she had brought Keller as a present. Keller initially struggled with lessons since she could not comprehend that every object had a word identifying it. When Sullivan was trying to teach Keller the word for "mug", Keller became so frustrated she broke the mug.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Wilkie-22) Keller remembered how she soon began imitating Sullivan's hand gestures: "I did not know that I was spelling a word or even that words existed. I was simply making my fingers go in monkey-like imitation."[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-23)

The next month, Keller made a breakthrough, when she realized that the motions her teacher was making on the palm of her hand, while running cool water over her other hand, symbolized the idea of "water". Writing in her autobiography, _The Story of My Life_, Keller recalled the moment:

> I stood still, my whole attention fixed upon the motions of her fingers. Suddenly I felt a misty consciousness as of something forgotten—a thrill of returning thought; and somehow the mystery of language was revealed to me. I knew then that w-a-t-e-r meant the wonderful cool something that was flowing over my hand. The living word awakened my soul, gave it light, hope, set it free![[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Helen_Keller's_Moment-18)

Keller quickly demanded that Sullivan sign the names of all the other familiar objects in her world.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-24)

## Formal education

[![Image 9](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Helen_A._Keller_1904.jpg/250px-Helen_A._Keller_1904.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_A._Keller_1904.jpg)

Keller in the [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College "Radcliffe College") yearbook, 1904

In May 1888, Keller started attending the Perkins Institute for the Blind. In 1893, Keller, along with Sullivan, attended William Wade House and Finishing School.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-25) In 1894, Keller and Sullivan moved to New York to attend the [Wright-Humason School for the Deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright-Humason_School_for_the_Deaf "Wright-Humason School for the Deaf"), and to learn from [Sarah Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Fuller_(educator) "Sarah Fuller (educator)") at the [Horace Mann School for the Deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Mann_School_for_the_Deaf "Horace Mann School for the Deaf"). In 1896, they returned to Massachusetts, and Keller entered [The Cambridge School for Young Ladies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cambridge_School_of_Weston "The Cambridge School of Weston") before gaining admittance, in 1900, to [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College "Radcliffe College") of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"),[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-radcliff-26) where she lived in Briggs Hall, [South House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabot_House "Cabot House"). Her admirer, [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain "Mark Twain"), had introduced her to [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil "Standard Oil") magnate [Henry Huttleston Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_H._Rogers "Henry H. Rogers"), who, with his wife Abbie, paid for her education. In 1904, at the age of 24, Keller graduated from Radcliffe as a member of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa"),[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-27) becoming the first [deafblind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deafblind "Deafblind") person to earn a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-28) She maintained a correspondence with the Austrian [pedagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogue "Pedagogue") and philosopher [Wilhelm Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Jerusalem "Wilhelm Jerusalem"), who was one of the first to discover her literary talent.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-29)

Determined to communicate with others as conventionally as possible, Keller learned to speak and spent much of her life giving speeches and lectures on aspects of her life. She learned to "hear" people's speech using the [Tadoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadoma "Tadoma") method, which means using her fingers to feel the lips and throat of the speaker.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-30) She became proficient at using [braille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braille "Braille"),[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-31) and also used [fingerspelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerspelling "Fingerspelling") to communicate.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-32) Shortly before World War I, with the assistance of the [Zoellner Quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoellner_Quartet "Zoellner Quartet"), she determined that by placing her fingertips on a resonant tabletop she could experience music played close by.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Spectrum2-33)

## Companions

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/PSM_V63_D081_Helen_keller_and_miss_sullivan.png/250px-PSM_V63_D081_Helen_keller_and_miss_sullivan.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PSM_V63_D081_Helen_keller_and_miss_sullivan.png)

Helen Keller in 1899 with lifelong companion and teacher Anne Sullivan. Photo taken by [Alexander Graham Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Graham_Bell "Alexander Graham Bell") at his School of Vocal Physiology and Mechanics of Speech.

[Anne Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Sullivan "Anne Sullivan") stayed as a companion to Keller long after she taught her. Sullivan married John Macy in 1905, and her health started failing around 1914. Polly Thomson (February 20, 1885[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Herrmann1999-34) – March 21, 1960) was hired to keep house. She was a young woman from Scotland who had no experience with deaf or blind people. She progressed to working as a secretary as well, and eventually became a constant companion to Keller.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-grace-35) Sullivan's teaching methods, based on tactile fingerspelling, objects and direct experience, became an influential example in the history of education for people with [deafblindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deafblindness "Deafblindness").

Keller moved to [Forest Hills, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Hills,_Queens "Forest Hills, Queens"), together with Sullivan and Macy, and used the house as a base for her efforts on behalf of the [American Foundation for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Foundation_for_the_Blind "American Foundation for the Blind").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-rnib-36) While in her 30s, Keller had a love affair and became secretly engaged; she also defied her teacher and family by attempting an [elopement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elopement "Elopement") with the man she loved,[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-37) Peter Fagan, who was known as "the fingerspelling socialist",[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen2007-9) was a young _[Boston Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston\_Herald "Boston Herald")_ reporter sent to Keller's home to act as her private secretary when Sullivan fell ill. At the time, her father had died and Sullivan was recovering in [Lake Placid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Placid,_New_York "Lake Placid, New York") and [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"). Keller had moved with her mother to [Montgomery, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery,_Alabama "Montgomery, Alabama").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen2007-9)

Sullivan died in 1936, with Keller holding her hand,[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-holding_hand-38) after falling into a coma as a result of [coronary thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_thrombosis "Coronary thrombosis").[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen-39):266 Keller and Thomson moved to [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut "Connecticut"). They traveled worldwide and raised funds for the blind. Thomson had a stroke in 1957 from which she never fully recovered and died in 1960. Winnie Corbally, a nurse originally hired to care for Thomson in 1957, stayed on after Thomson's death and was Keller's companion for the rest of her life.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-rnib-36)

## Akita dogs

[![Image 11](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Helen_Keller_with_her_Akita_Kenzan-Go.jpg/250px-Helen_Keller_with_her_Akita_Kenzan-Go.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_Keller_with_her_Akita_Kenzan-Go.jpg)

Helen Keller with her second Akita _Kenzan-Go_

Keller was very fond of [Akita dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akita_dog "Akita dog"). She visited [Akita Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akita_Prefecture "Akita Prefecture") in June 1937 and asked about [Hachikō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachik%C5%8D "Hachikō"), a loyal dog who searched for his deceased master for about 10 years. She began keeping Akitas as pets and introduced them to America. In 1948, she touched the [re-made statue of Hachikō at Shibuya Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Hachik%C5%8D "Statue of Hachikō").[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-keller-40) The first Akita given to her was _Kamikaze-Go_, who was the first Akita to travel overseas from Japan and the first Akita in America. He died of an infection two months after arriving in America and Keller was given _Kamikaze-Go'_ s brother, _Kenzan-Go_.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-41)[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-42)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Ogasawara-43) Keller nicknamed the dog Go-Go and they were great companions from day one. Go-Go even spent his first night at Keller's home sleeping at the foot of her bed.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-44)

## Career, writing and political activities

[![Image 12](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Helen_Keller_%28circa_1904%29.jpg/250px-Helen_Keller_%28circa_1904%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_Keller_(circa_1904).jpg)

Helen Keller portrait, 1904. Due to a protruding left eye, Keller was usually photographed in profile until she had her eyes replaced, c.1911, with [glass replicas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocular_prosthesis "Ocular prosthesis") for "medical and cosmetic reasons".[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Herrmann-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-46)

> The few own the many because they possess the means of livelihood of all... The country is governed for the richest, for the corporations, the bankers, the land speculators, and for the exploiters of labor. The majority of mankind are working people. So long as their fair demands—the ownership and control of their livelihoods—are set at naught, we can have neither men's rights nor women's rights. The majority of mankind is ground down by industrial oppression in order that the small remnant may live in ease.

—Helen Keller, 1911[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Rebel_Lives-47)

On January 22, 1916, Keller and Sullivan traveled to the small town of [Menomonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menomonie,_Wisconsin "Menomonie, Wisconsin") in western [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin "Wisconsin") to deliver a lecture at the [Mabel Tainter Memorial Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabel_Tainter_Memorial_Building "Mabel Tainter Memorial Building"). Details of her talk were provided in the weekly _Dunn County News_ on January 22, 1916:

> A message of optimism, of hope, of good cheer, and of loving service was brought to Menomonie Saturday—a message that will linger long with those fortunate enough to have received it. This message came with the visit of Helen Keller and her teacher, Mrs. John Macy, and both had a hand in imparting it Saturday evening to a splendid audience that filled The Memorial. The wonderful girl who has so brilliantly triumphed over the triple afflictions of blindness, dumbness and deafness, gave a talk with her own lips on "Happiness", and it will be remembered always as a piece of inspired teaching by those who heard it.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Koser-48)

Keller became a world-famous speaker and author. She was an [advocate for people with disabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_disability_rights_activists "List of disability rights activists"), amid numerous other causes. She traveled to twenty-five different countries giving motivational speeches about deaf people's conditions.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-49) She was a [suffragist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage "Women's suffrage"), [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist "Pacifist"), [Christian socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_socialist "Christian socialist"), [birth control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_control "Birth control") supporter, and opponent of [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson "Woodrow Wilson"). In 1915, she and George A. Kessler founded the [Helen Keller International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller_International "Helen Keller International") (HKI) organization. This organization is devoted to research in vision, health, and nutrition. In 1916, she sent money to the [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP "NAACP"), as she was ashamed of the Southern un-Christian treatment of "[colored people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans")".[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen2007-9)

In 1920, Keller helped to found the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union "American Civil Liberties Union") (ACLU). She traveled to over 40 countries with Sullivan, making several trips to Japan and becoming a favorite of the Japanese people. Keller met every U.S. president from [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland "Grover Cleveland") to [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson") and was friends with many famous figures, including [Alexander Graham Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Graham_Bell "Alexander Graham Bell"), [Charlie Chaplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin "Charlie Chaplin"), and [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain "Mark Twain"). Keller and Twain were both considered [political radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_radical "Political radical") allied with [leftist politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leftist_politics "Leftist politics").[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-loewen-50)

Keller, who believed that the poor were "ground down by industrial oppression",[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Rebel_Lives-47) wanted children born into poor families to have the same opportunities to succeed that she had enjoyed. She wrote, "I owed my success partly to the advantages of my birth and environment. I have learned that the power to rise is not within the reach of everyone."[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Hubbard-51)

In 1909, Keller became a member of the [Socialist Party of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_America "Socialist Party of America") (SPA); she actively campaigned and wrote in support of the working class from 1909 to 1921. Many of her speeches and writings were about women's right to vote and the effects of war; in addition, she supported causes that opposed [military intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interventionism_(politics) "Interventionism (politics)").[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-52) She had speech therapy to have her voice understood better by the public. When the Rockefeller-owned press refused to print her articles, she protested until her work was finally published.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen-39)

Keller supported the SPA candidate [Eugene V. Debs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_V._Debs "Eugene V. Debs") in each of his campaigns for the presidency. Before reading _[Progress and Poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress\_and\_Poverty "Progress and Poverty")_ by [Henry George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_George "Henry George"), she was already a [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist "Socialist") who believed that [Georgism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgism "Georgism") was a good step in the right direction.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-53) She later wrote of finding "in Henry George's philosophy a rare beauty and power of inspiration, and a splendid faith in the essential nobility of human nature".[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-54) Keller stated that newspaper columnists who had praised her courage and intelligence before she expressed her socialist views now called attention to her disabilities. The editor of the _[Brooklyn Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn\_Eagle "Brooklyn Eagle")_ wrote that her "mistakes sprung out of the manifest limitations of her development". Keller responded to that editor, referring to having met him before he knew of her political views:

> At that time the compliments he paid me were so generous that I blush to remember them. But now that I have come out for socialism he reminds me and the public that I am blind and deaf and especially liable to error. I must have shrunk in intelligence during the years since I met him.... Oh, ridiculous _Brooklyn Eagle_! Socially blind and deaf, it defends an intolerable system, a system that is the cause of much of the physical blindness and deafness which we are trying to prevent.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-55)

In 1912, Keller joined the [Industrial Workers of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Workers_of_the_World "Industrial Workers of the World") (the IWW, known as the Wobblies),[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-loewen-50) saying that [parliamentary socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_socialism "Parliamentary socialism") was "sinking in the political bog". She wrote for the IWW between 1916 and 1918. In _Why I Became an IWW_, Keller explained that her motivation for activism came in part from her concern about blindness and other disabilities:[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Bindley_1916-56)

> I was appointed on a commission to investigate the conditions of the blind. For the first time I, who had thought blindness a misfortune beyond human control, found that too much of it was traceable to wrong industrial conditions, often caused by the selfishness and greed of employers. And the social evil contributed its share. I found that poverty drove women to a life of shame that ended in blindness.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Bindley_1916-56)

The last sentence refers to prostitution and [syphilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syphilis "Syphilis"), the former a "life of shame" that women used to support themselves, which contributed to their contracting syphilis. Untreated, it was a leading cause of blindness. In the same interview, Keller also cited the [1912 strike of textile workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_Lawrence_Textile_Strike "1912 Lawrence Textile Strike") in [Lawrence, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Massachusetts "Lawrence, Massachusetts"), for instigating her support of socialism.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Bindley_1916-56) As a result of her advocacy, she was placed on the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI")'s watchlist;[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-57) the FBI wrote on July 1, 1953, that although they have not "conducted an investigation with regard to Helen Adams Keller", their files of Keller "reflect the following pertinent information concerning this individual".[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-58)

Keller supported [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics "Eugenics"), which had become popular with both new understandings and misapprehensions of principles of biological inheritance. In 1915, she wrote in favor of refusing life-saving medical procedures to infants with severe mental impairments or physical deformities, saying that their lives were not worthwhile and they would likely become criminals.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Nielsen-39):pp. 36–37[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-59) From 1946 to 1957, Keller visited 35 countries.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-60) In 1948, she went to New Zealand and visited deaf schools in [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch "Christchurch") and [Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland "Auckland"). She met Deaf Society of Canterbury Life Member Patty Still in Christchurch.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-61)

## Works

[![Image 13](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Helen_Keller13.jpg/250px-Helen_Keller13.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_Keller13.jpg)

Helen Keller, c. November 1912

Keller wrote a total of 12 published books and several articles. One of her earliest pieces of writing, at age 11, was _[The Frost King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Frost\_King "The Frost King")_ (1891). There were allegations that this story had been [plagiarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagiarized "Plagiarized") from _The Frost Fairies_ by Margaret Canby. An [investigation into the matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frost_King "The Frost King") revealed that the allegations were correct. However, it was felt by the Perkins schoolmaster Michael Anagnos that Keller may have experienced a case of [cryptomnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptomnesia "Cryptomnesia"), which was that she had Canby's story read to her but forgot about it, while the memory remained in her subconscious.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-rnib-36)[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Lash80-62):141

At age 22, with help from Sullivan and Sullivan's husband John Macy, Keller published her autobiography, _[The Story of My Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Story\_of\_My\_Life\_(biography) "The Story of My Life (biography)")_ (1903).[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-63) It recounts the story of her life up to age 21 and was written during her time in college. In an article Keller wrote in 1907, she brought to public attention the fact that many cases of childhood blindness could be prevented by washing the eyes of every newborn baby with a disinfectant solution. At the time, only a fraction of doctors and midwives were doing this. Thanks to Keller's advocacy, this commonsense public health measure was swiftly and widely adopted.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Hubbard-51)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-64)

[![Image 14](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Helen_Keller_circa_1920_-_restored.jpg/250px-Helen_Keller_circa_1920_-_restored.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_Keller_circa_1920_-_restored.jpg)

Keller holding a [magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia "Magnolia"), c.1920

In 1908, Keller wrote _The World I Live In_, giving readers an insight into how she felt about the world.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-65)_Out of the Dark_, a series of essays on socialism, was published in 1913.

Keller, along with her friend Mark Twain, also became involved in the [dispute about the authorship of the Shakespeare plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare_authorship_question "Shakespeare authorship question"), which they discussed extensively during Keller's, Sullivan's and Macy's visit to Twain's home in 1909. In a review Keller published of [George Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Greenwood "George Greenwood")'s book _The Shakespeare Problem Restated_, she wrote that "Shakespeare of Stratford is not to be even thought of as a possible author of the most wonderful plays in the world."[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-66) She soon became convinced by the claims of William Stone Booth that the works' true author was Francis Bacon and that Bacon had hidden an acrostic in every one of the plays contained in the First Folio. She asked [Richard Watson Gilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Watson_Gilder "Richard Watson Gilder"), the editor of _[The Century Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Century\_Magazine "The Century Magazine")_, if he'd be willing to publish an article by her advocating for Booth's hypothesis, but he declined.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-67)

When Keller was young, Anne Sullivan introduced her to [Phillips Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Brooks "Phillips Brooks"), who introduced her to Christianity, Keller famously saying: "I always knew He was there, but I didn't know His name!"[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Knowledge_of_God_1-68)[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Knowledge_of_God_2-69)[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Knowledge_of_God_3-70) Her [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") autobiography, _[My Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My\_Religion\_(Keller\_book) "My Religion (Keller book)")_,[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-MyReligion-71) was published in 1927 and then in 1994 extensively revised by Ray Silverman,[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-72) and re-issued under the title _[Light in My Darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light\_in\_My\_Darkness "Light in My Darkness")_. It advocates the teachings of [Emanuel Swedenborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Swedenborg "Emanuel Swedenborg"), the Christian theologian and mystic who gave a spiritual interpretation of the teachings of the Bible and who claimed that the [Second Coming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Coming "Second Coming") of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ "Jesus Christ") had already taken place. Keller described the core of her belief in these words:

> But in Swedenborg's teaching it [Divine Providence] is shown to be the government of God's Love and Wisdom and the creation of uses. Since His Life cannot be less in one being than another, or His Love manifested less fully in one thing than another, His Providence must needs be universal... He has provided religion of some kind everywhere, and it does not matter to what race or creed anyone belongs if he is faithful to his ideals of right living.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-MyReligion-71)

### Archival material

The Helen Keller Archives in New York are owned by the [American Foundation for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Foundation_for_the_Blind "American Foundation for the Blind").[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-74) The archive is currently housed at the [Museum of the American Printing House for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_American_Printing_House_for_the_Blind "Museum of the American Printing House for the Blind") on a long-term loan.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-75) Archival material of Keller stored in New York at the offices of [Helen Keller International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller_International "Helen Keller International") was lost when the [Twin Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_(1973%E2%80%932001) "World Trade Center (1973–2001)") were destroyed in the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"). The material lost included first editions of her books, correspondence with the director of the [Royal National Institute of Blind People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Institute_of_Blind_People "Royal National Institute of Blind People"), and photographs.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-76)[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-77)[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-78) There is also some archival material at [Perkins School for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perkins_School_for_the_Blind "Perkins School for the Blind").[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-79)

## Later life and death

Keller had a series of [strokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke "Stroke") in 1961 and spent the last years of her life at her home.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-rnib-36) In 1964, she was made an honorary member of the [Southern Dames of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dames_of_America "Southern Dames of America") during their second national assembly in Washington, D.C.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-80)[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-shelby-81) On September 14, 1964, President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson") awarded her the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom"), one of the United States' two highest civilian honors. In 1965, she was elected to the [National Women's Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Hall_of_Fame "National Women's Hall of Fame") at the [New York World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_New_York_World's_Fair "1964 New York World's Fair").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-rnib-36) Keller devoted much of her later life to raising funds for the [American Foundation for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Foundation_for_the_Blind "American Foundation for the Blind"). She died in her sleep on June 1, 1968, at her home, Arcan Ridge, located in [Easton, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easton,_Connecticut "Easton, Connecticut"), at the age of 87. A service was held at the [Washington National Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_National_Cathedral "Washington National Cathedral") in Washington, D.C., and her body was cremated in [Bridgeport, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport,_Connecticut "Bridgeport, Connecticut"). Her ashes were buried at the Washington National Cathedral next to her constant companions, Anne Sullivan and Polly Thomson.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-82)[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-83)

## Portrayals

[![Image 15](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Anne_Sullivan_-_Helen_Keller_memorial_-_Tewksbury%2C_Massachusetts_-_DSC00072.JPG/250px-Anne_Sullivan_-_Helen_Keller_memorial_-_Tewksbury%2C_Massachusetts_-_DSC00072.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Anne_Sullivan_-_Helen_Keller_memorial_-_Tewksbury,_Massachusetts_-_DSC00072.JPG)

_[Anne Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne\_Sullivan "Anne Sullivan")– Helen Keller Memorial_, bronze sculpture in [Tewksbury, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tewksbury,_Massachusetts "Tewksbury, Massachusetts")

Keller's life has been interpreted many times. She and her companion Anne Sullivan appeared in a silent film, _[Deliverance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deliverance\_(1919\_movie) "Deliverance (1919 movie)")_ (1919), which told her story in a melodramatic, allegorical style.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-84) She was also the subject of the [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature "Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature")-winning 1954 documentary _[Helen Keller in Her Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen\_Keller\_in\_Her\_Story "Helen Keller in Her Story")_, narrated by her friend and noted theatrical actress [Katharine Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharine_Cornell "Katharine Cornell");[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Herrmann_1999,_p._310-85)[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-86) in 2023, the film was added to the [National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Registry "National Film Registry") by the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") for being deemed "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-87) She was also profiled in _The Story of Helen Keller_, part of the Famous Americans series produced by [Hearst Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearst_Entertainment "Hearst Entertainment"). In the 1950s, when she was considered by many worldwide the greatest woman alive, Hearst reporter [Adela Rogers St. Johns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adela_Rogers_St._Johns "Adela Rogers St. Johns") told friends that she did not plan to include Keller in the book she was writing about the most famous women of the United States.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Herrmann_1999,_p._310-85)

_[The Miracle Worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Miracle\_Worker "The Miracle Worker")_ is a [literature cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_cycle "Literature cycle") of dramatic works ultimately derived from her autobiography, _[The Story of My Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Story\_of\_My\_Life\_(biography) "The Story of My Life (biography)")_. The various dramas each describe the relationship between Keller and Sullivan, depicting how the teacher led her from a state of almost [feral wildness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral_children "Feral children") into education, activism, and intellectual celebrity. The common title of the cycle echoes [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain "Mark Twain")'s description of Sullivan as a "miracle worker".[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-88) Its first realization, starring [Patty McCormack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_McCormack "Patty McCormack") as Keller and [Teresa Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_Wright "Teresa Wright") as Sullivan, was the 1957 _[Playhouse 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse\_90 "Playhouse 90")_ teleplay of that title by [William Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gibson_(playwright) "William Gibson (playwright)"). When Keller heard about it, she was enthusiastic, saying: "Never did I dream a drama could be devised out of the story of my life."[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-89) Within the cultural context of the early [civil rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights_movement "Civil rights movement"),[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-90) Gibson adapted it for a [Broadway production in 1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(play) "The Miracle Worker (play)"), which was praised by critics as a contemporary classic,[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Herrmann_1999,_p._321-91) and an Oscar-winning [feature film in 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(1962_film) "The Miracle Worker (1962 film)"), starring [Anne Bancroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Bancroft "Anne Bancroft") and [Patty Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Duke "Patty Duke").[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Herrmann_1999,_p._321-91) It was remade for television in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(1979_film) "The Miracle Worker (1979 film)"),[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-Herrmann_1999,_p._321-91) and then again in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(2000_film) "The Miracle Worker (2000 film)").[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-92)[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-93)

[![Image 16](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Helen_keller_patty_duke.jpg/250px-Helen_keller_patty_duke.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Helen_keller_patty_duke.jpg)

Helen Keller with [Patty Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Duke "Patty Duke"), who portrayed Keller in both the play and film _[The Miracle Worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Miracle\_Worker "The Miracle Worker")_ (1962). In a [1979 remake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(1979_film) "The Miracle Worker (1979 film)"), Patty Duke played [Anne Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Sullivan "Anne Sullivan").

An anime movie called _The Story of Helen Keller: Angel of Love and Light_ was made in 1981.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-94) In 1984, Keller's life story was made into a TV movie called _[The Miracle Continues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Miracle\_Continues "The Miracle Continues")_.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-95) This film, a semi-sequel to _The Miracle Worker_, recounts her college years and her early adult life. None of the early movies hint at the social activism that would become the hallmark of Keller's later life, although a [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") version produced in 2000 states in the credits that she became an activist for [social equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality "Social equality"). The [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood "Bollywood") movie _[Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black\_(2005\_film) "Black (2005 film)")_ (2005) was largely based on Keller's story from her childhood to her graduation.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-96)

A documentary called _Shining Soul: Helen Keller's Spiritual Life and Legacy_ was produced by the Swedenborg Foundation in 2005. The film focuses on the role played by [Emanuel Swedenborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Swedenborg "Emanuel Swedenborg")'s spiritual theology in her life and how it inspired Keller's triumph over her triple disabilities of blindness, deafness, and a severe speech impediment.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-97) On March 6, 2008, the [New England Historic Genealogical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Historic_Genealogical_Society "New England Historic Genealogical Society") announced that a staff member had discovered a rare 1888 photograph showing Helen and Anne, which, although previously published, had escaped widespread attention.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-independent_picture-98) Depicting Helen holding one of her many dolls, it is believed to be the earliest surviving photograph of Keller with Anne Sullivan Macy.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-99) Video footage showing Keller speaking also exists.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-100)

A biography of Keller was written by the German Jewish author [H. J. Kaeser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._J._Kaeser "H. J. Kaeser").[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-101)

In 2020, the documentary essay _Her Socialist Smile_ by [John Gianvito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gianvito "John Gianvito") evolves around Keller's first public talk in 1913 before a general audience, when she started speaking out on behalf of [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States "Progressivism in the United States") causes.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-102)

## Legacy and posthumous honors

In 1999, Keller was listed fifth (at 30 percent) in [Gallup's List of Most Widely Admired People of the 20th Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup's_List_of_Most_Widely_Admired_People_of_the_20th_Century "Gallup's List of Most Widely Admired People of the 20th Century").[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-103)[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-104) That same year, Keller was also named one of _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time\_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ magazine's 100 Most Important People of the 20th Century.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-105) In 2003, [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama "Alabama") honored its native daughter on its [state quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50_State_Quarters "50 State Quarters").[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-106) The Alabama state quarter is the only circulating U.S. coin to feature braille.[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-107) The Helen Keller Hospital in [Sheffield, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield,_Alabama "Sheffield, Alabama"), is dedicated to her.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-108) Streets are named after Keller in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich "Zurich"), Switzerland; in [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama "Alabama") and [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)") in the United States; in [Getafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe "Getafe"), Spain; in Vienna, Austria; in [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod "Lod"), Israel;[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-109) in [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon"), Portugal;[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-110) in [Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen "Caen"), France; and in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo "São Paulo"), Brazil. A preschool for the deaf and hard of hearing in [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore "Mysore"), India, was originally named after Keller by its founder, [K. K. Srinivasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._K._Srinivasan "K. K. Srinivasan").[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-111) In 1973, Keller was inducted into the [National Women's Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Hall_of_Fame "National Women's Hall of Fame").[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-112)

A [commemorative stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_stamp "Commemorative stamp") was issued in 1980 (pictured) by the [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service "United States Postal Service"), depicting Keller and Sullivan, to mark the centennial of Keller's birth.[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-113)[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-114) That year, her birth was also recognized by a presidential proclamation from U.S. President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter").[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-115)[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-116) Pennsylvania annually commemorates her June 27 birthday as Helen Keller Day.[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-117)[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-118) On October 7, 2009, the State of Alabama donated a [bronze statue of Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Helen_Keller "Statue of Helen Keller") to the [National Statuary Hall Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Statuary_Hall_Collection "National Statuary Hall Collection"), as a replacement for its 1908 statue of education reformer [Jabez Lamar Monroe Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabez_Lamar_Monroe_Curry "Jabez Lamar Monroe Curry").[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-119) Keller was posthumously inducted into the [Alabama Women's Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_Women's_Hall_of_Fame "Alabama Women's Hall of Fame") in 1971.[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-120) She was one of twelve inaugural inductees to the Alabama Writers Hall of Fame on June 8, 2015.[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-121)

In 2026, the British alternative rock band [Placebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo_(band) "Placebo (band)") released a single called _"Helen Keller",_ which features the keyboard melody of the hidden instrumental track _"H.K. Farewell"_ from the band's 1996 [self-titled debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo_(Placebo_album) "Placebo (Placebo album)"). Speaking about the single, [Brian Molko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Molko "Brian Molko") explained: "This is a new rock song inspired by the piano melody from '_H.K. Farewell'_ and named after one of my favourite people from history."[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-122) In the 1996 hidden track _"H.K. Farewell,"_ however, the initials "H.K." stand for [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong"), where the band recorded the track.[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller#cite_note-123)
