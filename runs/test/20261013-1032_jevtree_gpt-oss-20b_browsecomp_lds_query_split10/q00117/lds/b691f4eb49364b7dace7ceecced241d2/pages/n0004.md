# Native American History Timeline

[HISTORY.com Editors](https://www.history.com/authors/history)

![Image 3](https://res.cloudinary.com/aenetworks/image/upload/c_fill,ar_2,w_3840,h_1920,g_auto/dpr_auto/f_auto/q_auto:eco/v1/native-american-timeline_topic_feature?_a=BAVMn6E80)

Buyenlarge/Getty Images

Published: November 27, 2018 Last Updated: August 04, 2026

Long before Christopher Columbus stepped foot on what would come to be known as the Americas, the expansive territory was inhabited by Native Americans. Throughout the 16th and 17th centuries, as more explorers sought to colonize their land, Native Americans responded in various stages, from cooperation to indignation to revolt.

After siding with the French in numerous battles during the French and Indian War and eventually being forcibly removed from their homes under Andrew Jackson’s Indian Removal Act, Native American populations were diminished in size and territory by the end of the 19th century.

Below are events that shaped Native Americans’ tumultuous history following the arrival of foreign settlers.

## Native American Cultures

The buffalo was an essential part of Native American life, used in everything from religious rituals to teepee construction.

1:52m watch

**C. 1350-1600:**The [Iroquois Confederacy](https://www.potawatomiheritage.com/encyclopedia/iroquois-confederacy/), also called Haudenosaunee, is formed as an alliance between five nations—Cayuga, Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga and Seneca—hoping to end constant warring. The Tuscarora tribe joined in 1722. The alliance had its own constitution called "[The Great Law of Peace](https://www.history.com/articles/iroquois-confederacy-hiawatha-peacemaker-great-law-of-peace)."

**1492**: [Christopher Columbus](https://www.history.com/topics/exploration/columbus-controversy) lands on a [Caribbean Island](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/columbus-reaches-the-new-world) after three months of traveling. Believing at first that he had reached the East Indies, he describes the natives he meets as “Indians.” On his first day, he orders six natives to be seized as servants.

**April 1513**: Spanish explorer [Juan Ponce de Leon](https://www.history.com/topics/exploration/juan-ponce-de-leon) lands on continental North America in Florida and makes contact with Native Americans.

**February 1521**: Ponce de Leon departs on another voyage to Florida from San Juan to start a colony. Months after landing, Ponce de Leon is attacked by local Native Americans and fatally wounded.

**May 1539**: Spanish explorer and conquistador [Hernando de Soto](https://www.history.com/topics/exploration/hernando-de-soto) lands in Florida to conquer the region. He explores the South under the guidance of Native Americans who had been captured along the way.

**October 1540**: De Soto and the Spaniards plan to rendezvous with ships in Alabama when they’re attacked by Native Americans. Hundreds of Native Americans are killed in the ensuing battle.

**C. 1595**: [Pocahontas](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/pocahontas) is born, daughter of Chief Powhatan.

**1607**: Pocahontas’ brother kidnaps Captain John Smith from the [Jamestown](https://www.history.com/topics/colonial-america/jamestown) colony. Smith later writes that after being threatened by Chief Powhatan, he was saved by Pocahontas. This scenario is debated by historians.

**C. 1613:** First Covenant Chain formed between the Iroquoois Confederacy and Dutch settlers. Codified in a famed [two-row wampum belt](https://aetn-editorial-activate-history.aenetworks.cloud/articles/what-is-wampum-and-how-was-it-used#:~:text=Almost%20two%C2%A0centuries,domination%20and%20balance.), the agreement promised harmonious mutual coexistence at a time when European settlers and traders were beginning to encroach on Iroquois territory.

**1613**: Pocahontas is captured by Captain Samuel Argall in the first Anglo-Powhatan War. While captive, she learns to speak English, converts to Christianity and is given the name “Rebecca.”

**1622**: The [Powhatan Confederacy](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/american-indian-wars) nearly wipes out Jamestown colony.

**1680**: A revolt of Pueblo Native Americans in New Mexico threatens Spanish rule over New Mexico.

## The French and Indian War

The French and Indian War saw two European imperialists go head-to-head over territory and marked the debut of the soldier who would become America's first president.

3:09m watch

**May 15, 1756**: The [Seven Years’ War](https://www.history.com/topics/france/seven-years-war) between the British and the French begins, with Native American alliances aiding the French.

**May 7, 1763**: Ottawa Chief Pontiac leads Native American forces into battle against the British in Detroit. The British retaliate by attacking Pontiac’s warriors in Detroit on July 31, in what is known as the [Battle of Bloody Run](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/pontiacs-rebellion-begins). Pontiac and company successfully fend them off, but there are several casualties on both sides.

**1778:**U.S. government signs the Treaty of Fort Pitt with the Delawares—the [first official treaty](https://www.bia.gov/bia/history/IndianLawPolicy#:~:text=In%201778%2C%20the%20U.S.%20signed%20its%20first%20treaty%20with%20an%20Indian%20tribe%2C%20the%20Delaware%20Indians.) of some 374 made between the U.S. government and a tribal nation.

**1785**: The [Treaty of Hopewell](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/indian-reservations) is signed in Georgia, protecting Cherokee Native Americans in the United States and sectioning off their land.

**1788/89**: [Sacagawea](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/sacagawea) is born.

**1791**: The Treaty of Holston is signed, in which the Cherokee give up all their land outside of the borders previously established.

**August 20, 1794**: The [Battle of Fallen Timbers](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/battle-of-fallen-timbers), the last major battle over Northwest territory between Native Americans and the United States following the [Revolutionary War](https://www.history.com/topics/american-revolution), commences and results in U.S. victory.

**November 2, 1804:**Native American Sacagawea, while 6 months pregnant, meets explorers [Meriwether Lewis and William Clark](https://www.history.com/topics/westward-expansion/lewis-and-clark) during their exploration of the territory of the [Louisiana Purchase](https://www.history.com/topics/westward-expansion/louisiana-purchase). The explorers realize her value as a translator.

**April 7, 1805:**Sacagawea, along with her baby and husband Toussaint Charbonneau, join Lewis and Clark on their voyage.

## Sacagawea

In 1804, Jefferson sends a team to explore lands acquired in the Louisiana Purchase. The Corps of Discovery will travel nearly 8,000 miles over three years, reaching the Pacific Ocean and clearing the path for westward expansion.

1:46m watch

**November 1811**: U.S. forces attack Native American War Chief [Tecumseh](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/tecumseh)and his younger brother Lalawethika.Their community at the juncture of the Tippecanoe and Wabash rivers is destroyed.

**June 18, 1812**: President James Madison signs a [declaration of war](https://www.history.com/topics/war-of-1812/war-of-1812) against Britain, beginning the war between U.S. forces and the British, French and Native Americans over independence and territory expansion.

**March 27, 1814**: Andrew Jackson, along with U.S. forces and Native American allies attack Creek (now Muscogee) Indians who opposed American expansion and encroachment of their territory in the Battle of Horseshoe Bend. The Creeks cede more than [20 million acres of land](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/indian-reservations) after their loss.

**May 28, 1830**: President Andrew Jackson signs the [Indian Removal Act](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/indian-reservations), which gives plots of land west of the Mississippi River to Native American tribes in exchange for land that is taken from them.

**March 3, 1832**: In [_Worcester v. Georgia_](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/law/worcester-v-georgia), the Supreme Court asserted the sovereignty of the Cherokee nation, establishing a precedent for modern tribal self-government.

**1836**: The last of the Muscogee (Creek) Native Americans leave their land for Oklahoma as part of the [Indian removal process](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/trail-of-tears). Of the 15,000 Muscogees who [make the voyage to Oklahoma](https://www.history.com/news/trail-of-tears-conditions-cherokee), more than 3,500 don’t survive.

**1838**: With only 2,000 Cherokees having left their land in Georgia to cross the Mississippi River, [President Martin Van Buren](https://www.history.com/topics/us-presidents/martin-van-buren) enlists General Winfield Scott and 7,000 troops to speed up the process by holding them at gunpoint and marching them 1,200 miles. More than 5,000 Cherokee die as a result of the journey. The series of relocations of Native American tribes and their hardships and deaths during the journey would become known as the [Trail of Tears](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/trail-of-tears).

**1851**: Congress passes the Indian Appropriations Act, creating the [Indian reservation system](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/indian-reservations). Native Americans aren’t allowed to leave their reservations without permission.

**October 1860**: A group of Apache Native Americans attack and kidnap a white American, resulting in the U.S. military falsely accusing the Native American leader of the Chiricahua Apache tribe, [Cochise](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/cochise). Cochise and the Apache increase raids on white Americans for a decade afterwards.

**December 26, 1862**: Following the [U.S.-Dakota War](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-17/dakota-uprising-begins-in-minnesota), sparked by broken treaties and government failures that left many Dakota facing starvation, 38 Dakota (Santee Sioux) men are [hanged](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/november-5/300-santee-sioux-sentenced-to-hang-in-minnesota) in Mankato, Minnesota, in the largest mass execution in U.S. history.

**November 29, 1864**: 650 Colorado volunteer forces attack Cheyenne and Arapaho encampments along Sand Creek, killing and mutilating more than 150 American Indians during what would become known as the [Sand Creek Massacre](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/sand-creek-massacre).

**April 29, 1868:** The U.S. Government and the Sioux Nation sign the Treaty of Fort Laramie. In this treaty, the United States recognizes the Black Hills of Dakota as the Great Sioux Reservation, the exclusive territory of the Sioux (Dakota, Lakota and Nakota) and Arapaho people. But after gold is discovered in the Black Hills, miners and settlers begin moving onto the land en masse.Native resistance to the treaty’s violation culminates in the [Battle of the Little Bighorn](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/battle-of-the-little-bighorn) in 1876.In 1980, the Supreme Court rules that the Black Hills were illegally confiscated, and awards the Sioux more than $100 million in reparations. [Sioux leaders](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/america-the-story-of-us-videos-the-last-of-the-sioux-video) reject the payment, saying the land had never been for sale.

**March 4, 1929**: Charles Curtis serves as the [first Native American U.S. Vice President](https://www.history.com/news/vice-president-charles-curtis) under President [Herbert Hoover](https://www.history.com/topics/us-presidents/herbert-hoover).

**July 1968:**Dennis Banks and Clyde Bellecourt found the[American Indian Movement (AIM)](https://www.history.com/topics/native-american-history/american-indian-movement-aim)in Minneapolis, along with Bellecourt’s brother Vernon and Banks’ friend George Mitchell.Originally an urban-focused movement formed in response to police brutality and racial profiling, AIM grows rapidly in the 1970s to become the driving force behind the Indigenous civil rights movement.

**November 20, 1969:** A group of San Francisco Bay-area Native Americans, calling themselves“Indians of All Tribes,”[journey to Alcatraz Island](https://www.history.com/news/native-american-activists-occupy-alcatraz-island-45-years-ago), declaring their intention to use the island for an Indian school, cultural center and museum. Referencing Europeans' colonization of North America, they claim Alcatraz is theirs “by right of discovery.” On June 11, 1971 armed federal marshals descend on the island and remove the last of its Indian residents.

**August 29, 1970**: A group of Native Americans, led by the San Francisco-based United Native Americans, [ascend 3,000 feet to the top of Mount Rushmore](https://www.history.com/news/mount-rushmore-native-american-protests) and set up camp to protest the broken Treaty of Fort Laramie.

**November 26, 1970:** On [Thanksgiving Day](https://www.history.com/topics/thanksgiving/history-of-thanksgiving), AIM members [seize a replica of the Mayflower](https://www.massmoments.org/moment-details/first-national-day-of-mourning-held-in-plymouth.html) in Boston Harbor, declaring the holiday a[National Day of Mourning](http://www.uaine.org/).

**June 6, 1971**: A group of Native Americans, led by AIM, occupy [Mount Rushmore](https://www.history.com/topics/us-presidents/mount-rushmore-1) to demand the 1868 Treaty of Fort Laramie be honored. Twenty Native Americans—nine men and 11 women—are eventually arrested.

**October 1972**: Hundreds of Native Americans drive in caravans, beginning at the West Coast, to the offices of the Department of the Interior in Washington, D.C. in a movement called the [Trail of Broken Treaties](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/trail-of-broken-treaties.htm). During the occupation, AIM releases the [Twenty Points](http://www.aimovement.org/archives/), a list of demands that includes the re-recognition of Native tribes, abolition of the Bureau of Indian Affairs and federal protections for Indigenous cultures and religions. The occupiers hold the BIA office for a week.

The act requires federal agencies and museums that receive federal funds to repatriate Native American cultural items to their respective peoples.

**October, 1991**: The [National Coalition of Racism in Sports and Media](http://www.aimovement.org/ncrsm/) (NCRSM) is established by leaders at the National Congress of American Indians to organize against the use of Indian names, logos, symbols and mascots in sports.

**July 13, 2020**:The Washington National Football League franchise announces it is dropping its name, the “Redskins,” as well as its Indian head logo. The move is in response to decades of criticism that they are offensive to Native Americans. The team is eventually renamed the Commanders.
