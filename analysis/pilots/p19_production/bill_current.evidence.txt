_**Kinsey**_ is a 2004 American [biographical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biographical_film "Biographical film")[drama film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_(film_and_television) "Drama (film and television)") written and directed by [Bill Condon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon "Bill Condon").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_(film)#cite_note-4) It describes the life of [Alfred Charles Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kinsey "Alfred Kinsey") (played by [Liam Neeson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Neeson "Liam Neeson")), a pioneer in the area of [sexology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexology "Sexology"). His 1948 publication, _Sexual Behavior in the Human Male_ (the first of the [Kinsey Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_Reports "Kinsey Reports")) was one of the first recorded works that tried to scientifically address and investigate [sexual behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexual_activity "Human sexual activity") in humans. The film also stars [Laura Linney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Linney "Laura Linney") (in a performance nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress")), [Chris O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_O'Donnell "Chris O'Donnell"), [Peter Sarsgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarsgaard "Peter Sarsgaard"), [Timothy Hutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Hutton "Timothy Hutton"), [John Lithgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lithgow "John Lithgow"), [Tim Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Curry "Tim Curry"), [Oliver Platt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Platt "Oliver Platt"), and [Dylan Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Baker "Dylan Baker").

_Kinsey_ premiered at [Telluride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telluride_Film_Festival "Telluride Film Festival") on September 4, 2004 before being released by [Fox Searchlight Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Searchlight_Pictures "Fox Searchlight Pictures") in the United States on November 12, 2004. The film received positive reviews from critics and grossed $16.9 million against an $11 million budget.
Professor [Alfred Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kinsey "Alfred Kinsey") is interviewed about his sexual history. Interspersed with the interview are flashbacks from his childhood and young-adulthood. The early years show his father, a [lay minister of the Methodist church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_local_preacher "Methodist local preacher"), denouncing modern inventions as leading to sexual sin, then in early adolescence, humiliating Kinsey in a store by denouncing its keeper for showing him cigarettes, while his adolescence shows his experiences as a [Boy Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scouts_(Boy_Scouts_of_America) "Boy Scouts (Boy Scouts of America)") and a late teenage scene shows Kinsey disappointing his father by his chosen vocational intentions. The adult Kinsey teaches at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington "Indiana University Bloomington") as a professor of biology lecturing on [gall wasps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall_wasp "Gall wasp").
Kinsey falls in love with a student in his class, whom he calls [Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_McMillen "Clara McMillen"), and marries her. Consummation of their marriage is difficult at first, because of a medical problem Mac has that is fixed easily with minor surgery. At the university, Professor Kinsey, who is affectionately called "Prok" by his graduate students, meets with them after hours to offer individual sexual advice.

At a book party celebrating Kinsey's latest publication on gall wasps, Kinsey approaches the [dean of students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_students "Dean of students") about an open-forum [sex education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_education "Sex education") course as opposed to the anti-sex propaganda taught in a general [health education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_education "Health education") class. It is approved, but on the grounds that it is open only to teachers, graduate or senior students, and married students. Kinsey begins teaching the sex course to a packed auditorium.
Kinsey continues answering students' questions in personal meetings but his answers are severely limited by the paucity of scientific data about human sexual behavior. This leads Kinsey to pass out questionnaires in his sexual education class from which he learns of the enormous disparity between what society had assumed people do and what their actual practices are. After securing financial support from the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation"), Kinsey and his research assistants, including his closest assistant, [Clyde Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Martin "Clyde Martin"), travel the country, interviewing subjects about their sexual histories.
As time progresses Kinsey realizes that sexuality within humans, including himself, is a lot more varied than was originally thought. The range of expression he creates becomes known as the [Kinsey scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_scale "Kinsey scale"), which ranks overall sexuality from completely [heterosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexual "Heterosexual") to completely [homosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual "Homosexual").

**William Condon** (born October 22, 1955) is an American director and screenwriter. Condon is known for writing and/or directing numerous successful and acclaimed films including _[Gods and Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gods\_and\_Monsters\_(film) "Gods and Monsters (film)")_, _[Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago\_(2002\_film) "Chicago (2002 film)")_, _[Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey\_(film) "Kinsey (film)")_, _[Dreamgirls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamgirls\_(film) "Dreamgirls (film)")_, _[The Twilight Saga: Breaking Dawn – Part 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Twilight\_Saga:\_Breaking\_Dawn\_%E2%80%93\_Part\_1 "The Twilight Saga: Breaking Dawn – Part 1")_, _[Part 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Twilight\_Saga:\_Breaking\_Dawn\_%E2%80%93\_Part\_2 "The Twilight Saga: Breaking Dawn – Part 2")_, and _[Beauty and the Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty\_and\_the\_Beast\_(2017\_film) "Beauty and the Beast (2017 film)")_.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-CondonDisneylive-actionBeautyandtheBeast-1) He has received two nominations for the [Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay"), _Gods and Monsters_ and _Chicago_, winning for the former.

Condon emerged a few years later directing a series of made-for-TV thrillers, including _[Murder 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder\_101\_(1991\_film) "Murder 101 (1991 film)")_ (1991), which earned Condon and co-writer Roy Johansen a 1992 [Edgar Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Award "Edgar Award") for their screenplay.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-mysterynet-11) During this period he also wrote the screenplay for the thriller _[F/X2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/X2 "F/X2")_ (1991), which was directed by Australian director [Richard Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Franklin_(director) "Richard Franklin (director)").[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-12)
In 1994, he directed the television movie _[The Man Who Wouldn't Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Man\_Who\_Wouldn't\_Die\_(1995\_film) "The Man Who Wouldn't Die (1995 film)"),_ which was met with mixed reviews.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-13) He directed _[Candyman: Farewell to the Flesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candyman:\_Farewell\_to\_the\_Flesh "Candyman: Farewell to the Flesh")_ (1995), a sequel to [Bernard Rose's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Rose_(director) "Bernard Rose (director)") 1992 horror film. It was a critical and commercial failure.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-14) Reminded years later of this phase of his career, Condon said: "It's hard to be lower on the totem pole than being the director for a sequel to a horror movie."[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-columbiacollege-4)

Condon wrote and directed _[Gods and Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gods\_and\_Monsters\_(film) "Gods and Monsters (film)")_ (1998), which was based on a novel by [Christopher Bram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Bram "Christopher Bram"). His screenplay won the [Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Writing_Adapted_Screenplay "Academy Award for Writing Adapted Screenplay"). _The New York Times_ said Condon "may have been the most stunned person at the Academy Awards when his name was announced as the winner for the best adapted screenplay. He has struggled for years in Hollywood as a screenwriter and journalist and is unaccustomed to the hoopla that is now around him."[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-15) He was nominated for the same award for his screenplay for _[Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago\_(2002\_film) "Chicago (2002 film)")_, based on the Broadway musical of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_(musical) "Chicago (musical)").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-16) He received a second Edgar Award for his _Chicago_ screenplay as well.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-17)

In 2004 he wrote and directed the film _[Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey\_(film) "Kinsey (film)")_, chronicling the life of the controversial sex researcher [Alfred Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kinsey "Alfred Kinsey"). In _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_, [A.O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.O._Scott "A.O. Scott") wrote that "Bill Condon's smart, stirring [film] has a lot to say on the subject of sex, which it treats with sobriety, sensitivity and a welcome measure of humor." He continued: "I can't think of another movie that has dealt with sex so knowledgeably and, at the same time, made the pursuit of knowledge seem so sexy. There are some explicit images and provocative scenes, but it is your intellect that is most likely to be aroused.... Mr. Condon's great achievement is to turn Kinsey's complicated and controversial career into a grand intellectual drama."[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-18)
In 2005, he received the [Stephen F. Kolzak Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAAD_Media_Awards "GLAAD Media Awards") at the [GLAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAAD "GLAAD") Media Awards.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-19)
Condon wrote the screenplay for and directed _[Dreamgirls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamgirls\_(film) "Dreamgirls (film)")_, an adaptation of the acclaimed Broadway musical of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamgirls "Dreamgirls"). It was released in December 2006. Condon received [Directors Guild of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directors_Guild_of_America "Directors Guild of America") and [Broadcast Film Critics Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Film_Critics_Association "Broadcast Film Critics Association") nominations for directing. The film was nominated for eight Academy Awards in six categories.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-20)

Condon was executive producer of the [81st Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81st_Academy_Awards "81st Academy Awards") television broadcast that aired on February 22, 2009, working with producer [Laurence Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Mark "Laurence Mark").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-21)
Condon directed both parts of _[The Twilight Saga: Breaking Dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking\_Dawn\_(disambiguation) "Breaking Dawn (disambiguation)")_ (2011 and 2012), adapted from the fourth and final novel in _[The Twilight Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight\_(novel\_series) "Twilight (novel series)")_ by [Stephenie Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephenie_Meyer "Stephenie Meyer"). He was twice nominated for a [Razzie for Worst Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Director "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Director") for these films, winning for _Part 2_.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-22)
Condon directed _[The Fifth Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Fifth\_Estate\_(film) "The Fifth Estate (film)")_ (2013), a thriller about [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks "WikiLeaks") starring [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch"). He said he chose the project for a change of pace and liked its non-ideological approach to a very complex subject. He saw it "in the great tradition of journalistic thrillers".[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-23) It received mixed responses from critics and underperformed at the box office.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-24)

In late 2013, he directed a revised version of the 1997 stage musical _[Side Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side\_Show\_(musical) "Side Show (musical)")_ at the [La Jolla Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla_Playhouse "La Jolla Playhouse"). A production of this revision played in June and July 2014 at the [Kennedy Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center "Kennedy Center"). [Charles Isherwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Isherwood "Charles Isherwood") described it in _The New York Times_ as "a full-scale reimagining" of the musical that involved "the addition and subtraction of several songs ..., the reordering of others", and new dialogue contributed by Condon.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-25) That production received excellent reviews when it moved to Broadway in the fall, but it proved a failure at the box office and closed after just seven weeks.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-26)
In 2015, Condon directed _[Mr. Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr.\_Holmes "Mr. Holmes")_ starring [Ian McKellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_McKellen "Ian McKellen"). Condon noted its similarity to _Gods and Monsters_, not only because of its lead actor but because "[b]oth movies are about aging and mortality. You have a celebrated man facing the decline of his public image."[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-27)

In December 2023, it was announced that Condon would write and direct a [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_of_the_Spider_Woman_(2025_film) "Kiss of the Spider Woman (2025 film)") of the [musical version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_of_the_Spider_Woman_(musical) "Kiss of the Spider Woman (musical)") of _[Kiss of the Spider Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss\_of\_the\_Spider\_Woman\_(novel) "Kiss of the Spider Woman (novel)")_, with [Jennifer Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lopez "Jennifer Lopez") attached to star and co-executive produce.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-33)
In July 2026, it was revealed that Condon was filming his next film, _The Road Home_, in South Africa. It is about [Hugh Masekela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Masekela "Hugh Masekela") and the conflict he faced from recording with [Paul Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Simon "Paul Simon") during [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid").[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-34)
Condon is [gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay "Gay").[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-35) He is in a long-term relationship with Jack Morrissey.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-budget-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Condon#cite_note-37)
