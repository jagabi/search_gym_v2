_**Another Time, Another Place**_ is a 1983 British [drama film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_film "Drama film") directed by [Michael Radford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Radford "Michael Radford") and starring [Phyllis Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Logan "Phyllis Logan"), Giovanni Mauriello and [Denise Coffey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denise_Coffey "Denise Coffey").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Time,_Another_Place_(1983_film)#cite_note-BFIsearch-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Time,_Another_Place_(1983_film)#cite_note-3) The screenplay was based on the 1983 novel by [Jessie Kesson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Kesson "Jessie Kesson").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Time,_Another_Place_(1983_film)#cite_note-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Time,_Another_Place_(1983_film)#cite_note-5)

In Scotland in 1943 during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Janie is a young Scottish housewife married to Dougal, who is 15 years older. Participating in a war rehabilitation program, the couple take in three Italian [prisoners of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoners_of_war "Prisoners of war") to work on their farm. Janie soon falls in love with one of the three, Luigi. She begins a secret relationship with Luigi that is doomed from the start.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Time,_Another_Place_(1983_film)#cite_note-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Time,_Another_Place_(1983_film)#cite_note-7)

**John Francis Lane** (1 December 1928 – 15 January 2018)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-2) was an English journalist, critic and actor. He was known for being a small-part actor in many [Italian films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Italy "Cinema of Italy"),[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-3) and as a contributor to _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Guardian "The Guardian")_ writing obituaries for Italian cultural figures.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-Mayes-1)
Lane was born in [Tankerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tankerton "Tankerton"), a suburb of [Whitstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitstable "Whitstable") in [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent "Kent"), to a middle-class family in 1928. He became a boarder at the [Royal Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Grammar_School_Worcester "Royal Grammar School Worcester") in [Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_England "Worcester, England"), and towards the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") attended [Dulwich College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulwich_College "Dulwich College") in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-Timesobit-4) He then had a spell as a stage manager in London's [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_of_London "West End of London").

Lane first visited Italy in 1949, following a German boyfriend from Paris, where he was then studying at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"). After learning Italian at the [University of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florence "University of Florence"), he had settled in Rome by 1951, finding Italy more tolerant of his homosexuality than the Britain of the time.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-Mayes-1)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-Timesobit-4) Learning Italian in Florence, he became entranced by the country after seeing [Franco Zeffirelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Zeffirelli "Franco Zeffirelli")'s production of _[Troilus and Cressida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troilus\_and\_Cressida "Troilus and Cressida")_. Zeffirelli later became one of his boyfriends.
He was made a [Cavaliere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavaliere "Cavaliere") in the 1975 Italian Order of Merit.
In retirement Lane lived in the town of [Rende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rende "Rende") in the region of [Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabria "Calabria"). He wrote frequently on Italian life for _[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Economist "The Economist")_.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-Mayes-1) His memoir, _To Each His Own Dolce Vita_, was published in 2013.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-Timesobit-4)

Lane died in January 2018 in [Cosenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosenza "Cosenza") in southern Italy.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-5) Knowledge he had died was delayed and only emerged following the end of Zeffirelli's life.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_Lane#cite_note-Timesobit-4)
