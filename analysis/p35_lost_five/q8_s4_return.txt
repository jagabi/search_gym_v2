_**Nineteen Eighty-Four**_ (stylized as _**1984**_) is a 1984 British [dystopian film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopian_film "Dystopian film") written and directed by [Michael Radford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Radford "Michael Radford"), based on [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell "George Orwell")'s [1949 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four "Nineteen Eighty-Four"). Starring [John Hurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hurt "John Hurt"), [Richard Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Burton "Richard Burton"), [Suzanna Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanna_Hamilton "Suzanna Hamilton") and [Cyril Cusack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Cusack "Cyril Cusack"), it follows the life of [Winston Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Smith_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "Winston Smith (Nineteen Eighty-Four)") (Hurt), a low-ranking civil servant in a war-torn London in [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "Oceania (Nineteen Eighty-Four)"), a [totalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian "Totalitarian") superstate ruled by [Ingsoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingsoc "Ingsoc") and its leader [Big Brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "Big Brother (Nineteen Eighty-Four)").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-6) Smith struggles to maintain his sanity and his grip on reality as the regime's overwhelming power and influence persecute [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism "Individualism") and individual thinking on both a political and a personal level.

_Nineteen Eighty-Four_ was Burton's last screen appearance; it was released two months after his death and is dedicated to him.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-tcmnotes-7) It was released in the United Kingdom on 10 October 1984 by [Virgin Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Films "Virgin Films"). It received positive reviews from critics, with many highlighting Deakins' cinematography and use of [bleach bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleach_bypass "Bleach bypass") and it was a modarate sucsess at the box office. It was nominated for a [BAFTA Award for Best Art Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Art_Direction "BAFTA Award for Best Art Direction"), and won two [Evening Standard British Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Standard_British_Film_Awards "Evening Standard British Film Awards") for Best Film and Best Actor.

In a dystopian 1984, Winston Smith endures a squalid existence in the [totalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian "Totalitarian") superstate of [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_geography_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#Oceania "Political geography of Nineteen Eighty-Four") under the constant surveillance of the [Thought Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought_Police "Thought Police"). He resides in London, the capital city of the territory of [Airstrip One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstrip_One "Airstrip One"), formerly Britain, and works in a small office cubicle at the [Ministry of Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Truth "Ministry of Truth"), rewriting history as dictated by [the Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingsoc "Ingsoc") and its supreme leader, [Big Brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "Big Brother (Nineteen Eighty-Four)"), who never appears publicly but instead appears only on propaganda posters, advertising billboards and television monitors. Smith attends mandatory public rallies at Victory Square (formerly [Trafalgar Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafalgar_Square "Trafalgar Square")), where citizens are shown propaganda films of the current war situation as well as contradictory and false news stories about Oceania's war effort to unite the civilized world under Big Brother's rule. While his co-worker and neighbour Parsons seems content to follow the state's laws, Winston, haunted by painful childhood memories and restless carnal desires, keeps a secret diary of his private thoughts, thus creating evidence of his [thoughtcrime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoughtcrime "Thoughtcrime"). However, he tries to do it out of sight of the telescreens, to maintain his safety.

His life greatly changes when he is accosted by his fellow Outer Party worker [Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "Julia (Nineteen Eighty-Four)"), a mysterious, bold-looking, sensual and free-spirited young woman who works as a print machine mechanic in the Ministry of Truth, and they begin an illicit affair. During their first meeting in the remote countryside, they exchange subversive ideas before having sex. Shortly after, Winston rents a room above a pawn shop in the less restrictive proletarian area where they continue their liaison. Julia procures [contraband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraband "Contraband") food and clothing on the black market, and for a brief few months they secretly meet and enjoy an idyllic life of relative freedom and contentment together.
Their affair comes to an end one evening, when the Thought Police suddenly raid the flat and arrest them both. It is later revealed that a [telescreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescreen "Telescreen") hidden behind a picture on the wall in their room recorded their transgressions and that the elderly proprietor of the pawn shop, Mr. Charrington, is a covert agent of the Thought Police. Winston and Julia are taken away to the [Ministry of Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Love "Ministry of Love") to be detained, questioned and "rehabilitated" separately. There [O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Brien_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "O'Brien (Nineteen Eighty-Four)"), a high-ranking member of the Inner Party whom Winston had believed to be a fellow thoughtcriminal and agent of the resistance movement led by the Party's archenemy, [Emmanuel Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Goldstein "Emmanuel Goldstein"), systematically tortures him.

O'Brien instructs Winston about the state's true purpose and schools him in a kind of [catechism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism "Catechism") on the principles of _[doublethink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doublethink "Doublethink")_–the practice of holding two contradictory thoughts in the mind simultaneously. For his final rehabilitation, Winston is brought to [Room 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_101 "Room 101"), where O'Brien tells him he will be subjected to the "worst thing in the world", designed specifically around Smith's personal phobias. When confronted with this unbearable horror–a cage filled with wild rats–Winston's psychological resistance finally and irretrievably breaks down; he hysterically repudiates his allegiance to Julia. Seemingly subjugated and purged of any rebellious thoughts, impulses or personal attachments, Winston is restored to physical health and released.
Winston returns to the Chestnut Tree Café, where he had seen the rehabilitated thoughtcriminals Jones, Aaronson and Rutherford (themselves once prominent but later disgraced members of the Inner Party) who have since been "vaporized" and rendered [unpersons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspeak#Newspeak_vocabulary "Newspeak"). While sitting at the chess table, Winston is approached by Julia, who was similarly "rehabilitated". They share a bottle of Victory Gin and impassively exchange a few words about how they have betrayed each other. In spite of everything they have gone through, they still reaffirm their bond and express desire to see each other again. After she leaves, Winston watches a broadcast of himself on the large telescreen humbly and remorsefully confessing his "crimes" against the state and imploring forgiveness from the populace.

Upon hearing a news report declaring the Oceanian army's utter rout of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_geography_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#Eurasia_and_Eastasia "Political geography of Nineteen Eighty-Four")'s forces in North Africa, Winston, appearing to have been deprived of his freedom to think and feel for himself and reduced to a mere shell of a man, soon to be deprived of his physical existence, looks at the still image of Big Brother that appears on the telescreen but then quickly turns away from it and looks in the direction of Julia with tears in his eyes as the words "I love you" are heard whispered in his voice.

In the winter of 1983 the director [Michael Radford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Radford "Michael Radford") asked his producer to try to obtain the rights to Orwell's novel, with low expectations that they were available. It turned out that the rights were held by Marvin Rosenblum, a lawyer in Chicago who had been trying on his own to get such a film produced.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-DenGeek-8) Rosenblum agreed to become an executive producer, and while the producer Simon Perry raised the production money from [Richard Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Branson "Richard Branson"), Radford wrote the script, inspired by his idea to make a "science fiction film made in 1948".[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-DenGeek-8) The script was finished in three weeks.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-DenGeek-8)

## Release and reception
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Release and reception")]
_Nineteen Eighty-Four_ made its theatrical debut on 10 October in London and on 14 December in New York City.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-auto-19) It was released in the United States for one week in December 1984 at the [Egyptian Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grauman's_Egyptian_Theatre "Grauman's Egyptian Theatre") in Los Angeles to qualify for the [1985 Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_Academy_Awards "57th Academy Awards") and grossed a house record of US$62,121, despite stormy weather.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-hv-20)
[Vincent Canby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Canby "Vincent Canby") of _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ said the film was "admirable, bleakly beautiful", although "not an easy film to watch".[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-canby-2)
[Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") awarded it 3.5/4 stars, writing that it "penetrates much more deeply into the novel's heart of darkness" than previous adaptations, and describing Hurt as "the perfect Winston Smith".[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-21)

## Release and reception
The [review aggregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") has given it a 76% approval rating, based on 33 reviews, with an average rating of 6.8/10. _Filmink_ called it "brilliant, incredible to look at, and emotionally devastating."[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-al-23)
The review aggregation website [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") has given it a rating of 67 out of 100 based on 8 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-24)

## Release and reception
[Virgin Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Films "Virgin Films") (formerly part of the [Virgin Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Group "Virgin Group")), who financed the film, commissioned the British rock/pop duo [Eurythmics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurythmics "Eurythmics") to produce the music for the soundtrack after initially approaching [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie"), who demanded a fee that Virgin deemed to be too high.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-25) Radford objected to Virgin's insistence on using the more pop-oriented [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music") Eurythmics music, as the traditional [orchestral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestral "Orchestral")[score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score "Film score") originally intended for the film had been composed entirely by [Dominic Muldowney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Muldowney "Dominic Muldowney") a few months earlier.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-al-23)
Against Radford's wishes, Virgin exercised their right of final cut and replaced Muldowney's musical [cues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cue_(theatrical) "Cue (theatrical)") with the new Eurythmics contributions. One Eurythmics song, "[Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_(Eurythmics_song) "Julia (Eurythmics song)")", was also heard in its entirety during the film's closing credits. However, Muldowney's main theme music (particularly the state anthem, "Oceania, 'tis for thee") was still prominently used in the film.

## Release and reception
In November 1984 Virgin Records released the Eurythmics soundtrack album, containing considerably altered versions of their music heard in the film, under the title _[1984 (For the Love of Big Brother)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984\_(For\_the\_Love\_of\_Big\_Brother) "1984 (For the Love of Big Brother)")_. The album reached number 23 on the UK Album Chart, and was later certified Gold by the [BPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry "British Phonographic Industry") for sales in excess of 100,000 copies.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-BPI-26) A song from the album, "[Sexcrime (Nineteen Eighty-Four)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexcrime_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four)_(Eurythmics_song) "Sexcrime (Nineteen Eighty-Four) (Eurythmics song)")", was released as a single just prior to the album and became one of Eurythmics' biggest hits, peaking at number 4 and was awarded a Silver disc for sales in excess of 250,000 copies.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-BPI-26) The music video for the single made use of clips from the film. The track "Julia" was also released as a single which peaked just outside the Top 40.

## Release and reception
Radford disowned Virgin's edit of the film containing the predominantly Eurythmics score and, during his acceptance speech at the [_Evening Standard_ British Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Standard_British_Film_Awards "Evening Standard British Film Awards"), expressed his displeasure at Virgin's "foisting" the Eurythmics music on his film.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-auto-19) Radford withdrew the film from consideration at the [BAFTA awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA "BAFTA") in protest of Virgin's decision to change the musical score. Eurythmics responded with a statement of their own claiming no knowledge of prior agreements between Virgin and Radford/Muldowney and that they had accepted the offer to compose music for the film in good faith.
In 1999 Muldowney's complete orchestral score (24 tracks in total) was released on a special limited edition CD album under the title _Nineteen Eighty-Four: The Music of Oceania_, to commemorate the film's 15th anniversary. The CD booklet featured previously unseen production photographs and artwork as well as liner notes by Radford.

## Release and reception
As of today, [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer "Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer") owns the film's rights worldwide since they own both the Atlantic Releasing Corporation and the Virgin Films catalogues, and on their DVD release in North America in 2003, the film's colour is restored to a normal level of saturation and the Eurythmics contributions to the score were removed entirely and replaced with Muldowney's musical cues as Radford had originally intended—although both Eurythmics and Muldowney are still jointly credited in the opening and closing titles. This DVD release was quickly discontinued and currently remains out of print. This version had previously been shown by [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4") in the UK in the late 1980s. However, while the MGM DVD release of the film in the UK in 2004 features the desaturated visuals, it has the mixed Eurythmics/Muldowney soundtrack on the English- and French-language audio tracks, but the purely Muldowney soundtrack on the German- and Spanish-language audio tracks.
In 2013 the film was re-released on DVD in North America by TGG Direct on a double feature with _[Megaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaville "Megaville")_ (1990), under licence from MGM. This DVD release also features the original mix of Eurythmics/Muldowney soundtracks, as well as the theatrical desaturated colour palette. In 2015 the film was released on Blu-ray in North America by Twilight Time in a limited 3,000 copy run, again, licensed by MGM. This release features the Eurythmics/Muldowney Soundtrack on one audio channel and Muldowney's orchestral score on another, as well as keeping true to the original colour scheme.
The version of the film which has been released on [digital download](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_distribution "Digital distribution") services is the 2015 HD edition. It is available only with the original Eurythmics/Muldowney soundtrack.

## Release and reception
The 2019 [Criterion Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criterion_Collection "Criterion Collection") release of the film features the original colour scheme (with a transfer supervised by the cinematographer [Roger Deakins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Deakins "Roger Deakins")) and includes both the Eurythmics soundtrack and the original score composed by Dominic Muldowney.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-27)
It won the Best British Film of the Year award at the [Evening Standard British Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Standard_British_Film_Awards "Evening Standard British Film Awards"). It also won The [Golden Tulip Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Tulip_Award "Golden Tulip Award") at the 1985 [Istanbul International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_International_Film_Festival "Istanbul International Film Festival").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

## Release and reception
[International Video Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Video_Entertainment "International Video Entertainment") paid US$2 million for North American [home video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_video "Home video") rights, the fifth-biggest deal ever at the time.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film)#cite_note-hv-20) Their initial VHS & Betamax releases came out in 1985, with a later release in 1989. [Avid Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avid_Home_Entertainment "Avid Home Entertainment") released it on VHS in 1990 and again in 1994. In between the Avid releases, [GoodTimes Home Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GoodTimes_Home_Video "GoodTimes Home Video") released a VHS in LP mode in 1992. After acquiring ITC Entertainment Group, [PolyGram Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PolyGram_Video "PolyGram Video") released their VHS in conjunction with [Sundance Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Channel_(United_States) "Sundance Channel (United States)") in 1996.
In 2003 [MGM Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM_Home_Entertainment "MGM Home Entertainment") released it on DVD in North America and in the UK a year later; the American DVD only contains Muldowney's score while the UK DVD contains the mix of his and Eurythmics' soundtrack. TGG Direct re-released it on DVD in 2013 as a double feature with Megaville. It has been released on Blu-ray twice, first by [Twilight Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_Time_(home_video_label) "Twilight Time (home video label)") in 2015 as a limited 3,000-copy run and by [The Criterion Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Criterion_Collection "The Criterion Collection") in 2019. Both of these editions contain both the original theatrical Eurythmics score and the alternate Muldowney score.

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell "George Orwell")'s 1949 [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopia "Dystopia")[political novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_fiction "Political fiction")_[Nineteen Eighty-Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen\_Eighty-Four "Nineteen Eighty-Four")_, has been adapted for the cinema, radio, television, theatre, opera and ballet.
*   The first feature-length adaptation, [_1984_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(1956_film) "1984 (1956 film)"), was directed by [Michael Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Anderson_(director) "Michael Anderson (director)") and was released in 1956. It starred [Edmond O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond_O'Brien "Edmond O'Brien") as protagonist [Winston Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Smith_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "Winston Smith (Nineteen Eighty-Four)") with [Donald Pleasence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Pleasence "Donald Pleasence") as Mr Parsons, [Jan Sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Sterling "Jan Sterling") as [Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "Julia (Nineteen Eighty-Four)"), and [Michael Redgrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Redgrave "Michael Redgrave") as [O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Brien_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four) "O'Brien (Nineteen Eighty-Four)") (renamed O'Connor).

### _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ (1984)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Nineteen Eighty-Four (1984)")]
*   The second feature-length adaptation, [_Nineteen Eighty-Four_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film) "Nineteen Eighty-Four (1984 film)"), was directed by [Michael Radford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Radford "Michael Radford") and was released in 1984. It is a reasonably faithful adaptation of the novel and was critically acclaimed. Many of the film's scenes were shot on the actual dates mentioned in the novel. For example, the scene in which Winston Smith writes the date "April 4, 1984" in his diary was filmed on April 4, 1984. The film's soundtrack was performed by the band [Eurythmics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurythmics "Eurythmics") and a single from it, "[Sexcrime (1984)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexcrime_(Nineteen_Eighty-Four)_(song) "Sexcrime (Nineteen Eighty-Four) (song)")", was a hit in several countries. The film contained [Richard Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Burton "Richard Burton")'s last performance.

### _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ (1984)
In November 2022 it was announced that the feature-length adaptation by Finnish director [Diana Ringo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Ringo "Diana Ringo") was filmed and would be released in 2023. The film is co-produced with Russia and is the first Russian language adaptation of the novel.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-3) The film _[1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984\_(2023\_film) "1984 (2023 film)")_ was released on 15 November 2023.

### _1984_ (Unproduced Paul Greengrass project)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 1984 (Unproduced Paul Greengrass project)")]
In March 2012 it was announced that a consortium of Hollywood production companies including [Imagine Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagine_Entertainment "Imagine Entertainment") was set to reboot and make a new feature film based on the novel.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-5) Reportedly the consortium secured rights from Orwell's estate. However, no further developments were revealed for some time. In November 2015, [Paul Greengrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Greengrass "Paul Greengrass") was attached to direct with Scott Rudin and Gina Rosenblum producing and James Graham writing the screenplay. Rudin and Greengrass had also previously worked together on _[Captain Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain\_Phillips\_(film) "Captain Phillips (film)")_. [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca "Michael De Luca") would oversee production of this version of _1984_ film for the studio.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-6)

### _1984_ (Unproduced Paul Greengrass project)
In 2017, Graham began a rewrite of the film in response to the [2016 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election") and [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "First presidency of Donald Trump"), which had increased interest in the book. He claimed that the film would be released by 2019.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-8)
In 2020, Graham confirmed that the project had been postponed after they had difficulty writing a script:
> "Paul and I got very excited about it and then [we realized] it's a difficult project. The book is just so bloody perfect, we started going: 'Let's just pause for a second.' The world of surveillance and tech moves on so quickly, we just needed to have a broader view of it."[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-9)

## Television adaptations
### CBS's Studio One: _1984_ (1953)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: CBS's Studio One: 1984 (1953)")]
The [first television version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(1953_TV_program) "1984 (1953 TV program)") of _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ appeared in [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS")'s [Studio One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_One_(CBS_series) "Studio One (CBS series)") series in 1953. In it American actor [Eddie Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Albert "Eddie Albert") played Winston Smith and Canadian [Lorne Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorne_Greene "Lorne Greene") played O'Brien.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-10)

## Television adaptations
### Sunday Night Play: _1984_ (1954)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Sunday Night Play: 1984 (1954)")]
The [second television version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(UK_TV_programme) "Nineteen Eighty-Four (UK TV programme)") was adapted by [Nigel Kneale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Kneale "Nigel Kneale") for the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") as a _[Sunday-Night Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday-Night\_Play "Sunday-Night Play")_ in 1954 featuring [Peter Cushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Cushing "Peter Cushing") as Smith, [André Morell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Morell "André Morell") as O'Brien, [Yvonne Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvonne_Mitchell "Yvonne Mitchell") as Julia and [Donald Pleasence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Pleasence "Donald Pleasence") as Syme.

## Television adaptations
### The World of George Orwell: _1984_ (1965)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: The World of George Orwell: 1984 (1965)")]
Kneale's 1954 adaptation was produced again by the BBC, with some modifications in 1965, directed by [Christopher Morahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Morahan "Christopher Morahan"). Starring [David Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Buck "David Buck"), [Joseph O'Conor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_O'Conor "Joseph O'Conor"), [Jane Merrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Merrow "Jane Merrow") and [Cyril Shaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Shaps "Cyril Shaps"), it was broadcast in [BBC2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Two "BBC Two")'s _[Theatre 625](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre\_625 "Theatre 625")_ anthology series as part of a season of Orwell adaptations sub-titled _The World of George Orwell_, on 28 November 1965. Long believed lost, on 12 September 2010 it was announced in the media that a copy had been located at the American [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress"), although an approximately seven-minute segment in the middle was damaged and irrecoverable from the [NTSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTSC "NTSC") videotape. It was found amongst a hoard of over 80 lost British television episodes dating from 1957 to 1970.

## Television adaptations
### NBC: _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ (1949)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: NBC: Nineteen Eighty-Four (1949)")]
The first radio broadcast of _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ was a one-hour adaptation transmitted by the United States' [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") radio network at 9pm. on August 27, 1949 as number 55 in the series _NBC University Theater_, which adapted the world's great novels for broadcast; it starred [David Niven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Niven "David Niven") as Smith.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-11)

## Television adaptations
### NBC: _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ (1953)

Another broadcast on the NBC radio network was made by [The Theatre Guild on The Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Guild_on_the_Air_(radio_series) "Theatre Guild on the Air (radio series)") on Sunday April 26, 1953 for _[The United States Steel Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_United\_States\_Steel\_Hour "The United States Steel Hour")_ starring [Richard Widmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Widmark "Richard Widmark") as Smith, [Marian Seldes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Seldes "Marian Seldes") as Julia and [Alan Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hewitt "Alan Hewitt") as O'Brien.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-12)

## Television adaptations
### NBC: _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ (1953)
In November, the [CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation "Canadian Broadcasting Corporation") broadcast a 90-minute adaptation written by [Melwyn Breen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melwyn_Breen?action=edit&redlink=1 "Melwyn Breen (page does not exist)") and directed by [Esse Ljungh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esse_Ljungh "Esse Ljungh") as an episode of its CBC Wednesday Night program. The production featured [Bill Needles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Needles "William Needles") as Winston Smith, [Budd Knapp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budd_Knapp?action=edit&redlink=1 "Budd Knapp (page does not exist)") as Big Brother, [Kate Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Reid "Kate Reid") as Julia, Frank Peddie as Charington, and [Paul Kligman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kligman "Paul Kligman"). In April 1954, it won an [Ohio State Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Award?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ohio State Award (page does not exist)") at the 18th American Exhibition of Educational Radio-Television Programs for "powerful and significant drama, superbly presented ... an accurate, gripping projection of a book which hereby gains a vastly wider audience."[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four#cite_note-13)

### BBC: _Nineteen Eighty-Four_ (1965)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adaptations_of_Nineteen_Eighty-Four&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: BBC: Nineteen Eighty-Four (1965)")]
In the United Kingdom, the [BBC Home Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Home_Service "BBC Home Service") produced a 90-minute version with [Patrick Troughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Troughton "Patrick Troughton") as Smith and [Sylvia Syms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Syms "Sylvia Syms") as Julia, scripted by Eric Ewens and first broadcast on October 11, 1965.
